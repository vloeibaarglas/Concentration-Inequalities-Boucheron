**Central idea.** A median minimizes expected absolute deviation: $\mathbb{E}|Z - M_Z| \leq \mathbb{E}|Z - m|$ for all $m$, in particular for $m = \mathbb{E}Z$. Combining this with Jensen's inequality ($|M_Z - \mathbb{E}Z| \leq \mathbb{E}|Z - M_Z|$) and Cauchy–Schwarz ($\mathbb{E}|Z - \mathbb{E}Z| \leq \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(Z)}$) gives the bound.

**Solution.** Let $M = M_Z$ be a median of $Z$, so $\mathbb{P}\{Z \geq M\} \geq 1/2$ and $\mathbb{P}\{Z \leq M\} \geq 1/2$. Since $Z$ is square-integrable, $\mathbb{E}|Z| < \infty$.

First, $|M - \mathbb{E}Z| = |\mathbb{E}(M - Z)| \leq \mathbb{E}|Z - M|$ by Jensen's inequality.

Next, we show $M$ minimizes $f(m) = \mathbb{E}|Z - m|$ over $m \in \mathbb{R}$. Let $m > M$. Decomposing on $\{Z \leq M\}$, $\{Z \geq m\}$, $\{M < Z < m\}$:
$$|Z - m| - |Z - M| = \begin{cases} m - M & Z \leq M \\ -(m - M) & Z \geq m \\ m + M - 2Z & M < Z < m \end{cases}$$
and $|m + M - 2Z| \leq m - M$ on $\{M < Z < m\}$. Hence
\begin{align*}
f(m) - f(M) &\geq (m-M)\mathbb{P}\{Z \leq M\} - (m-M)\mathbb{P}\{Z \geq m\} - (m-M)\mathbb{P}\{M < Z < m\} \\
&= (m-M)\big[\mathbb{P}\{Z \leq M\} - \mathbb{P}\{Z > M\}\big] \\
&= (m-M)\big[2\mathbb{P}\{Z \leq M\} - 1\big] \geq 0,
\end{align*}
using $\{Z \geq m\} \cup \{M < Z < m\} = \{Z > M\}$ (disjoint) and $\mathbb{P}\{Z \leq M\} \geq 1/2$. Similarly, for $m < M$,
$$f(m) - f(M) \geq (M-m)\big[2\mathbb{P}\{Z \geq M\} - 1\big] \geq 0.$$
Thus $\mathbb{E}|Z - M| \leq \mathbb{E}|Z - \mathbb{E}Z|$.

Finally, by Cauchy–Schwarz (or Jensen), $\mathbb{E}|Z - \mathbb{E}Z| \leq \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)^2]} = \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(Z)}$.

Chaining the three inequalities:
$$|M_Z - \mathbb{E}Z| \leq \mathbb{E}|Z - M_Z| \leq \mathbb{E}|Z - \mathbb{E}Z| \leq \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(Z)}.$$
