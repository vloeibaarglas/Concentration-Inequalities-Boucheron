**Central idea.** The sub-Gaussian MGF bound controls the second-order Taylor expansion of $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(X-\mathbb{E}X)}$ at $\lambda = 0$: comparing coefficients of $\lambda^2$ as $\lambda \to 0$ forces $\mathrm{Var}(X) \le v$. (We assume $X$ centered, i.e. $\mathbb{E}X = 0$; otherwise apply the argument to $X - \mathbb{E}X$, whose variance factor is the same.)

**Solution.** Let $\mu = \mathbb{E}X$ and assume $\mu = 0$ (replacing $X$ by $X-\mu$ otherwise). Sub-Gaussianity gives $\mathbb{E}[e^{t|X|}] < \infty$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$, since $e^{t|X|} \le e^{tX} + e^{-tX}$ and both terms have finite expectation by the MGF bound; in particular $\mathrm{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}[X^2] < \infty$.

For $|\lambda| \le 1$, Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives
$$e^{\lambda X} = 1 + \lambda X + \lambda^2 X^2 \int_0^1 (1-s)\,e^{s\lambda X}\,ds.$$
Taking expectations and using $\mathbb{E}X = 0$,
$$\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda X}] = 1 + \lambda^2\,\mathbb{E}\left[X^2 \int_0^1 (1-s)\,e^{s\lambda X}\,ds\right].$$
As $\lambda \to 0$, the bracket converges almost surely to $X^2\int_0^1(1-s)\,ds = X^2/2$, and is dominated by $X^2 e^{|X|}$ (since $|s\lambda X| \le |X|$ for $|\lambda| \le 1$), which is integrable because $X^2e^{|X|} \le e^{2|X|}$ (using $x^2 \le e^{|x|}$ for all $x$). By dominated convergence,
$$\mathbb{E}\left[X^2 \int_0^1 (1-s)\,e^{s\lambda X}\,ds\right] \longrightarrow \frac{\mathbb{E}[X^2]}{2} = \frac{\mathrm{Var}(X)}{2}.$$
Hence
$$\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda X}] = 1 + \frac{\lambda^2}{2}\,\mathrm{Var}(X) + o(\lambda^2) \qquad (\lambda \to 0).$$
The sub-Gaussian hypothesis gives $\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda X}] \le e^{v\lambda^2/2} = 1 + \frac{v\lambda^2}{2} + o(\lambda^2)$. Therefore
$$\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\,\mathrm{Var}(X) + o(\lambda^2) \le \frac{v\lambda^2}{2} + o(\lambda^2),$$
and dividing by $\lambda^2/2$ and letting $\lambda \to 0$ yields $\mathrm{Var}(X) \le v$.
