**Central idea.** The $n$ bin counts $B_i$ are multinomial (hence dependent), but each is marginally Binomial$(m,1/n)$, and the maximal-inequality mgf step $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda\max_i B_i}\leq\sum_i\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda B_i}$ uses only marginal mgfs — no independence is needed. The Exercise 2.19 optimization therefore applies verbatim with $\mu=m/n$. The two special cases are direct substitutions; the last uses the lower bound $W(z)\geq\log z-\log\log z$ ($z\geq e$) from Exercise 2.18.

**Solution.** Let $B_i$ be the number of balls in bin $i$; marginally $B_i\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(m,1/n)$, and $M=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}B_i$ (so $0\leq M\leq m$). For $\lambda>0$,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda M}=\mathbb{E}\max_i e^{\lambda B_i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda B_i}=n\,\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda B_1}\leq n\,e^{\frac{m}{n}(e^{\lambda}-1)},$$
using the binomial mgf bound $(1-\frac1n+\frac1n e^{\lambda})^m\leq e^{\frac{m}{n}(e^{\lambda}-1)}$ from Exercise 2.19. Hence, by Jensen's inequality as in 2.19 (the maximal inequality, Theorem 2.5, cited as Theorem 2.4),
$$\mathbb{E}M\leq\inf_{\lambda>0}\frac{\log n+\frac{m}{n}(e^{\lambda}-1)}{\lambda}=\frac{m}{n}e^{1+W((\log(n)-m/n)/(em/n))},$$
by the identical optimization with $\mu=m/n$.

If $m=cn\log n$, then $\mu=m/n=c\log n$ and
$$\frac{\log n-\mu}{e\mu}=\frac{\log n-c\log n}{ec\log n}=\frac{1-c}{ec},$$
so $\mathbb{E}M\leq c\log(n)\,e^{1+W((1-c)/(ce))}$.

If $m=cn$, then $\mu=c$ and, using $\mu e^{1+w}=\frac{\log n-\mu}{w}$ for $w=W\!\left(\frac{\log n-\mu}{e\mu}\right)$,
$$\mathbb{E}M\leq ce^{1+w}=\frac{\log n-c}{w},\qquad w=W(z),\; z=\frac{\log n-c}{ec}.$$
By Exercise 2.18, $W(z)\geq\log z-\log\log z$ for $z\geq e$ (the hypothesis $z\geq e$ is exactly what the Exercise 2.18 estimate requires). This requires $\frac{\log n-c}{ec}\geq e$, i.e. $\log n\geq(1+e^2)c$ — see the remark below. Under this condition, $\log z-\log\log z$ is positive and increasing for $z\geq e$ (it equals $1$ at $z=e$), and since $\log n>c$,
$$\mathbb{E}M\leq\frac{\log n-c}{w}\leq\frac{\log n-c}{\log z-\log\log z}=\frac{\log(n)-c}{\log((\log(n)-c)/(ec))-\log(\log((\log(n)-c)/(ec)))}.$$

*Remark.* The hypothesis "$\log n>c$" as stated does not suffice for the last conclusion: the right-hand side is not even real unless $\frac{\log n-c}{ec}>1$, and the appeal to Exercise 2.18 needs $\frac{\log n-c}{ec}\geq e$. The bound holds as written whenever $\log n\geq(1+e^2)c$.
