**Central idea.** Forward: the variance bound comes from Fatou applied to $(\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X}-1)/\lambda^2$ (no finite-variance assumption needed); moments follow from Bernstein's tail bound via $\mathbb{E}[Z^q]=q\int_0^\infty t^{q-1}P(Z>t)\,dt$ split into Gaussian and exponential regimes. Converse: write $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X} = 1+\mathbb{E}[\phi(\lambda X)]$ with $\phi(x) = e^x-1-x$ so the linear term vanishes exactly ($\mathbb{E}X=0$); use $\phi(x)\le x^2/2$ for $x\le0$ and the power series for $x\ge0$, summing with $q!\ge(q/e)^q$.

**Solution.** Recall $X\in\Gamma_+(v,c)$, centered, means $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X}\le \frac{\lambda^2v}{2(1-c\lambda)}$ for $0<\lambda<1/c$.

*Forward.* For the variance bound, let $\phi(u) = e^u-1-u \ge 0$. Since $\mathbb{E}X = 0$,
$$\frac{\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X}-1}{\lambda^2} = \mathbb{E}\frac{\phi(\lambda X)}{\lambda^2} \le \frac{e^{\lambda^2v/(2(1-c\lambda))}-1}{\lambda^2} \xrightarrow[\lambda\downarrow0]{} \frac{v}{2}.$$
As $\phi(\lambda X)/\lambda^2 \to X^2/2$ pointwise, Fatou gives $\mathbb{E}[X^2]/2 \le v/2$, i.e. $\mathrm{Var}(X) \le v$ (no a priori finite-variance assumption needed).

The mgf bound implies Bernstein's inequality $P(X>t)\le\exp(-\tfrac{t^2}{2(v+ct)})$ for $t>0$ (standard Chernoff optimization). Since $\tfrac{t^2}{2(v+ct)}\ge\min(\tfrac{t^2}{4v},\tfrac{t}{4c})$ (if $ct\le v$ then $v+ct\le2v$; else $v+ct<2ct$),
$$P(X_+>t)\le e^{-t^2/(4v)}+e^{-t/(4c)}.$$
For $q\ge2$, $\mathbb{E}[X_+^q]=q\int_0^\infty t^{q-1}P(X_+>t)\,dt$, so with $(a+b)^{1/q}\le a^{1/q}+b^{1/q}$,
$$(\mathbb{E}[X_+^q])^{1/q}\le \Big[q\!\int_0^\infty\! t^{q-1}e^{-t^2/(4v)}dt\Big]^{1/q}+\Big[q\!\int_0^\infty\! t^{q-1}e^{-t/(4c)}dt\Big]^{1/q}.$$
First integral: $u=t^2/(4v)$ gives $q2^{q-1}v^{q/2}\Gamma(q/2)\le q2^{q-1}(qv)^{q/2}$ (using $\Gamma(q/2)\le q^{q/2}$); $1/q$ power $\le4\sqrt{qv}$ for $q\ge2$. Second: $q(4c)^q\Gamma(q)=(4c)^qq!$; $1/q$ power $\le4cq$ (using $q!\le q^q$). Hence $(\mathbb{E}[X_+^q])^{1/q}\le4(\sqrt{qv}+cq)$, i.e. $C=4$.

*Converse.* Write $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X} = 1 + \mathbb{E}[\phi(\lambda X)]$ with $\phi(x) = e^x - 1 - x$; the linear term vanishes exactly since $\mathbb{E}X = 0$. For $x \le 0$, $\phi(x) \le x^2/2$; for $x \ge 0$, $\phi(x) = \sum_{q\ge2}x^q/q!$. Hence, with $\mathbb{E}[X^2] = \mathrm{Var}(X) \le A$,
$$\mathbb{E}[\phi(\lambda X)] \le \frac{\lambda^2}{2}\mathbb{E}[X^2\mathbf{1}_{X\le0}] + \sum_{q\ge2}\frac{\lambda^q\,\mathbb{E}[X_+^q]}{q!} \le \frac{\lambda^2A}{2} + \sum_{q\ge2}\frac{\lambda^q(\sqrt{qA}+Bq)^q}{q!}.$$
Now $(\sqrt{qA}+Bq)^q \le 2^{q-1}((qA)^{q/2} + (qB)^q)$, and $q! \ge (q/e)^q$. The $B$-terms give $\frac12\sum_{q\ge2}(2eB\lambda)^q \le \frac{2e^2B^2\lambda^2}{1-2eB\lambda}$ for $0 < \lambda < 1/(2eB)$. For the $A$-terms, $q!\ge(q/e)^q$ gives $\frac{\lambda^q2^{q-1}(qA)^{q/2}}{q!}\le\frac12\frac{w^q}{q^{q/2}}$ with $w=2e\lambda\sqrt A$. Since $q!\le q^q$, $\frac{w^q}{q^{q/2}}\le\frac{w^qq^{q/2}}{q!}$, and splitting even and odd terms,
$$\sum_{q\ge2}\frac{w^qq^{q/2}}{q!}\le\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(2w^2)^k}{k!}+2w\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(2w^2)^k}{k!}=(1+2w)(e^{2w^2}-1),\qquad w\ge0,$$
using $(2k)!\ge k!\,k^k$ for the even terms ($q=2k$) and $(2k+1)!\ge k!(k+1)^{k+1}$ for the odd terms ($q=2k+1\ge3$). A calculus check ($h(0)=h'(0)=0$, $h''\ge0$) gives $(1+2w)(e^{2w^2}-1)\le e^{6w^2}-1$ for $w\ge0$. Hence the $A$-terms are at most $\frac12(e^{6w^2}-1)=\frac12(e^{24e^2A\lambda^2}-1)$, valid for every $\lambda\ge0$. With $z=A\lambda^2$,
$$1+\frac{\lambda^2A}{2}+\text{($A$-terms)}\le1+\frac{z}{2}+\frac{e^{24e^2z}-1}{2}=\frac{1+e^{24e^2z}}{2}+\frac{z}{2}\le e^{(24e^2+1/2)z},$$
using $\frac{1+e^u}{2}\le e^u$ and $1+v\le e^v$. Thus $1+\frac{\lambda^2A}{2}+\text{($A$-terms)}\le e^{K_1A\lambda^2}$ for all $\lambda\ge0$ with $K_1=24e^2+\frac12$. Consequently, for $0<\lambda<\frac1{2eB}$,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X}\le e^{K_1A\lambda^2}+\frac{2e^2B^2\lambda^2}{1-2eB\lambda}\le\exp\!\Big(K_1A\lambda^2+\frac{2e^2B^2\lambda^2}{1-2eB\lambda}\Big)\le\exp\!\Big(\frac{\lambda^2v}{2(1-c\lambda)}\Big),$$
with $c=2eB$ and $v=(48e^2+1)A+4e^2B^2$, using $1+u\le e^u$ and $K_1A\lambda^2\le K_1A\lambda^2/(1-c\lambda)$. Taking $C=48e^2+1$ gives $v\le C(A+B^2)$ and $c\le CB$, i.e.\ $X\in\Gamma_+(v,c)$. ∎
