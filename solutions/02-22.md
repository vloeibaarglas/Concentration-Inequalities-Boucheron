**Central idea.** For the exponential law the moments come from the gamma integral $\int_0^\infty u^q e^{-u}\,du = q!$ after the change of variables $u = ax$. For a sub-exponential $X$, expand the mgf as a power series and use monotone convergence (all terms nonnegative since $X \geq 0$) to read off each moment from the series coefficients; each single term is bounded by the whole sum, and evaluating at $\lambda = a/2$ gives the constant $2^{q+1}$.

**Solution.** Let $X$ have exponential distribution with parameter $a > 0$, i.e. density $ae^{-ax}$ on $x \geq 0$. For a positive integer $q$,
$$
\mathbb{E}[X^q] = \int_0^\infty x^q a e^{-ax}\,dx.
$$
Substituting $u = ax$ ($dx = du/a$),
$$
\mathbb{E}[X^q] = a\int_0^\infty \left(\frac{u}{a}\right)^q e^{-u}\,\frac{du}{a}
= \frac{1}{a^q}\int_0^\infty u^q e^{-u}\,du.
$$
Writing $I_q = \int_0^\infty u^q e^{-u}\,du$, integration by parts gives
$$
I_q = \Bigl[-u^q e^{-u}\Bigr]_0^\infty + q\int_0^\infty u^{q-1}e^{-u}\,du = qI_{q-1},
$$
since $u^q e^{-u} \to 0$ as $u \to \infty$ and vanishes at $u = 0$ for $q \geq 1$. With $I_0 = \int_0^\infty e^{-u}\,du = 1$, induction yields $I_q = q!$. Hence $\mathbb{E}[X^q] = q!/a^q$.

Now suppose $X \geq 0$ is sub-exponential: $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X} \leq 1/(1-\lambda/a)$ for all $0 < \lambda < a$. Fix such a $\lambda$. Since $X \geq 0$, the partial sums of $e^{\lambda X} = \sum_{q=0}^\infty \lambda^q X^q/q!$ are nonnegative and increase monotonically to $e^{\lambda X}$, so by monotone convergence (in $[0,\infty]$),
$$
\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X} = \sum_{q=0}^\infty \frac{\lambda^q}{q!}\,\mathbb{E}[X^q].
$$
Combining with the sub-exponential bound,
$$
\sum_{q=0}^\infty \frac{\lambda^q}{q!}\,\mathbb{E}[X^q] \leq \frac{1}{1-\lambda/a} < \infty,
\qquad 0 < \lambda < a.
$$
Every term of the series is nonnegative, so each term is bounded by the sum: for every positive integer $q$ and every $0 < \lambda < a$,
$$
\frac{\lambda^q}{q!}\,\mathbb{E}[X^q] \leq \frac{1}{1-\lambda/a},
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\mathbb{E}[X^q] \leq \frac{q!}{\lambda^q}\,\frac{1}{1-\lambda/a}.
$$
(The finiteness of the right-hand side also shows $\mathbb{E}[X^q] < \infty$.) Taking $\lambda = a/2$, for which $1 - \lambda/a = 1/2$,
$$
\mathbb{E}[X^q] \leq \frac{q!}{(a/2)^q}\cdot 2 = 2^{q+1}\frac{q!}{a^q},
$$
for every positive integer $q$, as required.
