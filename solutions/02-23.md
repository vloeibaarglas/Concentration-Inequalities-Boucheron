**Central idea.** Expand $e^{\lambda X}$ as a power series and take expectations term by term. The hypothesis bounds each signed moment $\mathbb{E}[X^q]$, turning the series into a geometric one. The technical work is justifying the interchange: dominate the partial sums by $e^{\lambda|X|}$ and show it is integrable using the even-moment bounds plus Cauchy–Schwarz for the odd moments.

**Solution.** Fix $0 < \lambda < a$. For even $q \geq 2$, $|X|^q = X^q \geq 0$, so the hypothesis gives $\mathbb{E}|X|^q = \mathbb{E}[X^q] \leq q!/a^q < \infty$. For odd $q \geq 1$, Lyapunov's inequality gives $\mathbb{E}|X|^q \leq (\mathbb{E}|X|^{q+1})^{q/(q+1)} = (\mathbb{E}[X^{q+1}])^{q/(q+1)} \leq ((q+1)!/a^{q+1})^{q/(q+1)} < \infty$. Hence all absolute moments of $X$ are finite.

We show $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda|X|} < \infty$. By Tonelli's theorem,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda|X|} = \sum_{q=0}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda^q}{q!}\,\mathbb{E}|X|^q.$$
The even terms satisfy $\lambda^{2k}\mathbb{E}|X|^{2k}/(2k)! \leq (\lambda/a)^{2k}$. For the odd terms, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\mathbb{E}|X|^{2k+1} \leq \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[X^{2k}]\,\mathbb{E}[X^{2k+2}]} \leq \sqrt{(2k)!(2k+2)!}/a^{2k+1}$, so
$$\frac{\lambda^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\,\mathbb{E}|X|^{2k+1} \leq \left(\frac{\lambda}{a}\right)^{2k+1}\frac{\sqrt{(2k)!(2k+2)!}}{(2k+1)!} = \left(\frac{\lambda}{a}\right)^{2k+1}\sqrt{\frac{2k+2}{2k+1}} \leq \sqrt{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{a}\right)^{2k+1}.$$
Since $\lambda/a < 1$, both the even and odd series converge, so $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda|X|} < \infty$.

Now $S_N := \sum_{q=0}^{N} \lambda^q X^q/q! \to e^{\lambda X}$ pointwise, and $|S_N| \leq e^{\lambda|X|}$, which is integrable. By dominated convergence,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X} = \sum_{q=0}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda^q}{q!}\,\mathbb{E}[X^q],$$
the series converging absolutely since $|\lambda^q\mathbb{E}[X^q]/q!| \leq \lambda^q\mathbb{E}|X|^q/q!$ is summable. For $q \geq 1$, the hypothesis and $\lambda^q/q! > 0$ give $\lambda^q\mathbb{E}[X^q]/q! \leq (\lambda/a)^q$; for $q = 0$ the term is $\mathbb{E}[1] = 1 = (\lambda/a)^0$. Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X} \leq \sum_{q=0}^{\infty} \left(\frac{\lambda}{a}\right)^q = \frac{1}{1-\lambda/a},$$
the geometric series converging because $\lambda < a$. This is the sub-exponential moment-generating bound (cf. Exercise 2.22).
