**Central idea.** The hypothesis $\mathbb{E}[(X-a)_+] \leq \mathbb{E}[(Y-a)_+]$ for all $a$ is the increasing convex order, so any upper bound on $\mathbb{P}\{X \geq t\}$ obtained from $\mathbb{E}[(X-s)_+]$ via Markov's inequality transfers to $Y$, whose exponential tail integrates explicitly. The shift $s = t - 1/b$ is chosen to optimize the resulting bound, and the $e^1$ factor is the price of that optimization.

**Solution.** Let $t \geq 0$. If $t < 1/b$, then $1 - bt > 0$, so $\kappa e^{1-bt} > \kappa \geq 1 \geq \mathbb{P}\{X \geq t\}$ and there is nothing to prove. Assume henceforth $t \geq 1/b$ and set $s := t - 1/b \geq 0$.

Since $t - s = 1/b > 0$, we have $\{X \geq t\} = \{(X-s)_+ \geq t-s\}$: indeed, $X \geq t$ implies $X - s \geq t - s > 0$, so $(X-s)_+ = X - s \geq t - s$; conversely, $(X-s)_+ \geq t - s > 0$ forces $X - s > 0$ and hence $X \geq t$. By Markov's inequality and the hypothesis,
$$\mathbb{P}\{X \geq t\} = \mathbb{P}\{(X-s)_+ \geq t-s\} \leq \frac{\mathbb{E}[(X-s)_+]}{t-s} \leq \frac{\mathbb{E}[(Y-s)_+]}{t-s}.$$
(The expectations are finite: for $s \geq 0$, $\mathbb{E}[(Y-s)_+] \leq \int_s^{\infty} \kappa e^{-bu}\,du < \infty$ by the layer-cake identity below, hence $\mathbb{E}[(X-s)_+] < \infty$ too.)

By the layer-cake representation, for any real $s$,
$$\mathbb{E}[(Y-s)_+] = \int_0^{\infty} \mathbb{P}\{(Y-s)_+ > v\}\,dv = \int_s^{\infty} \mathbb{P}\{Y \geq u\}\,du.$$
Since $s \geq 0$, the tail bound on $Y$ applies throughout the range of integration:
$$\mathbb{E}[(Y-s)_+] \leq \int_s^{\infty} \kappa e^{-bu}\,du = \frac{\kappa e^{-bs}}{b}.$$
Therefore, with $t - s = 1/b$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{X \geq t\} \leq \frac{\kappa e^{-bs}/b}{1/b} = \kappa e^{-bs} = \kappa e^{-b(t-1/b)} = \kappa e^{1-bt}.$$

*Remark.* The choice $s = t - 1/b$ is optimal: minimizing $\phi(s) := e^{-bs}/(t-s)$ over $s \in [0,t)$ gives $\phi'(s) = e^{-bs}(1-b(t-s))/(t-s)^2 = 0$ exactly at $s = t-1/b$. This is Panchenko's argument (Panchenko, 2003).
