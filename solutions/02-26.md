**Central idea.** As stated the claim is **false**: with $g$ allowed to change sign, no conclusion follows. A two-point counterexample kills it. The intended statement needs $g\geq0$ (or $\mathbb{E}[h(X)g(X)]\geq0$); then tilting by $h$ and applying Chebyshev's association inequality to $\phi=1-f$ and $g$ gives the result.

**Solution.** *The statement as written is false.* Take $X\in\{1,2\}$ with $\mathbb{P}\{X=1\}=\mathbb{P}\{X=2\}=1/2$, $h\equiv1$, $f(1)=1$, $f(2)=0$ (nonnegative, nonincreasing), and $g(1)=-10$, $g(2)=-9$ (nondecreasing, real-valued). Then $\mathbb{E}[h(X)f(X)]=1/2\leq1=\mathbb{E}[h(X)]$, but
$$\mathbb{E}[f(X)g(X)h(X)]=-5>-9.5=\mathbb{E}[h(X)g(X)],$$
contradicting the claim. The sign of $g$ is the obstruction.

*Corrected statement.* Assume additionally $\mathbb{E}[h(X)g(X)]\geq0$ (this holds in particular if $g\geq0$), and that the expectations below are absolutely convergent. Then $\mathbb{E}[f(X)g(X)h(X)]\leq\mathbb{E}[h(X)g(X)]$.

*Proof.* If $\mathbb{E}[h(X)]=0$ then $h(X)=0$ a.s. (as $h\geq0$), and both sides equal $0$. Otherwise, define the probability measure $d\mu=h\,d\mathbb{P}/\mathbb{E}[h]$. The claim is equivalent to $\mathbb{E}_\mu[fg]\leq\mathbb{E}_\mu[g]$. Now $\mathbb{E}_\mu[f]=\mathbb{E}[hf]/\mathbb{E}[h]\leq1$, so $\phi:=1-f$ is nondecreasing with $\mathbb{E}_\mu[\phi]\geq0$. For independent copies $X,X'\sim\mu$,
$$\mathbb{E}_\mu[\phi g]-\mathbb{E}_\mu[\phi]\mathbb{E}_\mu[g]=\tfrac12\,\mathbb{E}_{\mu\otimes\mu}\big[(\phi(X)-\phi(X'))(g(X)-g(X'))\big]\geq0,$$
since $\phi$ and $g$ are both nondecreasing, so the integrand is a.s. nonnegative (this is Chebyshev's association inequality, Theorem 2.14). Hence
$$\mathbb{E}_\mu[(1-f)g]\geq\mathbb{E}_\mu[1-f]\,\mathbb{E}_\mu[g]\geq0,$$
using $\mathbb{E}_\mu[1-f]\geq0$ and $\mathbb{E}_\mu[g]=\mathbb{E}[hg]/\mathbb{E}[h]\geq0$. That is, $\mathbb{E}_\mu[g]\geq\mathbb{E}_\mu[fg]$, i.e. $\mathbb{E}[h(X)g(X)]\geq\mathbb{E}[f(X)g(X)h(X)]$. ∎
