**Central idea.** As stated the claim is **false** without centering: a positive mean shifts the whole sum and no $n$-independent $L$ can work (explicit Exp$(1)$ counterexample below). Under $\mathbb{E}[X_i]=0$, the tail $\mathbb{P}\{|X_i|\geq u\}\leq e^{-u^p}$ yields the mgf bound $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Y_i}\leq C(\lambda^2+|\lambda|^q)$ for all $\lambda$ ($q=p/(p-1)$) — by power series for $|\lambda|\leq1$ and a Laplace-type integral estimate for $|\lambda|\geq1$ — and Chernoff optimization over $\lambda$ produces exactly the $\min(t^2/\|s\|_2^2,\,t^p/\|s\|_q^p)$ exponent. The case $p=1$ is Bernstein's inequality.

**Solution.** *The statement needs $\mathbb{E}[X_i]=0$.* Counterexample: let $p=1$, $X_i\sim$ Exp$(1)$ iid (so $\mathbb{P}\{|X_i|\geq u\}=e^{-u}$), $s_i\equiv1$. Then $\mathbb{E}[X_i]=1$, $Z=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i$, and for $t=n/2$, $\mathbb{P}\{Z\geq n/2\}\to1$ by the law of large numbers, whereas the claimed bound gives $L\exp(-\frac1L\min(n/4,n/2))\to0$. No $L$ independent of $n$ works. We henceforth assume the $X_i$ are centered; write $Y_i=X_i$.

*Moment bound.* For $k\geq1$,
$$\mathbb{E}|Y_i|^k=\int_0^\infty ku^{k-1}\mathbb{P}\{|Y_i|\geq u\}\,du\leq\int_0^\infty ku^{k-1}e^{-u^p}du=\frac{k}{p}\Gamma\!\Big(\frac{k}{p}\Big)=:m_k,$$
via $v=u^p$. In particular $v:=\mathbb{E}[Y_i^2]\leq m_2<\infty$. For $p>1$, $\sum_{k\geq1}m_k/k!<\infty$ by the ratio test, since $\frac{m_{k+1}/(k+1)!}{m_k/k!}=\frac{\Gamma((k+1)/p)}{\Gamma(k/p)}\frac1k\lesssim k^{1/p-1}\to0$ (using $\Gamma(x+\alpha)\lesssim x^\alpha\Gamma(x)$ for $\alpha=1/p\in(0,1)$). (For $p=1$, $m_k=k!$ and the series diverges; that case is handled separately below.)

*Case $p=1$ (Bernstein).* Here $m_k=k!$, so for $|\lambda|<1$,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Y_i}\leq1+\sum_{k=2}^\infty|\lambda|^k=1+\frac{\lambda^2}{1-|\lambda|}\leq\exp\!\Big(\frac{\lambda^2}{1-|\lambda|}\Big).$$
Chernoff with $\lambda=\min\big(\frac{t}{4\|s\|_2^2},\frac1{4\|s\|_\infty}\big)<1/\|s\|_\infty$ gives
$$\mathbb{P}\Big\{\sum s_iY_i\geq t\Big\}\leq\exp\!\Big(-\lambda t+\frac{\lambda^2\|s\|_2^2}{1-\lambda\|s\|_\infty}\Big)\leq\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac16\min\!\Big(\frac{t^2}{\|s\|_2^2},\frac{t}{\|s\|_\infty}\Big)\Big),$$
the two cases $\lambda=t/(4\|s\|_2^2)$ and $\lambda=1/(4\|s\|_\infty)$ each yielding one term. Since $q=\infty$ for $p=1$, $\|s\|_q=\|s\|_\infty$, matching the claim.

*Case $p>1$: mgf bound for all $\lambda$.* Let $q=p/(p-1)$. For $|\lambda|\leq1$, since $\mathbb{E}[Y_i]=0$,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Y_i}\leq1+\frac{v\lambda^2}{2}+\sum_{k=3}^\infty\frac{|\lambda|^k m_k}{k!}.$$
Split the sum at $\max(3,q)$: for $3\leq k<q$, $|\lambda|^k\leq|\lambda|^3\leq\lambda^2$; for $k\geq q$, $|\lambda|^k\leq|\lambda|^q$. With $\sum m_k/k!<\infty$,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Y_i}\leq1+A\lambda^2+B|\lambda|^q\leq\exp(A\lambda^2+B|\lambda|^q),\qquad|\lambda|\leq1,$$
for $A,B$ depending only on $p$. For $|\lambda|\geq1$,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Y_i}\leq\mathbb{E}e^{|\lambda||Y_i|}\leq1+|\lambda|\int_0^\infty e^{|\lambda|u-u^p}du.$$
With $u=|\lambda|^{q-1}v$ (using $(q-1)p=q$), the integral equals $|\lambda|^q\int_0^\infty e^{c(v-v^p)}dv$ with $c=|\lambda|^q\geq1$. Since $\int_0^2e^{c(v-v^p)}dv\leq2e^{cm_0}$ ($m_0=\max_{[0,2]}(v-v^p)>0$) and, for $v\geq2$, $v-v^p\leq-(2^{p-1}-1)v$, giving $\int_2^\infty\leq1/(cc_p)$, we get $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Y_i}\leq C_1e^{C_2|\lambda|^q}$, i.e. $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Y_i}\leq C|\lambda|^q$ for $|\lambda|\geq1$. Combined,
$$\log\mathbb{E}\exp(\lambda Y_i)\leq C\big(\lambda^2+|\lambda|^q\big)\quad\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\tag{*}$$
with $C=C(p)$.

*Chernoff optimization.* For $t>0$ (else trivial) and $s\neq0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^n s_iY_i\geq t\Big\}\leq\inf_{\lambda\geq0}\exp\!\big(-\lambda t+C\lambda^2\|s\|_2^2+C\lambda^q\|s\|_q^q\big).$$
Take $\lambda=\min(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$ with $\lambda_1=\frac{t}{4C\|s\|_2^2}$ and $\lambda_2=\big(\frac{t}{4C\|s\|_q^q}\big)^{1/(q-1)}$. Since $\lambda\leq\lambda_1$ gives $C\lambda^2\|s\|_2^2\leq\lambda t/4$ and $\lambda\leq\lambda_2$ gives $C\lambda^q\|s\|_q^q\leq\lambda t/4$, the exponent is at most $-\lambda t/2$. Moreover
$$\frac{\lambda t}{2}=\frac12\min\!\Big(\frac{t^2}{4C\|s\|_2^2},\,t\Big(\frac{t}{4C\|s\|_q^q}\Big)^{\!1/(q-1)}\Big),\qquad t\Big(\frac{t}{\|s\|_q^q}\Big)^{\!1/(q-1)}=\frac{t^p}{\|s\|_q^p}$$
because $\frac{q}{q-1}=p$. Hence
$$\mathbb{P}\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^n s_iY_i\geq t\Big\}\leq\exp\!\Big(-c\min\!\Big(\frac{t^2}{\|s\|_2^2},\frac{t^p}{\|s\|_q^p}\Big)\Big)$$
for $c=c(p)>0$, which is of the required form $L\exp(-\frac1L\min(\cdots))$ with $L=\max(1,1/c)$.

*Remark.* The constants $A,B,C,c$ depend on $p$ and blow up as $p\downarrow1$; this is permitted since $L$ may depend on $p$. For a two-sided bound on $|\sum s_iX_i|$, apply the result to $-X_i$ (which satisfy the same hypotheses) and double $L$. ∎
