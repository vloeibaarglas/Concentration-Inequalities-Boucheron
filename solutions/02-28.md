**Central idea.** The mgf of the Gumbel law is out of reach directly, but Rényi's representation writes $\max(Y_1,\dots,Y_n)$ of iid Exp$(1)$ variables as $\sum_{i=1}^n E_i/i$, a sum of *independent* variables. Means, variances, and mgf bounds all pass through this sum explicitly; convergence in distribution plus uniform integrability (from bounded 4th moments) then transfers them to the Gumbel limit. The mgf bound itself needs only Fatou's lemma, not uniform integrability.

**Erratum.** The statement's range "for all $\lambda \ge 0$" is wrong as printed (the hint's "$\max(X_1,\dots,X_n)$" is also a typo for $\max(Y_1,\dots,Y_n)$). Here $\mathrm{Var}(X) = \pi^2/6$ and $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X} = \Gamma(1-\lambda)$ only for $\lambda < 1$; for $\lambda \ge 1$, $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(X-\mathbb{E}X)} = +\infty$. For $\lambda > 1$ the printed right side $\mathrm{Var}(X)\lambda^2/(2(1-\lambda))$ is finite and negative — e.g. at $\lambda = 2$ it equals $(\pi^2/6)\cdot 4/(2\cdot(-1)) = -\pi^2/3 \approx -3.29$ — so the inequality is false there; at $\lambda = 1$ the right side is undefined. The corrected statement, proved below, is the bound for $0 \le \lambda < 1$ (sub-gamma with variance factor $\pi^2/6$ and scale $1$).

**Solution.** Let $Y_1,\dots,Y_n$ be iid Exp$(1)$ and $M_n=\max(Y_1,\dots,Y_n)$. By Rényi's representation (given in the hint),
$$M_n \stackrel{d}{=} \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{E_i}{i},$$
where the $E_i$ are iid Exp$(1)$, and $M_n-\log n \Rightarrow X$ where $X$ is Gumbel. (The hint's "$\max(X_1,\dots,X_n)$" is a typo for $\max(Y_1,\dots,Y_n)$.) Hence, with $H_n=\sum_{i=1}^n 1/i$,
$$\mathbb{E}[M_n]=H_n,\qquad \mathrm{Var}(M_n)=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{i^2},$$
using $\mathbb{E}[E_i]=1$, $\mathrm{Var}(E_i)=1$, and independence.

*Mean.* $\mathbb{E}[(M_n-\log n)^2]=\sum_{i=1}^n 1/i^2+(H_n-\log n)^2\leq \pi^2/6+C<\infty$ uniformly in $n$, since $H_n-\log n\to\gamma$ is bounded. Bounded second moments imply uniform integrability, so with $M_n-\log n\Rightarrow X$,
$$\mathbb{E}[X]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[M_n-\log n]=\lim_{n\to\infty}(H_n-\log n)=\gamma,$$
the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

*Variance.* Put $Z_i=(E_i-1)/i$ (centered, independent). Then $M_n-\log n=\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i+(H_n-\log n)$, and
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i\Big)^4\Big]=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}[Z_i^4]+6\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}[Z_i^2]\mathbb{E}[Z_j^2],$$
odd cross-terms vanishing. Since $\mathbb{E}[(E_1-1)^4]=9$ and $\mathbb{E}[Z_i^2]=1/i^2$,
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i\Big)^4\Big]=9\sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{i^4}+3\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{i^2}\Big)^2-3\sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{i^4}\leq C<\infty$$
uniformly in $n$. Hence $\sup_n\mathbb{E}[(M_n-\log n)^4]<\infty$, so $(M_n-\log n)^2$ is uniformly integrable and
$$\mathbb{E}[X^2]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[(M_n-\log n)^2]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{i^2}+(H_n-\log n)^2\Big)=\frac{\pi^2}{6}+\gamma^2,$$
using $\sum_{i=1}^\infty 1/i^2=\pi^2/6$. Therefore $\mathrm{Var}(X)=\pi^2/6$.

*Sub-gamma mgf bound.* For $E_1\sim$ Exp$(1)$ and $t<1$, $\mathbb{E}[e^{t(E_1-1)}]=e^{-t}/(1-t)$, and for $t\in[0,1)$,
$$-t-\log(1-t)=\sum_{k=2}^\infty\frac{t^k}{k}\leq\frac12\sum_{k=2}^\infty t^k=\frac{t^2}{2(1-t)}.$$
For $\lambda\in[0,1)$ and every $i\geq1$ (so $\lambda/i<1$),
$$\log\mathbb{E}\exp\!\Big(\lambda\frac{E_i-1}{i}\Big)\leq\frac{(\lambda/i)^2}{2(1-\lambda/i)}\leq\frac{\lambda^2/i^2}{2(1-\lambda)}.$$
By independence and Rényi's representation,
$$\log\mathbb{E}\exp(\lambda(M_n-\mathbb{E}M_n))\leq\frac{\lambda^2}{2(1-\lambda)}\sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{i^2}\leq\frac{\lambda^2}{2(1-\lambda)}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{6},\qquad 0\leq\lambda<1.$$
Now $M_n-\mathbb{E}M_n=(M_n-\log n)-(H_n-\log n)\Rightarrow X-\gamma=X-\mathbb{E}X$ by Slutsky's theorem. Since $x\mapsto e^{\lambda x}$ is continuous and nonnegative, Fatou's lemma gives
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(X-\mathbb{E}X)}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(M_n-\mathbb{E}M_n)},$$
hence
$$\log\mathbb{E}\exp(\lambda(X-\mathbb{E}X))\leq\frac{\mathrm{Var}(X)\,\lambda^2}{2(1-\lambda)},\qquad 0\leq\lambda<1.$$

*Remark on the range.* As noted in the Erratum, the bound cannot extend to $\lambda\geq1$: the left side is $+\infty$ there, while the right side is negative for $\lambda>1$ ($-\pi^2/3\approx-3.29$ at $\lambda=2$) and undefined at $\lambda=1$. The correct range is $0\leq\lambda<1$, i.e., $X$ is sub-gamma with variance factor $\mathrm{Var}(X)=\pi^2/6$ and scale $1$.
