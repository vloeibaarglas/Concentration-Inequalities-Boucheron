**Central idea.** (Paley–Zygmund.) Split $\mathbb{E}Y$ according to whether $Y \geq a\mathbb{E}Y$: the small part contributes at most $a\mathbb{E}Y$, and the large part is controlled by Cauchy–Schwarz as $\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[Y^2]\,\mathbb{P}\{Y \geq a\mathbb{E}Y\}}$. Rearranging isolates the tail probability.

**Solution.** If $\mathbb{E}Y = 0$, then $Y = 0$ a.s. (as $Y \geq 0$), so $\mathbb{P}\{Y \geq a\mathbb{E}Y\} = 1 \geq 0$ and the inequality holds. If $\mathbb{E}[Y^2] = \infty$, the right-hand side is $0$ and there is nothing to prove. Assume $0 < \mathbb{E}Y < \infty$ and $\mathbb{E}[Y^2] < \infty$.

Write
$$\mathbb{E}Y = \mathbb{E}\big[Y\mathbf{1}_{\{Y \geq a\mathbb{E}Y\}}\big] + \mathbb{E}\big[Y\mathbf{1}_{\{Y < a\mathbb{E}Y\}}\big].$$
The second term is at most $a\mathbb{E}Y$. By Cauchy–Schwarz,
$$\mathbb{E}\big[Y\mathbf{1}_{\{Y \geq a\mathbb{E}Y\}}\big] \leq \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[Y^2]\,\mathbb{P}\{Y \geq a\mathbb{E}Y\}}.$$
Hence
$$\mathbb{E}Y \leq \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[Y^2]\,\mathbb{P}\{Y \geq a\mathbb{E}Y\}} + a\mathbb{E}Y,$$
i.e.,
$$(1-a)\mathbb{E}Y \leq \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[Y^2]\,\mathbb{P}\{Y \geq a\mathbb{E}Y\}}.$$
Squaring both sides (all terms nonnegative, $a \in (0,1)$) gives
$$\mathbb{P}\{Y \geq a\mathbb{E}Y\} \geq \frac{(1-a)^2(\mathbb{E}Y)^2}{\mathbb{E}[Y^2]}.$$
