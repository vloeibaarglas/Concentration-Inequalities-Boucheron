**Central idea.** The inequality is false as stated: for $Y \equiv 2t$ the moment side equals $2$ while the Chernoff side collapses to $1$. The flaw is that the minimum is taken over $q \geq 1$ only, excluding the trivial bound $\mathbb{P}\{Y \geq t\} \leq 1$ (the $q = 0$ case). Including $q = 0$ fixes it: with $m = \min(1, \inf_{q \geq 1}\mathbb{E}[Y^q]t^{-q})$, term-by-term integration of the exponential series gives $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Y-t)} \geq m$ for every $\lambda > 0$.

**Solution.** The stated inequality
$$\min_{q \geq 1} \mathbb{E}[Y^q]t^{-q} \leq \inf_{\lambda>0} \mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Y-t)}$$
is false in general. Counterexample: $Y \equiv 2t$ constant. Then $\mathbb{E}[Y^q]t^{-q} = 2^q$, so the left side is $2$, while $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Y-t)} = e^{\lambda t} \to 1$ as $\lambda \downarrow 0$, so the right side is $1$.

The corrected statement includes $q = 0$. Set $m = \inf_{q \in \{0,1,2,\dots\}}\mathbb{E}[Y^q]t^{-q} = \min(1, \inf_{q \geq 1}\mathbb{E}[Y^q]t^{-q})$. Then $\mathbb{E}[Y^q] \geq m t^q$ for every integer $q \geq 0$ (for $q = 0$ since $m \leq 1$). As $Y \geq 0$, monotone convergence justifies term-by-term integration:
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Y-t)} = e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^q\,\mathbb{E}[Y^q]}{q!} \geq e^{-\lambda t}\, m\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda t)^q}{q!} = m, \qquad \lambda > 0.$$
Taking $\inf_{\lambda > 0}$ gives $m \leq \inf_{\lambda>0}\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Y-t)}$. Since the Chernoff bound tends to $1$ as $\lambda \downarrow 0$ (dominated convergence, when the mgf is finite near $0$), this proves the stated inequality whenever the Chernoff bound is nontrivial ($< 1$).

*Sharper form.* With $m_1 = \inf_{q\ge1}\mathbb{E}[Y^q]t^{-q}$, splitting the $q=0$ term gives $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Y-t)} \ge e^{-\lambda t} + m_1(1-e^{-\lambda t})$. So whenever the Chernoff bound is below $1$, $m_1$ is below it too — the $q\ge1$ moment bounds win exactly where it matters. ∎
