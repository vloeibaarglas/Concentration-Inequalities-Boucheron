**Central idea.** Finiteness of $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}$ propagates downward ($\lambda_0 \in S$, $0 < \lambda < \lambda_0 \Rightarrow \lambda \in S$) via $e^{\lambda Z} \leq e^{\lambda_0 Z} + 1$. Log-convexity of the mgf follows from Hölder; $C^\infty$ regularity from differentiation under the integral, dominated using that exponentials beat polynomials ($|Z|^k \leq C(e^{\delta Z} + e^{-\delta Z})$). At $\lambda = 0$, dominated convergence gives right-differentiability with derivative $\mathbb{E}Z = 0$. The Cramér transform's supremum can be restricted to $I$ since $\psi_Z = +\infty$ beyond $b$ and the value at $0$ is a limit from $I$.

**Solution.** Let $M(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}$ and $\psi_Z(\lambda) = \log M(\lambda)$.

*The set $S$.* If $\lambda_0 \in S$ and $0 < \lambda < \lambda_0$, then for $Z \geq 0$, $e^{\lambda Z} \leq e^{\lambda_0 Z}$, and for $Z < 0$, $e^{\lambda Z} \leq 1$. Hence $e^{\lambda Z} \leq e^{\lambda_0 Z} + 1$, so $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z} \leq \mathbb{E}e^{\lambda_0 Z} + 1 < \infty$, i.e., $\lambda \in S$. Thus $S$ is either empty or an interval of the form $(0,b)$ or $(0,b]$ with $b = \sup S \in (0,\infty]$.

*Convexity on $I = (0,b)$.* For $\lambda_1,\lambda_2 \in I$ and $\theta \in (0,1)$, Hölder's inequality gives
$$M(\theta\lambda_1+(1-\theta)\lambda_2) = \mathbb{E}\big[e^{\theta\lambda_1 Z}e^{(1-\theta)\lambda_2 Z}\big] \leq M(\lambda_1)^\theta M(\lambda_2)^{1-\theta}.$$
Taking logarithms, $\psi_Z$ is convex on $I$ (all terms finite and $M > 0$).

*$C^\infty$ on $I$.* Fix $\lambda_0 \in I$ and choose $\delta > 0$ with $[\lambda_0-2\delta,\lambda_0+2\delta] \subset I$. Since $|z|^k \leq C_{k,\delta}(e^{\delta z} + e^{-\delta z})$ for all $z \in \mathbb{R}$ (exponentials dominate polynomials), for $|\lambda - \lambda_0| \leq \delta$,
$$|Z|^k e^{\lambda Z} \leq C_{k,\delta}\big(e^{(\lambda_0+2\delta)Z} + e^{(\lambda_0-2\delta)Z}\big),$$
whose expectation is finite as both exponents lie in $S$. By dominated convergence, differentiation under the integral is justified to all orders:
$$M^{(k)}(\lambda_0) = \mathbb{E}\big[Z^k e^{\lambda_0 Z}\big], \qquad k = 1,2,\ldots$$
Hence $M \in C^\infty(I)$, and since $M > 0$, $\psi_Z = \log M \in C^\infty(I)$.

*Behavior at $0$ when $\mathbb{E}Z = 0$.* (This assumes $\mathbb{E}|Z| < \infty$.) Clearly $\psi_Z(0) = \log 1 = 0$. For continuity at $0$: if $0 < \lambda \leq \delta < b$, then $e^{\lambda Z} \leq e^{\delta Z} + 1$ (splitting on $\{Z \geq 0\}$, $\{Z < 0\}$), which is integrable, so $M(\lambda) \to 1$ by dominated convergence, i.e., $\psi_Z(\lambda) \to 0$.

For right-differentiability at $0$:
$$\frac{M(h)-1}{h} = \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{e^{hZ}-1}{h}\right], \qquad 0 < h \leq \delta.$$
For $Z \geq 0$, $h \mapsto \frac{e^{hZ}-1}{h}$ is nondecreasing (derivative $\frac{hZe^{hZ}-e^{hZ}+1}{h^2} \geq 0$), so $\frac{e^{hZ}-1}{h} \leq \frac{e^{\delta Z}-1}{\delta}$; for $Z < 0$, $\big|\frac{e^{hZ}-1}{h}\big| = \frac{1-e^{hZ}}{h} \leq |Z|$ since $1-e^{-y} \leq y$ for $y \geq 0$. Thus $\big|\frac{e^{hZ}-1}{h}\big| \leq \frac{e^{\delta Z}}{\delta} + |Z|$, integrable. By dominated convergence, $\frac{M(h)-1}{h} \to \mathbb{E}Z = 0$. Hence $M$ is right-differentiable at $0$ with $M'_+(0) = 0$, so $\psi_Z$ is right-differentiable at $0$ with $\psi'_{Z,+}(0) = M'_+(0)/M(0) = 0$. A similar domination shows $\psi'_Z(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}[Ze^{\lambda Z}]/\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}] \to 0$ as $\lambda \downarrow 0$, so $\psi_Z \in C^1[0,b)$ with $\psi'_Z(0) = 0$.

*Cramér transform.* The Cramér transform is $\sup_{\lambda \geq 0}(\lambda t - \psi_Z(\lambda))$. If $b < \infty$ and $\lambda > b$, then $\lambda \notin S$ (as $b = \sup S$), so $\psi_Z(\lambda) = +\infty$ and $\lambda t - \psi_Z(\lambda) = -\infty$, contributing nothing. If $b \in S$ (i.e. $S = (0,b]$), then $M$ is left-continuous at $b$: for $\lambda \uparrow b$, $e^{\lambda Z} \to e^{bZ}$ pointwise with dominating integrable function $1 + e^{bZ}$, so $M(\lambda) \to M(b)$ by dominated convergence, and $\lambda t - \psi_Z(\lambda)$ at $b$ is a limit of values from $I = (0,b)$. At $\lambda = 0$ the value is $0 - 0 = 0$, which equals $\lim_{\lambda \downarrow 0}(\lambda t - \psi_Z(\lambda))$ by continuity at $0$. Hence
$$\sup_{\lambda \geq 0}\big(\lambda t - \psi_Z(\lambda)\big) = \sup_{\lambda \in (0,b)}\big(\lambda t - \psi_Z(\lambda)\big) = \psi_Z^*(t).$$
(If $b = \infty$, then $I = (0,\infty)$ and there is nothing to exclude.)
