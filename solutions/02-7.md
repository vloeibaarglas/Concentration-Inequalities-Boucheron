**Central idea.** By scaling, reduce to $\sigma = 1$. Write $\mathbb{P}\{Z \geq t\}e^{t^2/2} = \int_0^\infty \phi(u)e^{-ut}\,du$ (shift $x = u+t$), which is $\leq \int_0^\infty \phi = 1/2$ since $e^{-ut} \leq 1$, with equality in the limit $t \to 0^+$ by dominated convergence. Hence the supremum is $1/2$, showing the Chernoff exponent $t^2/(2\sigma^2)$ is sharp up to the constant $1/2$.

**Solution.** If $Z \sim N(0,\sigma^2)$, then $W = Z/\sigma \sim N(0,1)$ and
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z \geq t\}\exp\!\left(\tfrac{t^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) = \mathbb{P}\{W \geq t/\sigma\}\exp\!\left(\tfrac{(t/\sigma)^2}{2}\right).$$
As $t$ ranges over $(0,\infty)$, so does $s = t/\sigma$; hence it suffices to prove the claim for $\sigma = 1$. Let $\phi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}$.

For $t > 0$,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{P}\{Z \geq t\}e^{t^2/2} &= e^{t^2/2}\int_t^\infty \phi(x)\,dx = \int_t^\infty \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-(x^2-t^2)/2}\,dx \\
&= \int_0^\infty \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-((u+t)^2-t^2)/2}\,du \quad (u = x - t) \\
&= \int_0^\infty \phi(u)\,e^{-ut}\,du \leq \int_0^\infty \phi(u)\,du = \frac{1}{2},
\end{align*}
since $e^{-ut} \leq 1$ for $u, t \geq 0$. Thus $\sup_{t>0}\big(\mathbb{P}\{Z\geq t\}e^{t^2/2}\big) \leq 1/2$.

For the reverse, as $t \downarrow 0$, $\phi(u)e^{-ut} \to \phi(u)$ pointwise with dominating integrable function $\phi$, so by dominated convergence
$$\lim_{t \downarrow 0} \mathbb{P}\{Z \geq t\}e^{t^2/2} = \int_0^\infty \phi(u)\,du = \frac{1}{2}.$$
Hence the supremum over $t > 0$ is at least $1/2$. Combined with the upper bound, the supremum equals $1/2$ (not attained, but approached as $t \to 0^+$).
