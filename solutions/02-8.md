**Central idea.** Each inequality is proved by the "derivative comparison" method: set $f = \text{LHS} - \text{RHS}$, verify $f(0) = f'(0) = 0$, and show $f''(u) \geq 0$ on the domain, which forces $f' \geq 0$ and hence $f \geq 0$. The second-derivative checks reduce to elementary polynomial inequalities.

**Solution.** In each case define $f(u) = \text{LHS} - \text{RHS}$; we show $f(0) = f'(0) = 0$ and $f''(u) \geq 0$.

*(a)* $f(u) = \frac{u^2}{2(1-u)} + \log(1-u) + u$ on $(0,1)$. Then $f(0) = 0$ and
$$f'(u) = \frac{u(2-u)}{2(1-u)^2} - \frac{1}{1-u} + 1 = \frac{u(2-u) - 2(1-u) + 2(1-u)^2}{2(1-u)^2} = \frac{u^2}{2(1-u)^2} \geq 0.$$
Since $f(0) = 0$ and $f' \geq 0$, $f(u) \geq 0$ on $(0,1)$, i.e., $-\log(1-u) - u \leq \frac{u^2}{2(1-u)}$.

*(b)* $f(u) = (1+u)\log(1+u) - u - \frac{u^2}{2(1+u/3)}$ for $u > 0$. Then $f(0) = 0$,
$$f'(u) = \log(1+u) - \frac{3u(6+u)}{2(3+u)^2}, \qquad f'(0) = 0,$$
$$f''(u) = \frac{1}{1+u} - \frac{27}{(3+u)^3}.$$
Now $f''(u) \geq 0 \iff (3+u)^3 \geq 27(1+u)$. With $g(u) = (3+u)^3 - 27(1+u)$: $g(0) = 0$ and $g'(u) = 3(3+u)^2 - 27 = 3u(u+6) \geq 0$ for $u \geq 0$, so $g(u) \geq 0$. Hence $f''(u) \geq 0$, so $f'$ is nondecreasing with $f'(0) = 0$, giving $f'(u) \geq 0$, and $f$ is nondecreasing with $f(0) = 0$, giving $f(u) \geq 0$. That is, $h(u) \geq \frac{u^2}{2(1+u/3)}$.

*(c)* $f(u) = 1 + u - \sqrt{1+2u} - \frac{u^2}{2(1+u)}$ for $u > 0$. Then $f(0) = 0$,
$$f'(u) = 1 - (1+2u)^{-1/2} - \frac{u(2+u)}{2(1+u)^2}, \qquad f'(0) = 0,$$
$$f''(u) = (1+2u)^{-3/2} - (1+u)^{-3}.$$
Now $f''(u) \geq 0 \iff (1+u)^3 \geq (1+2u)^{3/2} \iff (1+u)^2 \geq 1+2u \iff u^2 \geq 0$, true. Hence $f''(u) \geq 0$, so $f'(u) \geq f'(0) = 0$, so $f(u) \geq f(0) = 0$. That is, $h_1(u) \geq \frac{u^2}{2(1+u)}$.
