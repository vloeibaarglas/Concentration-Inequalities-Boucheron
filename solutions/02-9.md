**Central idea.** For $X \geq 0$, the one-sided mgf $\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda(X-\mathbb{E}X)}$ is controlled by the quadratic Taylor bound $e^{-y} \leq 1 - y + y^2/2$ ($y \geq 0$), giving a sub-Gaussian-type estimate with variance proxy $\mathbb{E}[X^2]$ rather than $\mathrm{Var}(X)$. Independence factorizes the mgf of the sum, and optimizing the Chernoff bound over $\lambda$ yields the Gaussian tail $\exp(-t^2/(2v))$.

**Solution.** Let $X \geq 0$ with $\mathbb{E}[X^2] < \infty$ (hence $\mathbb{E}X \leq \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[X^2]} < \infty$). For $y \geq 0$, $e^{-y} \leq 1 - y + y^2/2$: indeed $g(y) = 1-y+y^2/2-e^{-y}$ satisfies $g(0) = g'(0) = 0$ and $g''(y) = 1-e^{-y} \geq 0$, so $g \geq 0$ on $[0,\infty)$.

For $\lambda > 0$, with $y = \lambda X \geq 0$,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda X} \leq \mathbb{E}\left[1 - \lambda X + \tfrac{\lambda^2}{2}X^2\right] = 1 - \lambda\mathbb{E}X + \tfrac{\lambda^2}{2}\mathbb{E}[X^2].$$
Therefore, using $1 + z \leq e^z$,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda(X-\mathbb{E}X)} = e^{\lambda\mathbb{E}X}\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda X} \leq e^{\lambda\mathbb{E}X}\big(1-\lambda\mathbb{E}X+\tfrac{\lambda^2}{2}\mathbb{E}[X^2]\big) \leq e^{\lambda^2\mathbb{E}[X^2]/2}.$$

Now let $X_1,\dots,X_n \geq 0$ be independent, $S = \sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \mathbb{E}X_i)$, $v = \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[X_i^2]$. If $v = 0$, then $X_i = 0$ a.s. for all $i$, so $S = 0$ and $\mathbb{P}\{S \leq -t\} = 0$ for $t > 0$. Assume $v > 0$. By independence,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda S} = \prod_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda(X_i-\mathbb{E}X_i)} \leq \prod_{i=1}^n e^{\lambda^2\mathbb{E}[X_i^2]/2} = e^{\lambda^2 v/2}.$$
By Chernoff's bound, for $t > 0$ and $\lambda > 0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{S \leq -t\} = \mathbb{P}\{e^{-\lambda S} \geq e^{\lambda t}\} \leq e^{-\lambda t}\,\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda S} \leq \exp\!\left(\tfrac{\lambda^2 v}{2} - \lambda t\right).$$
Minimizing $\frac{\lambda^2 v}{2} - \lambda t$ over $\lambda > 0$ gives $\lambda = t/v$ and minimum value $-\frac{t^2}{2v}$. Hence
$$\mathbb{P}\{S \leq -t\} \leq \exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2v}\right).$$
