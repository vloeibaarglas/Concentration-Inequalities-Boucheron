**Central idea.** Sandwich the weight of a minimizing path $\gamma$: below by $a|\gamma|$ (every edge costs at least $a>0$), above by the weight of the deterministic $\ell^1$-shortest path from $0$ to $v$ (exactly $\|v\|_1$ edges, each costing at most $b$). Minimality of $\gamma$ forces $a|\gamma|\le T(0,v)\le b\|v\|_1$, giving $|\gamma|\le (b/a)\|v\|_1$ deterministically, uniformly over the randomness.

**Solution.** Let $\{X_e\}$ be the edge weights, $a\le X_e\le b$ a.s.\ with $0<a<b<\infty$. For $v\in\mathbb Z^d$ let
$$T(0,v)=\min_{\gamma:0\to v}\sum_{e\in\gamma}X_e$$
be the first passage time, the minimum over all lattice paths $\gamma$ from $0$ to $v$, and let $\gamma^*$ be a minimizing path (which exists a.s.\ since $X_e\ge a>0$ forces the infimum to be attained over paths with boundedly many edges). Write $|\gamma^*|$ for its number of edges.

*Lower bound.* Every edge of $\gamma^*$ has weight at least $a$, so
$$T(0,v)=\sum_{e\in\gamma^*}X_e\ge a\,|\gamma^*|.$$

*Upper bound.* Consider the deterministic path from $0$ to $v$ that moves $|v_1|$ steps along the first coordinate axis, $|v_2|$ along the second, etc. It uses exactly $\|v\|_1=|v_1|+\cdots+|v_d|$ edges, each of weight at most $b$. Since $\gamma^*$ minimizes,
$$T(0,v)\le b\,\|v\|_1.$$

Combining, $a|\gamma^*|\le b\|v\|_1$, i.e.
$$|\gamma^*|\le \frac{b}{a}\,\|v\|_1.$$
The bound is deterministic (holds for every realization of the weights) and uses only $0<a\le X_e\le b$; no integrability or independence is needed. In particular geodesics cannot be much longer than the $\ell^1$ distance: their length is at most a constant factor $(b/a)$ times $\|v\|_1$. ∎
