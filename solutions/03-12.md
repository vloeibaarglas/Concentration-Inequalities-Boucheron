**Central idea.** If $G(n,p)$ is disconnected, some $k \le n/2$ vertices form a union of components, i.e. no edges cross the cut — a union bound over $\binom{n}{k}$ sets gives $\sum_{k\le n/2}\binom{n}{k}(1-p)^{k(n-k)}$. Split at $k_0=\frac{(c-1)n}{2c}$: for small $k$, $\binom{n}{k}\le n^k/k!$ turns the sum into the exponential series $e^{n^{(1-c)/2}}-1$; for large $k$, $\binom{n}{k}\le2^n$ and $k(n-k)\gtrsim n^2$ makes $(1-p)^{k(n-k)}$ superpolynomially small. (Note: the book writes the bound with $m$ where it means $n$ — a typo; we use $n$ throughout.)

**Solution.** Let $p=c\log n/n$ with $c>1$. If $G(n,p)$ is disconnected, its smallest component has $k$ vertices for some $1\le k\le n/2$. For a fixed set $S$ of $k$ vertices, $\mathbb{P}\{\text{no edges between }S\text{ and }S^c\}=(1-p)^{k(n-k)}$ by independence. Union bounding over the $\binom{n}{k}$ choices of $S$ and over $k$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{G(n,p)\text{ disconnected}\}\le\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{k}(1-p)^{k(n-k)}.\tag{1}$$
Put $k_0=\frac{(c-1)n}{2c}\le n/2$ and split the sum at $k_0$.

*Small $k\le k_0$.* Using $\binom{n}{k}\le n^k/k!$,
$$\binom{n}{k}(1-p)^{k(n-k)}\le\frac{[n(1-p)^{\,n-k}]^k}{k!}.$$
We claim $n(1-p)^{n-k}\le n^{(1-c)/2}$ for $k\le k_0$. Indeed, with $(1-p)\le e^{-p}$ it suffices that $ne^{-p(n-k)}\le n^{(1-c)/2}$, i.e. $\log n-p(n-k)\le\frac{1-c}{2}\log n$, i.e. $p(n-k)\ge\frac{1+c}{2}\log n$. Since $p=\frac{c\log n}{n}$, this is $\frac{c(n-k)}{n}\ge\frac{1+c}{2}$, i.e. $k\le n-\frac{(1+c)n}{2c}=\frac{(c-1)n}{2c}=k_0$. Hence
$$\sum_{1\le k\le k_0}\binom{n}{k}(1-p)^{k(n-k)}\le\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(n^{(1-c)/2})^k}{k!}=e^{\,n^{(1-c)/2}}-1.$$

*Large $k_0<k\le n/2$.* Here $\binom{n}{k}\le2^n$, so it suffices to bound $(1-p)^{k(n-k)}$. For $k\le n/2$, $n-k\ge n/2$, so $k(n-k)\ge kn/2>k_0n/2=\frac{(c-1)n^2}{4c}$. With $(1-p)\le e^{-p}$,
$$(1-p)^{k(n-k)}\le\exp\!\big(-pk(n-k)\big)<\exp\!\left(-\frac{c\log n}{n}\cdot\frac{(c-1)n^2}{4c}\right)=n^{-(c-1)n/4}.$$
Therefore
$$\sum_{k_0<k\le n/2}\binom{n}{k}(1-p)^{k(n-k)}\le n^{-(c-1)n/4}\sum_{k}\binom{n}{k}\le2^n\,n^{-(c-1)n/4}.$$

Combining with (1) and padding by factors of $2$ (both terms are nonnegative),
$$\mathbb{P}\{G(n,p)\text{ disconnected}\}\le2\big(e^{\,n^{(1-c)/2}}-1\big)+2^{\,n+1}n^{-(c-1)n/4},$$
which is the claimed bound with $m=n$. Since $c>1$, $n^{(1-c)/2}\to0$ gives $e^{n^{(1-c)/2}}-1\to0$, and $\frac{(c-1)n}{4}\log n\gg(n+1)\log2$ gives $2^{n+1}n^{-(c-1)n/4}\to0$; hence $G(n,c\log n/n)$ is connected with high probability. ∎
