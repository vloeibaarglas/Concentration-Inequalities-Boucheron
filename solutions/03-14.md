**Central idea.** Write $N = 2m$ and vertices as $(x,y) \in \{0,1\}^m \times \{0,1\}^m$. The "swap-halves" permutation $\sigma(x,y) = (y,x)$ forces every shortest path to pass through the diagonal vertex $(y,y)$ (dimension-order routing fixes the first $m$ coordinates first). A fixed edge leaving the diagonal in dimension $m+1$ is then traversed by all $2^{m-1}$ paths with the right midpoint and first flip, giving congestion $2^{N/2-1} \ge 2^{N/2}/N$.

**Solution.** Identify the hypercube with $\{0,1\}^N$ and assume $N = 2m$ is even (the odd case is analogous). Write vertices as $u = (x,y)$ with $x, y \in \{0,1\}^m$. Let $\sigma(x,y) = (y,x)$, an involution, hence a permutation.

The shortest-path strategy flips, in increasing coordinate order, each coordinate where the endpoints differ. For $(x,y) \to (y,x) = \sigma(x,y)$, the differing coordinates are $\{i \le m : x_i \ne y_i\} \cup \{m+i : x_i \ne y_i\}$. Flipping the first batch (in order) reaches $(y,y)$; flipping the second batch reaches $(y,x)$. Hence every routed path passes through the diagonal vertex $(y,y)$.

Fix $z \in \{0,1\}^m$ and let $e$ be the edge from $(z,z)$ to $(z,z')$, where $z'$ is $z$ with the first bit flipped (an edge in dimension $m+1$). The path $(x,y) \to (y,x)$ traverses $e$ iff its midpoint is $(z,z)$ (i.e.\ $y = z$) and its first flip in coordinates $\{m+1,\dots,N\}$ is coordinate $m+1$ (i.e.\ $x_1 \ne y_1$, since flips proceed in increasing order). Thus $e$ is used exactly by the pairs $(x,y)$ with $y = z$ and $x_1 \ne z_1$: there are $2^{m-1}$ such pairs.

So some edge has congestion $2^{m-1} = 2^{N/2}/2 \ge 2^{N/2}/N$ (for $N \ge 2$). Hence the maximal congestion over all edges is at least $2^{N/2}/N$. ∎
