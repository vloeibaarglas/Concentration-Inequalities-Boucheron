**Central idea.** Fix a directed edge $\vec{e}$ in dimension $i$. A leg $u \to W(u)$ traverses $\vec{e}$ only if $u$ matches the edge's tail on coordinates $i,\dots,N$ (else $0$) and then $W(u)$ satisfies $i$ independent coordinate constraints (probability $2^{-i}$); summing over $u$ gives mean $\le 1/2$, as a sum of $2^N$ independent indicators. The same holds for legs $W(u) \to \sigma(u)$ (using that $\sigma$ is a permutation to count preimages). Chernoff plus a union bound over the $N2^{N-1}$ edges then gives $\mathbb{E}[\max_e C_e] = O(N)$.

**Solution.** Work on $\{0,1\}^N$. Each $u$ chooses $W(u)$ iid uniform over $\{0,1\}^N$; legs $u \to W(u)$ and $W(u) \to \sigma(u)$ follow dimension-order shortest paths. For an edge $e$, let $C_e$ be the number of the $2^{N+1}$ legs traversing $e$ (either direction); $M = \max_e C_e \le 2^{N+1}$ always.

Fix a directed edge $\vec{e}$ in dimension $i$, from $x$ to $x \oplus \delta_i$. Under dimension-order routing, when coordinate $i$ is flipped the current vertex has the target's values on coordinates $1,\dots,i-1$ and the source's values on $i+1,\dots,N$. So a leg $a \to b$ traverses $\vec{e}$ iff $a_i = x_i$, $b_i = \bar{x}_i$, $b_j = x_j$ ($j < i$), $a_j = x_j$ ($j > i$).

For first legs ($a = u$, $b = W(u)$): if $u$ disagrees with $x$ on any coordinate $\ge i$ the indicator is $0$; for the $2^{i-1}$ vertices $u$ agreeing with $x$ on coordinates $i,\dots,N$, the conditions on $W(u)$ are $i$ independent coordinate constraints, so the probability is $2^{-i}$. Hence, with independent indicators across $u$ (the $W(u)$ are independent),
$$A_{\vec{e}} := \sum_u \mathbf{1}\{\text{leg }u \to W(u)\text{ uses }\vec{e}\}, \qquad \mathbb{E}[A_{\vec{e}}] = 2^{i-1} \cdot 2^{-i} = \tfrac12.$$
For second legs ($a = W(u)$, $b = \sigma(u)$): the indicator is $0$ unless $\sigma(u)$ agrees with $x$ on coordinates $1,\dots,i-1$ with $\sigma(u)_i = \bar{x}_i$; since $\sigma$ is a permutation there are $2^{N-i}$ such $u$, and for each the $N-i+1$ constraints on $W(u)$ hold with probability $2^{-(N-i+1)}$. Thus
$$B_{\vec{e}} := \sum_u \mathbf{1}\{\text{leg }W(u) \to \sigma(u)\text{ uses }\vec{e}\}, \qquad \mathbb{E}[B_{\vec{e}}] = 2^{N-i} \cdot 2^{-(N-i+1)} = \tfrac12,$$
again a sum of $2^N$ independent indicators.

Now $C_e \le \sum (A_{\vec{e}} + B_{\vec{e}})$ over the $2$ directed versions of $e$. For a sum $S$ of independent indicators with $\mu = \mathbb{E}S \le 1/2$, Chernoff gives $\mathbb{P}\{S \ge t\} \le (e\mu/t)^t \le (e/(2t))^t$ for $t \ge 1$. Hence
$$\mathbb{P}\{C_e \ge t\} \le 4\left(\frac{2e}{t}\right)^{t/4},$$
by a union bound over the $4$ directed sums (each must reach $t/4$). With $t = KN$ and a union bound over the $N2^{N-1}$ edges,
$$\mathbb{P}\{M \ge KN\} \le N2^{N-1} \cdot 4\left(\frac{2e}{KN}\right)^{KN/4} \le 2^{-N}$$
for $K$ a sufficiently large absolute constant (the exponent $\frac{KN}{4}\log\frac{KN}{2e}$ dominates $2N\log 2 + \log N$). Finally,
$$\mathbb{E}[M] \le KN + 2^{N+1}\mathbb{P}\{M > KN\} \le KN + 2 = O(N). \qquad\blacksquare$$
