**Central idea.** Diagonalize $A = U\Lambda U^T$, $B = VMV^T$; unitary invariance of the Hilbert–Schmidt norm gives $\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = \sum_{i,j} q_{ij}^2(\lambda_i-\mu_j)^2$ where $Q = U^TV$ is orthogonal, so $S = (q_{ij}^2)$ is doubly stochastic. The problem's claim that $\sum_{i,j} p_{ij}^2(\lambda_i-\mu_j)^2$ is minimized at $P = I$ over doubly stochastic $P$ is **false as stated** (counterexample below); the correct statement is the linear one, $\sum_{i,j} p_{ij}(\lambda_i-\mu_j)^2 \ge \sum_i(\lambda_i-\mu_i)^2$, proved via Birkhoff–von Neumann (reducing to permutation matrices) and the rearrangement inequality.

**Solution.** Write $\mu_j = \lambda_j(B)$. Since $A, B$ are symmetric, $A = U\Lambda U^T$ and $B = VMV^T$ with $U, V$ orthogonal, $\Lambda = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)$, $M = \mathrm{diag}(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n)$, $\lambda_1 \ge \cdots \ge \lambda_n$, $\mu_1 \ge \cdots \ge \mu_n$.

*The hint's identity.* By unitary invariance of $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$,
$$\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = \|U\Lambda U^T - VMV^T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = \|\Lambda - QMQ^T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2, \qquad Q := U^TV,$$
multiplying by $U^T$ on the left and $U$ on the right. Right-multiplying by the orthogonal $Q$,
$$\|\Lambda - QMQ^T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = \|\Lambda Q - QM\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.$$
Since $(\Lambda Q - QM)_{ij} = \lambda_i q_{ij} - q_{ij}\mu_j = q_{ij}(\lambda_i - \mu_j)$,
$$\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = \sum_{i,j=1}^n q_{ij}^2(\lambda_i - \mu_j)^2. \tag{1}$$
The matrix $S = (q_{ij}^2)$ is doubly stochastic: rows and columns of the orthogonal $Q$ are unit vectors.

*The $p_{ij}^2$ claim is false.* The problem asks to show $\sum_{i,j} p_{ij}^2 d_{ij}$, $d_{ij} := (\lambda_i-\mu_j)^2$, is minimized at $P = I$ over doubly stochastic $P$. This is false. Counterexample: $n = 2$, $\lambda = (2,0)$, $\mu = (1,1)$, so $d_{ij} \equiv 1$. For the uniform $P = \tfrac12\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T$, $\sum_{i,j} p_{ij}^2 d_{ij} = 4\cdot\tfrac14 = 1$, whereas at $P = I$ it equals $2$. Hence $I$ is not even a minimizer. The intended claim is the linear version.

*Correct lemma.* For $P$ doubly stochastic, $\sum_{i,j} p_{ij}d_{ij} \ge \sum_i d_{ii}$.

*Proof.* $F(P) := \sum_{i,j} p_{ij}d_{ij}$ is linear in $P$, hence attains its minimum over the convex compact set of doubly stochastic matrices at an extreme point. By the Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem, extreme points are permutation matrices, so $\min_P F(P) = \min_\pi \sum_i d_{i,\pi(i)}$. For a permutation $\pi$,
$$\sum_i d_{i,\pi(i)} - \sum_i d_{ii} = \sum_i(\lambda_i-\mu_{\pi(i)})^2 - \sum_i(\lambda_i-\mu_i)^2 = 2\left(\sum_i\lambda_i\mu_i - \sum_i\lambda_i\mu_{\pi(i)}\right) \ge 0$$
by the rearrangement inequality, since $(\lambda_i)$ and $(\mu_i)$ are both nonincreasing. ∎

*Remark on exchanges.* The hint's exchange works cleanly for the linear version: for $k < \ell$, the $2\times2$ swap moving $\epsilon > 0$ from $(k,\ell),(\ell,k)$ to $(k,k),(\ell,\ell)$ preserves double stochasticity and changes $\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}d_{ij}$ by $\epsilon\big(d_{kk}+d_{\ell\ell}-d_{k\ell}-d_{\ell k}\big) \le 0$, since $d_{kk}+d_{\ell\ell}-d_{k\ell}-d_{\ell k} = -2(\lambda_k-\lambda_\ell)(\mu_k-\mu_\ell) \le 0$ (Monge property). For the $p_{ij}^2$ version the linear terms do not cancel and no such exchange exists — consistent with the counterexample.

*Conclusion.* Applying the lemma to $S = (q_{ij}^2)$ in (1),
$$\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = \sum_{i,j} q_{ij}^2(\lambda_i-\mu_j)^2 \ge \sum_{i=1}^n (\lambda_i-\mu_i)^2,$$
which is Lidskii's (Hoffman–Wielandt) inequality. ∎
