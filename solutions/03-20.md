**Central idea.** Apply the Efron–Stein inequality to $\sqrt{Z}$ with the $X^{(i)}$-measurable approximants $\sqrt{Z_i}$: $\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt{Z})\le\sum_i\mathbb{E}[(\sqrt{Z}-\sqrt{Z_i})^2]$, and on $\{Z>0\}$, $(\sqrt{Z}-\sqrt{Z_i})^2=\frac{(Z-Z_i)^2}{(\sqrt{Z}+\sqrt{Z_i})^2}\le\frac{(Z-Z_i)^2}{Z}$. Summing gives $\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt Z)\le\mathbb{E}[W\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>0\}}]$. This is at most $\mathbb{E}W$ once $W\ge0$. The statement leaves that hypothesis out, and it cannot be dropped.

**Remark (erratum: missing hypothesis $W\ge 0$).** As printed, the hypothesis $V\le WZ$ puts no constraint on $W$ on the event $\{Z=0\}$, and the claim can then fail. Take $n=1$ and $Z=f(X_1)=X_1\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$. Then $Z_1=\inf_{x}f(x)=0$, so $V=Z^2=Z$. Let $W=1$ on $\{Z=1\}$ and $W=-100$ on $\{Z=0\}$. Then $V\le WZ$ holds ($1\le1$ on $\{Z=1\}$, $0\le0$ on $\{Z=0\}$), yet
$$\mathbb{E}W=\tfrac12\cdot1+\tfrac12\cdot(-100)=-49.5<\tfrac14=\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt Z).$$
On $\{Z>0\}$ the condition $W\ge0$ holds automatically, since $W\ge V/Z\ge0$ there.

The corrected statement reads: *if $V\le WZ$ and $W\ge0$ on $\{Z=0\}$ (equivalently, $W\ge0$ a.s.), then $\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt Z)\le\mathbb{E}W$.* With no sign condition at all, the bound $\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt Z)\le\mathbb{E}[W\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>0\}}]$ still holds; both are proved below.

**Solution.** Since $f\ge0$ and the infimum defining $Z_i$ includes $x_i'=X_i$, we have $0\le Z_i\le Z$. Moreover $\sqrt{Z_i}$ is a function of $X^{(i)}=(X_1,\dots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\dots,X_n)$ only.

*Step 1 (Efron–Stein with arbitrary $X^{(i)}$-measurable approximants).* Write $\mathbb{E}^{(i)}$ for integration with respect to $X_i$ only. For square-integrable $Y=g(X_1,\dots,X_n)$, the Efron–Stein inequality (Theorem 3.1) gives $\mathrm{Var}(Y)\le\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}[(Y-\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Y)^2]$. For any $X^{(i)}$-measurable $Y_i$, the conditional mean minimizes the conditional mean-square error: $\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[(Y-\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Y)^2]\le\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[(Y-Y_i)^2]$. Hence
$$\mathrm{Var}(Y)\le\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big[(Y-Y_i)^2\big].\tag{1}$$

*Step 2 (the pointwise bound).* Put
$$S=\sum_{i=1}^n(\sqrt{Z}-\sqrt{Z_i})^2 .$$
On $\{Z>0\}$, using $(\sqrt{Z}+\sqrt{Z_i})^2\ge Z$,
$$S=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{(Z-Z_i)^2}{(\sqrt{Z}+\sqrt{Z_i})^2}\le\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^n(Z-Z_i)^2=\frac{V}{Z}\le W.$$
On $\{Z=0\}$ every $Z_i=0$ (as $0\le Z_i\le Z$), so $S=0$. Thus $S\le W\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>0\}}$ pointwise, and
$$B:=\mathbb{E}S\le\mathbb{E}\big[W\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>0\}}\big].$$

*Step 3 (square integrability).* Assume $B<\infty$; otherwise the right-hand side is $+\infty$ and there is nothing to prove. For $K>0$ let $Y_K=\min(\sqrt Z,K)$ and $Y_{K,i}=\min(\sqrt{Z_i},K)$. Then $Y_{K,i}$ is $X^{(i)}$-measurable and $|Y_K-Y_{K,i}|\le|\sqrt Z-\sqrt{Z_i}|$, since $x\mapsto\min(x,K)$ is 1-Lipschitz. By (1), $\mathrm{Var}(Y_K)\le B$ for every $K$.

Let $Z'$ be an independent copy of $Z$. Then $\mathrm{Var}(Y_K)=\frac12\mathbb{E}\big(Y_K-Y_K'\big)^2$. By Fatou, as $K\to\infty$,
$$\tfrac12\mathbb{E}\big(\sqrt Z-\sqrt{Z'}\big)^2\le B<\infty.$$
By Fubini, $\mathbb{E}(\sqrt Z-c)^2<\infty$ for some constant $c$, so $\sqrt Z\in L^2$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt Z)=\frac12\mathbb{E}(\sqrt Z-\sqrt{Z'})^2\le B$. (When $\mathbb{E}Z<\infty$ is known in advance, (1) with $Y=\sqrt Z$, $Y_i=\sqrt{Z_i}$ gives this directly.)

*Step 4 (conclusion).* With no sign assumption on $W$,
$$\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt{Z})\le\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{V}{Z}\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>0\}}\right]\le\mathbb{E}\left[W\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>0\}}\right].$$
Under the hypothesis $W\ge0$ on $\{Z=0\}$, which is necessary by the Remark,
$$\mathbb{E}\left[W\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>0\}}\right]=\mathbb{E}W-\mathbb{E}\left[W\mathbf{1}_{\{Z=0\}}\right]\le\mathbb{E}W.$$
Hence $\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt Z)\le\mathbb{E}W$. ∎
