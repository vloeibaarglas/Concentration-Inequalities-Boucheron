**Central idea.** Write $X$ as a sum of $n$ iid $\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu/n)$ variables and apply the Efron–Stein inequality (Theorem 3.1): resampling one coordinate changes $f$ by a telescoping sum of discrete derivatives, and Cauchy–Schwarz reduces the problem to an exact Poisson computation where the cross-term collapses to $2\lambda p_{\ell}$ via the identity $kp_k=\lambda p_{k-1}$.

**Solution.** If $\mathbb{E}[(Df(X))^2]=\infty$ there is nothing to prove. We first prove the inequality for bounded $f$; the general case follows by truncation (see below).

Let $n\ge1$, $\lambda=\mu/n$, and write $X\stackrel{d}{=}\sum_{i=1}^nX_i$ with $X_i$ iid $\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)$. By the Efron–Stein inequality (Theorem 3.1), in the resampling form $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}[(Z-Z_i')^2]$ (since $\mathbb{E}[(Z-\mathbb{E}_iZ)^2]=\frac12\mathbb{E}[(Z-Z_i')^2]$ for $Z_i'$ resampling coordinate $i$),
$$\mathrm{Var}(f(X))\le\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big[(f(X)-f(X^{(i)}))^2\big],$$
where $X^{(i)}=X-X_i+X_i'$ with $X_i'$ an independent copy of $X_i$.

Fix $i$ and write $S_i=\sum_{j\ne i}X_j$. Conditional on $S_i=s$, with $Y,Y'$ iid $\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)$ and $p_k=e^{-\lambda}\lambda^k/k!$,
$$\mathbb{E}\big[(f(s+Y)-f(s+Y'))^2\big]=\sum_{j,k\ge0}p_jp_k\big(f(s+j)-f(s+k)\big)^2.$$
Telescoping, $f(s+j)-f(s+k)=\sum_{\ell=\min(j,k)}^{\max(j,k)-1}\sigma_{j,k}\,Df(s+\ell)$ with $\sigma_{j,k}=\mathrm{sgn}(j-k)$, so by Cauchy–Schwarz,
$$\big(f(s+j)-f(s+k)\big)^2\le|j-k|\sum_{\ell=\min(j,k)}^{\max(j,k)-1}(Df(s+\ell))^2.$$
Hence, by Tonelli's theorem,
$$\mathbb{E}\big[(f(s+Y)-f(s+Y'))^2\big]\le\sum_{\ell\ge0}(Df(s+\ell))^2\,c_{\ell},\qquad c_{\ell}:=\sum_{\substack{j,k\ge0\\\min(j,k)\le\ell<\max(j,k)}}p_jp_k|j-k|.$$
By symmetry, $c_{\ell}=2\sum_{j\le\ell<k}p_jp_k(k-j)$. Using $kp_k=\lambda p_{k-1}$,
$$\sum_{k>\ell}p_k(k-j)=\lambda\,\mathbb{P}\{Y\ge\ell\}-j\,\mathbb{P}\{Y>\ell\},\qquad\sum_{j\le\ell}jp_j=\lambda\,\mathbb{P}\{Y\le\ell-1\},$$
so $c_{\ell}=2\lambda\big[\mathbb{P}\{Y\ge\ell\}\mathbb{P}\{Y\le\ell\}-\mathbb{P}\{Y>\ell\}\mathbb{P}\{Y\le\ell-1\}\big]$. Writing $a=\mathbb{P}\{Y\le\ell-1\}$, $b=p_{\ell}$, $c=\mathbb{P}\{Y>\ell\}$ (so $a+b+c=1$), the bracket equals $(b+c)(a+b)-ca=b(a+b+c)=b=p_{\ell}$. Thus $c_{\ell}=2\lambda p_{\ell}$, and
$$\mathbb{E}\big[(f(s+Y)-f(s+Y'))^2\big]\le2\lambda\sum_{\ell\ge0}p_{\ell}(Df(s+\ell))^2=2\lambda\,\mathbb{E}\big[(Df(s+Y))^2\big].$$
Unconditioning, $\mathbb{E}[(f(X)-f(X^{(i)}))^2]\le2\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[(Df(X))^2]$. Summing over $i$,
$$\mathrm{Var}(f(X))\le\frac12\cdot n\cdot2\lambda\,\mathbb{E}\big[(Df(X))^2\big]=\mu\,\mathbb{E}\big[(Df(X))^2\big].$$

For general $f$ with $\mathbb{E}[(Df(X))^2]<\infty$, truncate via $f_m(k)=f(\min(k,m))$. Then $f_m$ is bounded, $(Df_m(k))^2\le(Df(k))^2$ for all $k$ (equality for $k<m$, both sides $0$ for $k\ge m$ up to the single point $k=m$ where $Df_m(m)=0$), so $\mathrm{Var}(f_m(X))\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}[(Df(X))^2]$. With $X'$ an independent copy of $X$, $(f_m(X)-f_m(X'))^2\to(f(X)-f(X'))^2$ a.s., so Fatou's lemma gives $\mathrm{Var}(f(X))=\frac12\mathbb{E}[(f(X)-f(X'))^2]\le\liminf_m\mathrm{Var}(f_m(X))\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}[(Df(X))^2]$. ∎
