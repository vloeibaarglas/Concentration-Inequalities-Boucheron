**Central idea.** Apply the given integration-by-parts identity to $h(x)=(f(x)-f(0))^2$ rather than to $f$ itself: then the boundary term $h(0)$ vanishes, and Cauchy–Schwarz turns $2\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{sgn}(X)(f(X)-f(0))f'(X)]$ into the sharp bound $\mathbb{E}[(f(X)-f(0))^2]\le4\mathbb{E}[(f'(X))^2]$. Since variance is the minimum mean squared error over all constants, the claim follows.

**Solution.** If $\mathbb{E}[(f'(X))^2]=\infty$ there is nothing to prove; assume it is finite. Since $\mathrm{Var}(f(X))<\infty$, also $\mathbb{E}[(f(X)-f(0))^2]=\mathrm{Var}(f(X))+(\mathbb{E}f(X)-f(0))^2<\infty$.

The identity $\mathbb{E}[h(X)]=h(0)+\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{sgn}(X)h'(X)]$ follows by integration by parts on $(0,\infty)$ and $(-\infty,0)$: for instance, $\int_0^A h(x)\frac12e^{-x}\,dx=\frac12h(0)-\frac12e^{-A}h(A)+\int_0^A h'(x)\frac12e^{-x}\,dx$, and the boundary term $e^{-A}h(A)\to0$ as $A\to\infty$ whenever $\int_0^\infty e^{-x}|h(x)|\,dx$ and $\int_0^\infty e^{-x}|h'(x)|\,dx$ are finite (then $e^{-x}h(x)$ is Cauchy at $\infty$, hence convergent, and the limit must be $0$ lest the first integral diverge).

Apply the identity to $h(x)=(f(x)-f(0))^2$, for which $h(0)=0$ and $h'(x)=2(f(x)-f(0))f'(x)$. The integrability holds since, by Cauchy–Schwarz,
$$\mathbb{E}\big[|f(X)-f(0)|\cdot|f'(X)|\big]\le\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[(f(X)-f(0))^2\big]\,\mathbb{E}\big[(f'(X))^2\big]}<\infty.$$
We obtain
$$\mathbb{E}\big[(f(X)-f(0))^2\big]=2\,\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{sgn}(X)(f(X)-f(0))f'(X)\big].$$
Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the right-hand side (using $\mathrm{sgn}(X)^2=1$ a.s.),
$$\mathbb{E}\big[(f(X)-f(0))^2\big]\le2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[(f(X)-f(0))^2\big]\,\mathbb{E}\big[(f'(X))^2\big]}.$$
If $\mathbb{E}[(f(X)-f(0))^2]=0$ the claim is trivial; otherwise divide by its square root and square both sides to get
$$\mathbb{E}\big[(f(X)-f(0))^2\big]\le4\,\mathbb{E}\big[(f'(X))^2\big].$$
Finally, $\mathrm{Var}(f(X))=\min_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[(f(X)-c)^2]\le\mathbb{E}[(f(X)-f(0))^2]$, which yields $\mathrm{Var}(f(X))\le4\,\mathbb{E}[(f'(X))^2]$. ∎
