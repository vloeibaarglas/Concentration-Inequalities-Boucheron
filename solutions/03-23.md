**Central idea.** Apply the Poisson Poincaré inequality (Exercise 3.21) to $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$; the discrete derivative rationalizes to $(\sqrt{x+1}-\sqrt{x})^2=1/(\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt{x})^2$, and $(\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt{x})^2=2x+1+2\sqrt{x^2+x}\ge4x+1$ gives the pointwise bound $(Df(x))^2\le1/(4x+1)$.

**Solution.** Let $X\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)$ with $\mu=\mathbb{E}X$, and set $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$ for integers $x\ge0$. By the Poisson Poincaré inequality (Exercise 3.21),
$$\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt{X})=\mathrm{Var}(f(X))\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}\big[(Df(X))^2\big],\qquad Df(x)=f(x+1)-f(x).$$
For $x\ge0$, rationalizing the difference,
$$Df(x)=\sqrt{x+1}-\sqrt{x}=\frac{(\sqrt{x+1}-\sqrt{x})(\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt{x})}{\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt{x}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt{x}},$$
so that
$$(Df(x))^2=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt{x})^2}.$$
Now $(\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt{x})^2=(x+1)+x+2\sqrt{(x+1)x}=2x+1+2\sqrt{x^2+x}$. Since $\sqrt{x^2+x}\ge\sqrt{x^2}=x$ for $x\ge0$,
$$(\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt{x})^2\ge2x+1+2x=4x+1,$$
hence
$$(Df(x))^2\le\frac{1}{4x+1}\qquad\text{for every }x\ge0.$$
Therefore
$$\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt{X})\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}\big[(Df(X))^2\big]\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{4X+1}\right]=(\mathbb{E}X)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{4X+1}\right].$$
∎
