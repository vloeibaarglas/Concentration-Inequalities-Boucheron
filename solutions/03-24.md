**Central idea.** Write the centered Gaussian vector as a linear image of iid $N(0,1)$ variables, so $Z=\max_tX_t$ becomes the maximum of linear forms — a Lipschitz function whose gradient, at points where the maximum is uniquely attained, is exactly the coefficient vector of the maximizing coordinate. The Gaussian Poincaré inequality (Theorem 3.2) then bounds $\mathrm{Var}(Z)$ by the expected squared gradient norm, which is at most the largest coordinate variance.

**Solution.** Let $m=|\mathcal{T}|<\infty$ and identify $(X_t)_{t\in\mathcal{T}}$ with a centered Gaussian vector in $\mathbb{R}^m$ with covariance matrix $\Sigma\succeq0$. Write $\Sigma=AA^{\top}$; with $g=(g_1,\dots,g_m)$ iid $N(0,1)$, $(X_t)_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\stackrel{d}{=}(Ag)_{t\in\mathcal{T}}$. Denoting by $a_t\in\mathbb{R}^m$ the $t$-th row of $A$, we have $X_t\stackrel{d}{=}\langle a_t,g\rangle$ and $\mathrm{Var}(X_t)=\|a_t\|^2$.

Thus $Z\stackrel{d}{=}f(g)$ where $f(x)=\max_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\langle a_t,x\rangle$. The function $f$ is convex and Lipschitz with constant $\max_t\|a_t\|$ (since $|f(x)-f(y)|\le\max_t|\langle a_t,x-y\rangle|\le\max_t\|a_t\|\cdot\|x-y\|$), hence differentiable Lebesgue-a.e. by Rademacher's theorem. If at $x$ the maximum is uniquely attained at $t^*(x)$, then, $\mathcal{T}$ being finite, $f$ coincides with the linear map $\langle a_{t^*},\cdot\rangle$ in a neighborhood of $x$, so $\nabla f(x)=a_{t^*(x)}$ and
$$\|\nabla f(x)\|^2=\|a_{t^*(x)}\|^2=\mathrm{Var}(X_{t^*(x)})\le\max_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\mathrm{Var}(X_t)\quad\text{for a.e. }x.$$

By the Gaussian Poincaré inequality (Theorem 3.2), extended from $C^1$ to Lipschitz $f$ by mollification (which preserves the Lipschitz constant and gives a.e. convergence of gradients, so dominated convergence applies),
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)=\mathrm{Var}(f(g))\le\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f(g)\|^2\big]\le\max_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\mathrm{Var}(X_t).$$
∎
