**Central idea.** Use the Efron–Stein inequality in the form $\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[(Z - Z_i')_+^2]$, with $Z = \max_j X_j$ and $Z_i'$ the max after resampling $X_i$. Since $(Z - Z_i')_+ \le (X_i - \max_{j \ne i} X_j)_+$ and at most one index can be the unique maximizer, the sum collapses to $(X_{(n)} - X_{(n-1)})^2$. Both sides are then computed explicitly: via Rényi's representation of exponential spacings, and via the joint density of the top two uniform order statistics.

**Solution.** Let $Z = X_{(n)} = \max_{1 \le j \le n} X_j$ and let $Z_i'$ be $Z$ with $X_i$ replaced by an independent copy $X_i'$. Write $M_{-i} = \max_{j \ne i} X_j$, so $Z = \max(X_i, M_{-i})$ and $Z_i' = \max(X_i', M_{-i})$. Then
$$(Z - Z_i')_+ = \big(\max(X_i, M_{-i}) - \max(X_i', M_{-i})\big)_+ \le (X_i - M_{-i})_+,$$
since if $X_i \le M_{-i}$ both sides are $0$, and if $X_i > M_{-i}$ then $Z = X_i$ and $Z_i' \ge M_{-i}$. The Efron–Stein inequality gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(X_{(n)}) \le \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[(Z - Z_i')_+^2] \le \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[(X_i - M_{-i})_+^2].$$
Now $(X_i - M_{-i})_+ > 0$ implies $X_i > \max_{j \ne i} X_j$, i.e.\ $X_i$ is the unique maximizer; two distinct indices cannot both be unique maximizers. Hence at most one term of the sum is positive, and when $(X_{i_0} - M_{-i_0})_+ > 0$ we have $X_{i_0} = X_{(n)}$, $M_{-i_0} = X_{(n-1)}$. Therefore
$$\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - M_{-i})_+^2 \le (X_{(n)} - X_{(n-1)})^2 \quad \text{a.s.},$$
and $\mathrm{Var}(X_{(n)}) \le \mathbb{E}[(X_{(n)} - X_{(n-1)})^2]$, with no assumption on the laws of the $X_i$.

*Exponential case.* Let $S_k = X_{(k)} - X_{(k-1)}$ ($X_{(0)} := 0$); the $S_k$ are independent exponentials with means $1/n, 1/(n-1), \ldots, 1$. Then $X_{(n)} = \sum_{k=1}^n S_k$, so
$$\mathrm{Var}(X_{(n)}) = \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{1}{(n-k+1)^2} = \sum_{j=1}^n \frac{1}{j^2},$$
while $X_{(n)} - X_{(n-1)} = S_n \sim \mathrm{Exp}(1)$, so $\mathbb{E}[(X_{(n)} - X_{(n-1)})^2] = 2$. Indeed $\sum_{j=1}^n 1/j^2 \le \pi^2/6 < 2$.

*Uniform case.* $X_{(n)}$ has d.f.\ $t^n$ on $[0,1]$, so $\mathbb{E}X_{(n)} = n/(n+1)$, $\mathbb{E}[X_{(n)}^2] = n/(n+2)$, and
$$\mathrm{Var}(X_{(n)}) = \frac{n}{n+2} - \frac{n^2}{(n+1)^2} = \frac{n}{(n+1)^2(n+2)}.$$
The pair $(X_{(n-1)}, X_{(n)})$ has density $n(n-1)s^{n-2}$ on $0 \le s \le t \le 1$, so with $u = s/t$,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}[(X_{(n)} - X_{(n-1)})^2] &= \int_0^1\!\!\int_0^t (t-s)^2\, n(n-1)s^{n-2}\,ds\,dt \\
&= n(n-1)\int_0^1 t^{n+1}\!\!\int_0^1 (1-u)^2 u^{n-2}\,du\,dt \\
&= n(n-1) \cdot \frac{2}{(n+1)n(n-1)} \cdot \frac{1}{n+2} = \frac{2}{(n+1)(n+2)},
\end{align*}
using $\int_0^1 (1-u)^2 u^{n-2}\,du = B(n-1,3) = 2/[(n+1)n(n-1)]$. Again $\frac{n}{(n+1)^2(n+2)} \le \frac{2}{(n+1)(n+2)}$. ∎
