**Central idea.** Apply the Efron–Stein inequality in jackknife (leave-one-out) form. Deleting $X_i$ shifts the median $X_{(m)}$ ($m=n/2$) to $X_{(m+1)}$ exactly when $\mathrm{rank}(X_i)\le m$, and leaves it unchanged otherwise; precisely $m=n/2$ deletions are effective, giving $\mathrm{Var}(X_{(m)})\le \frac{n}{2}\mathbb{E}[(X_{(m+1)}-X_{(m)})^2]$, and exchangeability of uniform spacings converts the upper gap to the lower gap. Both sides are then explicit via Beta/Dirichlet laws: $\mathrm{Var}(X_{(m)})=n/[4(n+1)^2]\sim 1/(4n)$ while the jackknife bound is $n/[(n+1)(n+2)]\sim 1/n$. The jackknife is inconsistent: $n$ times the estimate converges to $\frac12E^2$ ($E\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)$), a non-degenerate law with mean $1\ne 1/4$.

**Solution.** Write $m=n/2$ and $Z=X_{(m)}$. For each $i$, let $Z^{(i)}$ be the median of the $n-1$ observations with $X_i$ deleted, i.e.\ the $m$-th order statistic of the reduced sample (which has odd size $n-1$, so the median is uniquely defined). By the Efron–Stein inequality in jackknife form,
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le \sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big[(Z-Z^{(i)})^2\big].$$
Let $k=\mathrm{rank}(X_i)$ in the full sample (a.s.\ unique). If $k>m$, deleting $X_i$ does not affect the $m$ smallest values, so $Z^{(i)}=X_{(m)}=Z$. If $k\le m$, the $m$-th smallest of the remaining values is $X_{(m+1)}$, so $Z^{(i)}=X_{(m+1)}\ge Z$. Hence, with $\mathbf 1_i=\mathbf 1_{\{\mathrm{rank}(X_i)\le m\}}$,
$$(Z-Z^{(i)})^2=(X_{(m+1)}-X_{(m)})^2\,\mathbf 1_i,$$
and since exactly $m$ observations have rank $\le m$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n(Z-Z^{(i)})^2=(X_{(m+1)}-X_{(m)})^2\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1_i=\frac{n}{2}\,(X_{(m+1)}-X_{(m)})^2\quad\text{a.s.}$$
Taking expectations and using exchangeability of the uniform spacings (the $n+1$ gaps $(X_{(1)},X_{(2)}-X_{(1)},\dots,1-X_{(n)})$ are exchangeable, Dirichlet$(1,\dots,1)$),
$$\mathrm{Var}(X_{(m)})\le \frac{n}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\big[(X_{(m+1)}-X_{(m)})^2\big]=\frac{n}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\big[(X_{(m)}-X_{(m-1)})^2\big].$$

*Explicit values.* $X_{(m)}\sim\mathrm{Beta}(m,n-m+1)=\mathrm{Beta}(n/2,n/2+1)$, so
$$\mathrm{Var}(X_{(m)})=\frac{\frac{n}{2}\cdot\frac{n+2}{2}}{(n+1)^2(n+2)}=\frac{n}{4(n+1)^2}\sim\frac{1}{4n}.$$
The gap $D=X_{(m)}-X_{(m-1)}\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1,n)$, so $\mathbb{E}[D^2]=\frac{2}{(n+1)(n+2)}$ and
$$\frac{n}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[(X_{(m)}-X_{(m-1)})^2\big]=\frac{n}{(n+1)(n+2)}\sim\frac1n.$$
Indeed $\frac{n}{4(n+1)^2}\le\frac{n}{(n+1)(n+2)}$, confirming the inequality.

*Consistency.* The jackknife estimate $\hat V_n=\frac{n}{2}(X_{(m)}-X_{(m-1)})^2$ satisfies $n\hat V_n=\frac12(nD)^2$. Since $D\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1,n)$, $\mathbb{P}\{nD>t\}=(1-t/n)^n\to e^{-t}$, i.e.\ $nD\Rightarrow E\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)$, so $n\hat V_n\Rightarrow \frac12E^2$. This limit is non-degenerate, hence $n\hat V_n\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\not\to}1/4$ (the true asymptotic variance, as $\sqrt n\,(X_{(m)}-\tfrac12)\Rightarrow N(0,\tfrac14)$). The jackknife is **inconsistent** for the median: it neither concentrates nor has the right mean ($\mathbb{E}[\tfrac12E^2]=1\ne\tfrac14$, a fourfold overestimate). ∎
