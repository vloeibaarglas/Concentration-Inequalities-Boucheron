**Central idea.** For $Z=\mathbb{E}_\sigma\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\sum_i\sigma_i f(X_i)$ with $0\le f\le 1$, take $Z_i$ to be the Rademacher average with coordinate $i$ dropped. Then $0\le Z-Z_i\le 1$: the upper bound because $\sup_f\sigma_i f(X_i)\le 1$, the lower bound by Jensen ($\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_i}\sup_f\ge\sup_f\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_i}$, killing the $i$-th term since $\mathbb{E}\sigma_i=0$). For the sum bound, couple through the $\sigma$-wise (near-)maximizer $\hat f$: $Z-Z_i\le\mathbb{E}_\sigma[\sigma_i\hat f(X_i)]$, and $\sum_i\sigma_i\hat f(X_i)=\sup_f\sum_i\sigma_i f(X_i)$, so $\sum_i(Z-Z_i)\le Z$. Hence $Z$ is $(1,0)$-self-bounding.

**Solution.** Let $\mathcal F$ be a countable class of measurable $f:\mathcal X\to[0,1]$, $X_1,\dots,X_n$ independent, and $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_n$ iid Rademacher independent of $X$. The conditional Rademacher average is
$$Z=Z(X)=\mathbb{E}_\sigma\Big[\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\sum_{i=1}^n\sigma_i f(X_i)\Big],$$
a nonnegative function of $X=(X_1,\dots,X_n)$. For each $i$ put
$$Z_i=\mathbb{E}_\sigma\Big[\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_j f(X_j)\Big],$$
which does not depend on $X_i$ or $\sigma_i$. We show $0\le Z-Z_i\le 1$ for all $i$ and $\sum_i(Z-Z_i)\le Z$.

*Upper bound.* For fixed $\sigma$,
$$\sup_f\sum_{j=1}^n\sigma_j f(X_j)\le \sup_f\big[\sigma_i f(X_i)\big]+\sup_f\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_j f(X_j)\le 1+\sup_f\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_j f(X_j),$$
since $\sigma_i f(X_i)\le 1$ ($0\le f\le 1$). Taking $\mathbb{E}_\sigma$ gives $Z\le Z_i+1$.

*Lower bound.* By Jensen, for fixed $\sigma^{(i)}=(\sigma_j)_{j\ne i}$,
$$\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_i}\Big[\sup_f\sum_{j=1}^n\sigma_j f(X_j)\Big]\ge \sup_f\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_i}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^n\sigma_j f(X_j)\Big]=\sup_f\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_j f(X_j),$$
as $\mathbb{E}[\sigma_i]=0$. Taking $\mathbb{E}_{\sigma^{(i)}}$ yields $Z\ge Z_i$. Thus $0\le Z-Z_i\le 1$.

*Sum bound.* Fix $\sigma$ and $\epsilon>0$, and choose $f_{\epsilon,\sigma}\in\mathcal F$ with $\sum_j\sigma_j f_{\epsilon,\sigma}(X_j)\ge \sup_f\sum_j\sigma_j f(X_j)-\epsilon$. Then
$$\sup_f\sum_{j=1}^n\sigma_j f(X_j)-\sup_g\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_j g(X_j)\le \sigma_i f_{\epsilon,\sigma}(X_i)+\epsilon,$$
using the near-maximizer for the first supremum and evaluating the second at $f_{\epsilon,\sigma}$. Taking $\mathbb{E}_\sigma$,
$$Z-Z_i\le \mathbb{E}_\sigma\big[\sigma_i f_{\epsilon,\sigma}(X_i)\big]+\epsilon.$$
Summing over $i$ and noting $\sum_i\sigma_i f_{\epsilon,\sigma}(X_i)\le \sup_f\sum_i\sigma_i f(X_i)$ pointwise,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n(Z-Z_i)\le \mathbb{E}_\sigma\Big[\sup_f\sum_{i=1}^n\sigma_i f(X_i)\Big]+n\epsilon=Z+n\epsilon.$$
Since $\epsilon>0$ is arbitrary, $\sum_i(Z-Z_i)\le Z$.

Therefore $Z$ is $(1,0)$-self-bounding: with the functions $Z_i$ above, $0\le Z-Z_i\le 1$ and $\sum_i(Z-Z_i)\le Z$. In particular the Efron–Stein inequality gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\mathbb{E}[Z]$. ∎
