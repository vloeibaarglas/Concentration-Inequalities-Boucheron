**Central idea.** Write $Z=\sum_tT_t$ over the $\binom n3$ triples, $T_t$ the indicator that triple $t$ is a triangle. Then $\mathrm{Var}(Z)$ has only two non-zero covariance types: $\mathrm{Var}(T_t)=p^3(1-p^3)$, and for triples sharing exactly an edge, $\mathrm{Cov}(T_t,T_s)=p^5-p^6$ (five distinct edges involved); triples sharing $\le 1$ vertex are independent. Counting $\binom n2(n-2)(n-3)$ ordered edge-sharing pairs gives the exact variance. For Efron–Stein, resampling one edge $e$ changes $Z$ by $(X_e-X_e')\sum_{t\ni e}M_t$ with $M_t$ the product of the other two edges; independence of $X_e$ from the $M_t$'s and $\mathbb{E}|X_e-X_e'|=2p(1-p)$ yield a bound whose $n^4$-order (covariance) term matches the truth exactly, the looseness being confined to the $n^3$-order term.

**Solution.** Let $X_e\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ be iid over the $N=\binom n2$ potential edges. For a triple $t=\{i,j,k\}$ put $T_t=X_{ij}X_{jk}X_{ik}$; then $Z=\sum_tT_t$ over the $\binom n3$ triples, $\mathbb{E}[Z]=\binom n3p^3$.

*Exact variance.* $\mathrm{Var}(T_t)=p^3(1-p^3)$. For $t\ne s$, $\mathrm{Cov}(T_t,T_s)$ depends on the shared edges: if $t,s$ share at most one vertex they involve disjoint edge sets, so the covariance is $0$. If they share exactly two vertices, i.e.\ an edge $e$ (say $t=\{1,2,3\}$, $s=\{1,2,4\}$), then with five distinct edges,
$$\mathbb{E}[T_tT_s]=\mathbb{E}[X_{12}^2X_{23}X_{13}X_{24}X_{14}]=p^5,$$
so $\mathrm{Cov}(T_t,T_s)=p^5-p^6=p^5(1-p)$. The number of ordered pairs $(t,s)$, $t\ne s$, sharing an edge: choose the edge ($\binom n2$), the third vertex of $t$ ($n-2$), the third vertex of $s$ ($n-3$, must differ). Hence
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)=\binom n3p^3(1-p^3)+\binom n2(n-2)(n-3)\,p^5(1-p).$$

*Efron–Stein.* Resample edge $e$ to $X_e'$. Only the $n-2$ triangles containing $e$ can change: with $M_t$ the product of the two edges of $t$ other than $e$,
$$Z-Z_e'=(X_e-X_e')\sum_{t\ni e}M_t.$$
By $(X_e,X_e')$-symmetry, $\mathbb{E}[(Z-Z_e')_+^2]=\tfrac12\mathbb{E}[(Z-Z_e')^2]$, and $(X_e-X_e')^2=|X_e-X_e'|$ is independent of the $M_t$'s with mean $2p(1-p)$. For $e=\{i,j\}$, $\sum_{t\ni e}M_t=\sum_{k\ne i,j}X_{ik}X_{jk}$, so
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{t\ni e}M_t\Big)^2\Big]=(n-2)p^2+(n-2)(n-3)p^4.$$
Efron–Stein ($\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sum_e\mathbb{E}[(Z-Z_e')_+^2]$) gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le \binom n2p(1-p)\Big[(n-2)p^2+(n-2)(n-3)p^4\Big].$$
The $n^4$ term is $\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}{2}p^5(1-p)$, exactly matching the true covariance contribution; the $n^3$ term exceeds the truth by the factor $\frac{3}{1+p+p^2}\in[1,3]$. Thus Efron–Stein is sharp on the dominant (dependent-pair) term and loose only on the lower-order diagonal term. ∎
