**Central idea.** Raise the Kraft sum to the $n$-th power and count: the $n$-fold product expands into $\sum_k N_k D^{-k}$ where $N_k$ counts $n$-tuples of codewords with total length $k$. Unique decodability makes the concatenation map injective, so $N_k \le D^k$ (the number of length-$k$ strings over $\mathcal{Y}$); the $n$-th root then kills the polynomial factor as $n \to \infty$. The entropy bound follows by applying Gibbs' inequality to $p$ against the normalized code-length distribution $q(x) = D^{-|\phi(x)|}/S$, where the Kraft inequality forces the normalizing constant $S \le 1$ to only help.

**Solution.** Let $D = |\mathcal{Y}|$ and $\ell_x = |\phi(x)|$.

*Kraft–McMillan.* First let $\mathcal{X} = \{x_1,\dots,x_m\}$ be finite, with $\ell_i = \ell_{x_i}$ and $\ell_{\max} = \max_i \ell_i$. Then
$$\left(\sum_{i=1}^m D^{-\ell_i}\right)^n = \sum_{i_1,\dots,i_n} D^{-(\ell_{i_1}+\cdots+\ell_{i_n})} = \sum_{k=1}^{n\ell_{\max}} N_k D^{-k},$$
where $N_k$ is the number of $n$-tuples $(x_{i_1},\dots,x_{i_n})$ with $\ell_{i_1}+\cdots+\ell_{i_n} = k$. By unique decodability, distinct $n$-tuples give distinct concatenated codewords, and a concatenation of total length $k$ is a string of length $k$ over $\mathcal{Y}$, of which there are exactly $D^k$. Hence $N_k \le D^k$, so
$$\left(\sum_{i=1}^m D^{-\ell_i}\right)^n \le \sum_{k=1}^{n\ell_{\max}} 1 = n\ell_{\max}.$$
Taking $n$-th roots and letting $n \to \infty$ gives $\sum_{i=1}^m D^{-\ell_i} \le 1$. For countable $\mathcal{X}$, every finite subset satisfies the bound; the inequality extends by monotone convergence.

*Entropy lower bound.* If $\mathbb{E}\,|\phi(X)| = \infty$ there is nothing to prove; assume it finite. Let $S = \sum_{x} D^{-\ell_x} \le 1$ (so $S > 0$) and $q(x) = D^{-\ell_x}/S$, a probability distribution on $\mathcal{X}$. By Gibbs' inequality (Jensen on $t \mapsto t\log t$), with $0\log 0 := 0$,
$$0 \le \sum_x p(x)\log_D\frac{p(x)}{q(x)} = \sum_x p(x)\log_D p(x) - \sum_x p(x)\log_D q(x).$$
Since $\log_D q(x) = -\ell_x - \log_D S$,
$$\sum_x p(x)\log_D q(x) = -\mathbb{E}\,|\phi(X)| - \log_D S.$$
Hence $-\frac{H(X)}{\log D} \ge -\mathbb{E}\,|\phi(X)| - \log_D S$, i.e.
$$\frac{H(X)}{\log D} \le \mathbb{E}\,|\phi(X)| + \log_D S \le \mathbb{E}\,|\phi(X)|,$$
because $S \le 1$ gives $\log_D S \le 0$. The left side is $\mathbb{E}[-\log_D p(X)]$. ∎
