**Central idea.** The duality formula (Corollary 4.15) writes each atomic divergence $D(P\|Q|\mathcal{G})$ as a supremum over $\mathcal{G}$-measurable test functions, so shrinking the $\sigma$-algebra shrinks the supremum (data processing), and quantizing test functions shows $D(P\|Q)$ is the supremum over finite partitions. To lift Han's inequality (Theorem 4.9) to uncountable $\mathcal{X}$, discretize each coordinate by finite partitions and push forward to a finite product space where the countable 4.9 applies; a diagonal common-refinement argument makes one discretization serve all $n$ marginals at once. Theorem 4.10 then follows exactly as in the countable case, using $dQ^{(i)}/dP^{(i)}=\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Z$.

**Solution.** Recall the duality formula (Corollary 4.15): for probability measures $P,Q$,
$$D(P\|Q)=\sup_Z\big\{\mathbb{E}_PZ-\log\mathbb{E}_Qe^Z\big\}$$
over all measurable $Z$ with $\mathbb{E}_Qe^Z<\infty$. If $\mathcal{G}$ has countably many atoms, $D(P\|Q|\mathcal{G})$ is the divergence of the restrictions to $(\mathrm{atom}(\mathcal{G}),\mathcal{G})$, so
$$D(P\|Q|\mathcal{G})=\sup\big\{\mathbb{E}_PZ-\log\mathbb{E}_Qe^Z:Z\ \mathcal{G}\text{-measurable},\ \mathbb{E}_Qe^Z<\infty\big\}.\tag{1}$$

*Data processing.* If $\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{G}$ (both with countably many atoms), every $\mathcal{H}$-measurable $Z$ is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable, so the supremum in (1) for $\mathcal{H}$ is over a smaller set: $D(P\|Q|\mathcal{H})\le D(P\|Q|\mathcal{G})$.

*Supremum over finite partitions.* Clearly $D(P\|Q|\mathcal{G})\le D(P\|Q)$ for every finite $\mathcal{G}$, so $\sup_{\mathcal{G}}D(P\|Q|\mathcal{G})\le D(P\|Q)$. For the reverse, let $Z$ be bounded, $|Z|\le M$. Quantizing $[-M,M]$ into intervals of length $<\delta$ gives a finite $\mathcal{G}=\sigma(\{Z\in I_k\})$ and a $\mathcal{G}$-measurable $Z'$ with $\|Z-Z'\|_\infty<\delta$. Then $|\mathbb{E}_PZ-\mathbb{E}_PZ'|<\delta$ and, $\log$ being $e^M$-Lipschitz on $[e^{-M},e^M]$, $|\log\mathbb{E}_Qe^Z-\log\mathbb{E}_Qe^{Z'}|\le e^{2M}(e^\delta-1)$. Hence $D(P\|Q|\mathcal{G})\ge\mathbb{E}_PZ-\log\mathbb{E}_Qe^Z-\epsilon(\delta)$ with $\epsilon(\delta)\to0$. Thus $\sup_{\mathcal{G}}D(P\|Q|\mathcal{G})\ge\sup_{\text{bounded }Z}\{\mathbb{E}_PZ-\log\mathbb{E}_Qe^Z\}$, and the latter equals $D(P\|Q)$ by truncation ($Z_n=(-n)\vee(\log\frac{dP}{dQ}\wedge n)$; if $D(P\|Q)<\infty$ then $\mathbb{E}_P|\log\frac{dP}{dQ}|<\infty$ since $\int_{\{f<1\}}|\log f|\,dP=\int_{\{g>1\}}\frac{\log g}{g}\,dQ\le1/e$, so dominated convergence gives $\mathbb{E}_PZ_n\to D(P\|Q)$, $\mathbb{E}_Qe^{Z_n}\to1$; if $D(P\|Q)=\infty$ the same truncations, or $Z_n=n1_A$ with $P(A)>0=Q(A)$, drive the functional to $\infty$). Therefore
$$D(P\|Q)=\sup\big\{D(P\|Q|\mathcal{G}):\mathcal{G}\text{ has finitely many atoms}\big\}.\tag{2}$$

*Product partitions are cofinal.* Let $\mu,\nu$ be probabilities on $\mathcal{X}^m$ with the product $\sigma$-algebra. Then $D(\mu\|\nu)=\sup D(\mu\|\nu|\mathcal{G})$ over finite product partitions $\mathcal{G}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^m\mathcal{P}_j$. Indeed, "$\le$" is clear; for "$\ge$", given bounded $Z$ ($|Z|\le M$) and $\delta>0$, approximate $Z$ in $L^1(\mu+\nu)$ by $W_0=\sum_kc_k1_{R_k}$ with $R_k$ measurable rectangles (finite linear combinations of rectangles are $L^1$-dense), truncate to $W=(-M-1)\vee(W_0\wedge(M+1))$ (still $\|Z-W\|_{L^1}\le\delta$), and let $\mathcal{G}$ be the product partition generated by the sides of the $R_k$, so $W$ is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable. By (1), $D(\mu\|\nu|\mathcal{G})\ge\mathbb{E}_\mu W-\log\mathbb{E}_\nu e^W\ge\mathbb{E}_\mu Z-\log\mathbb{E}_\nu e^Z-C\delta$. Letting $\delta\to0$ and taking $\sup$ over bounded $Z$ gives the claim.

*Theorem 4.9 for general $\mathcal{X}$.* Let $P=\bigotimes_{j=1}^nP_j$ on $\mathcal{X}^n$ and $Q$ arbitrary. For each $i$ and $\epsilon>0$, the lemma gives finite partitions $\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_j$ ($j\ne i$) of $\mathcal{X}$ with $D(Q^{(i)}\|P^{(i)}|\bigotimes_{j\ne i}\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_j)\ge D(Q^{(i)}\|P^{(i)})-\epsilon$ (with the obvious $M\to\infty$ modification if the divergence is infinite). Put $\mathcal{F}_j=\bigvee_{i\ne j}\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_j$; then $\bigotimes_{j\ne i}\mathcal{F}_j\supset\bigotimes_{j\ne i}\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_j$, so by data processing $D(Q^{(i)}\|P^{(i)}|\bigotimes_{j\ne i}\mathcal{F}_j)\ge D(Q^{(i)}\|P^{(i)})-\epsilon$ for every $i$. Let $\pi_j:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}_j:=\mathrm{atom}(\mathcal{F}_j)$ and $\pi=\bigotimes_j\pi_j:\mathcal{X}^n\to\mathcal{Y}:=\prod_j\mathcal{Y}_j$, with pushforwards $\bar Q=Q\circ\pi^{-1}$, $\bar P=P\circ\pi^{-1}=\bigotimes_j\bar P_j$ (a product since $P$ is). The countable Theorem 4.9 on the finite space $\mathcal{Y}$ gives $D(\bar Q\|\bar P)\ge\frac1{n-1}\sum_iD(\bar Q^{(i)}\|\bar P^{(i)})$. Here $D(\bar Q\|\bar P)=D(Q\|P|\mathcal{G})\le D(Q\|P)$ for $\mathcal{G}=\bigotimes_j\sigma(\mathcal{F}_j)$, and $D(\bar Q^{(i)}\|\bar P^{(i)})=D(Q^{(i)}\|P^{(i)}|\bigotimes_{j\ne i}\sigma(\mathcal{F}_j))$. Hence
$$D(Q\|P)\ge\frac1{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\big(D(Q^{(i)}\|P^{(i)})-\epsilon\big),$$
and $\epsilon\to0$ yields Theorem 4.9 for general $\mathcal{X}$.

*Theorem 4.10 for general $\mathcal{X}$.* Let $X_1,\dots,X_n$ be independent, $Z=f(X_1,\dots,X_n)\ge0$ measurable. If $\mathbb{E}Z=0$ there is nothing to prove; otherwise scale to $\mathbb{E}Z=1$ (both sides are homogeneous of degree one). Define $Q$ on $\mathcal{X}^n$ by $dQ/dP=Z$, so $D(Q\|P)=\mathbb{E}_P[Z\log Z]=\mathrm{Ent}(Z)$. By the general Theorem 4.9, in its equivalent form,
$$D(Q\|P)\le\sum_{i=1}^n\big[D(Q\|P)-D(Q^{(i)}\|P^{(i)})\big].$$
For $B\subset\mathcal{X}^{n-1}$, $Q^{(i)}(B)=\int_{B\times\mathcal{X}}Z\,dP=\int_B\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Z\,dP^{(i)}$ (using that $P$ is a product), so $dQ^{(i)}/dP^{(i)}=\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Z$. Thus $D(Q^{(i)}\|P^{(i)})=\mathbb{E}_P[(\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Z)\log(\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Z)]$, while $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Ent}^{(i)}(Z)]=\mathbb{E}_P[\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[Z\log Z]]-\mathbb{E}_P[(\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Z)\log(\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Z)]=\mathbb{E}_P[Z\log Z]-D(Q^{(i)}\|P^{(i)})$. Hence $D(Q\|P)-D(Q^{(i)}\|P^{(i)})=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Ent}^{(i)}(Z)]$, and
$$\mathrm{Ent}(Z)\le\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Ent}^{(i)}(Z)\big],$$
which is Theorem 4.10 for general $\mathcal{X}$. ∎
