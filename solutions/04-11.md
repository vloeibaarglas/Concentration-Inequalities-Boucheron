**Central idea.** The Donsker–Varadhan duality representation (Lemma 4.14) writes $D(Q\|P)$ as a supremum, over bounded measurable $f$, of functionals that are affine in $Q$; a supremum of affine functions is convex, which is exactly the claim.

**Solution.** Recall the duality representation of the Kullback–Leibler divergence (Lemma 4.14): for probability measures $Q, P$ on $\mathcal{X}$,
$$D(Q\|P) = \sup_{f} \left\{ \int f\,dQ - \log \int e^{f}\,dP \right\},$$
where the supremum is taken over all bounded measurable functions $f: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ (the supremum equals $+\infty$ when $Q \not\ll P$, in agreement with $D(Q\|P) = \infty$). For each fixed $f$, the map
$$Q \longmapsto \int f\,dQ - \log \int e^{f}\,dP$$
is affine in $Q$, since $\int f\,dQ$ is linear in $Q$ and the second term does not depend on $Q$.

Let $Q_{1}, Q_{2}$ be probability measures on $\mathcal{X}$ and $\lambda \in [0,1]$, and set $Q_{\lambda} = \lambda Q_{1} + (1-\lambda) Q_{2}$. Then
\begin{align*}
D(Q_{\lambda}\|P) &= \sup_{f} \left\{ \int f\,dQ_{\lambda} - \log \int e^{f}\,dP \right\} \\
&= \sup_{f} \left\{ \lambda\left(\int f\,dQ_{1} - \log \int e^{f}\,dP\right) + (1-\lambda)\left(\int f\,dQ_{2} - \log \int e^{f}\,dP\right) \right\} \\
&\le \lambda \sup_{f} \left\{ \int f\,dQ_{1} - \log \int e^{f}\,dP \right\} + (1-\lambda) \sup_{f} \left\{ \int f\,dQ_{2} - \log \int e^{f}\,dP \right\} \\
&= \lambda D(Q_{1}\|P) + (1-\lambda) D(Q_{2}\|P),
\end{align*}
using linearity of the integral in $Q$ and the elementary inequality $\sup_{f}[\lambda a_{f} + (1-\lambda) b_{f}] \le \lambda \sup_{f} a_{f} + (1-\lambda) \sup_{f} b_{f}$, valid in $[-\infty,\infty]$. Thus $Q \mapsto D(Q\|P)$ is convex on the set of probability distributions over $\mathcal{X}$. ∎
