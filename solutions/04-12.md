**Central idea.** Split the log-likelihood ratio $\log \frac{d\mathbf{P}}{d(Q_X \otimes Q_Y)}$ into the "dependence" part $\log \frac{d\mathbf{P}}{d(P_X \otimes P_Y)}$ plus the two marginal log-ratios $\log \frac{dP_X}{dQ_X}(x) + \log \frac{dP_Y}{dQ_Y}(y)$; integrating against $\mathbf{P}$, the marginal terms collapse to $D(P_X\|Q_X)$ and $D(P_Y\|Q_Y)$ because the marginals of $\mathbf{P}$ are $P_X$ and $P_Y$.

**Solution.** The identity is understood in $[0,\infty]$. If $D(\mathbf{P}\|Q_X \otimes Q_Y) = \infty$, then at least one term on the right is infinite: finiteness of all three would give $\mathbf{P} \ll P_X \otimes P_Y$ and $P_X \otimes P_Y \ll Q_X \otimes Q_Y$ (the latter by the $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem from $P_X \ll Q_X$, $P_Y \ll Q_Y$), hence $\mathbf{P} \ll Q_X \otimes Q_Y$ by transitivity, a contradiction. Assume therefore $\mathbf{P} \ll Q_X \otimes Q_Y$.

Then $P_X \ll Q_X$ and $P_Y \ll Q_Y$: if $Q_X(A) = 0$ then $(Q_X \otimes Q_Y)(A \times \mathcal{Y}) = 0$, so $P_X(A) = \mathbf{P}(A \times \mathcal{Y}) = 0$, and likewise for $P_Y$. Write $f = \frac{d\mathbf{P}}{d(Q_X \otimes Q_Y)}$, $g_X = \frac{dP_X}{dQ_X}$, $g_Y = \frac{dP_Y}{dQ_Y}$. On rectangles, $(P_X \otimes P_Y)(A \times B) = \int_{A \times B} g_X(x) g_Y(y)\,d(Q_X \otimes Q_Y)$, so by the $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem
$$\frac{d(P_X \otimes P_Y)}{d(Q_X \otimes Q_Y)} = g_X \otimes g_Y.$$
Moreover, $\frac{d\mathbf{P}}{d(P_X \otimes P_Y)} = \frac{f}{g_X \otimes g_Y}$ (with $\frac{f}{0} := 0$): for any measurable $E$,
$$\int_E \frac{f}{g_X g_Y}\,d(P_X \otimes P_Y) = \int_E \frac{f}{g_X g_Y}(g_X g_Y)\,d(Q_X \otimes Q_Y) = \int_E f\,d(Q_X \otimes Q_Y) = \mathbf{P}(E),$$
since on $\{g_X g_Y = 0\}$ one has $f = 0$ a.e. $[Q_X \otimes Q_Y]$ (e.g. $\mathbf{P}(\{g_X = 0\} \times \mathcal{Y}) = P_X(\{g_X = 0\}) = 0$, and likewise for $g_Y$).

Hence, with $h = \frac{f}{g_X \otimes g_Y} = \frac{d\mathbf{P}}{d(P_X \otimes P_Y)}$ a.e.,
\begin{align*}
D(\mathbf{P}\|Q_X \otimes Q_Y) &= \int \log f\,d\mathbf{P} = \int \log h\,d\mathbf{P} + \int \log\big(g_X(x) g_Y(y)\big)\,d\mathbf{P}(x,y) \\
&= D(\mathbf{P}\|P_X \otimes P_Y) + \int \log g_X(x)\,dP_X(x) + \int \log g_Y(y)\,dP_Y(y) \\
&= D(\mathbf{P}\|P_X \otimes P_Y) + D(P_X\|Q_X) + D(P_Y\|Q_Y),
\end{align*}
where the marginal collapse uses that the $x$-marginal of $\mathbf{P}$ is $P_X$ and the $y$-marginal is $P_Y$, and $\log f = \log h + \log(g_X g_Y)$ holds $\mathbf{P}$-a.e. since $f > 0$ $\mathbf{P}$-a.e. ∎
