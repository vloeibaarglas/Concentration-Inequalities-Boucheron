**Central idea.** This is Fenchel–Legendre duality for the log-moment-generating function.

*Direction $\le$.* The Gibbs variational inequality $\mathbb{E}_Qf\le D(Q\|P)+\log\mathbb{E}_Pe^f$, applied with $f=\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)$, gives $\psi^*(t)\le\inf\{D(Q\|P):\mathbb{E}_QZ-\mathbb{E}Z\ge t\}$.

*Direction $\ge$.* The exponentially tilted laws $dQ_\lambda/dP=e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)-\psi(\lambda)}$ satisfy $D(Q_\lambda\|P)=\lambda m(\lambda)-\psi(\lambda)$, where $m(\lambda)=\psi'(\lambda)$ is the tilted mean. Whenever $t$ is a tilted mean, $Q_\lambda$ attains $\psi^*(t)$. The remaining values of $t$ fall into two cases:
- *Atom at the essential supremum.* Condition on it.
- *No exponential moments beyond the abscissa $b$ of convergence.* Use a heavy-tail lemma: a law $R$ with no exponential moments has $\liminf_M\frac1M\log\frac1{R(W>M)}=0$. Mixing $R$ with $R(\cdot\mid W>M)$ along such $M$ raises the mean to $t$ at vanishing extra divergence.

(An earlier version claimed $\varepsilon_M\log(1/R(W\ge M))\to0$ along *all* $M$ via a Chernoff bound. That uses Chernoff in the wrong direction; for a Gaussian the quantity grows like $M/2$. The subsequence argument below replaces it.)

**Solution.** The statement involves $\mathbb{E}Z$, so $Z$ is assumed integrable. Let $P$ be the underlying probability measure and set $W:=Z-\mathbb{E}Z$, so $\mathbb{E}_PW=0$. Let $\psi(\lambda):=\log\mathbb{E}_Pe^{\lambda W}\in[0,\infty]$; it is convex, $\psi(0)=0$, and $\psi\ge0$ by Jensen. The constraint set consists of the $Q$ for which $\mathbb{E}_QW$ exists (in $(-\infty,\infty]$) and is $\ge t$. Only $Q\ll P$ matter, since otherwise $D(Q\|P)=\infty$. Write $I(t)$ for the infimum of $D(Q\|P)$ over this set and fix $t>0$.

For $\lambda<0$, $\lambda t-\psi(\lambda)<0=0\cdot t-\psi(0)$, hence
$$\psi^*(t)=\sup_{\lambda\ge0}\big(\lambda t-\psi(\lambda)\big)\ge0.$$

**(a) $\psi^*(t)\le I(t)$.**

*Gibbs inequality.* Let $Q\ll P$ with $D(Q\|P)<\infty$, $h=dQ/dP$, and let $f$ satisfy $\mathbb{E}_Pe^f<\infty$. Then
$$\mathbb{E}_Qf\le D(Q\|P)+\log\mathbb{E}_Pe^f.$$

*Proof.* First, $\mathbb{E}_Qf^+<\infty$. Young's inequality $ab\le a\log a-a+e^b$ (valid for $a\ge0$) gives $hf^+\le h\log h-h+e^{f^+}$. Moreover $\mathbb{E}_P|h\log h|<\infty$, because $h\log h\ge-1/e$ and $\mathbb{E}_Ph\log h=D(Q\|P)<\infty$. Hence
$$\mathbb{E}_Qf^+\le D(Q\|P)-1+\mathbb{E}_Pe^{f^+}\le D(Q\|P)+\mathbb{E}_Pe^f<\infty.$$
If $\mathbb{E}_Qf=-\infty$, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise $f\in L^1(Q)$. Also $\log h\in L^1(Q)$: we have $\mathbb{E}_Q(\log h)^-=\mathbb{E}_P[h(\log h)^-]\le1/e$, and $\mathbb{E}_Q\log h=D(Q\|P)$. Jensen for the concave $\log$ under $Q$ then gives
$$\mathbb{E}_Qf-D(Q\|P)=\mathbb{E}_Q\log\frac{e^f}{h}\le\log\mathbb{E}_Q\frac{e^f}{h}=\log\mathbb{E}_P\big[e^f\mathbf{1}_{\{h>0\}}\big]\le\log\mathbb{E}_Pe^f.\qquad\square$$

*Applying it.* Let $Q$ be feasible with $D(Q\|P)<\infty$; otherwise the bound $\psi^*(t)\le D(Q\|P)$ is trivial. Let $\lambda\ge0$; if $\psi(\lambda)=\infty$, then $\lambda t-\psi(\lambda)=-\infty$. Otherwise apply the Gibbs inequality to $f=\lambda W$. Since $\mathbb{E}_QW$ exists and is $\ge t$,
$$\lambda t-\psi(\lambda)\le\lambda\,\mathbb{E}_QW-\psi(\lambda)\le D(Q\|P).$$
(For $\lambda>0$ the Gibbs step also shows $\mathbb{E}_QW<\infty$.) Take the supremum over $\lambda\ge0$, then the infimum over feasible $Q$.

**(b) $I(t)\le\psi^*(t)$.** Put $L:=\psi^*(t)$ and assume $L<\infty$; otherwise there is nothing to prove.

*Tilted laws.* Let $b:=\sup\{\lambda\ge0:\psi(\lambda)<\infty\}\in[0,\infty]$. For $0\le\lambda<b$:
- $\mathbb{E}_P[|W|e^{\lambda W}]<\infty$. On $\{W\le0\}$ the integrand is at most $|W|$. On $\{W>0\}$ it is at most $C\,e^{\lambda'W}$ for any $\lambda<\lambda'<b$.
- Hence, by dominated convergence, $\psi$ is differentiable on $[0,b)$ (right derivative at $0$), with
$$\psi'(\lambda)=m(\lambda):=\mathbb{E}_{Q_\lambda}W,\qquad \frac{dQ_\lambda}{dP}:=e^{\lambda W-\psi(\lambda)}.$$
- $m(0)=\mathbb{E}_PW=0$.
- $m$ is nondecreasing and continuous on $[0,b)$: it is the derivative of a convex function, and right-continuity at $0$ follows by dominated convergence.
- $D(Q_\lambda\|P)=\mathbb{E}_{Q_\lambda}[\lambda W-\psi(\lambda)]=\lambda m(\lambda)-\psi(\lambda)$.

Set $t_{\max}:=\sup_{[0,b)}m=\lim_{\lambda\uparrow b}m(\lambda)\in[0,\infty]$, with $t_{\max}:=0$ if $b=0$.

*Case A: $0<t<t_{\max}$.* By continuity and $m(0)=0$, there is $\lambda\in(0,b)$ with $m(\lambda)=t$. Then $Q_\lambda$ is feasible ($\mathbb{E}_{Q_\lambda}W=t$), and
$$D(Q_\lambda\|P)=\lambda t-\psi(\lambda)\le L.$$

So assume from now on that $t\ge t_{\max}$.

*Heavy-tail lemma.* Let $R$ be a probability measure with $\mathbb{E}_R|W|<\infty$ and $\mathbb{E}_Re^{\varepsilon W}=\infty$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. Then $R(W>M)>0$ for all $M$, and
$$\liminf_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{M}\log\frac{1}{R(W>M)}=0.$$

*Proof.* If $R(W>M_0)=0$, then $W\le M_0$ $R$-a.s. and every exponential moment is finite. The $\liminf$ is $\ge0$ since $R(W>M)\le1$. If it were strictly positive (possibly $+\infty$), pick a finite $c>0$ with $2c$ below it; then $R(W>u)\le e^{-cu}$ for all $u\ge M_0$, for some $M_0$. With $\varepsilon=c/2$, the layer-cake formula gives
$$\mathbb{E}_Re^{\varepsilon W}\le e^{\varepsilon M_0}+\int_{M_0}^\infty\varepsilon e^{\varepsilon u}R(W>u)\,du<\infty,$$
a contradiction. $\square$

*Mixture construction.* Let $R\ll P$ satisfy the lemma's hypotheses, with $r:=\mathbb{E}_RW<t$. Choose $M_j\to\infty$ with $M_j>t$ and $\frac1{M_j}\log\frac1{R(A_j)}\to0$, where $A_j=\{W>M_j\}$. Define:
- $R_j:=R(\cdot\mid A_j)$ and $\mu_j:=\mathbb{E}_{R_j}W\in[M_j,\infty)$; finiteness holds because $\mathbb{E}_R|W|<\infty$;
- $\varepsilon_j:=\frac{t-r}{\mu_j-r}\in(0,1)$;
- $Q_j:=(1-\varepsilon_j)R+\varepsilon_jR_j$.

Then $\mathbb{E}_{Q_j}W=r+\varepsilon_j(\mu_j-r)=t$, so $Q_j$ is feasible.

$D(\cdot\|P)$ is convex: integrate the pointwise inequality $\phi((1-\varepsilon)h_1+\varepsilon h_2)\le(1-\varepsilon)\phi(h_1)+\varepsilon\phi(h_2)$ for $\phi(x)=x\log x$. Hence
$$D(Q_j\|P)\le(1-\varepsilon_j)D(R\|P)+\varepsilon_jD(R_j\|P).$$
Also $\frac{dR_j}{dP}=\frac{dR}{dP}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_j}}{R(A_j)}$, so
$$D(R_j\|P)=\mathbb{E}_{R_j}\Big[\log\frac{dR}{dP}\Big]+\log\frac1{R(A_j)}.$$
Finally,
$$\varepsilon_j\log\frac1{R(A_j)}\le\frac{t-r}{M_j-r}\log\frac1{R(A_j)}\longrightarrow0.\tag{$\ast$}$$

*Case B: $b=0$.* Then $\psi(\lambda)=\infty$ for every $\lambda>0$, so $L=0$. Take $R=P$; then $r=0<t$, and $\mathbb{E}_Pe^{\varepsilon W}=\infty$ for all $\varepsilon>0$. Here $dR/dP=1$, so $D(R\|P)=0$, and by $(\ast)$
$$D(Q_j\|P)\le\varepsilon_j\log\frac1{P(A_j)}\to0.$$
Hence $I(t)=0=L$.

*Case C: $0<b<\infty$ and $t\ge t_{\max}$.*

Step 1: $\psi(b)<\infty$. On $[0,b)$, $\psi(\lambda)=\int_0^\lambda m\le\lambda t_{\max}\le bt$. As $\lambda\uparrow b$, $\mathbb{E}_Pe^{\lambda W}\to\mathbb{E}_Pe^{bW}$ by monotone convergence on $\{W>0\}$ and dominated convergence on $\{W\le0\}$. So $\psi(b)=\lim_{\lambda\uparrow b}\psi(\lambda)\le bt<\infty$.

Step 2: $L=bt-\psi(b)$. The map $\lambda\mapsto\lambda t-\psi(\lambda)$ has derivative $t-m(\lambda)\ge0$ on $[0,b)$, is continuous up to $b$, and equals $-\infty$ for $\lambda>b$.

Step 3: the law $R:=Q_b$. Define $dQ_b/dP=e^{bW-\psi(b)}$. As $\lambda\uparrow b$,
$$\mathbb{E}_P[W^+e^{\lambda W}]\uparrow\mathbb{E}_P[W^+e^{bW}]\ \text{(monotone convergence)},\qquad\mathbb{E}_P[W^-e^{\lambda W}]\to\mathbb{E}_P[W^-e^{bW}]\ \text{(dominated by }W^-\text{)}.$$
Hence $\mathbb{E}_{Q_b}W=\lim_{\lambda\uparrow b}m(\lambda)=t_{\max}$, which is finite, so $\mathbb{E}_{Q_b}|W|<\infty$. Moreover $D(Q_b\|P)=bt_{\max}-\psi(b)$.

If $t=t_{\max}$, then $Q_b$ is feasible with $D(Q_b\|P)=bt-\psi(b)=L$.

If $t>t_{\max}$, apply the mixture construction with $R=Q_b$ and $r=t_{\max}$. The lemma applies, since $\mathbb{E}_{Q_b}e^{\varepsilon W}=e^{-\psi(b)}\mathbb{E}_Pe^{(b+\varepsilon)W}=\infty$ for $\varepsilon>0$. Here $\log\frac{dR}{dP}=bW-\psi(b)$, so
$$D(R_j\|P)=b\mu_j-\psi(b)+\log\frac1{R(A_j)},$$
and therefore
$$D(Q_j\|P)\le(1-\varepsilon_j)\big(bt_{\max}-\psi(b)\big)+\varepsilon_j\big(b\mu_j-\psi(b)\big)+\varepsilon_j\log\frac1{R(A_j)}=bt-\psi(b)+\varepsilon_j\log\frac1{R(A_j)}.$$
By $(\ast)$ this tends to $bt-\psi(b)=L$. Hence $I(t)\le L$.

*Case D: $b=\infty$ and $t\ge t_{\max}$.* Let $w^*:=\operatorname{ess\,sup}W\in[0,\infty]$. If $w^*=0$, then $W=0$ a.s., so $\psi\equiv0$ and $L=\infty$, which is excluded. So $w^*>0$.

Step 1: $t_{\max}=w^*$. Trivially $m(\lambda)\le w^*$. Conversely, fix $0<c'<c''<w^*$. Then $P(W>c'')>0$, and $\mathbb{E}_Pe^{\lambda W}\ge e^{\lambda c''}P(W>c'')\to\infty$. Hence
$$Q_\lambda(W\le c')\le\frac{e^{\lambda c'}}{e^{\lambda c''}P(W>c'')}\to0,\qquad\mathbb{E}_{Q_\lambda}W^-\le\frac{\mathbb{E}_PW^-}{\mathbb{E}_Pe^{\lambda W}}\to0,$$
using $e^{\lambda W}\le1$ on $\{W<0\}$. Since $m(\lambda)\ge c'Q_\lambda(W>c')-\mathbb{E}_{Q_\lambda}W^-$, we get $\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty} m(\lambda)\ge c'$. As $c'<w^*$ was arbitrary, $t_{\max}=w^*$.

Step 2: the remaining values of $t$.
- If $w^*=\infty$, no finite $t\ge t_{\max}$ exists.
- If $t>w^*$, then $\psi(\lambda)\le\lambda w^*$, so $\lambda t-\psi(\lambda)\ge\lambda(t-w^*)\to\infty$. Thus $L=\infty$, which is excluded.
- If $t=w^*<\infty$, write $\lambda t-\psi(\lambda)=-\log\mathbb{E}_Pe^{-\lambda(w^*-W)}$. The integrand decreases to $\mathbf{1}_{\{W=w^*\}}$, so $\lambda t-\psi(\lambda)\to\log\frac1{p_0}$ with $p_0:=P(W=w^*)$.
  - If $p_0=0$, then $L=\infty$, which is excluded.
  - If $p_0>0$, then $Q^*:=P(\cdot\mid W=w^*)$ is feasible ($\mathbb{E}_{Q^*}W=w^*=t$), and $D(Q^*\|P)=\log\frac1{p_0}\le L$.

Cases A–D exhaust all possibilities for $t>0$ (Case A covers $t<t_{\max}$ whenever $b>0$). Hence $I(t)\le\psi^*(t)$. Together with (a), for all $t>0$,
$$\psi^*(t)=\inf\big\{D(Q\|P):\ \mathbb{E}_QZ-\mathbb{E}Z\ge t\big\}.\qquad\blacksquare$$
