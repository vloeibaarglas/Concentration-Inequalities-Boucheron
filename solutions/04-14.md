**Central idea.** Couple each Bernoulli summand with an independent Poisson of the same mean so that the $i$-th pair disagrees with probability $V(\mathrm{Ber}(p_i), \mathrm{Po}(p_i))$ (Exercise 4.5); a union bound tensorizes the one-coordinate errors into a bound on $V(\mathbf{P}, \mathrm{Po}(\mu))$, because sums of independent Poissons are Poisson (infinite divisibility). The one-coordinate distance is computed explicitly: $V(\mathrm{Ber}(p), \mathrm{Po}(p)) = p(1-e^{-p}) \le p^2$, using only $e^{-p} \ge 1-p$. For $p_i = \mu/n$ this yields $V(\mathrm{Binomial}(n,\mu/n), \mathrm{Po}(\mu)) \le \mu^2/n$, the law of rare events with an explicit $O(1/n)$ rate.

**Solution.** For each $i$, let $(X_i, Y_i)$ be a coupling with $X_i \sim \mathrm{Ber}(p_i)$, $Y_i \sim \mathrm{Po}(p_i)$, and $\mathbb{P}\{X_i \ne Y_i\} = V(\mathrm{Ber}(p_i), \mathrm{Po}(p_i))$, which exists by the coupling characterization of total variation (Exercise 4.5). Take the $n$ pairs independent across $i$. Then $\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \sim \mathbf{P}$ and, by stability of the Poisson law, $\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i \sim \mathrm{Po}(\sum_i p_i) = \mathrm{Po}(\mu)$. Since $\{\sum_i X_i \ne \sum_i Y_i\} \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^n \{X_i \ne Y_i\}$,
$$V(\mathbf{P}, \mathrm{Po}(\mu)) \le \mathbb{P}\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \ne \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i\Big\} \le \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{P}\{X_i \ne Y_i\} = \sum_{i=1}^n V(\mathrm{Ber}(p_i), \mathrm{Po}(p_i)).$$
It remains to bound the one-coordinate distance. For discrete laws, $V(P,Q) = \frac12 \sum_k |P(k) - Q(k)|$. Let $X \sim \mathrm{Ber}(p)$, $Y \sim \mathrm{Po}(p)$. Since $e^{-p} \ge 1-p$ for all $p$,
\begin{align*}
k = 0:&\quad |(1-p) - e^{-p}| = e^{-p} - (1-p), \\
k = 1:&\quad |p - pe^{-p}| = p(1-e^{-p}), \\
k \ge 2:&\quad |0 - \mathbb{P}\{Y \ge 2\}| = 1 - e^{-p}(1+p) \ge 0,
\end{align*}
the last inequality because $e^p \ge 1+p$. Summing and halving,
$$V(\mathrm{Ber}(p), \mathrm{Po}(p)) = \tfrac12\big[(e^{-p}-1+p) + p(1-e^{-p}) + (1-e^{-p}(1+p))\big] = p(1-e^{-p}) \le p^2,$$
using $1 - e^{-p} \le p$. Combining with the tensorization gives $V(\mathbf{P}, \mathrm{Po}(\mu)) \le \sum_{i=1}^n p_i^2$.

*Interpretation.* With $p_i = \mu/n$, $\mathbf{P} = \mathrm{Binomial}(n, \mu/n)$ and $V(\mathrm{Binomial}(n,\mu/n), \mathrm{Po}(\mu)) \le \mu^2/n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$: the sum of many rare independent Bernoulli trials is asymptotically Poisson in total variation, at the explicit rate $O(1/n)$ — the classical law of rare events.
