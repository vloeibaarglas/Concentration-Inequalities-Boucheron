**Central idea.** Pass from the dependent vector $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ to an independent Poisson vector via the chain rule: the KL divergence of the joint law $Q$ from the product Poisson law $\mathbf{R} = \bigotimes_i \mathrm{Po}(p_i)$ splits into the sum of the one-coordinate divergences $D(\mathrm{Ber}(p_i)\|\mathrm{Po}(p_i))$ plus the entropy gap $\sum_i H(X_i) - H(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ measuring the dependence. Each one-coordinate term is at most $p_i^2$, and the data processing inequality (Exercise 4.10) transfers the bound from the vector to its sum $S_n$, whose image under $\mathbf{R}$ is $\mathrm{Po}(\mu)$ by infinite divisibility.

**Solution.** Let $Q$ be the joint law of $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ on $\{0,1\}^n$, with marginals $Q_i = \mathrm{Ber}(p_i)$, and let $\mathbf{R} = \bigotimes_{i=1}^n R_i$ where $R_i = \mathrm{Po}(p_i)$. Since $\mathbf{R}$ charges every point of $\mathbb{N}^n \supset \{0,1\}^n$, $Q \ll \mathbf{R}$ and
$$D(Q\|\mathbf{R}) = \sum_{x \in \{0,1\}^n} Q(x) \ln \frac{Q(x)}{\prod_{i=1}^n R_i(x_i)} = -H(X_1,\ldots,X_n) - \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}_Q[\ln R_i(X_i)],$$
with entropy in nats. Similarly, for each marginal,
$$D(Q_i\|R_i) = \sum_{x_i \in \{0,1\}} Q_i(x_i) \ln \frac{Q_i(x_i)}{R_i(x_i)} = -H(X_i) - \mathbb{E}_{Q_i}[\ln R_i(X_i)].$$
Since $\mathbb{E}_Q[\ln R_i(X_i)] = \mathbb{E}_{Q_i}[\ln R_i(X_i)]$ (the integrand depends only on coordinate $i$), subtracting gives the chain rule
$$D(Q\|\mathbf{R}) = \sum_{i=1}^n D(Q_i\|R_i) + \Big[\sum_{i=1}^n H(X_i) - H(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\Big].$$
Next, the one-coordinate divergence: with $R_i(0) = e^{-p_i}$ and $R_i(1) = p_i e^{-p_i}$,
\begin{align*}
D(\mathrm{Ber}(p)\|\mathrm{Po}(p)) &= (1-p)\ln\frac{1-p}{e^{-p}} + p\ln\frac{p}{pe^{-p}} \\
&= (1-p)\ln(1-p) + p(1-p) + p^2.
\end{align*}
Since $\ln(1-p) \le -p$ for $p \in [0,1]$, $(1-p)\ln(1-p) \le -p(1-p)$, whence $D(\mathrm{Ber}(p)\|\mathrm{Po}(p)) \le p^2$. (This is the previous exercise, 4-14, in KL form.)

Finally, let $T(x_1,\ldots,x_n) = \sum_i x_i$. By the data processing inequality (Exercise 4.10),
$$D(\mathbf{P}\|\mathrm{Po}(\mu)) = D(Q \circ T^{-1}\|\mathbf{R} \circ T^{-1}) \le D(Q\|\mathbf{R}),$$
where $Q \circ T^{-1} = \mathbf{P}$ is the law of $S_n$, and $\mathbf{R} \circ T^{-1}$ is the law of a sum of independent $\mathrm{Po}(p_i)$ variables, hence $\mathrm{Po}(\mu)$ with $\mu = \sum_i p_i$ by infinite divisibility of the Poisson law. Combining with the chain rule and the one-coordinate bound,
$$D(\mathbf{P}\|\mathrm{Po}(\mu)) \le \sum_{i=1}^n p_i^2 + \sum_{i=1}^n H(X_i) - H(X_1,\ldots,X_n),$$
as claimed. The entropy gap is nonnegative by subadditivity of entropy, vanishing when the $X_i$ are independent.
