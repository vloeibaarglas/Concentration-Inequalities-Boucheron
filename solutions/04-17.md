**Central idea.** The key geometric inclusion $\frac{2}{t+1}(tA)^c+\frac{t-1}{t+1}A\subseteq A^c$ (which uses symmetry to write $x/t$ as a convex combination of $z\in A$ and $-y\in A$) feeds into Brunn–Minkowski; weighted AM–GM then eliminates the dimension $n$, yielding the dimension-free exponential bound. Convexity is essential (counterexample below); symmetry is needed for the inclusion.

**Solution.** Write $p=\mathbf{P}\{A\}>1/2$ and $q=\mathbf{P}\{(tA)^c\}$, where complements are in $C$. Note $\frac{2}{t+1}+\frac{t-1}{t+1}=1$.

*The inclusion.* Let $t>1$, $x\in (tA)^c=C\setminus tA$, $y\in A$. Since $x,y\in C$ and $C$ is convex, $z:=\frac{2}{t+1}x+\frac{t-1}{t+1}y\in C$. Suppose $z\in A$. Then
$$\frac{x}{t} \;=\; \frac{t+1}{2t}\,z \;+\; \frac{t-1}{2t}\,(-y).$$
By symmetry $-y\in A$, and $\frac{t+1}{2t}+\frac{t-1}{2t}=1$ with both coefficients nonnegative, so convexity of $A$ gives $x/t\in A$, i.e.\ $x\in tA$ — contradiction. Hence $z\notin A$, so $z\in A^c$, proving
$$\frac{2}{t+1}(tA)^c+\frac{t-1}{t+1}A \;\subseteq\; A^c.$$

*Brunn–Minkowski.* Both sides are Borel, so with $\mathbf{P}$ uniform on $C$,
$$(1-p)^{1/n} \;\ge\; \mathbf{P}\!\left(\tfrac{2}{t+1}(tA)^c+\tfrac{t-1}{t+1}A\right)^{1/n} \;\ge\; \frac{2}{t+1}\,q^{1/n} + \frac{t-1}{t+1}\,p^{1/n}.$$
By weighted AM–GM,
$$(1-p)^{1/n} \;\ge\; \frac{2}{t+1}q^{1/n}+\frac{t-1}{t+1}p^{1/n} \;\ge\; \big(q^{1/n}\big)^{\frac{2}{t+1}}\big(p^{1/n}\big)^{\frac{t-1}{t+1}}.$$
Raising to the power $(t+1)/2$ gives $(1-p)^{(t+1)/2}\ge q\,p^{(t-1)/2}$, i.e.
$$\mathbf{P}\{(tA)^c\} \;\le\; p\left(\frac{1-p}{p}\right)^{\!(t+1)/2},$$
the dimension $n$ having cancelled. Since $\frac{1-p}{p}<1$, the right side decays exponentially in $t$, uniformly in $n$ and $C$.

*On the hypotheses.* Convexity cannot be dropped: with $C=[-1,1]$, $A=[-1,-0.4]\cup[0.4,1]$ is symmetric with $p=0.6$, but for $t=2$, $(tA)^c=(-0.8,0.8)$ has $\mathbf{P}\{(tA)^c\}=0.8\not\le 0.6\,(2/3)^{3/2}\approx0.327$. Symmetry is needed for the inclusion: with $C=[-1,1]$, $A=[-0.1,1]$ convex, $t=2$, $x=-1/2\in(tA)^c$, $y=1\in A$ gives $\frac23x+\frac13y=0\in A\not\subseteq A^c$. ∎
