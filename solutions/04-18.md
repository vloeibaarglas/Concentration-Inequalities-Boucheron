**Central idea.** For $Z = \sum_i y_i X_i$ with $\sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}Z^2 > 0$, take the symmetric convex slab $S = \{x : |y\cdot x| \le 2\sigma\}$ and $A = C\cap S$, so $\mathbf{P}(A) \ge 3/4$ by Chebyshev. $A$ itself need not be symmetric (only $\mathbb{E}X = 0$ is assumed, not $C=-C$), so Borell's lemma (Exercise 4.17) is not quoted but its proof is rerun: the key inclusion $\frac{2}{t+1}(C\setminus tA)+\frac{t-1}{t+1}A\subseteq C\setminus A$ still holds using only the symmetry of $S$ and the fact $0=\mathbb{E}X\in C$ (so $C\subseteq tC$). Brunn–Minkowski then gives the exponential tail $\mathbf{P}\{|Z| > u\} \le C_0 e^{-cu/\sigma}$ for $u>2\sigma$, and integrating $pu^{p-1}$ against it yields $\|Z\|_p < 4p\,\sigma$. For special bodies (e.g. the cube) the linear form is sub-Gaussian and the bound tightens to order $\sqrt{p}\,\sigma$.

**Solution.** Fix $y \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and put $Z = \sum_{i=1}^n y_i X_i = y\cdot X$. Since $\mathbb{E}X = 0$, $\mathbb{E}Z = 0$; write $\sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}Z^2$ (finite, $C$ being bounded) and $\|Z\|_p = (\mathbb{E}|Z|^p)^{1/p}$. If $\sigma = 0$ then $Z = 0$ a.s. and both sides vanish; we read the claim for $\sigma>0$ (with $\sigma=0$ the strict inequality degenerates to $0<0$, so the statement is meant for nondegenerate $Z$). Assume $\sigma > 0$.

*Step 1: $0\in C$ and $C\subseteq tC$ for $t\ge1$.* If $0\notin C$, then since $C$ is compact and convex there is $w$ and $\delta>0$ with $w\cdot x\ge\delta$ for all $x\in C$ (strict separation), whence $w\cdot\mathbb{E}X\ge\delta>0$, contradicting $\mathbb{E}X=0$. So $0\in C$, and for $t\ge1$ and $x\in C$, $x/t = \frac1t x + (1-\frac1t)\,0\in C$, i.e. $x\in tC$.

*Step 2: the set $A$.* Let $S = \{x\in\mathbb{R}^n : |y\cdot x|\le 2\sigma\}$ (closed, convex, symmetric) and $A = C\cap S$ (compact, convex). By Chebyshev, $\mathbf{P}\{|Z|>2\sigma\}\le \sigma^2/(4\sigma^2) = 1/4$, so $p_0 := \mathbf{P}(A) = \mathbf{P}\{|Z|\le2\sigma\}\ge 3/4$. For $t\ge1$, $tA = tC\cap tS$ and, by Step 1,
$$C\setminus tA = C\setminus tS = \{x\in C : |y\cdot x| > 2t\sigma\},\qquad\text{so}\qquad \mathbf{P}(C\setminus tA) = \mathbf{P}\{|Z|>2t\sigma\}.$$

*Step 3: the inclusion.* Let $t>1$, $x\in C\setminus tA$ and $a\in A$. By convexity of $C$, $w := \frac{2}{t+1}x+\frac{t-1}{t+1}a\in C$. Suppose $w\in A$. Then
$$\frac{x}{t} \;=\; \frac{t+1}{2t}\,w \;+\; \frac{t-1}{2t}\,(-a),$$
with nonnegative coefficients summing to $1$. Here $w\in A\subseteq S$ and $-a\in S$ (as $a\in S$ and $S$ is symmetric), so convexity of $S$ gives $x/t\in S$, i.e. $x\in tS$. Also $x\in C\subseteq tC$ by Step 1. Thus $x\in tC\cap tS = tA$, a contradiction. Hence $w\in C\setminus A$, proving
$$\tfrac{2}{t+1}\,(C\setminus tA)+\tfrac{t-1}{t+1}\,A \;\subseteq\; C\setminus A. \tag{1}$$

*Step 4: Brunn–Minkowski.* Fix $t>1$ and let $q = \mathbf{P}(C\setminus tA)$; if $q=0$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise let $K\subseteq C\setminus tA$ be any nonempty compact set (the latter set is Borel and bounded). $A$ is nonempty compact, so $\frac2{t+1}K+\frac{t-1}{t+1}A$ is compact and, by (1), contained in $C\setminus A$. Writing $|\cdot|$ for Lebesgue measure and dividing the Brunn–Minkowski inequality by $|C|^{1/n}$,
$$(1-p_0)^{1/n}\;\ge\;\frac{2}{t+1}\Big(\frac{|K|}{|C|}\Big)^{1/n}+\frac{t-1}{t+1}\,p_0^{1/n}\;\ge\;\Big(\frac{|K|}{|C|}\Big)^{\frac{2}{n(t+1)}}p_0^{\frac{t-1}{n(t+1)}}$$
by weighted AM–GM. Taking the supremum over $K$ (inner regularity) and raising to the power $n(t+1)/2$ gives $(1-p_0)^{(t+1)/2}\ge q\,p_0^{(t-1)/2}$, i.e.
$$\mathbf{P}\{|Z|>2t\sigma\} = q\;\le\;\varphi(p_0),\qquad \varphi(p) := p\Big(\frac{1-p}{p}\Big)^{(t+1)/2}.$$
Since $\frac{d}{dp}\log\varphi(p) = \frac{1-t}{2p}-\frac{t+1}{2(1-p)}<0$ on $(0,1)$ for $t>1$, $\varphi$ is decreasing, and $p_0\ge3/4$ gives
$$\mathbf{P}\{|Z|>2t\sigma\}\;\le\;\tfrac34\big(\tfrac13\big)^{(t+1)/2},\qquad t>1.$$
Writing $u = 2t\sigma$, this is the exponential tail
$$\mathbf{P}\{|Z|>u\}\;\le\;C_0\,e^{-cu/\sigma}\quad(u>2\sigma),\qquad C_0 = \tfrac{3}{4\sqrt3}\approx 0.433,\quad c = \tfrac14\ln3\approx 0.2747,$$
with $C_0,c$ universal (independent of $n$, $C$, $y$).

*Step 5: moments.* We use the elementary bound $\Gamma(r+1)\le 2\,(2/e)^r r^r$ for $r>0$: from $\ln s\le s-1$, $\tau^r = (2r)^r(\tau/2r)^r\le(2r)^r e^{\tau/2-r}$, so $\Gamma(r+1)=\int_0^\infty\tau^re^{-\tau}d\tau\le(2r)^re^{-r}\int_0^\infty e^{-\tau/2}d\tau = 2(2/e)^rr^r$. Now for $p\ge2$,
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}|Z|^p &= \int_0^\infty pu^{p-1}\,\mathbf{P}\{|Z|>u\}\,du \;\le\; \int_0^{2\sigma}pu^{p-1}\,du + C_0\int_0^\infty pu^{p-1}e^{-cu/\sigma}\,du \\
&= (2\sigma)^p + C_0\,\Gamma(p+1)\,(\sigma/c)^p \;\le\; (2\sigma)^p + 2C_0\,(2/e)^p\,p^p\,(\sigma/c)^p .
\end{aligned}$$
With $(a+b)^{1/p}\le a^{1/p}+b^{1/p}$, $(2C_0)^{1/p}\le 1$ (as $2C_0 = \sqrt3/2<1$) and $2\le p$,
$$\|Z\|_p\;\le\;2\sigma+\frac{2}{e\,c}\,p\,\sigma\;\le\;\Big(1+\frac{2}{e\,c}\Big)p\,\sigma\;\approx\;3.679\,p\,\sigma\;<\;4\,p\,\sigma .$$
Hence the inequality holds with the universal constant $\kappa = 4$:
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{i=1}^ny_iX_i\Big|^p\Big]^{1/p}\;<\;4\,p\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{i=1}^ny_iX_i\Big|^2\Big]^{1/2}\qquad(p\ge2,\ y\in\mathbb{R}^n).$$

*Tightening for special bodies.* Yes. For the cube $C=[-1,1]^n$ (which has $\mathbb{E}X=0$), the coordinates $X_i$ are independent and uniform on $[-1,1]$, so $Z=\sum_iy_iX_i$ is a sum of independent centred terms with $y_iX_i\in[-|y_i|,|y_i|]$. With $v = \sum_iy_i^2 = 3\sigma^2$ (as $\mathbb{E}X_i^2 = 1/3$), Hoeffding's inequality gives $\mathbf{P}\{|Z|>u\}\le 2e^{-u^2/(2v)}$, so (substituting $w = u^2/(2v)$)
$$\mathbb{E}|Z|^p\le\int_0^\infty pu^{p-1}\,2e^{-u^2/(2v)}\,du = 2\,(2v)^{p/2}\,\Gamma(p/2+1)\le 4\,(2v)^{p/2}(2/e)^{p/2}(p/2)^{p/2},$$
using the Gamma bound above with $r=p/2$. Hence, for $p\ge2$, $\|Z\|_p\le 4^{1/p}\sqrt{6/e}\,\sqrt p\,\sigma\le 2\sqrt{6/e}\,\sqrt p\,\sigma\approx 2.97\sqrt p\,\sigma$: the growth in $p$ improves from linear to $\sqrt p$, uniformly in $n$. ∎
