**Central idea.** For boxes the Minkowski sum is coordinatewise: $A + B = \prod_i [a_i+c_i,\,b_i+d_i]$. Normalizing by $\mathrm{Vol}(A+B)^{1/n}$ reduces the claim to $1 \ge \prod_i(\frac{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i+\delta_i})^{1/n} + \prod_i(\frac{\delta_i}{\alpha_i+\delta_i})^{1/n}$, which follows from AM–GM applied to each geometric mean, since the two fractions in each coordinate sum to $1$.

**Solution.** Write $A = \prod_{i=1}^n [a_i,b_i]$ and $B = \prod_{i=1}^n [c_i,d_i]$ with $a_i \le b_i$, $c_i \le d_i$. The Minkowski sum is
$$A + B = \prod_{i=1}^n [a_i + c_i,\ b_i + d_i],$$
since $[a_i,b_i] + [c_i,d_i] = [a_i+c_i,\,b_i+d_i]$ in each coordinate. Put $\alpha_i = b_i - a_i \ge 0$ and $\delta_i = d_i - c_i \ge 0$. Then
$$\mathrm{Vol}(A) = \prod_{i=1}^n \alpha_i, \qquad \mathrm{Vol}(B) = \prod_{i=1}^n \delta_i, \qquad \mathrm{Vol}(A+B) = \prod_{i=1}^n (\alpha_i + \delta_i).$$
If $\alpha_i + \delta_i = 0$ for some $i$, then $\alpha_i = \delta_i = 0$, so $\mathrm{Vol}(A) = \mathrm{Vol}(B) = \mathrm{Vol}(A+B) = 0$ and the inequality holds trivially. Otherwise all $\alpha_i + \delta_i > 0$; dividing through by $\mathrm{Vol}(A+B)^{1/n}$ the claim becomes
$$1 \;\ge\; \left(\prod_{i=1}^n \frac{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i+\delta_i}\right)^{1/n} + \left(\prod_{i=1}^n \frac{\delta_i}{\alpha_i+\delta_i}\right)^{1/n}.$$
By the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality,
$$\left(\prod_{i=1}^n \frac{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i+\delta_i}\right)^{1/n} \le \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i+\delta_i}, \qquad \left(\prod_{i=1}^n \frac{\delta_i}{\alpha_i+\delta_i}\right)^{1/n} \le \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\delta_i}{\alpha_i+\delta_i}.$$
Adding, the right-hand side is $\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\alpha_i+\delta_i}{\alpha_i+\delta_i} = 1$, which proves the claim. ∎
