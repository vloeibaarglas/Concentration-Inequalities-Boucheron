**Central idea.** Normalize both sequences to probability distributions ($\bar a_i = a_i/a$, $\bar b_i = b_i/b$ with $a = \sum a_i$, $b = \sum b_i$): the difference between the two sides is exactly $a$ times the relative entropy $D(\bar a\|\bar b)$, which is nonnegative by Jensen's inequality applied to the concave logarithm (Gibbs' inequality). The statement holds for positive reals, not just integers.

**Solution.** Let $a = \sum_i a_i > 0$, $b = \sum_i b_i > 0$, and set $\bar a_i = a_i/a$, $\bar b_i = b_i/b$, so $\sum_i \bar a_i = \sum_i \bar b_i = 1$. Then
\begin{align*}
\sum_i a_i \log\frac{a_i}{b_i} - a\log\frac{a}{b}
&= \sum_i a_i\left[\log\frac{a_i}{b_i} - \log\frac{a}{b}\right]
= \sum_i a_i \log\frac{a_i/a}{b_i/b} \\
&= a\sum_i \bar a_i \log\frac{\bar a_i}{\bar b_i}.
\end{align*}
By Jensen's inequality (concavity of $\log$),
$$\sum_i \bar a_i \log\frac{\bar b_i}{\bar a_i} \le \log\sum_i \bar a_i\frac{\bar b_i}{\bar a_i} = \log\sum_i \bar b_i = \log 1 = 0,$$
so $\sum_i \bar a_i \log(\bar a_i/\bar b_i) \ge 0$. Multiplying by $a > 0$ gives
$$\sum_i a_i \log\frac{a_i}{b_i} \ge a\log\frac{a}{b} = \left(\sum_i a_i\right)\log\frac{\sum_i a_i}{\sum_i b_i}. \qquad\blacksquare$$
