**Central idea.** Apply Han's inequality (Theorem 4.1) to $X$ uniform on $A$: then $H(X)=\log|A|$, while each $(d-1)$-dimensional marginal $X^{(i)}$ is supported on the projection $A_i$, so $H(X^{(i)})\le\log|A_i|$. Han's inequality $\sum_{i=1}^d H(X^{(i)})\ge(d-1)H(X)$ then yields $(d-1)\log|A|\le\sum_{i=1}^d\log|A_i|$.

**Solution.** If $A=\emptyset$ the inequality is trivial for $d\ge2$; assume $A\ne\emptyset$ (the case $d=1$ is trivial since $A_1$ is then a singleton). Let $X=(X_1,\dots,X_d)$ be uniformly distributed on the finite set $A\subset\mathbb{Z}^d$, so with logarithms to any fixed base $>1$,
$$H(X)=\log|A|.$$
For $i\le d$, let $X^{(i)}=(X_1,\dots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\dots,X_d)$ be $X$ with the $i$-th coordinate dropped. By definition $X^{(i)}$ takes values in $A_i$, the projection of $A$ along the $i$-th coordinate, hence
$$H(X^{(i)})\le\log|A_i|,\qquad i=1,\dots,d,$$
since the uniform distribution maximizes entropy on a finite set. Han's inequality (Theorem 4.1) gives
$$H(X)\le\frac1{d-1}\sum_{i=1}^d H(X^{(i)}).$$
Combining,
$$\log|A|\le\frac1{d-1}\sum_{i=1}^d\log|A_i|=\frac1{d-1}\log\prod_{i=1}^d|A_i|,$$
and exponentiating yields $|A|^{\,d-1}\le\prod_{i=1}^d|A_i|$. ∎
