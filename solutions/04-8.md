**Central idea.** Take $h_i$ to be $h$ itself on $\mathcal{X}^{n-1}$ (the log-trace on $n-1$ points). Since $\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})$ is the coordinate projection of $\mathrm{tr}(x)$, we get $0\le h(x)-h_i(x^{(i)})\le1$ from projection-monotonicity and the hypothesis. The sum bound $\sum_i(h-h_i)\le h$ is exactly Loomis–Whitney (Exercise 4.6) applied to $A=\mathrm{tr}(x)\subset\{0,1\}^n$: $|T|^{n-1}\le\prod_i|T_i|$ becomes $(n-1)\log_b|T|\le\sum_i\log_b|T_i|$.

**Solution.** Let $\mathrm{tr}(x)\subset\{0,1\}^n$ be the trace on $x=(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ and $T(x)=|\mathrm{tr}(x)|\ge1$, so that $h(x)=\log_bT(x)$ is well defined and nonnegative; the requirement $0\le h(x)-h(x^{(i)})$ forces $b>1$, which we assume. For $y\in\mathcal{X}^{\,n-1}$ define $h_i(y)=\log_b|\mathrm{tr}(y)|$, i.e. $h_i$ is the same combinatorial entropy on $n-1$ points.

Fix $x$ and $i$. The trace $\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})$ is precisely the projection of $\mathrm{tr}(x)\subset\{0,1\}^n$ along the $i$-th coordinate: dropping the $i$-th entry of each pattern $(1_{x_1\in A},\dots,1_{x_n\in A})$ gives exactly the patterns of $\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})$. Hence $|\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})|\le|\mathrm{tr}(x)|$, so with $b>1$,
$$h(x)-h_i(x^{(i)})=\log_b\frac{|\mathrm{tr}(x)|}{|\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})|}\ge0,$$
and $h(x)-h_i(x^{(i)})\le1$ is the hypothesis. Thus $0\le h(x)-h_i(x^{(i)})\le1$ for all $x,i$.

For the sum, apply Exercise 4.6 (discrete Loomis–Whitney) to the finite set $A=\mathrm{tr}(x)\subset\mathbb{Z}^n$: with $A_i$ the projection along coordinate $i$ (so $|A_i|=|\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})|$),
$$|\mathrm{tr}(x)|^{\,n-1}\le\prod_{i=1}^n|\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})|.$$
Taking $\log_b$ gives $(n-1)h(x)\le\sum_{i=1}^n h_i(x^{(i)})$, i.e.
$$\sum_{i=1}^n\big(h(x)-h_i(x^{(i)})\big)=nh(x)-\sum_{i=1}^n h_i(x^{(i)})\le h(x).$$
Hence $h$ has the self-bounding property. ∎
