**Central idea.** The product $A\times B$ is compact (Tychonoff's theorem), and the Minkowski sum $A+B$ is its continuous image under the addition map $(a,b)\mapsto a+b$; continuous images of compact sets are compact. (This is needed for the Brunn–Minkowski inequality in Section 4.14.)

**Solution.** Let $A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ be compact, hence with the subspace topology. The product $A\times B$ with the product topology is compact by Tychonoff's theorem (for a finite product this is elementary). The addition map
$$s:\mathbb{R}^d\times\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d,\qquad s(a,b)=a+b,$$
is continuous. By definition of the Minkowski sum,
$$A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,\ b\in B\}=s(A\times B).$$
A continuous image of a compact set is compact: if $\{U_\alpha\}$ is an open cover of $s(A\times B)$, then $\{s^{-1}(U_\alpha)\}$ is an open cover of $A\times B$, which has a finite subcover $s^{-1}(U_{\alpha_1}),\dots,s^{-1}(U_{\alpha_m})$, and then $U_{\alpha_1},\dots,U_{\alpha_m}$ cover $s(A\times B)$. Hence $A+B$ is compact. ∎
