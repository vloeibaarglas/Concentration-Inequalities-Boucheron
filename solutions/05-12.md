**Central idea.** Prove the inequality for $C_c^\infty$ functions, then approximate a general $C^1$ function $f$ (with $\mathbb{E}_\gamma f^2,\mathbb{E}_\gamma\|\nabla f\|^2<\infty$) in two stages: truncation by smooth cutoffs $\eta_k$ gives compactly supported $C^1$ approximants converging in $H^1(\gamma_n)$, and mollification makes them smooth; a diagonal sequence $f_k\in C_c^\infty$ then satisfies $f_k\to f$, $\nabla f_k\to\nabla f$ in $L^2(\gamma_n)$. The entropy passes to the limit by lower semicontinuity (via its variational formula), while the Dirichlet energy converges directly.

**Solution.** Let $\gamma_n$ be the standard Gaussian law on $\mathbb{R}^n$ and assume the logarithmic Sobolev inequality
$$\mathrm{Ent}_{\gamma_n}(g^2) \le 2\,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma_n}\|\nabla g\|^2 \tag{*}$$
proved for $g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Let $f$ be continuously differentiable with $\mathbb{E}_{\gamma_n}[f^2]<\infty$ and $\mathbb{E}_{\gamma_n}\|\nabla f\|^2<\infty$ (if the latter is infinite there is nothing to prove).

*Step 1: truncation.* Fix $\eta\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ with $0\le\eta\le1$, $\eta\equiv1$ on the unit ball, $\mathrm{supp}\,\eta\subset B(0,2)$, and set $\eta_k(x)=\eta(x/k)$, so $\|\nabla\eta_k\|_\infty\le C_0/k$. Put $g_k=f\eta_k\in C_c^1$. Then
$$\|g_k-f\|_{L^2(\gamma_n)}^2 = \int(1-\eta_k)^2f^2\,d\gamma_n \le \int_{|x|>k}f^2\,d\gamma_n \longrightarrow 0,$$
and, with $\nabla g_k=\eta_k\nabla f+f\nabla\eta_k$,
$$\|\nabla g_k-\nabla f\|_{L^2(\gamma_n)} \le \|(\eta_k-1)\nabla f\|_{L^2(\gamma_n)} + \frac{C_0}{k}\|f\|_{L^2(\gamma_n)} \longrightarrow 0,$$
the first term by dominated convergence ($|\eta_k\nabla f|\le|\nabla f|\in L^2(\gamma_n)$).

*Step 2: mollification and diagonalization.* Let $\rho_\varepsilon$ be a standard mollifier and $g_{k,j}=g_k*\rho_{1/j}\in C_c^\infty$. For fixed $k$, $g_k$ and $\nabla g_k$ are uniformly continuous with compact support, so $g_{k,j}\to g_k$ and $\nabla g_{k,j}=(\nabla g_k)*\rho_{1/j}\to\nabla g_k$ uniformly, hence in $L^2(\gamma_n)$. Choosing $j_k$ with $\|g_{k,j_k}-g_k\|_{H^1(\gamma_n)}<1/k$ and setting $f_k=g_{k,j_k}\in C_c^\infty$ gives
$$f_k \longrightarrow f, \qquad \nabla f_k \longrightarrow \nabla f \quad \text{in }L^2(\gamma_n),$$
in particular $\mathbb{E}_{\gamma_n}\|\nabla f_k\|^2\to\mathbb{E}_{\gamma_n}\|\nabla f\|^2$ and $f_k^2\to f^2$ in $L^1(\gamma_n)$.

*Step 3: passage to the limit.* By $(*)$, $\mathrm{Ent}_{\gamma_n}(f_k^2)\le2\,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma_n}\|\nabla f_k\|^2$. The entropy is $L^1$-lower semicontinuous: from the variational formula $\mathrm{Ent}(g)=\sup\{\mathbb{E}[gh]:h\text{ bounded},\ \mathbb{E}e^h\le1\}$ (Section 5.1), $\mathrm{Ent}$ is a supremum of $L^1$-continuous functionals, so with $f_k^2\to f^2$ in $L^1(\gamma_n)$,
$$\mathrm{Ent}_{\gamma_n}(f^2) \le \liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{Ent}_{\gamma_n}(f_k^2) \le \lim_{k\to\infty}2\,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma_n}\|\nabla f_k\|^2 = 2\,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma_n}\|\nabla f\|^2.$$
This is the logarithmic Sobolev inequality for $f$. ∎
