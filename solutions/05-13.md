**Central idea.** Write $X=\Gamma^{1/2}Z$ with $Z\sim N(0,I_n)$ and transfer the standard Gaussian inequalities to $g=f\circ\Gamma^{1/2}$; the chain rule gives $\nabla g(z)=\Gamma^{1/2}\nabla f(\Gamma^{1/2}z)$, so $\|\nabla g(z)\|^2=\langle\Gamma\nabla f(\Gamma^{1/2}z),\nabla f(\Gamma^{1/2}z)\rangle$, and since $f(X)\stackrel{d}=g(Z)$ the variance and entropy are preserved.

**Solution.** Let $\Gamma$ be symmetric positive semidefinite and $\Gamma^{1/2}$ its symmetric positive semidefinite square root. For $Z\sim N(0,I_n)$, $X:=\Gamma^{1/2}Z$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}e^{i\langle t,X\rangle}=e^{-\|\Gamma^{1/2}t\|^2/2}=e^{-\langle\Gamma t,t\rangle/2}$, so $X\sim N(0,\Gamma)$ (this holds also when $\Gamma$ is singular).

Let $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ be continuously differentiable and put $g=f\circ\Gamma^{1/2}$, i.e. $g(z)=f(\Gamma^{1/2}z)$, which is continuously differentiable. By the chain rule and symmetry of $\Gamma^{1/2}$,
$$\nabla g(z) = \Gamma^{1/2}\,\nabla f(\Gamma^{1/2}z), \qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^n,$$
hence
$$\|\nabla g(z)\|^2 = \big\langle\Gamma^{1/2}\nabla f(\Gamma^{1/2}z),\Gamma^{1/2}\nabla f(\Gamma^{1/2}z)\big\rangle = \big\langle\Gamma\,\nabla f(\Gamma^{1/2}z),\nabla f(\Gamma^{1/2}z)\big\rangle.$$
Since $f(X)\stackrel{d}=g(Z)$, we have $\mathrm{Var}(f(X))=\mathrm{Var}(g(Z))$ and $\mathrm{Ent}(f(X)^2)=\mathrm{Ent}(g(Z)^2)$ (both depend only on the law), and
$$\mathbb{E}\|\nabla g(Z)\|^2 = \mathbb{E}\big[\big\langle\Gamma\,\nabla f(\Gamma^{1/2}Z),\nabla f(\Gamma^{1/2}Z)\big\rangle\big] = \mathbb{E}\big[\langle\Gamma\nabla f(X),\nabla f(X)\rangle\big].$$
Applying the standard Gaussian Poincaré inequality to $g$ (the estimate being trivial if the right-hand side is infinite),
$$\mathrm{Var}(f(X)) = \mathrm{Var}(g(Z)) \le \mathbb{E}\|\nabla g(Z)\|^2 = \mathbb{E}\big[\langle\Gamma\nabla f(X),\nabla f(X)\rangle\big],$$
and applying the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality (Theorem 5.4),
$$\mathrm{Ent}(f(X)^2) = \mathrm{Ent}(g(Z)^2) \le 2\,\mathbb{E}\|\nabla g(Z)\|^2 = 2\,\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\Gamma\nabla f(X),\nabla f(X)\rangle\big].$$
This is the claim. ∎
