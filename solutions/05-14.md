**Central idea.** A.s. continuity on the totally bounded index set lets us replace $\mathcal{T}$ by a countable dense $\mathcal{D}$; for finite $F\subset\mathcal{T}$ the Gaussian Poincaré inequality (Exercise 5.13, via mollification of the max function) gives $\mathrm{Var}(\max_{F}X_t)\le\sigma^2:=\sup_t\mathrm{Var}(X_t)$. With $F_k\uparrow\mathcal{D}$, the maxima $Z_k\uparrow Z$; monotone medians $M_k\uparrow\le M$ and Exercise 2.1 ($|\mathbb{E}Z_k-M_k|\le\sigma$) bound $\mathbb{E}Z_k\le M+\sigma$, so monotone convergence yields $\mathbb{E}Z<\infty$. The finite-set concentration bound then passes to the limit by monotone convergence.

**Solution.** Let $\{X_t\}_{t\in\mathcal{T}}$ be centered Gaussian, $\mathcal{T}$ totally bounded for $d(s,t)=(\mathbb{E}(X_s-X_t)^2)^{1/2}$, with a.s. continuous (hence a.s. bounded) sample paths. Put $\sigma^2=\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\mathbb{E}X_t^2<\infty$ (finiteness: $t\mapsto\|X_t\|_2$ is $1$-Lipschitz for $d$, hence bounded on the totally bounded set $\mathcal{T}$). Let $\mathcal{D}=\{t_1,t_2,\dots\}$ be countable dense in $\mathcal{T}$.

*Reduction to $\mathcal{D}$.* For a.e. $\omega$ the path $t\mapsto X_t(\omega)$ is $d$-continuous, so $\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}X_t(\omega)=\sup_{t\in\mathcal{D}}X_t(\omega)$: if $t_n\in\mathcal{D}$, $d(t_n,t)\to0$ then $X_{t_n}(\omega)\to X_t(\omega)$. Hence $Z:=\sup_{\mathcal{T}}X_t=\sup_{\mathcal{D}}X_t$ a.s.; $Z<\infty$ a.s. and $Z\ge X_{t_1}>-\infty$ a.s., so $Z$ is real-valued.

*Finite subsets.* For finite $F\subset\mathcal{T}$ put $Z_F=\max_{t\in F}X_t=g(X_F)$ with $g(x)=\max_{t\in F}x_t$, $X_F\sim N(0,\Sigma_F)$. The function $g$ is $1$-Lipschitz, differentiable a.e. with $\nabla g(x)=e_{t^*(x)}$ (the maximizing coordinate, a.e. unique). Mollifying, $g_\varepsilon=g*\rho_\varepsilon$ is smooth with $\nabla g_\varepsilon\to\nabla g$ a.e. and $\|\nabla g_\varepsilon\|\le1$; applying Exercise 5.13 to $g_\varepsilon$ and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ (dominated convergence, using $\langle\Sigma_Fv,v\rangle\le\|\Sigma_F\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ for $\|v\|\le1$) gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z_F) \le \mathbb{E}\big[\langle\Sigma_F\nabla g(X_F),\nabla g(X_F)\rangle\big] = \mathbb{E}\big[(\Sigma_F)_{t^*(X_F),t^*(X_F)}\big] \le \max_{t\in F}\mathrm{Var}(X_t) \le \sigma^2.$$

*Finiteness of $\mathbb{E}Z$.* Let $F_k=\{t_1,\dots,t_k\}$ and $Z_k=Z_{F_k}\uparrow Z$ a.s. Since $Z_k\ge X_{t_1}\in L^1$, monotone convergence applied to $Z_k-X_{t_1}\ge0$ gives $\mathbb{E}Z_k\uparrow\mathbb{E}Z\in(-\infty,+\infty]$. Let $M_k=\inf\{t:\mathbb{P}\{Z_k\le t\}\ge1/2\}$ (a median of $Z_k$) and $M$ the corresponding median of $Z$ (finite since $Z$ is real-valued). From $Z_k\le Z_{k+1}$ we get $\{Z_{k+1}\le t\}\subset\{Z_k\le t\}$, whence $M_k\le M_{k+1}$; from $Z_k\le Z$, $M_k\le M$. Each $Z_k$ is square-integrable, so by Exercise 2.1, $|\mathbb{E}Z_k-M_k|\le\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(Z_k)}\le\sigma$, i.e. $\mathbb{E}Z_k\le M_k+\sigma\le M+\sigma$. Passing to the limit,
$$\mathbb{E}Z = \lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}Z_k \le M+\sigma < \infty,$$
so $Z$ has finite expectation.

*Conclusion.* For finite $F$, the Herbst argument from Exercise 5.13 (using $\langle\Sigma_F\nabla g,\nabla g\rangle\le\sigma^2$) gives the Gaussian concentration $\mathbb{P}\{|Z_F-\mathbb{E}Z_F|\ge t\}\le e^{-t^2/(2\sigma^2)}$. Since $Z_k\uparrow Z$ a.s. and $\mathbb{E}Z_k\uparrow\mathbb{E}Z<\infty$, monotone (indeed setwise) convergence gives for the upper tail
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z-\mathbb{E}Z\ge t\} = \lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\{Z_k-\mathbb{E}Z\ge t\} = \lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\{Z_k-\mathbb{E}Z_k\ge t+(\mathbb{E}Z-\mathbb{E}Z_k)\} \le e^{-t^2/(2\sigma^2)},$$
and the lower tail follows by applying the same to $\{-X_t\}$. ∎
