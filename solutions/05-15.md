**Central idea.** Direct computation: $\mathbb{E}Z^2=D+\delta^2$, $\mathrm{Var}(Z^2)=2D+4\delta^2$, and the noncentral chi-square mgf $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z^2}=(1-2\lambda)^{-D/2}e^{\lambda\delta^2/(1-2\lambda)}$ satisfies the sub-gamma bound with $(v,c)=(2\mathbb{E}Z^2+2\delta^2,2)$ by a second-derivative comparison. Since $Z(x)=\|x+\delta e_1\|$ is 1-Lipschitz with $\|\nabla Z\|=1$ a.e., Poincaré gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z)<1$ (strict, as $Z$ is not affine) and Herbst gives sub-Gaussianity with variance factor $1$; running Herbst on $Z^2$ itself, using $\|\nabla(Z^2)\|^2=4Z^2$, yields the sub-gamma bound with variance factor $4\mathbb{E}Z^2$ and scale $2$.

**Solution.** Write $Z^2=(X_1+\delta)^2+\sum_{i=2}^DX_i^2$ with $X_i$ iid $N(0,1)$.

*Mean and variance.* $\mathbb{E}[(X_1+\delta)^2]=1+\delta^2$ and $\mathbb{E}[X_i^2]=1$, so
$$\mathbb{E}Z^2 = 1+\delta^2+(D-1) = D+\delta^2.$$
Moreover $\mathrm{Var}((X_1+\delta)^2)=\mathrm{Var}(X_1^2+2\delta X_1)= \mathrm{Var}(X_1^2)+4\delta^2\mathrm{Var}(X_1)=2+4\delta^2$ (using $\mathbb{E}X_1^3=0$), and by independence
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z^2) = (2+4\delta^2)+2(D-1) = 2D+4\delta^2.$$

*$Z^2$ is sub-gamma.* For $\lambda<1/2$, completing the square gives $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(X_1+\delta)^2}=(1-2\lambda)^{-1/2}\exp(\frac{\lambda\delta^2}{1-2\lambda})$, whence by independence
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z^2} = (1-2\lambda)^{-D/2}\exp\!\Big(\frac{\lambda\delta^2}{1-2\lambda}\Big), \qquad \lambda<\tfrac12.$$
Put $\psi(\lambda)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z^2-\mathbb{E}Z^2)}=-\frac{D}{2}\log(1-2\lambda)+\frac{\lambda\delta^2}{1-2\lambda}-\lambda(D+\delta^2)$. Then $\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0$ and
$$\psi''(\lambda) = \frac{2D}{(1-2\lambda)^2}+\frac{4\delta^2}{(1-2\lambda)^3}.$$
With $v=2D+4\delta^2=2\mathbb{E}Z^2+2\delta^2$ and $u(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^2v}{2(1-2\lambda)}$, we have $u(0)=\psi(0)$, $u'(0)=\psi'(0)=0$ and $u''(\lambda)=\frac{v}{(1-2\lambda)^3}\ge\psi''(\lambda)$ on $[0,\frac12)$, since $v-2D(1-2\lambda)-4\delta^2=4D\lambda\ge0$. Integrating twice gives $\psi\le u$, i.e. $Z^2$ is sub-gamma with variance factor $v=2\mathbb{E}Z^2+2\delta^2$ and scale factor $2$.

*The function $Z$.* As a function of $x\in\mathbb{R}^D$, $Z(x)=\|x+\delta e_1\|$ is $1$-Lipschitz and $\|\nabla Z(x)\|=1$ for $x\ne-\delta e_1$ (hence a.e.). By the Gaussian Poincaré inequality, $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\mathbb{E}\|\nabla Z\|^2=1$, with strict inequality since equality in Poincaré holds only for affine functions and $Z$ is strictly convex; thus $\mathrm{Var}(Z)<1$. By the Gaussian concentration inequality (Herbst's argument), the $1$-Lipschitz $Z$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}e^{t(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}\le e^{t^2/2}$ for all $t$, i.e. $Z$ is sub-Gaussian with variance factor $1$.

*Sub-gamma bound with $4\mathbb{E}Z^2$.* Apply Herbst to $Z^2$ itself. $F=e^{\lambda Z^2/2}$ is smooth ( $Z^2$ is a polynomial in $x$), $\nabla F=\lambda Z e^{\lambda Z^2/2}\nabla Z$, so $\|\nabla F\|^2=\lambda^2Z^2e^{\lambda Z^2}$ a.e. The logarithmic Sobolev inequality gives, for $0<\lambda<1/2$,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z^2}) \le 2\,\mathbb{E}\|\nabla F\|^2 = 2\lambda^2\,\mathbb{E}\big[Z^2e^{\lambda Z^2}\big].$$
With $\phi(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z^2}$ this reads $\lambda\phi'-\phi\log\phi\le2\lambda^2\phi'$. Setting $\psi(\lambda)=\log\phi(\lambda)-\lambda\mathbb{E}Z^2$ and $E=\mathbb{E}Z^2$,
$$\lambda\psi'-\psi \le 2\lambda^2\psi'+2\lambda^2E.$$
Put $u=\psi/\lambda$ (so $u(0)=\psi'(0)=0$); then $u'-\frac{2}{1-2\lambda}u\le\frac{2E}{1-2\lambda}$. With integrating factor $1-2\lambda$,
$$\frac{d}{d\lambda}\big[(1-2\lambda)u(\lambda)\big] \le 2E,$$
whence $(1-2\lambda)u(\lambda)\le2E\lambda$ and therefore
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z^2-\mathbb{E}Z^2)} = \psi(\lambda) \le \frac{2\lambda^2E}{1-2\lambda} = \frac{\lambda^2(4\mathbb{E}Z^2)}{2(1-2\lambda)}, \qquad 0<\lambda<\tfrac12.$$
Thus $Z^2$ is sub-gamma with variance factor $4\mathbb{E}Z^2$ and scale factor $2$. ∎
