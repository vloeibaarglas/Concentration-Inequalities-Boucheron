**Central idea.** Apply the logarithmic Sobolev inequality to the perturbation $1+\varepsilon f$ and Taylor-expand in $\varepsilon$: $\mathrm{Ent}((1+\varepsilon f)^2) = 2\varepsilon^2\mathrm{Var}(f)+O(\varepsilon^3)$ while the Dirichlet form is exactly quadratic, $\mathcal{E}(1+\varepsilon f) = \varepsilon^2\mathcal{E}(f)$. Dividing by $2\varepsilon^2$ and letting $\varepsilon \to 0$ turns LSI into Poincar\'e. The same computation with $\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f\|^2$ in place of $\mathcal{E}(f)$ derives Gaussian Poincar\'e from Gaussian LSI.

**Solution.** Let $m_1 = \mathbb{E}[f]$, $m_2 = \mathbb{E}[f^2]$, so $\mathrm{Var}(f) = m_2-m_1^2$. For $|\varepsilon|$ small put $Y_\varepsilon = (1+\varepsilon f)^2 = 1+2\varepsilon f+\varepsilon^2 f^2 \ge 0$. With $\phi(y) = y\log y = \delta+\delta^2/2+O(\delta^3)$ near $y = 1+\delta$,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}[\phi(Y_\varepsilon)] &= \mathbb{E}[2\varepsilon f+\varepsilon^2f^2] + \tfrac12\mathbb{E}[(2\varepsilon f)^2] + O(\varepsilon^3) = 2\varepsilon m_1 + 3\varepsilon^2 m_2 + O(\varepsilon^3),\\
\phi(\mathbb{E}[Y_\varepsilon]) &= \phi(1+2\varepsilon m_1+\varepsilon^2m_2) = 2\varepsilon m_1 + \varepsilon^2m_2 + \tfrac12(2\varepsilon m_1)^2 + O(\varepsilon^3),
\end{align*}
using $\mathbb{E}[Y_\varepsilon] = 1+2\varepsilon m_1+\varepsilon^2m_2$. Subtracting,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(Y_\varepsilon) = \mathbb{E}[\phi(Y_\varepsilon)]-\phi(\mathbb{E}[Y_\varepsilon]) = 2\varepsilon^2(m_2-m_1^2)+O(\varepsilon^3) = 2\varepsilon^2\mathrm{Var}(f)+O(\varepsilon^3),$$
valid for bounded $f$ (hence on $\{-1,1\}^n$), and for $f$ with $\mathbb{E}|f|^3 < \infty$ in general.

*Hypercube.* Theorem 5.1 gives $\mathrm{Ent}(g^2) \le 2\mathcal{E}(g)$ for all $g$. Take $g = 1+\varepsilon f$. Since $\mathcal{E}$ is a quadratic form vanishing on constants, $\mathcal{E}(1+\varepsilon f) = \varepsilon^2\mathcal{E}(f)$. Hence
$$2\varepsilon^2\mathrm{Var}(f)+O(\varepsilon^3) = \mathrm{Ent}((1+\varepsilon f)^2) \le 2\varepsilon^2\mathcal{E}(f).$$
Dividing by $2\varepsilon^2$ and letting $\varepsilon \to 0$ yields $\mathrm{Var}(f(X)) \le \mathcal{E}(f)$.

*Gaussian.* Theorem 5.4 gives $\mathrm{Ent}(g^2) \le 2\,\mathbb{E}\|\nabla g\|^2$. With $g = 1+\varepsilon f$ (for smooth $f$ with the requisite integrability; the general case follows by approximation), $\mathbb{E}\|\nabla(1+\varepsilon f)\|^2 = \varepsilon^2\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f\|^2$, so
$$2\varepsilon^2\mathrm{Var}(f)+O(\varepsilon^3) \le 2\varepsilon^2\,\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f\|^2,$$
and letting $\varepsilon \to 0$ gives $\mathrm{Var}(f) \le \mathbb{E}\|\nabla f\|^2$, the Gaussian Poincar\'e inequality (Theorem 3.20). ∎
