**Central idea.** For $u(t)=T_{e^{-t}}h_n$, the heat equation $\dot u=LT_{e^{-t}}h_n$ from Exercise 5.18 combined with $L\circ T_{e^{-t}}=T_{e^{-t}}\circ L$ (Stein's lemma) and $Lh_n=-nh_n$ gives the scalar ODE $\dot u=-nu$, whence $u(t)=e^{-nt}h_n$, i.e. $T_\gamma h_n=\gamma^n h_n$. Since $T_\gamma$ is a tensor product over coordinates, it acts diagonally on product Hermite polynomials, and $L^2$-continuity (contraction) extends this to the Hermite expansion.

**Solution.** Let $h_n$ be the $n$-th Hermite polynomial, $Lh_n=-nh_n$, and put $u(t)=T_{e^{-t}}h_n$ (the formal computations below are justified for polynomials by differentiation under the integral and Gaussian integration by parts). By Exercise 5.18, $\frac{d}{dt}T_{e^{-t}}g=L\,T_{e^{-t}}g$ for nice $g$, so $\dot u(t)=L\,T_{e^{-t}}h_n$. Moreover $L$ commutes with $T_\gamma$: indeed,
$$T_\gamma Lg(y)-LT_\gamma g(y)=\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma^2g''(\gamma y+\sigma X)-\sigma Xg'(\gamma y+\sigma X)\big]=0,$$
with $\sigma=\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}$, by Stein's lemma $\mathbb{E}[X\psi(X)]=\mathbb{E}[\psi'(X)]$ applied to $\psi(x)=\sigma g'(\gamma y+\sigma x)$. Hence
$$\dot u(t)=L\,T_{e^{-t}}h_n=T_{e^{-t}}Lh_n=T_{e^{-t}}(-nh_n)=-n\,u(t).$$
For each fixed $x$, $t\mapsto u(t,x)$ solves $\dot u=-nu$ with $u(0,x)=h_n(x)$, so $u(t,x)=e^{-nt}h_n(x)$. With $\gamma=e^{-t}$,
$$T_\gamma h_n=\gamma^n h_n,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots$$
($n=0$ since $h_0\equiv 1$ and $T_\gamma 1=1$).

Now let $H_{\bar k}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^n h_{k_i}(x_i)$ for $\bar k=(k_1,\ldots,k_n)\in\mathbb{N}^n$, $|\bar k|=\sum_i k_i$. Since $T_\gamma=T_\gamma^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes T_\gamma^{(n)}$ and the defining expectation factorizes over the independent $X_i$,
$$T_\gamma H_{\bar k}=\prod_{i=1}^n T_\gamma h_{k_i}=\prod_{i=1}^n \gamma^{k_i}h_{k_i}=\gamma^{|\bar k|}H_{\bar k}.$$
The $\{H_{\bar k}\}$ are orthonormal in $L^2(\gamma_n)$ (product of the one-dimensional orthonormality). For $f=\sum_{\bar k}\alpha_{\bar k}H_{\bar k}$ with $\sum_{\bar k}\alpha_{\bar k}^2<\infty$, the partial sums converge to $f$ in $L^2$, and $T_\gamma$, being an $L^2$-contraction (proved at the start of Exercise 5.18, tensored to $n$ dimensions), is $L^2$-continuous. Thus
$$T_\gamma f=\lim_{N\to\infty}T_\gamma\!\sum_{|\bar k|\leq N}\alpha_{\bar k}H_{\bar k}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{|\bar k|\leq N}\gamma^{|\bar k|}\alpha_{\bar k}H_{\bar k}=\sum_{\bar k}\gamma^{|\bar k|}\alpha_{\bar k}\prod_{i=1}^n h_{k_i}$$
in $L^2$, since $\sum_{\bar k}|\gamma^{|\bar k|}\alpha_{\bar k}|^2\leq \sum_{\bar k}\alpha_{\bar k}^2<\infty$. This is the exact Gaussian analog of Theorem 5.16: the Hermite polynomials diagonalize the hypercontractive semigroup. ∎
