**Central idea.** With the Rodrigues definition $h_k(x) = \frac{(-1)^k}{\sqrt{k!}}e^{x^2/2}D^ke^{-x^2/2}$, the generating function is the Taylor series of a translated Gaussian (the operator $e^{-\lambda D}$ is translation). Orthonormality follows by differentiating $\mathbb{E}[F_\lambda(X)F_\mu(X)] = e^{\lambda\mu}$ under the expectation and matching Taylor coefficients. The recurrences come from the PDEs $\partial_\lambda F = (x-\lambda)F$ and $\partial_x F = \lambda F$ satisfied by $F(\lambda,x) = e^{\lambda x-\lambda^2/2}$; Christoffel–Darboux is a telescoping sum using the three-term recurrence.

**Solution.** We use the Rodrigues formula $h_k(x) = \frac{(-1)^k}{\sqrt{k!}}e^{x^2/2}\frac{d^k}{dx^k}e^{-x^2/2}$.

*Generating function.* For fixed $x$ and $g(y) = e^{-y^2/2}$ (entire),
\begin{align*}
\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{\lambda^k}{\sqrt{k!}}h_k(x)
&= e^{x^2/2}\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{(-\lambda)^k}{k!}\,g^{(k)}(x)
= e^{x^2/2}g(x-\lambda) \\
&= e^{x^2/2}e^{-(x-\lambda)^2/2} = e^{\lambda x - \lambda^2/2},
\end{align*}
where the Taylor series converges for all $\lambda$ since $g$ is entire. Thus, with $F(\lambda,x) = e^{\lambda x-\lambda^2/2}$,
$$F(\lambda,x) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{\lambda^k}{\sqrt{k!}}h_k(x) \qquad \forall\,\lambda,x\in\mathbb{R}. \tag{1}$$

*The bivariate identity.* Let $(X_1,X_2)$ be Gaussian with standard Gaussian marginals and $\rho = \mathbb{E}[X_1X_2]$. Then
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda X_1-\lambda^2/2}e^{\mu X_2-\mu^2/2}\big]
&= e^{-(\lambda^2+\mu^2)/2}\,\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda X_1+\mu X_2}\big] \\
&= e^{-(\lambda^2+\mu^2)/2}\,e^{(\lambda^2+2\lambda\mu\rho+\mu^2)/2}
= e^{\lambda\mu\rho},
\end{align*}
since $\lambda X_1+\mu X_2 \sim N(0,\lambda^2+2\lambda\mu\rho+\mu^2)$.

*Orthonormality.* Take $X_1 = X_2 = X$ (so $\rho=1$): $\mathbb{E}[F(\lambda,X)F(\mu,X)] = e^{\lambda\mu}$ for all $\lambda,\mu$. Differentiating under the expectation is justified by dominated convergence: on compact $(\lambda,\mu)$-sets, $|\partial_\lambda^i\partial_\mu^j e^{\lambda x-\lambda^2/2+\mu x-\mu^2/2}| \le C(1+|x|^{i+j})e^{C|x|}$, integrable against the Gaussian law. From (1), $\partial_\lambda^i F(\lambda,x)|_{\lambda=0} = \sqrt{i!}\,h_i(x)$, so
$$\sqrt{i!j!}\,\mathbb{E}[h_i(X)h_j(X)] = \left.\frac{\partial^{i+j}}{\partial\lambda^i\partial\mu^j}\right|_{0} e^{\lambda\mu}.$$
Since $e^{\lambda\mu} = \sum_{k\ge0}\lambda^k\mu^k/k!$, the right-hand side is $i!$ if $i=j$ and $0$ otherwise. Hence $\mathbb{E}[h_i(X)h_j(X)] = \delta_{ij}$.

*Three-term recurrences.* From $\partial_\lambda F = (x-\lambda)F$: writing $F = \sum_n c_n\lambda^n$ with $c_n = h_n/\sqrt{n!}$,
$$\sum_{n\ge0}(n+1)c_{n+1}\lambda^n = \sum_{n\ge0}\big[xc_n - c_{n-1}\big]\lambda^n \quad (c_{-1}:=0),$$
so $(n+1)c_{n+1} = xc_n - c_{n-1}$. Substituting $c_n = h_n/\sqrt{n!}$ and multiplying by $\sqrt{n!}$ gives $\sqrt{n+1}\,h_{n+1} = xh_n - \sqrt{n}\,h_{n-1}$, i.e.
$$xh_n(x) = \sqrt{n+1}\,h_{n+1}(x) + \sqrt{n}\,h_{n-1}(x) \tag{2}$$
(with $h_{-1}:=0$; for $n=0$ this reads $x = h_1(x)$). From $\partial_x F = \lambda F$:
$$\sum_n \frac{h_n'(x)}{\sqrt{n!}}\lambda^n = \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{h_{n-1}(x)}{\sqrt{(n-1)!}}\lambda^n,$$
so $h_n'(x) = \sqrt{n}\,h_{n-1}(x)$ for $n\ge1$ (and $h_0'\equiv0$). Inserting this into (2) yields
$$xh_n(x) = \sqrt{n+1}\,h_{n+1}(x) + h_n'(x). \tag{3}$$

*Christoffel–Darboux.* From (2),
\begin{align*}
(x-y)h_i(x)h_i(y) &= \big[xh_i(x)\big]h_i(y) - h_i(x)\big[yh_i(y)\big] \\
&= \sqrt{i+1}\big[h_{i+1}(x)h_i(y)-h_i(x)h_{i+1}(y)\big] \\
&\quad - \sqrt{i}\big[h_i(x)h_{i-1}(y)-h_{i-1}(x)h_i(y)\big].
\end{align*}
With $D_i = \sqrt{i+1}\,[h_{i+1}(x)h_i(y)-h_i(x)h_{i+1}(y)]$ (and $D_{-1}=0$), this is $D_i - D_{i-1}$. Summing $i=0$ to $n-1$ telescopes:
$$(x-y)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i(x)h_i(y) = D_{n-1} = \sqrt{n}\,\big[h_n(x)h_{n-1}(y)-h_{n-1}(x)h_n(y)\big],$$
and dividing by $x-y$ gives the formula.

*Remark on completeness.* Each $h_k$ is a polynomial of exact degree $k$ (leading coefficient $1/\sqrt{k!}$ from Rodrigues), so $\{h_0,\dots,h_n\}$ spans all polynomials of degree $\le n$. Polynomials are dense in $L_2(\gamma)$: approximate $f\in L_2(\gamma)$ by bounded compactly supported continuous functions, then uniformly on large compacts by polynomials (Weierstrass), with Gaussian tails controlling the remainder. With orthonormality, $\{h_k\}$ is therefore an orthonormal basis of $L_2(\gamma)$. ∎
