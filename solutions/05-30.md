**Central idea.** Right-multiplying $\mathrm{Jac}(J)$ by the real matrix $M$ of the isometry $A \mapsto U^*AU$ block-diagonalizes $C = \mathrm{Jac}(J)M$: the $\lambda$-rows give $I_n$ by (5.8), and each column of the $p$-block carries a factor $(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)$ by (5.7), so $\det C$ is $\Delta(\lambda)^2$ times a function of $U$ alone. (The $M$ printed in the exercise is wrong; see the Erratum. The proof below uses the exact real matrix of $A\mapsto U^*AU$.)

**Erratum.** The statement is wrong as printed: the displayed $M$ is not the real matrix of $A\mapsto U^*AU$, so with it the claims of part 1 fail. For $j'<k'$, the true coefficients are, in the $\mathrm{Re}\,A[j',k']$ rows, $\mathrm{Re}/\mathrm{Im}$ of $\bar U[j',j]U[k',k]+\bar U[k',j]U[j',k]$ (output entry $[j,k]$, $j\le k$), and in the $\mathrm{Im}\,A[j',k']$ rows, $\mathrm{Re}/\mathrm{Im}$ of $i\bigl(\bar U[j',j]U[k',k]-\bar U[k',j]U[j',k]\bigr)$. The printed $M$ has two independent defects in the $(j',k')$ rows: (a) in the first column block ($k=j$) it omits the factor $2$ (printed $\mathrm{Re}\,\bar U[j',j]U[k',j]$ and $-\mathrm{Im}\,\bar U[j',j]U[k',j]$; true $2\mathrm{Re}$ and $-2\mathrm{Im}$); (b) in the off-diagonal column blocks ($j<k$) it doubles the first term, $2\bar U[j',j]U[k',k]$, in place of $\bar U[j',j]U[k',k]\pm\bar U[k',j]U[j',k]$. (The diagonal rows are correct.) Counterexample: $n=2$, $U=2^{-1/2}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. Then the printed $M$ gives, for $i=1$, $C^{1,1}[1,\cdot]=(3/4,\,1/4)$ and $C^{1,2}[1,1]=-1/2$ instead of $(1,0)$ and $0$. Defect (a) alone accounts for $C^{1,1}$; defect (b) alone produces $C^{1,2}[1,1]=-1/2$: the $(\mathrm{Re}\,A[1,2],\mathrm{Re}\,[1,2])$ entry is printed $2\mathrm{Re}\,\bar U[1,1]U[2,2]=-1$ against the true $0$. Doubling only the first column block restores $C^{1,1}=\mathrm{Id}_2$ but leaves $C^{1,2}[1,1]=-1/2$, and for a generic complex unitary $U$ with $n=3$ it leaves $C^{1,2},C^{1,3}\ne0$ (checked numerically). For the Hadamard $U$ above, $\det M_{\text{printed}}=1/2$, while the true matrix has $\det M=1$. Corrected statement: with $M$ the exact real matrix of $A\mapsto U^*AU$ (described above and in the Solution), parts 1–4 hold as stated; this is what is proved below.

**Solution.** Let $M$ be the $n^2\times n^2$ real matrix of the linear isometry $\Phi(A) = U^*AU$ on $\mathcal{H}_n$ in real coordinates (diagonal; $\mathrm{Re}$/$\mathrm{Im}$ of upper triangle). Explicitly, with $A$ Hermitian,
$$(U^*AU)[j,j] = \sum_a |U[a,j]|^2A[a,a] + 2\sum_{a<b}\mathrm{Re}\!\left(A[a,b]\,\bar{U}[a,j]U[b,j]\right),$$
so the first column block of $M$ is $|U[a,j]|^2$, $2\mathrm{Re}\,\bar{U}[a,j]U[b,j]$, $-2\mathrm{Im}\,\bar{U}[a,j]U[b,j]$; similarly $(U^*AU)[j,k]$, $j<k$, gives the off-diagonal column blocks $\bar U[a,j]U[a,k]$ (diagonal rows) and $\bar U[a,j]U[b,k]+\bar U[b,j]U[a,k]$, $i(\bar U[a,j]U[b,k]-\bar U[b,j]U[a,k])$ (real and imaginary rows; take $\mathrm{Re}$/$\mathrm{Im}$). These are the corrections to the printed $M$ (Erratum). Since $\Phi$ is a real-linear bijection, $M$ is invertible with $\det M$ depending only on $U$.

1. For $A = \partial H/\partial\lambda_i$, $(U^*AU)[j,k] = \mathbb{1}_{\{i=j=k\}}$ by (5.8). Hence with the exact $M$,
$$C^{1,1}[i,j] = (U^*\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_i}U)[j,j] = \delta_{ij},$$
i.e.\ $C^{1,1} = \mathrm{Id}_n$, and for $m = r(j,k)$, $j<k$,
$$C^{1,2}[i,m] = \mathrm{Re}\,(U^*\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_i}U)[j,k] = 0, \qquad C^{1,3}[i,m] = \mathrm{Im}\,(U^*\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_i}U)[j,k] = 0.$$

2. For $A = \partial H/\partial p_\ell$ and $m = r(i,j)$, $i<j$, with $B_\ell = U^*AU$,
$$C^{2,2}[\ell,m] = \mathrm{Re}\,B_\ell[i,j], \qquad C^{2,3}[\ell,m] = \mathrm{Im}\,B_\ell[i,j],$$
and by (5.7), $B_\ell[i,j] = S_\ell[i,j](\lambda_j-\lambda_i)$. (The diagonal block $C^{2,1}[\ell,j] = B_\ell[j,j] = 0$ since $\lambda_j-\lambda_j = 0$.)

3. Thus $C = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} \mathrm{Id}_n & 0 \\ 0 & [C^{2,2}\ C^{2,3}] \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ is block diagonal, and $\det C = \det[C^{2,2}\ C^{2,3}]$. Each of the $n(n-1)$ columns of $[C^{2,2}\ C^{2,3}]$ (indexed by $m = r(i,j)$, $i<j$, in each half) carries the factor $(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)$, so
$$\det[C^{2,2}\ C^{2,3}] = \prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)^2 \cdot g(U) = \Delta(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)^2\,g(U),$$
where $g$ depends on the $S_\ell[i,j]$ (hence on $U$) but not on $\lambda$. Since $\det C = \det(\mathrm{Jac}(J))\det(M)$,
$$\det(\mathrm{Jac}(J)) = \Delta(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)^2 \cdot f(U), \qquad f(U) = g(U)/\det(M),$$
a product of the squared Vandermonde determinant and a function of $U$ alone.

4. Lemma 5.24 follows: by Exercise 5.28, $(\Lambda,p) \mapsto H = U\Lambda U^*$ is a diffeomorphism onto $\mathcal{H}_n^{d,g}$; the change-of-variables formula with the Jacobian just computed gives the joint density of $(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)$ as $\Delta(\lambda)^2$ times the pushforward density, and the $U$-dependent factor integrates to a constant independent of $\lambda$ (by unitary invariance of the GUE law), yielding the eigenvalue density proportional to $\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)^2$ times the radial part. ∎
