**Central idea.** Write the Vandermonde determinant as $\det(x_i^{j-1})$ and use column operations to replace the monomials by monic Hermite polynomials (the book's orthonormal $h_j$ rescaled), which are orthogonal under the standard Gaussian. Expanding the squared determinant, orthogonality kills every cross term; only the $n!$ diagonal terms survive, each contributing $\prod_{j=0}^{n-1} j!$. (Note: as written the stated identity is false — see the remark at the end — the correct value carries an extra $n!$; the claimed right-hand side is the integral over the ordered simplex $\{x_1>\cdots>x_n\}$.)

**Solution.** Let $\gamma$ be the standard Gaussian law on $\mathbb{R}$ and
$$I_n = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \Delta(x_1,\ldots,x_n)^2 \, \gamma^{\otimes n}(dx), \qquad \Delta(x)=\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(x_i-x_j)=\det(x_i^{j-1})_{i,j=1}^n$$
up to a sign that disappears after squaring. Let $h_j$ be the normalized (orthonormal) Hermite polynomials of Theorem 5.23, so $\int h_i h_j\,d\gamma=\delta_{ij}$, and let $p_j$ be the monic Hermite polynomial of degree $j$. Since $h_j$ has leading coefficient $1/\sqrt{j!}$ (it is $He_j/\sqrt{j!}$ with $He_j$ monic), $p_j=\sqrt{j!}\,h_j$ and
$$\int p_i p_j\,d\gamma = j!\,\delta_{ij}.$$
Replacing, in the determinant, each column $(x_1^{j-1},\ldots,x_n^{j-1})^\top$ by $(p_{j-1}(x_1),\ldots,p_{j-1}(x_n))^\top$ is a sequence of column operations (subtracting multiples of earlier columns), so
$$\Delta(x)=\det\big(p_{j-1}(x_i)\big)_{i,j=1}^n.$$
Expanding the determinant,
$$\Delta(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^n p_{\sigma(i)-1}(x_i),$$
hence
$$\Delta(x)^2=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_n}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma\tau)\prod_{i=1}^n p_{\sigma(i)-1}(x_i)p_{\tau(i)-1}(x_i).$$
Integrating against $\gamma^{\otimes n}$ and using independence across coordinates,
$$I_n=\sum_{\sigma,\tau}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma\tau)\prod_{i=1}^n \langle p_{\sigma(i)-1},p_{\tau(i)-1}\rangle_{L^2(\gamma)}.$$
By orthogonality, the $i$-th factor vanishes unless $\sigma(i)=\tau(i)$; the product over $i$ is nonzero only when $\sigma=\tau$. For $\sigma=\tau$, $\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma\tau)=1$ and
$$\prod_{i=1}^n \langle p_{\sigma(i)-1},p_{\sigma(i)-1}\rangle = \prod_{i=1}^n (\sigma(i)-1)! = \prod_{j=0}^{n-1} j!,$$
independent of $\sigma$. With $n!$ permutations,
$$I_n = n!\prod_{j=0}^{n-1} j!.$$
*Remark (correction to the stated identity).* The identity as written is false: for $n=2$, $\Delta(x_1,x_2)=x_2-x_1$ and $I_2=\mathbb{E}[(X_1-X_2)^2]=2$ for $X_1,X_2$ i.i.d. standard Gaussian, whereas $\prod_{j=0}^{1}j!=0!\cdot1!=1$. The claimed value $\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}j!$ is instead the integral over the ordered region $\{x_1>\cdots>x_n\}$ (the region of Lemma 5.24), since $\Delta^2$ is permutation-symmetric and the $n!$ orderings partition $\mathbb{R}^n$ up to a null set.
