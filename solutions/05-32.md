**Central idea.** Substitute $x=\sin\theta$ to turn the moment integral into a difference of sine-power integrals; the sine reduction formula then yields both hinted identities, and the Wallis value $I_{2k}=\pi(2k)!/(2^{2k}(k!)^2)$ produces the Catalan number.

**Solution.** With the semi-circular density,
$$m_{2k}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}x^{2k}\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx.$$
Put $x=\sin\theta$, $dx=\cos\theta\,d\theta$; on $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$, $\sqrt{1-x^2}=\cos\theta\ge0$, so
$$m_{2k}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\sin^{2k}\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta.$$
Writing $\cos^2\theta=1-\sin^2\theta$ and $I_m=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\sin^m\theta\,d\theta$,
$$m_{2k}=\frac{2}{\pi}\big(I_{2k}-I_{2k+2}\big).$$
For $m\ge2$, integration by parts gives the reduction formula $I_m=\frac{m-1}{m}I_{m-2}$. Hence $I_{2k+2}=\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}I_{2k}$, and
$$m_{2k}=\frac{2}{\pi}I_{2k}\left(1-\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}\right)=\frac{2}{\pi(2k+2)}I_{2k},$$
the second hinted identity. Since $I_{2k}=\frac{2k+2}{2k+1}I_{2k+2}$, this also gives
$$m_{2k}=\frac{2}{\pi(2k+1)}I_{2k+2},$$
the first hinted identity. Iterating the reduction formula,
$$I_{2k}=\frac{2k-1}{2k}\cdot\frac{2k-3}{2k-2}\cdots\frac{1}{2}\cdot I_0,\qquad I_0=\pi.$$
Now $\frac{1\cdot3\cdots(2k-1)}{2\cdot4\cdots(2k)}=\frac{(2k)!}{2^{2k}(k!)^2}$, since $(2k)!=(2^kk!)(1\cdot3\cdots(2k-1))$. Thus $I_{2k}=\pi\frac{(2k)!}{2^{2k}(k!)^2}$, and
$$m_{2k}=\frac{2}{\pi(2k+2)}\cdot\pi\frac{(2k)!}{2^{2k}(k!)^2}=\frac{2(2k)!}{(2k+2)2^{2k}(k!)^2}=\frac{(2k)!}{(k+1)2^{2k}(k!)^2}=\frac{C_k}{2^{2k}},$$
where $C_k=\frac{1}{k+1}\binom{2k}{k}$ is the $k$-th Catalan number.
