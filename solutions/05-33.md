**Central idea.** Parametrize the GUE by a standard Gaussian vector $g \in \mathbb{R}^{n^2}$ via the linear isometry $g \mapsto H(g)$ (possible because the book's GUE has $\mathbb{E}|H_{ij}|^2 = \sigma^2 = 1/(4n)$ for all $i,j$). For fixed $1$-Lipschitz $f$, the map $g \mapsto L_n(f) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i f(\lambda_i(H(g)))$ is $(1/2n)$-Lipschitz: $f$ contributes the factor $1$, Cauchy–Schwarz and Lidskii's inequality (Exercise 3.16) turn $\frac{1}{n}\sum_i |\lambda_i - \lambda_i'|$ into $\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\|H-H'\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$, and the parametrization contributes $\sigma = 1/\sqrt{4n}$. Theorem 5.6 (applied to $\pm F$) then gives a tail bound $2e^{-2n^2t^2}$, which implies the claim uniformly in $f \in \mathcal{B}$.

**Solution.** Recall from Section 5.10 that $H$ is from the GUE if it is Hermitian with independent diagonal entries $H_{ii} \sim N(0,\sigma^2)$ and independent off-diagonal entries $H_{ij}$, $i<j$, whose real and imaginary parts are independent $N(0,\sigma^2/2)$, where $\sigma^2 = 1/(4n)$. Identify the space of $n \times n$ Hermitian matrices with $\mathbb{R}^{n^2}$ via
$$H_{ii} = \sigma g_i, \qquad H_{ij} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2}}\big(g_{ij}^R + i g_{ij}^I\big) \;\; (i<j), \qquad H_{ji} = \overline{H_{ij}},$$
so that $g = (g_i, g_{ij}^R, g_{ij}^I)$ is a standard Gaussian vector in $\mathbb{R}^{n^2}$. Since
$$\|H\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = \sum_i H_{ii}^2 + 2\sum_{i<j}|H_{ij}|^2 = \sigma^2\|g\|_2^2,$$
the map $g \mapsto H(g)$ satisfies $\|H(g)-H(g')\|_{\mathrm{HS}} = \sigma\|g-g'\|_2$.

Fix $f \in \mathcal{B}$ and set $F(g) = L_n(f) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n f(\lambda_i)$, where $\lambda_1 \ge \cdots \ge \lambda_n$ are the (continuously varying, hence measurable) eigenvalues of $H(g)$. For $g, g'$ with corresponding eigenvalues $\lambda_i, \lambda_i'$,
\begin{align*}
|F(g) - F(g')|
&\le \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n |f(\lambda_i) - f(\lambda_i')|
 \le \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n |\lambda_i - \lambda_i'| \\
&\le \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n (\lambda_i - \lambda_i')^2\Big)^{1/2}
 \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\|H(g) - H(g')\|_{\mathrm{HS}} \\
&= \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\|g - g'\|_2
 = \frac{1}{2n}\|g - g'\|_2,
\end{align*}
using that $f$ is $1$-Lipschitz, Cauchy–Schwarz, Lidskii's inequality in the Hoffman–Wielandt form of Exercise 3.16 (valid for Hermitian matrices), and $\sigma/\sqrt{n} = 1/(2n)$. Thus $F$ (and $-F$) is $L$-Lipschitz on $\mathbb{R}^{n^2}$ with $L = 1/(2n)$, independently of $f$.

Theorem 5.6 applied to $F$ and to $-F$, with a union bound for the two one-sided tails, gives, for $t \ge 0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\big\{|L_n(f) - \mathbb{E}[L_n(f)]| \ge t\big\}
 \le 2\exp\!\Big(-\frac{t^2}{2L^2}\Big)
 = 2e^{-2n^2t^2}
 \le 2e^{-n^2t^2/2}.$$
The right-hand side does not depend on $f$, so taking the supremum over $f \in \mathcal{B}$ yields
$$\sup_{f\in\mathcal{B}} \mathbb{P}\big\{|L_n(f) - \mathbb{E}[L_n(f)]| \ge t\big\} \le 2e^{-\frac{n^2t^2}{2}}, \qquad t \ge 0,$$
as claimed. (Only the $1$-Lipschitz property of $f$ was used; the bound $|f| \le 1$ is not needed for the concentration itself.)
