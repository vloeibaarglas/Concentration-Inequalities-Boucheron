**Central idea.** For fixed $f\in\mathcal{B}$, the map from the independent Gaussian entries of the GUE matrix to $L_n(f)$ is $(2n)^{-1}$-Lipschitz. Three facts give this: $f$ is $1$-Lipschitz, ordered eigenvalues are $1$-Lipschitz in Frobenius norm (Hoffman–Wielandt), and the GUE entries are $(4n)^{-1/2}$ times standard normals. A supremum of $(2n)^{-1}$-Lipschitz functions is $(2n)^{-1}$-Lipschitz, so Theorem 5.6 gives the tail bound. For $\mathbb{E}Z$ we use a self-contained net argument instead of the semicircle-law rates. First, $\|H\|\le5$ except with probability $e^{-2n}$. Second, $\mathcal{B}$ restricted to $[-5,5]$ has an $\varepsilon$-net of log-cardinality $O(1/\varepsilon)$. Third, each $L_n(h)-\mathbb{E}L_n(h)$ is sub-Gaussian with variance factor $1/(4n^2)$. One-step chaining with $\varepsilon=n^{-2/3}$ gives $\mathbb{E}Z=O(n^{-2/3})\le\kappa/\sqrt n$.

**Solution.** Write the GUE matrix with the book's normalization ($\mathbb{E}|H_{ij}|^2=\sigma^2=1/(4n)$). As in Exercise 5.33, $H=H(g)$ for a standard Gaussian vector $g\in\mathbb{R}^{n^2}$, through a linear map with $\|H(g)-H(g')\|_F=\sigma\|g-g'\|_2$. For $f\in\mathcal{B}$ put $X_f=L_n(f)-\mathbb{E}L_n(f)$, so $Z=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{B}}|X_f|$.

*Lipschitz property.* Fix $f\in\mathcal{B}$ and order eigenvalues decreasingly. Then
$$|L_n(f)(H)-L_n(f)(H')|\le\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|f(\lambda_i)-f(\lambda_i')|\le\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|\lambda_i-\lambda_i'|\le\frac1{\sqrt n}\|H-H'\|_F.$$
This uses that $f$ is $1$-Lipschitz, Cauchy–Schwarz, and the Hoffman–Wielandt inequality $\sum_i(\lambda_i-\lambda_i')^2\le\|H-H'\|_F^2$. Hence $g\mapsto X_f$ is $(2n)^{-1}$-Lipschitz, since $\sigma/\sqrt n=1/(2n)$. So is each $|X_f|$, and so is $Z$, because $|\sup_f a_f-\sup_f b_f|\le\sup_f|a_f-b_f|$.

*Measurability.* All eigenvalues lie in $[-\|H\|,\|H\|]$. By Arzelà–Ascoli, $\mathcal{B}$ is separable for uniform convergence on compacts; let $\{f_k\}$ be dense. For $f\in\mathcal{B}$ and $f_{k_j}\to f$ uniformly on compacts, $L_n(f_{k_j})\to L_n(f)$ pointwise. Also $\mathbb{E}L_n(f_{k_j})\to\mathbb{E}L_n(f)$ by dominated convergence ($|f|\le1$). Hence $Z=\sup_k|X_{f_k}|$ is measurable, and $0\le Z\le2$.

*Tail bound.* Theorem 5.6 applied to the $(2n)^{-1}$-Lipschitz function $Z$ gives, for $t\ge0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\le\exp\!\Big(-\frac{t^2}{2(2n)^{-2}}\Big)=e^{-2n^2t^2}\le2e^{-nt^2/2},$$
since $2n^2\ge n$. This is the claimed tail bound, and in fact a stronger one.

*Expectation bound. Step 1: the operator norm.* Let $\mathcal{N}$ be a $\tfrac14$-net of the unit sphere of $\mathbb{C}^n\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}$. The volumetric bound gives $|\mathcal{N}|\le(1+8)^{2n}=81^n$. Let $v$ be a unit vector with $\|H\|=|v^*Hv|$, and let $w\in\mathcal{N}$ satisfy $|v-w|\le\frac14$. Then
$$|v^*Hv-w^*Hw|\le|(v-w)^*Hv|+|w^*H(v-w)|\le\tfrac12\|H\|,$$
so $\|H\|\le2\max_{w\in\mathcal{N}}|w^*Hw|$. Each $w^*Hw=\langle H,ww^*\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}$ is a linear functional of $g$ with norm $\sigma\|ww^*\|_F=\sigma$, so it is $N(0,\sigma^2)$. The maximal inequality we use is $\mathbb{E}\max_{i\le N}Y_i\le\lambda^{-1}\log\sum_i\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Y_i}\le\lambda^{-1}\log N+\lambda v/2$ whenever $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Y_i}\le\lambda^2v/2$; optimizing at $\lambda=\sqrt{2\log N/v}$ gives $\mathbb{E}\max_iY_i\le\sqrt{2v\log N}$. Applied to the $2|\mathcal{N}|$ variables $\pm w^*Hw$, it gives
$$\mathbb{E}\|H\|\le2\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2\cdot81^n)}=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sqrt{2\log2+2n\log81}\le\sqrt{2\log 2+2\log 81}<3.2\le4.$$
Moreover $g\mapsto\|H(g)\|$ is $\sigma$-Lipschitz, because $\|\cdot\|\le\|\cdot\|_F$. Theorem 5.6 then gives
$$\mathbb{P}\{\|H\|>5\}\le\mathbb{P}\{\|H\|-\mathbb{E}\|H\|>1\}\le e^{-1/(2\sigma^2)}=e^{-2n}.$$

*Step 2: a net of $\mathcal{B}$ on $I=[-5,5]$.* Let $\varepsilon\in(0,1]$ and $\eta=\varepsilon/8$. Put $x_k=-5+k\eta$ for $k=0,\dots,K$, with $K=\lceil10/\eta\rceil$. For $f\in\mathcal{B}$, let $r_k\in\eta\mathbb{Z}$ be a nearest point to $f(x_k)$, so $|r_k-f(x_k)|\le\eta/2$. Then $|r_{k+1}-r_k|\le\eta+\eta=2\eta$, and $r_{k+1}-r_k\in\{0,\pm\eta,\pm2\eta\}$. Let $\tilde f$ be the piecewise linear interpolant of $(x_k,r_k)$. For $x\in[x_k,x_{k+1}]$,
$$|f(x)-\tilde f(x)|\le|f(x)-f(x_k)|+|f(x_k)-r_k|+|r_k-\tilde f(x)|\le\eta+\tfrac\eta2+2\eta<4\eta.$$
The interpolant is determined by $r_0$ and the $K$ increments. Since $r_0\in\eta\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1-\eta/2,1+\eta/2]$ takes at most $2/\eta+2$ values, there are at most $(2/\eta+2)\,5^{K}$ interpolants. For each interpolant whose $4\eta$-ball (sup norm on $I$) meets $\mathcal{B}$, choose one $h\in\mathcal{B}$ in that ball. The resulting set $\mathcal{N}_\varepsilon\subseteq\mathcal{B}$ has the property that every $f\in\mathcal{B}$ lies within $8\eta=\varepsilon$ of some $h\in\mathcal{N}_\varepsilon$ on $I$. Its size $N(\varepsilon)$ satisfies
$$\log N(\varepsilon)\le\log\Big(\frac{16}\varepsilon+2\Big)+\Big(\frac{80}\varepsilon+1\Big)\log5\le\frac{18}{\varepsilon}+\frac{81\cdot1.61}{\varepsilon}\le\frac{150}{\varepsilon}.$$

*Step 3: one-step chaining.* For $f\in\mathcal{B}$ pick $\pi f\in\mathcal{N}_\varepsilon$ with $\sup_I|f-\pi f|\le\varepsilon$. Note $|f-\pi f|\le2$ everywhere. Let $N_{\rm out}=\#\{i:|\lambda_i|>5\}$. Then
$$|L_n(f)-L_n(\pi f)|\le\varepsilon+\frac{2N_{\rm out}}n,\qquad |X_f-X_{\pi f}|\le2\varepsilon+\frac{2N_{\rm out}}n+\frac{2\,\mathbb{E}N_{\rm out}}n,$$
so
$$Z\le\max_{h\in\mathcal{N}_\varepsilon}|X_h|+2\varepsilon+\frac{2N_{\rm out}}n+\frac{2\,\mathbb{E}N_{\rm out}}n.$$
Each $\pm X_h$ ($h\in\mathcal{B}$) is a centered $(2n)^{-1}$-Lipschitz function of $g$. By Theorem 5.5, $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(\pm X_h)}\le\lambda^2/(8n^2)$ for all $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$. The maximal inequality, with $v=1/(4n^2)$ over $2N(\varepsilon)$ variables, then gives
$$\mathbb{E}\max_{h\in\mathcal{N}_\varepsilon}|X_h|\le\frac1{2n}\sqrt{2\log(2N(\varepsilon))}\le\frac1{2n}\sqrt{2+\frac{300}{\varepsilon}}.$$
Since $N_{\rm out}\le n\,\mathbb{1}\{\|H\|>5\}$, Step 1 gives $\mathbb{E}N_{\rm out}/n\le e^{-2n}$. Hence
$$\mathbb{E}Z\le\frac1{2n}\sqrt{2+\frac{300}\varepsilon}+2\varepsilon+4e^{-2n}.$$
Take $\varepsilon=n^{-2/3}\in(0,1]$. Then $\sqrt{2+300n^{2/3}}\le\sqrt{302}\,n^{1/3}$, and therefore
$$\mathbb{E}Z\le\Big(\frac{\sqrt{302}}2+2\Big)n^{-2/3}+4e^{-2n}\le\frac{10.7}{\sqrt n}+\frac{4\sqrt n\,e^{-2n}}{\sqrt n}\le\frac{12}{\sqrt n},$$
using $n^{-2/3}\le n^{-1/2}$ and $\sqrt n\,e^{-2n}\le e^{-2}<0.136$ for $n\ge1$. Thus $\mathbb{E}Z\le\kappa/\sqrt n$ with the universal constant $\kappa=12$ (indeed $\mathbb{E}Z=O(n^{-2/3})$). ∎

**Erratum (earlier version).** An earlier version of this solution derived $\mathbb{E}Z\le\kappa/\sqrt n$ from the Götze–Tikhomirov Kolmogorov-distance rates. It relied on the lemma "$\pi\le$ Lévy $\le d_K$, hence $d_{BL}\le3d_K$". That lemma is false in general: the Lévy metric is bounded by the Prokhorov metric, not conversely. Counterexample: $\mu$ uniform on $\{0,2,\dots,18\}$ and $\nu=\mu(\cdot-1)$ (uniform on $\{1,3,\dots,19\}$). Here $d_K(\mu,\nu)=0.1$. The triangle wave $f(x)=\tfrac12-\mathrm{dist}(x,2\mathbb{Z})$ lies in $\mathcal{B}$ ($|f|\le\frac12$, $1$-Lipschitz), and $\mu(f)-\nu(f)=\tfrac12-(-\tfrac12)=1$, so $d_{BL}(\mu,\nu)\ge1=10\,d_K(\mu,\nu)$ (checked numerically). The proof above avoids this route entirely.

The citation route can be repaired when one of the two measures is the semicircle law, because that law is atomless with compact support. *Claim:* if $\nu$ is atomless with $\nu([-a,a])=1$, then $d_{BL}(\mu,\nu)\le(4+2a)\,d_K(\mu,\nu)$ for every probability $\mu$. *Proof.* Let $G=F_\mu-F_\nu$, $\delta=\sup|G|$, $m=\mu-\nu$ and $f\in\mathcal{B}$. Since $f$ is absolutely continuous with $|f'|\le1$ a.e., Fubini gives
$$\int_{(-a,a]}f\,dm=f(-a)\big(G(a)-G(-a)\big)+\int_{-a}^af'(t)\big(G(a)-G(t)\big)\,dt=f(a)G(a)-f(-a)G(-a)-\int_{-a}^aGf'\,dt,$$
which has absolute value at most $(2+2a)\delta$. Outside $(-a,a]$, $\nu$ has no mass (it has no atom at $-a$). Moreover $\mu((-\infty,-a])=F_\mu(-a)=G(-a)\le\delta$ and $\mu((a,\infty))=F_\nu(a)-F_\mu(a)=-G(a)\le\delta$. So $|\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-a,a]}f\,dm|\le2\delta$. In the book's normalization the semicircle law is supported on $[-2\sigma\sqrt n,2\sigma\sqrt n]=[-1,1]$, so $d_{BL}(\cdot,\sigma_{sc})\le6\,d_K(\cdot,\sigma_{sc})$. Together with the cited rates and the triangle inequality, this gives $\mathbb{E}Z\le6(C_1+C_2)/\sqrt n$. The self-contained proof above does not need this.
