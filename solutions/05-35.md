**Central idea.** Lemma 5.25 writes $\mathbb{E}L_nf$ as an average of $\mathbb{E}[f(\sigma X)h_i(X)^2]$; Christoffel–Darboux sums the Hermite functions in closed form into the kernel $K_n$ (with the printed normalization $1/\sqrt{2\pi n}$, which is the correct one). The mgf $F_n(s)$ is then computed exactly via Mehler's formula, and dominated convergence reads off the termwise limit, which is the semicircular mgf with the Catalan moments $m_{2k}=C_k/4^k$ from Exercise 5.32.

**Erratum.** The hint is wrong as printed (the statement, including $K_n(x,x)=\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\bigl(h_n'h_{n-1}-h_nh_{n-1}'\bigr)(x)$, is correct: $K_n$ equals the one-point density $\rho_n$ below and $\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_n(x,x)\,dx=1$). The hint's display $\frac{d}{dx}K_n(x,x)=-e^{-x^2/2}h_n(x)h_{n-1}(x)=-e^{-x^2/2}\frac{h_n(x)h_n'(x)}{\sqrt n}$ drops the factor $1/\sqrt{2\pi n}$; the correct identity is
$$\frac{d}{dx}K_n(x,x)=-\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\,h_n(x)h_{n-1}(x)=-\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,n}\,h_n(x)h_n'(x).$$
For example, at $n=2$ the true derivative is $1/(2\sqrt{\pi})\approx0.2821$ times the printed one (in general the ratio is $1/\sqrt{2\pi n}$; checked symbolically for $n=1,2,3,5$). The second equality of the hint is correct, since $h_n'=\sqrt n\,h_{n-1}$.

**Solution.** The Christoffel–Darboux identity (Exercise 5.22, restated in 5.32) is, for $n\ge1$,
$$\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i(x)^2=\frac1{\sqrt n}\bigl(h_n'(x)h_{n-1}(x)-h_n(x)h_{n-1}'(x)\bigr),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.$$
Lemma 5.25 gives, with $X$ standard Gaussian and $\sigma=1/\sqrt{4n}$,
$$\mathbb{E}L_nf=\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[f(\sigma X)h_i(X)^2]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\sigma x)\,\rho_n(x)\,dx,\quad \rho_n(x)=\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i(x)^2\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.$$
By Christoffel–Darboux, $\rho_n(x)=\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sqrt n}\bigl(h_n'(x)h_{n-1}(x)-h_n(x)h_{n-1}'(x)\bigr)$. Hence, with the kernel as printed,
$$K_n(x,x)=\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\bigl(h_n'(x)h_{n-1}(x)-h_n(x)h_{n-1}'(x)\bigr)=\rho_n(x),$$
$$\mathbb{E}L_nf=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x/\sqrt{4n})\,K_n(x,x)\,dx\qquad\text{for bounded continuous }f.$$
(Consistency check: $\int K_n=\frac1n\sum_{i<n}\mathbb{E}[h_i(X)^2]=1$ by orthonormality of the $h_i$.)

For the hint, differentiating $K_n$ and using the Hermite ODE $h_k''-xh_k'+kh_k=0$ (the $x$-terms and the $h_n'h_{n-1}'$ terms cancel),
$$\frac{d}{dx}K_n(x,x)=-\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sqrt n}\,h_n(x)h_{n-1}(x)=-\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,n}\,h_n(x)h_n'(x),$$
since $h_n'=\sqrt n\,h_{n-1}$. (This is the corrected hint of the Erratum; the identity is not needed in what follows.)

Now $F_n(s)=\mathbb{E}L_n[e^{s\cdot}]=\int e^{s\sigma x}K_n(x,x)\,dx=\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}h_i(X)^2]$ with $t=s\sigma=s/(2\sqrt n)$. Mehler's formula for the orthonormal Hermite functions, $|r|<1$,
$$\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}h_i(x)h_i(y)r^i=\frac1{\sqrt{1-r^2}}\exp\!\left(\frac{2xyr-(x^2+y^2)r^2}{2(1-r^2)}\right),$$
with $y=x$ gives $\sum_i h_i(x)^2r^i=(1-r^2)^{-1/2}\exp(x^2r/(1+r))$. Multiplying by $e^{tx}$ and taking $\mathbb{E}$ (Tonelli, terms nonnegative),
$$\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}h_i(X)^2]r^i=\frac1{\sqrt{1-r^2}}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{tX+cX^2}\right],\quad c=\frac{r}{1+r}<\frac12,$$
and $\mathbb{E}[e^{tX+cX^2}]=e^{t^2/(2(1-2c))}/\sqrt{1-2c}$ with $1-2c=(1-r)/(1+r)$, so
$$\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_i(t)r^i=\frac{e^{t^2(1+r)/(2(1-r))}}{1-r},\qquad a_i(t):=\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}h_i(X)^2].$$
Expanding $e^{t^2(1+r)/(2(1-r))}=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(t^2/2)^k}{k!}\frac{(1+r)^k}{(1-r)^k}$ (uniform on $|r|\le\rho<1$) and extracting $[r^i]$,
$$a_i(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t^2/2)^k}{k!}\,c_{i,k},\qquad c_{i,k}=[r^i]\frac{(1+r)^k}{(1-r)^{k+1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(i,k)}\binom{k}{j}\binom{i-j+k}{k}\ge0,$$
with the convention $\binom{a}{k}=0$ for $a<k$. Summing over $i<n$ (finite sum) and swapping with the $k$-sum,
$$F_n(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t^2/2)^k}{k!}\,b_{n,k},\qquad b_{n,k}=\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\binom{i-j+k}{k},$$
where $t^2/2=s^2/(8n)$. By hockey-stick, $\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom{i-j+k}{k}=\binom{n-j+k}{k+1}$, so
$$\frac{(t^2/2)^k}{k!}b_{n,k}=\frac{(s^2/(8n))^k}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac1n\binom{n-j+k}{k+1}.$$
This is dominated uniformly in $n$ by $\frac{s^{2k}(1+k)^{k+1}}{4^k k!(k+1)!}$ (using $\binom{n+k}{k+1}\le(n+k)^{k+1}/(k+1)!$ and $n\ge1$), which is summable in $k$. Dominated convergence gives, for fixed $k$,
$$\frac{(s^2/(8n))^k}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{k}{j}\frac1n\binom{n-j+k}{k+1}\longrightarrow\frac{(s^2/8)^k}{k!}\cdot\frac{2^k}{(k+1)!}=\frac{s^{2k}}{4^k k!(k+1)!},$$
since $\frac1n\binom{n-j+k}{k+1}\to\frac{n^k}{(k+1)!}$ and $\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{k}{j}=2^k$. Hence
$$\lim_{n\to\infty}F_n(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{2k}}{4^k k!(k+1)!}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{m_{2k}s^{2k}}{(2k)!}\quad\text{for all }s\in\mathbb{R},$$
with $m_{2k}=C_k/4^k=\frac{(2k)!}{4^k k!(k+1)!}$ the even moments of the semicircular law of Exercise 5.32 (odd moments vanish by the symmetry $H\stackrel{d}=-H$). This is the moment-generating function of the semicircular distribution.
