**Central idea.** Match CDFs to write $Z=\max_{1\le i\le n}|X_i|$ as $f(Y)$, where $f(y)=U(2e^y)$, $Y=\max_{1\le i\le n}E_i$ and the $E_i\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ are iid. The map $f$ is concave, because its derivative is the Mills ratio $R=\bar\Phi/\phi$ evaluated at $f$, and $R$ is decreasing. Concavity gives the tangent bound $f(Y)-\mathbb{E}f(Y)\le\delta_n+f'(H_n)(Y-H_n)$, where $\delta_n>0$ is the Jensen gap. The bound $R(u)<1/u$ gives $f'(H_n)\le1/U(2n)$. Rényi's representation makes $Y-H_n$ a weighted sum of centered exponentials, so it is sub-gamma $\Gamma_+(2,1)$. The scale factor is $c=1$, not $1/3$. With $c=1/3$ the statement as printed is **false**, and we prove it with denominator $2+tU(2n)$. The rate $(2\log2n)^{3/2}\delta_n\to\pi^2/12$ comes from the second-order Taylor remainder: $|f''|\sim f^{-3}$ and $\mathrm{Var}(Y)\to\pi^2/6$.

**Solution.** **Erratum: the printed bound is false.** The printed inequality, with denominator $2+tU(2n)/3$, fails for large $n$. For $x\ge0$ we have $\mathbb{P}\{Z\le x\}=(1-2\bar\Phi(x))^n$, so tail probabilities and $\mathbb{E}Z=\int_0^\infty(1-(1-2\bar\Phi(x))^n)\,dx$ can be computed to high precision. We used mpmath at 60 digits, and cross-checked $\mathbb{E}Z=\mathbb{E}f(Y)$ by integrating against the density of $Y$. Take $s:=tU(2n)=30$, so the printed right-hand side is $e^{-900/(2\cdot12)}=e^{-37.5}\approx5.18\times10^{-17}$ for every $n$. With $\delta_n=f(H_n)-\mathbb{E}Z$ as below:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $10^{8}$ | $5.7307$ | $5.82424$ | $3.609\times10^{-3}$ | $1.90\times10^{-20}$ | $5.18\times10^{-17}$ | $7.81\times10^{-7}$ |
| $10^{20}$ | $9.3360$ | $9.39606$ | $9.348\times10^{-4}$ | $1.85\times10^{-16}$ | $5.18\times10^{-17}$ (**fails**) | $7.81\times10^{-7}$ |

| $10^{200}$ | $30.2285$ | $30.24755$ | $2.954\times10^{-5}$ | $3.05\times10^{-14}$ | $5.18\times10^{-17}$ (**fails**) | $7.81\times10^{-7}$ |

For example, at $n=10^{20}$ we have $t=30/9.3360\approx3.213>0$, and the true probability is $3.6$ times the printed bound. A heuristic explains why: $U(2n)(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)$ is asymptotically a centered Gumbel variable, whose tail at $s$ is about $e^{-s-\gamma}$, while the printed bound decays like $e^{9-3s/2}$. The error sits in the scale constant. $Y-H_n$ has an exponential right tail with scale $1$. Even for $n=1$, $\mathbb{P}\{Y-H_1\ge30\}=e^{-31}\approx3.4\times10^{-14}>e^{-37.5}$, so the tail inequality $\mathbb{P}\{Y-H_n\ge s\}\le e^{-s^2/(2(2+s/3))}$ is false too. (An earlier version of this solution obtained it by plugging $c=1$ into Theorem 2.10 and then writing $s/3$. In Bernstein's inequality the $1/3$ belongs to variables bounded by $b$, where $c=b/3$; it does not apply to exponentials.)

**Corrected statement.** For $t>0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z-\mathbb{E}Z\ge t+\delta_n\}\le\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2U(2n)^2}{2(2+tU(2n))}\right),$$
where $\delta_n>0$ and $\lim_n(2\log(2n))^{3/2}\delta_n=\pi^2/12$. We prove this below.

*Notation and Gaussian facts.* Let $\bar\Phi=1-\Phi$ and $R=\bar\Phi/\phi$. Then $U(t)=\bar\Phi^{-1}(1/t)$ for $t>1$, and $U$ is increasing. We have $R'(u)=uR(u)-1$, because $\phi'=-u\phi$. Integration by parts gives, for $u>0$,
$$\bar\Phi(u)=\frac{\phi(u)}u-\int_u^\infty\frac{\phi}{t^2}\,dt,\qquad \int_u^\infty\frac{\phi}{t^2}\,dt=\frac{\phi(u)}{u^3}-3\int_u^\infty\frac{\phi}{t^4}\,dt,\qquad\int_u^\infty\frac{\phi}{t^4}\,dt=\frac{\phi(u)}{u^5}-5\int_u^\infty\frac{\phi}{t^6}\,dt.$$
Hence, for $u>0$,
$$\frac1u-\frac1{u^3}<R(u)<\frac1u,\qquad R(u)<\frac1u-\frac1{u^3}+\frac3{u^5}.\tag{M}$$
In particular $R'(u)<0$ for all $u\in\mathbb{R}$: for $u\le0$ because $uR\le0$, and for $u>0$ because $uR<1$.

*Step 1: representation.* Let $f(y)=U(2e^y)=\bar\Phi^{-1}(e^{-y}/2)$ for $y\ge0$, and let $Y=\max_iE_i$. For $x\ge0$,
$$f(Y)\le x\iff \tfrac12e^{-Y}\ge\bar\Phi(x)\iff Y\le-\log(2\bar\Phi(x)),$$
so $\mathbb{P}\{f(Y)\le x\}=(1-2\bar\Phi(x))^n=\mathbb{P}\{Z\le x\}$. Both variables are nonnegative, hence $Z\overset d=f(Y)$. By Rényi's representation, $Y\overset d=\sum_{k=1}^nE_k'/k$ with $E_k'$ iid $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$. So $\mathbb{E}Y=H_n=\sum_{k\le n}1/k$, and $W:=Y-H_n$ has variance $v_n=\sum_{k\le n}k^{-2}<\pi^2/6<2$.

*Step 2: derivatives of $f$.* Differentiating $\bar\Phi(f(y))=e^{-y}/2$ gives $\phi(f)f'=e^{-y}/2=\bar\Phi(f)$. Hence
$$f'(y)=R(f(y))>0,\qquad f''(y)=R'(f(y))\,R(f(y))<0.$$
So $f$ is $C^2$, increasing and strictly concave on $[0,\infty)$, with $f(0)=0$. Write $u=f(y)\ge0$. Then $|f''(y)|=(1-uR(u))R(u)$. Since $0<1-uR\le1$ and $R(u)\le R(0)=\sqrt{\pi/2}$, we get $|f''|\le\sqrt{\pi/2}$ everywhere. By (M), for $u>\sqrt3$,
$$u^{-3}\big(1-3u^{-2}\big)\big(1-u^{-2}\big)\le|f''(y)|\le u^{-3},\tag{D}$$
because $1-uR\in(u^{-2}-3u^{-4},\,u^{-2})$ and $R\in(u^{-1}-u^{-3},\,u^{-1})$. By (M), also $f'(y)<1/f(y)$ for $y>0$.

*Growth.* Let $g(x)=\bar\Phi^{-1}(e^{-x})$, so $f(y)=g(y+\log2)$. If $u=g(x)\ge\sqrt2$, then $x=-\log\bar\Phi(u)=\frac{u^2}2+\log\sqrt{2\pi}-\log R(u)$. Also $\frac1{2u}\le\frac1u-\frac1{u^3}<R(u)<\frac1u$ by (M), which gives $\frac{u^2}2<x<\frac{u^2}2+\log(2\sqrt{2\pi})+\log u<\frac{(u+1)^2}2+\frac32$. Hence $\sqrt{2x-3}-1<g(x)<\sqrt{2x}$, so $g(x)/\sqrt{2x}\to1$ as $x\to\infty$.

*Step 3: sub-gamma tails of $W$.* For $0\le\lambda<1$, $-\lambda-\log(1-\lambda)=\sum_{k\ge2}\lambda^k/k\le\lambda^2/(2(1-\lambda))$. Therefore
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda W}=\sum_{k=1}^n\Big(-\frac\lambda k-\log\big(1-\tfrac\lambda k\big)\Big)\le\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{\lambda^2/k^2}{2(1-\lambda/k)}\le\frac{v_n\lambda^2}{2(1-\lambda)}\le\frac{2\lambda^2}{2(1-\lambda)}.$$
For $s>0$, Chernoff's bound with $\lambda=s/(2+s)$ (so that $1-\lambda=2/(2+s)$) gives
$$\mathbb{P}\{W\ge s\}\le\exp\Big(-\lambda s+\frac{\lambda^2}{1-\lambda}\Big)=\exp\Big(-\frac{s^2}{2(2+s)}\Big).\tag{T}$$
For the lower tail, $u-\log(1+u)\le u^2/2$ for $u\ge0$, so $\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda W}\le\lambda^2v_n/2\le\lambda^2$ for $\lambda\ge0$, and $\mathbb{P}\{W\le-s\}\le e^{-s^2/4}$.

*Step 4: the tail bound.* Let $\delta_n:=f(H_n)-\mathbb{E}f(Y)$. It is strictly positive by Jensen's inequality, since $f$ is strictly concave and $Y$ is non-degenerate. By concavity, $f(Y)\le f(H_n)+f'(H_n)W$, so
$$f(Y)-\mathbb{E}f(Y)\le\delta_n+f'(H_n)W.$$
Since $H_n\ge1$, $f(H_n)>0$, and Step 2 gives $1/f'(H_n)>f(H_n)=U(2e^{H_n})\ge U(2n)$; here $H_n>\log n$ and $U$ is increasing. So if $f(Y)-\mathbb{E}f(Y)\ge t+\delta_n$ with $t>0$, then $W\ge t/f'(H_n)\ge tU(2n)$. The map $s\mapsto s^2/(2(2+s))$ is increasing on $[0,\infty)$, so (T) gives
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z-\mathbb{E}Z\ge t+\delta_n\}\le\mathbb{P}\{W\ge tU(2n)\}\le\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2U(2n)^2}{2(2+tU(2n))}\right).$$
(For $n=1$, $U(2)=0$ and the bound is trivial.)

*Step 5: the rate of $\delta_n$.* Taylor's formula with integral remainder, between $H_n>0$ and $Y>0$, gives $f(Y)=f(H_n)+f'(H_n)W-W^2K_n$ with
$$K_n=\int_0^1(1-\theta)\,|f''(H_n+\theta W)|\,d\theta\in\big[0,\tfrac12\sqrt{\pi/2}\big].$$
All terms are integrable, since $W$ has exponential moments. Because $\mathbb{E}W=0$, we get $\delta_n=\mathbb{E}[W^2K_n]$.

Let $\ell_n=f(H_n)$, $a_n=\sqrt{\log n}$ and $A_n=\{|W|\le a_n\}$. Put $m_n=f(H_n-a_n)$ and $M_n=f(H_n+a_n)$, which are defined once $H_n>a_n$. Since $H_n-\log n\in(0,1]$, we have $(H_n\pm a_n+\log2)/(H_n+\log2)\to1$. The growth estimate then gives $m_n/\ell_n\to1$ and $M_n/\ell_n\to1$, and $m_n\to\infty$. On $A_n$, every point $y=H_n+\theta W$ satisfies $m_n\le f(y)\le M_n$, because $f$ is increasing. For $n$ large enough that $m_n>\sqrt3$, (D) gives
$$\alpha_n:=\Big(\frac{\ell_n}{M_n}\Big)^3\big(1-3m_n^{-2}\big)\big(1-m_n^{-2}\big)\le\ell_n^3|f''(y)|\le\Big(\frac{\ell_n}{m_n}\Big)^3=:\beta_n,$$
with $\alpha_n,\beta_n\to1$. Hence $\frac{\alpha_n}2\mathbb{E}[W^2;A_n]\le\ell_n^3\,\mathbb{E}[W^2K_n;A_n]\le\frac{\beta_n}2\mathbb{E}[W^2;A_n]$.

For $s\ge2$ we have $s^2/(2(2+s))\ge s/4$, so Step 3 gives $\mathbb{P}\{|W|>s\}\le2e^{-s/4}$. For $a\ge2$, therefore,
$$\mathbb{E}[W^2;|W|>a]=a^2\mathbb{P}\{|W|>a\}+\int_a^\infty2s\,\mathbb{P}\{|W|>s\}\,ds\le\big(2a^2+16a+64\big)e^{-a/4}.$$
The growth estimate gives $\ell_n^3=O((\log n)^{3/2})$, and $(2\log n+16\sqrt{\log n}+64)\,e^{-\sqrt{\log n}/4}(\log n)^{3/2}\to0$. Hence $\ell_n^3\,\mathbb{E}[W^2K_n;A_n^c]\le\frac12\sqrt{\pi/2}\,\ell_n^3\,\mathbb{E}[W^2;A_n^c]\to0$. Also $\mathbb{E}[W^2;A_n]=v_n-\mathbb{E}[W^2;A_n^c]\to\pi^2/6$. Therefore
$$\ell_n^3\,\delta_n\to\frac12\cdot\frac{\pi^2}6=\frac{\pi^2}{12}.$$
Finally, $\ell_n=g(H_n+\log2)$ and $H_n+\log2=\log(2n)+O(1)$, so the growth estimate gives $\ell_n/\sqrt{2\log(2n)}\to1$. Hence $\lim_n(2\log(2n))^{3/2}\delta_n=\pi^2/12$. ∎

*Numerical check of the rate.* The value of $(2\log2n)^{3/2}\delta_n$ is $0.852,\ 0.853,\ 0.845,\ 0.833,\ 0.828$ for $n=10^4,10^8,10^{20},10^{75},10^{200}$. It converges slowly to $\pi^2/12\approx0.8225$ (computed with the same 60-digit quadrature).
