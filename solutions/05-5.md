**Central idea.** This is the entropy-method (Herbst) proof of Theorem 5.3, but the full-square hypothesis lets us avoid the positive part. The key is the elementary inequality $(e^{z/2}-e^{y/2})^2 \le \frac{(z-y)^2}{8}(e^z+e^y)$ (proved via Cauchy–Schwarz on $\int_y^z \tfrac12 e^{s/2}\,ds$ plus $\tanh(u/2) \le u/2$), applied to $z = \lambda f(x)$, $y = \lambda f(x^{(i)})$ inside the discrete log-Sobolev inequality; the $e^{\lambda f(x)} + e^{\lambda f(x^{(i)})}$ symmetrizes to $2e^{\lambda f(x)}$ since $X^{(i)} \stackrel{d}{=} X$, closing the Herbst differential inequality $\frac{d}{d\lambda}(H(\lambda)/\lambda) \le v/8$.

**Solution.** Write $f_i(x) = f(x^{(i)})$ for the $i$-th coordinate flip, and $Z = f(X)$ with $X$ uniform on $\{-1,1\}^n$.

*Step 1: the elementary inequality.* Both sides of
$$(e^{z/2}-e^{y/2})^2 \le \frac{(z-y)^2}{8}(e^z+e^y) \tag{1}$$
are symmetric in $z,y$, so assume $z \ge y$. By the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy–Schwarz,
$$(e^{z/2}-e^{y/2})^2 = \left(\int_y^z \tfrac12 e^{s/2}\,ds\right)^2 \le (z-y)\int_y^z \tfrac14 e^s\,ds = \frac{z-y}{4}(e^z-e^y).$$
It remains to show $\frac{z-y}{4}(e^z-e^y) \le \frac{(z-y)^2}{8}(e^z+e^y)$. For $z = y$ both sides vanish; for $z > y$, dividing by $\frac{z-y}{4}$ and setting $u = z-y > 0$ gives the equivalent $\frac{e^u-1}{e^u+1} \le \frac{u}{2}$, i.e. $\tanh(u/2) \le u/2$. This holds since $\tanh 0 = 0$ and $\frac{d}{dv}\tanh v = \mathrm{sech}^2 v \le 1$ for $v \ge 0$. This proves (1).

*Step 2: the entropy bound.* Apply the discrete log-Sobolev inequality (Theorem 5.1) to $g = e^{\lambda f/2}$, $\lambda > 0$:
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda f(X)}) \le \tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}\left[\left(e^{\lambda f(X)/2}-e^{\lambda f_i(X)/2}\right)^2\right].$$
By (1) with $z = \lambda f(x)$, $y = \lambda f_i(x)$,
$$\left(e^{\lambda f(x)/2}-e^{\lambda f_i(x)/2}\right)^2 \le \frac{\lambda^2}{8}(f(x)-f_i(x))^2\left(e^{\lambda f(x)}+e^{\lambda f_i(x)}\right).$$
Now $X^{(i)} \stackrel{d}{=} X$ (a coordinate flip preserves the uniform law), and $(f(x)-f_i(x))^2$ is symmetric under the flip, so
$$\mathbb{E}\left[(f(X)-f_i(X))^2 e^{\lambda f_i(X)}\right] = \mathbb{E}\left[(f(X)-f_i(X))^2 e^{\lambda f(X)}\right].$$
Hence
$$\mathbb{E}\left[\left(e^{\lambda f(X)/2}-e^{\lambda f_i(X)/2}\right)^2\right] \le \frac{\lambda^2}{4}\,\mathbb{E}\left[(f(X)-f_i(X))^2 e^{\lambda f(X)}\right],$$
and summing over $i$,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda f(X)}) \le \frac{\lambda^2}{8}\,\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda f(X)}\sum_{i=1}^n (f(X)-f_i(X))^2\right] \le \frac{\lambda^2 v}{8}\,\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda f(X)}], \tag{2}$$
using the hypothesis $\sum_i (f(x)-f_i(x))^2 \le v$ for all $x$. This is exactly the hint's chain.

*Step 3: Herbst's argument.* Let $M(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f(X)}$, $K(\lambda) = \log M(\lambda)$, and $H(\lambda) = K(\lambda) - \lambda\mathbb{E}Z = \log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}$. Then
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda f(X)}) = \mathbb{E}[\lambda f e^{\lambda f}] - M(\lambda)K(\lambda) = M(\lambda)\big(\lambda K'(\lambda)-K(\lambda)\big).$$
With (2), $\lambda K'(\lambda) - K(\lambda) \le \lambda^2 v/8$. Since $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{H(\lambda)}{\lambda} = \frac{\lambda K'(\lambda)-K(\lambda)}{\lambda^2}$ (the $\mathbb{E}Z$ terms cancel),
$$\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{H(\lambda)}{\lambda} \le \frac{v}{8}, \qquad \lambda > 0.$$
Integrating from $\varepsilon$ to $\lambda$ and letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ (using $H(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon \to H'(0) = 0$) gives $\frac{H(\lambda)}{\lambda} \le \frac{v\lambda}{8}$, i.e.
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)} \le \frac{v\lambda^2}{8}, \qquad \lambda > 0. \tag{3}$$

*Step 4: Chernoff.* For $t > 0$ and any $\lambda > 0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z > \mathbb{E}Z + t\} \le e^{-\lambda t}\,e^{H(\lambda)} \le \exp\!\left(-\lambda t + \tfrac{v\lambda^2}{8}\right).$$
Optimizing at $\lambda = 4t/v$ yields $\exp(-2t^2/v)$, as claimed. ∎
