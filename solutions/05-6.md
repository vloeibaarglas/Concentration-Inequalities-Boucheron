**Central idea.** Apply the asymmetric log-Sobolev inequality (Theorem 5.2) to $e^{\lambda f/2}$ and use $(e^u-e^v)^2\le (u-v)_+^2e^{2u}+(u-v)_-^2e^{2v}$. For nondecreasing $f$, conditioning on $X^{(i)}$ shows the two resulting terms combine to $(f(X^{(i)},1)-f(X^{(i)},-1))^2e^{\lambda f(X^{(i)},1)}$; rewriting via $\mathbf{1}_{\{X_i=1\}}$ and using the hypothesis gives a factor $(1-p)$ after the $p(1-p)$ from the Dirichlet form cancels. Herbst's argument then yields the tail bound.

**Solution.** Theorem 5.2 states $\mathrm{Ent}_\mu(g^2)\le c(p)\mathcal{E}(g)$ with $\mathcal{E}(g)=p(1-p)\mathbb{E}[\sum_{i=1}^n(g(X)-g(\bar X^{(i)}))^2]$ and $c(p)=\frac{1}{1-2p}\log\frac{1-p}{p}$. Take $g=e^{\lambda f/2}$ ($\lambda>0$), so $\mathrm{Ent}_\mu(e^{\lambda f})\le c(p)p(1-p)\mathbb{E}[\sum_i(e^{\lambda f(X)/2}-e^{\lambda f(\bar X^{(i)})/2})^2]$.

For $u,v\in\mathbb{R}$, $(e^u-e^v)^2\le (u-v)_+^2e^{2u}+(u-v)_-^2e^{2v}$ (if $u\ge v$, $(e^u-e^v)^2=e^{2v}(e^{u-v}-1)^2\le (u-v)^2e^{2u}$ since $e^w-1\le we^w$ for $w\ge0$; symmetrically if $u<v$). With $u=\lambda f(X)/2$, $v=\lambda f(\bar X^{(i)})/2$,
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_i(e^{\lambda f/2}-e^{\lambda f^{(i)}/2})^2\Big]\le \frac{\lambda^2}{4}\sum_i\mathbb{E}\big[(f-f^{(i)})_+^2e^{\lambda f}+(f-f^{(i)})_-^2e^{\lambda f^{(i)}}\big],$$
writing $f^{(i)}=f(\bar X^{(i)})$.

Let $f$ be nondecreasing. Condition on $X^{(i)}$ and set $a=f(X^{(i)},1)\ge b=f(X^{(i)},-1)$. Then $\mathbb{E}[(f-f^{(i)})_+^2e^{\lambda f}\mid X^{(i)}]=p(a-b)^2e^{\lambda a}$ (only $X_i=1$ contributes) and $\mathbb{E}[(f-f^{(i)})_-^2e^{\lambda f^{(i)}}\mid X^{(i)}]=(1-p)(a-b)^2e^{\lambda a}$ (only $X_i=-1$ contributes, where $(f-f^{(i)})_-=a-b$ and $f^{(i)}=a$). Hence
$$\sum_i\mathbb{E}\big[(e^{\lambda f/2}-e^{\lambda f^{(i)}/2})^2\big]\le\frac{\lambda^2}{4}\sum_i\mathbb{E}\big[(f(X^{(i)},1)-f(X^{(i)},-1))^2e^{\lambda f(X^{(i)},1)}\big].$$
Since $\mathbb{E}_{X^{(i)}}[h]=\frac1p\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{\{X_i=1\}}h]$ and on $\{X_i=1\}$, $(f(X^{(i)},1)-f(X^{(i)},-1))^2=(f(X)-f(\bar X^{(i)}))^2=(f(X)-f(\bar X^{(i)}))_+^2$ (as $f$ is nondecreasing),
$$\sum_i\mathbb{E}[\cdots]=\frac1p\,\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\lambda f(X)}\sum_i(f(X)-f(\bar X^{(i)}))_+^2\Big]\le\frac{v}{p}\,\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f}$$
by the hypothesis. Therefore
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda f})\le c(p)p(1-p)\cdot\frac{\lambda^2}{4}\cdot\frac{v}{p}\,\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f}=\frac{(1-p)c(p)v\lambda^2}{4}\,\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f}.$$
Herbst's argument: with $H(\lambda)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(f-\mathbb{E}f)}$, $\lambda H'-H=\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda(f-\mathbb{E}f)})/\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(f-\mathbb{E}f)}\le\frac{(1-p)c(p)v\lambda^2}{4}$, so $(H/\lambda)'\le\frac{(1-p)c(p)v}{4}$ and $H(\lambda)\le\frac{(1-p)c(p)v\lambda^2}{4}$. Chernoff's bound gives $\mathbb{P}\{f-\mathbb{E}f>t\}\le\exp(-\lambda t+\frac{(1-p)c(p)v\lambda^2}{4})$; optimizing $\lambda=\frac{2t}{(1-p)c(p)v}$ yields $\exp(\frac{-t^2}{(1-p)c(p)v})$.

If $f$ is nonincreasing, then with $a=f(X^{(i)},1)\le b=f(X^{(i)},-1)$ the same computation gives $\mathbb{E}[(f-f^{(i)})_+^2e^{\lambda f}+(f-f^{(i)})_-^2e^{\lambda f^{(i)}}\mid X^{(i)}]=(b-a)^2e^{\lambda b}$, and $\mathbb{E}_{X^{(i)}}[h]=\frac1{1-p}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{\{X_i=-1\}}h]$ with $(f(X)-f(\bar X^{(i)}))^2=(f(X)-f(\bar X^{(i)}))_+^2$ on $\{X_i=-1\}$. Hence $\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda f})\le\frac{pc(p)v\lambda^2}{4}\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f}$, and Herbst gives $\exp(\frac{-t^2}{pc(p)v})$. ∎
