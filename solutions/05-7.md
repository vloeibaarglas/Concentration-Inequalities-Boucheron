**Central idea.** Adapt the proof of Lemma 5.19 (rather than its statement): for $s\le t$, write $Z_t=e^{-(t-s)}Z_s+(1-e^{-(t-s)})\alpha_0$ and dominate $\|Z_t\|$ by the scalar $Y_t=e^{-(t-s)}\|Z_s\|+(1-e^{-(t-s)})\mathbb{E}\|Z_s\|\ge0$, which satisfies $dY_t/dt=\mathbb{E}Y_t-Y_t$. Since $Y_t$ is a scalar function of the Rademacher $X$, the differentiation/entropy argument in Lemma 5.19's proof applies verbatim, giving monotonicity of $\frac1{q(t)}\log\mathbb{E}Y_t^{q(t)}$, hence of $\frac1{q(t)}\log\mathbb{E}\|Z_t\|^{q(t)}$.

**Solution.** Fix $0\le s<t$ and write $Z_u=\alpha_0+e^{-u}\alpha_1X$ for $u\ge s$. Then $Z_t=e^{-(t-s)}Z_s+(1-e^{-(t-s)})\alpha_0$, since $\mathbb{E}Z_s=\alpha_0$. By the triangle inequality and Jensen ($\|\mathbb{E}Z_s\|\le\mathbb{E}\|Z_s\|$),
$$\|Z_t\|\le e^{-(t-s)}\|Z_s\|+(1-e^{-(t-s)})\|\alpha_0\|\le e^{-(t-s)}\|Z_s\|+(1-e^{-(t-s)})\mathbb{E}\|Z_s\|=:Y_t.$$
Put $A=\|Z_s\|\ge0$ (a scalar function of $X$) and $b=\mathbb{E}A$, so $Y_u=e^{-(u-s)}A+(1-e^{-(u-s)})b$ for $u\ge s$. Then $Y_u\ge0$, $Y_s=A=\|Z_s\|$, $\|Z_t\|\le Y_t$, and differentiating,
$$\frac{dY_u}{du}=-e^{-(u-s)}A+e^{-(u-s)}b=e^{-(u-s)}(b-A)=\mathbb{E}Y_u-Y_u,$$
since $\mathbb{E}Y_u=b$. Thus $Y_u$ is a scalar nonnegative function of the Rademacher variable $X$ satisfying the same differential relation as in Lemma 5.19's proof. The differentiation there gives, with $q=q(u)$,
$$\frac{d}{du}\Big(\frac1{q}\log\mathbb{E}Y_u^{q}\Big)=\frac{q'}{q^2\mathbb{E}Y_u^{q}}\Big(\mathrm{Ent}(Y_u^{q})+\frac{q^2}{q'}\mathbb{E}\big[Y_u^{q-1}(\mathbb{E}Y_u-Y_u)\big]\Big),$$
and the log-Sobolev estimate in that proof (which uses only that $Y_u\ge0$ is a function of $X$ and $q(u)=\beta e^{2u}+1$) shows the right-hand side is $\le0$. Hence $\frac1{q(u)}\log\mathbb{E}Y_u^{q(u)}$ is nonincreasing for $u\ge s$. Consequently,
$$\frac1{q(t)}\log\mathbb{E}\|Z_t\|^{q(t)}\le\frac1{q(t)}\log\mathbb{E}Y_t^{q(t)}\le\frac1{q(s)}\log\mathbb{E}Y_s^{q(s)}=\frac1{q(s)}\log\mathbb{E}\|Z_s\|^{q(s)},$$
i.e.
$$\big(\mathbb{E}\|Z_t\|^{q(t)}\big)^{1/q(t)}\le\big(\mathbb{E}\|Z_s\|^{q(s)}\big)^{1/q(s)}.$$
∎

*Remark on the hint.* The hint's identities are misstated: in fact $\alpha_0+e^{-t}\alpha_1=v(1+e^{-t})/2+w(1-e^{-t})/2$ and $\alpha_0-e^{-t}\alpha_1=v(1-e^{-t})/2+w(1+e^{-t})/2$ (the hint has $e^{t}$ where $e^{-t}$ belongs), $|Z_t|$ should be $\|Z_t\|$, and $\beta_1$ should be $(\|v\|-\|w\|)/2$. Rather than repair the hint, the proof above adapts the argument of Lemma 5.19 directly to the dominating scalar process $Y_t$.
