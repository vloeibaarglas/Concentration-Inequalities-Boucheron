**Central idea.** Expand $S^4=(\sum_ib_iX_i)^4$: only even-multiplicity index patterns survive, giving $\mathbb{E}S^4=3(\mathbb{E}S^2)^2-2\sum_ib_i^4\le3(\mathbb{E}S^2)^2$. Hölder with exponents $3/2,3$ on $Z^2=Z^{2/3}Z^{4/3}$ yields $\mathbb{E}Z^2\le(\mathbb{E}Z)^{2/3}(\mathbb{E}Z^4)^{1/3}$; combining and cancelling $(\mathbb{E}Z^2)^{2/3}$ gives $\mathbb{E}Z^2\le3(\mathbb{E}Z)^2$. The constant $3$ in the fourth-moment bound is best possible (attained asymptotically by $b_i\equiv1$), though Szarek's inequality improves the final constant to $2$.

**Solution.** Write $S=\sum_{i=1}^nb_iX_i$, so $Z=|S|$, $Z^2=S^2$, $Z^4=S^4$. Since $\mathbb{E}[X_iX_j]=\delta_{ij}$, $\mathbb{E}S^2=\sum_ib_i^2$.

Expanding $S^4=\sum_{i,j,k,l}b_ib_jb_kb_lX_iX_jX_kX_l$, $\mathbb{E}[X_iX_jX_kX_l]\ne0$ only when each index has even multiplicity: (i) $i=j=k=l$, contributing $\sum_ib_i^4$; (ii) two distinct pairs, e.g.\ $i=j\ne k=l$, of which there are $3$ pairings, contributing $3\sum_{i\ne k}b_i^2b_k^2$. Hence
$$\mathbb{E}S^4=\sum_ib_i^4+3\sum_{i\ne k}b_i^2b_k^2=3\Big(\sum_ib_i^2\Big)^2-2\sum_ib_i^4\le3(\mathbb{E}S^2)^2,$$
i.e.\ $\mathbb{E}[Z^4]\le3(\mathbb{E}[Z^2])^2$.

By Hölder with $p=3/2$, $q=3$ applied to $Z^2=Z^{2/3}\cdot Z^{4/3}$,
$$\mathbb{E}[Z^2]\le\big(\mathbb{E}[(Z^{2/3})^{3/2}]\big)^{2/3}\big(\mathbb{E}[(Z^{4/3})^3]\big)^{1/3}=(\mathbb{E}Z)^{2/3}(\mathbb{E}[Z^4])^{1/3}.$$
Combining,
$$\mathbb{E}[Z^2]\le(\mathbb{E}Z)^{2/3}\big(3(\mathbb{E}[Z^2])^2\big)^{1/3}=3^{1/3}(\mathbb{E}Z)^{2/3}(\mathbb{E}[Z^2])^{2/3}.$$
If $\mathbb{E}[Z^2]=0$ there is nothing to prove; otherwise dividing by $(\mathbb{E}[Z^2])^{2/3}$ and cubing gives $\mathbb{E}[Z^2]\le3(\mathbb{E}Z)^2$.

The constant $3$ in $\mathbb{E}[Z^4]\le3(\mathbb{E}[Z^2])^2$ cannot be improved: with $b_1=\cdots=b_n=1$, $\mathbb{E}S^4/(\mathbb{E}S^2)^2=3-2/n\to3$. However, the resulting $(\mathbb{E}Z^2)^{1/2}\le\sqrt3\,\mathbb{E}Z$ is not sharp: Szarek's inequality (Theorem 5.20) gives the optimal $(\mathbb{E}Z^2)^{1/2}\le\sqrt2\,\mathbb{E}Z$. ∎
