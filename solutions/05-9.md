**Central idea.** The first inequality is Theorem 5.20 (Szarek's sharp Rademacher lower bound $\mathbb{E}_X|\sum a_iX_i| \ge \|a\|_2/\sqrt{2}$) applied conditionally on $Y$. The second inequality is false as stated --- $Y_1\equiv1$, $Y_2\equiv-1$ gives $\sqrt{2}\le 0$ --- and needs $\mathbb{E}Y_i=0$. Under centering, symmetrization does it directly: Jensen over an independent copy $Y'$ gives $\mathbb{E}|\sum X_iY_i|\le\mathbb{E}|\sum X_i(Y_i-Y_i')|$; since each $Y_i-Y_i'$ is symmetric, the signs $X_i$ can be removed, and the triangle inequality gives $\mathbb{E}|\sum X_iY_i|\le 2\,\mathbb{E}|\sum Y_i|$, which is the claim after multiplying by $\sqrt2$.

**Solution.** Assume the $X_i$ are independent of the $Y_i$. Write $\mathbb{E}_X$ for expectation over $X$ only.

*First inequality.* Condition on $Y=(Y_1,\dots,Y_n)$. Theorem 5.20 (Szarek: $\sqrt{\mathbb{E}Z^2}\le\sqrt2\,\mathbb{E}Z$ for $Z=|\sum_i b_iX_i|$) applied to the Rademacher sum with frozen coefficients $b_i=Y_i$, together with $\mathbb{E}_X(\sum_iX_iY_i)^2=\sum_iY_i^2$, gives
$$\mathbb{E}_X\Big[\Big|\sum_{i=1}^n X_iY_i\Big|\Big]\ge \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i^2\Big)^{1/2}\quad\text{a.s.}$$
Taking $\mathbb{E}_Y$ yields $\mathbb{E}|\sum_iX_iY_i|\ge \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{E}[(\sum_iY_i^2)^{1/2}]$, i.e.
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i^2\Big)^{1/2}\Big]\le \sqrt{2}\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{i=1}^n X_iY_i\Big|\Big].$$

*Remark on the second inequality.* As stated it is false without centering: for $Y_1\equiv1$, $Y_2\equiv-1$ (independent, finite variance), $\sqrt{2}\,\mathbb{E}|X_1-X_2|=\sqrt{2}$ (as $|X_1-X_2|$ equals $0$ or $2$ with probability $1/2$ each) while $2\sqrt{2}\,\mathbb{E}|Y_1+Y_2|=0$. Assume henceforth $\mathbb{E}Y_i=0$ for all $i$; we prove the second inequality in this corrected form.

*Second inequality (centered case).* All expectations below are finite since the $Y_i$ have finite variance. Let $Y_1',\dots,Y_n'$ be independent copies of $Y_1,\dots,Y_n$, independent of everything else, and put $Z_i = Y_i-Y_i'$.

(a) *Jensen.* By centering, $\sum_iX_iY_i=\mathbb{E}_{Y'}\big[\sum_iX_i(Y_i-Y_i')\big]$ (conditionally on $X,Y$). Jensen's inequality in $Y'$ gives $|\sum_iX_iY_i|\le\mathbb{E}_{Y'}|\sum_iX_iZ_i|$, and taking $\mathbb{E}_{X,Y}$,
$$\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_iX_iY_i\Big|\le\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_iX_iZ_i\Big|.$$

(b) *Removing the signs.* Each $Z_i$ is symmetric ($Y_i-Y_i'\stackrel{d}{=}Y_i'-Y_i$) and independent of $X_i$, so $X_iZ_i\stackrel{d}{=}Z_i$: conditionally on $X_i=\pm1$, $X_iZ_i$ is $\pm Z_i$, which has the law of $Z_i$. The pairs $(X_i,Z_i)$, $i=1,\dots,n$, are independent, hence the vector $(X_1Z_1,\dots,X_nZ_n)$ has the same law as $(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)$, and
$$\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_iX_iZ_i\Big| = \mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_iZ_i\Big| = \mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_iY_i-\sum_iY_i'\Big|\le\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_iY_i\Big|+\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_iY_i'\Big| = 2\,\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_iY_i\Big|,$$
by the triangle inequality and $Y'\stackrel{d}{=}Y$.

Combining (a) and (b), $\mathbb{E}|\sum_iX_iY_i|\le 2\,\mathbb{E}|\sum_iY_i|$, and multiplying by $\sqrt2$,
$$\sqrt2\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{i=1}^nX_iY_i\Big|\Big]\le 2\sqrt{2}\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\Big|\Big]\qquad(\mathbb{E}Y_i=0).$$
Together with the first inequality this proves Marcinkiewicz's inequalities for centred $Y_i$. ∎
