**Central idea.** Tensorize entropy and prove a sharp one-variable inequality with an independent copy: for i.i.d. bounded $Z,Z'$, $\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})\le\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}(Z-Z')_+^2]$. Its proof is by exponential tilting: with $\psi(s)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{sZ}$ and tilted laws $P_s$, the left side divided by $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}$ is $\int_0^\lambda s\,\mathrm{Var}_{P_s}(Z)\,ds=\int_0^\lambda s\,\mathbb{E}_{P_s\otimes P_s}(Z-Z')_+^2\,ds$, the right side is $\int_0^\lambda s\,ds\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P_\lambda\otimes P_0}(Z-Z')_+^2$, and the stochastic ordering $P_0\le_{\rm st}P_s\le_{\rm st}P_\lambda$ compares the integrands. Summing over coordinates gives $\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})\le\frac{\lambda^2v}{2}\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}$, and Herbst's argument yields the stated $e^{-t^2/(2v)}$.

**Solution.** As in Theorem 6.7, $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ are independent. Write $Z=f(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$, $Z'_i=f(X_1,\ldots,X_{i-1},X'_i,X_{i+1},\ldots,X_n)$ with $X'_i$ an independent copy of $X_i$, and $V_+=\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{i=1}^n(Z-Z'_i)_+^2\,\big|\,X\big]$. Assume $V_+\le v$ a.s. ($Z$ is integrable, since $\mathbb{E}Z$ appears in the statement).

*Reduction to bounded $Z$.* For $K>0$ let $\tau_K(z)=(z\wedge K)\vee(-K)$ and $Z_K=\tau_K(Z)$. Since $\tau_K$ is nondecreasing and $1$-Lipschitz, $(\tau_K(a)-\tau_K(b))_+\le(a-b)_+$, so $Z_K=(\tau_K\circ f)(X)$ satisfies the hypothesis with the same $v$. Suppose we have shown, for bounded $Z$, that
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}\le\frac{\lambda^2v}{2},\qquad\lambda\ge0.\tag{1}$$
Apply (1) to $Z_K$ and let $K\to\infty$: $Z_K\to Z$ pointwise and $\mathbb{E}Z_K\to\mathbb{E}Z$ by dominated convergence ($|Z_K|\le|Z|$), so by Fatou's lemma $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}\le\liminf_K\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z_K-\mathbb{E}Z_K)}\le e^{\lambda^2v/2}$. Hence it suffices to prove (1) for bounded $Z$, which we assume from now on.

*Lemma.* If $Z,Z'$ are i.i.d. bounded random variables, then for every $\lambda\ge0$,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})\le\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda Z}(Z-Z')_+^2\big].$$

*Proof of the lemma.* Let $P$ be the law of $Z$, $\psi(s)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{sZ}$ (finite and smooth since $Z$ is bounded), $M(s)=e^{\psi(s)}$, and let $P_s$ be the tilted law $dP_s(z)=e^{sz-\psi(s)}\,dP(z)$, so $P_0=P$. Then $\psi'(s)=\mathbb{E}_{P_s}Z$ and $\psi''(s)=\mathrm{Var}_{P_s}(Z)$. If $Z,Z'$ are i.i.d. with law $P_s$, then $\mathrm{Var}_{P_s}(Z)=\frac12\mathbb{E}_{P_s\otimes P_s}(Z-Z')^2=\mathbb{E}_{P_s\otimes P_s}(Z-Z')_+^2$, because $(Z-Z')^2=(Z-Z')_+^2+(Z'-Z)_+^2$ and the two terms have equal expectations by exchangeability.

Left side: $\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})=\mathbb{E}[\lambda Ze^{\lambda Z}]-M(\lambda)\log M(\lambda)=M(\lambda)\big(\lambda\psi'(\lambda)-\psi(\lambda)\big)$. Since $\frac{d}{ds}\big(s\psi'(s)-\psi(s)\big)=s\psi''(s)$ and the bracket vanishes at $s=0$,
$$\frac{\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})}{M(\lambda)}=\int_0^\lambda s\,\psi''(s)\,ds=\int_0^\lambda s\,\mathbb{E}_{P_s\otimes P_s}(Z-Z')_+^2\,ds.$$
Right side: for $Z,Z'$ independent with law $P$, $\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}(Z-Z')_+^2]/M(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}_{P_\lambda\otimes P_0}(Z-Z')_+^2$, so
$$\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\cdot\frac{\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}(Z-Z')_+^2]}{M(\lambda)}=\int_0^\lambda s\,ds\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P_\lambda\otimes P_0}(Z-Z')_+^2.$$
Comparison: for $0\le s\le\lambda$ the likelihood ratio $\ell=dP_\lambda/dP_s\propto e^{(\lambda-s)z}$ is nondecreasing in $z$ with $\mathbb{E}_{P_s}\ell=1$. For any $c$, $\mathbb{E}_{P_s}[(\ell(Z)-1)\mathbf{1}_{\{Z>c\}}]\ge0$, since $\ell-1$ is nondecreasing with $P_s$-mean $0$ (if $\ell(c)\ge1$ the integrand is $\ge0$; otherwise the integral equals $-\mathbb{E}_{P_s}[(\ell-1)\mathbf{1}_{\{Z\le c\}}]\ge0$). Thus $P_\lambda\{Z>c\}\ge P_s\{Z>c\}$ for all $c$, i.e. $P_\lambda\ge_{\rm st}P_s$; likewise $P_s\ge_{\rm st}P_0$. The function $h(z,z')=(z-z')_+^2$ is nondecreasing in $z$ and nonincreasing in $z'$. For a product measure, replacing the first marginal by a stochastically larger one does not decrease $\mathbb{E}h$ (apply the stochastic order to the nondecreasing function $z\mapsto\mathbb{E}h(z,Z')$), and replacing the second marginal by a stochastically smaller one does not decrease it either. Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}_{P_s\otimes P_s}h\le\mathbb{E}_{P_\lambda\otimes P_s}h\le\mathbb{E}_{P_\lambda\otimes P_0}h,\qquad0\le s\le\lambda.$$
Integrating against $s\,ds$ over $[0,\lambda]$ and multiplying by $M(\lambda)$ proves the lemma. $\square$

(The constant $\frac12$ is sharp: as $\lambda\to0$ both sides equal $\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\mathrm{Var}(Z)+O(\lambda^3)$.)

*Tensorization.* By the sub-additivity of entropy (Theorem 4.10), $\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})\le\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Ent}^{(i)}(e^{\lambda Z})\big]$, where $\mathrm{Ent}^{(i)}$ is the entropy with respect to $X_i$ with $X^{(i)}=(X_1,\ldots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\ldots,X_n)$ fixed. Conditionally on $X^{(i)}$, $Z=g(X_i)$ and $Z'_i=g(X'_i)$ with $g=f(X_1,\ldots,X_{i-1},\cdot,X_{i+1},\ldots,X_n)$, so $Z$ and $Z'_i$ are conditionally i.i.d. and bounded, and the lemma gives $\mathrm{Ent}^{(i)}(e^{\lambda Z})\le\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda Z}(Z-Z'_i)_+^2\,\big|\,X^{(i)}\big]$. Summing, and conditioning on $X$ (with respect to which $e^{\lambda Z}$ is measurable),
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})\le\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda Z}(Z-Z'_i)_+^2\big]=\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda Z}V_+\big]\le\frac{\lambda^2v}{2}\,\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z},\qquad\lambda\ge0.$$

*Herbst's argument.* Let $F(\lambda)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}$. For $\lambda>0$,
$$\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Big(\frac{F(\lambda)}{\lambda}\Big)=\frac{\lambda F'(\lambda)-F(\lambda)}{\lambda^2}=\frac{\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})}{\lambda^2\,\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}}\le\frac v2,$$
and $F(\lambda)/\lambda\to F'(0)=0$ as $\lambda\downarrow0$. Integrating gives $F(\lambda)\le\lambda^2v/2$, which is (1). By Markov's inequality, for $t>0$ and $\lambda>0$, $\mathbb{P}\{Z>\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\le e^{\lambda^2v/2-\lambda t}$; for $v>0$ take $\lambda=t/v$, and for $v=0$ let $\lambda\to\infty$. Hence
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z>\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\le e^{-t^2/(2v)},\qquad t>0$$
(read as $0$ when $v=0$).

*Lower tail.* Apply the upper-tail result to $-Z=(-f)(X)$. Since $\big((-Z)-(-Z'_i)\big)_+=(Z'_i-Z)_+=(Z-Z'_i)_-$, the hypothesis $\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_i(Z-Z'_i)_-^2\,\big|\,X\big]\le v$ is exactly the upper-tail hypothesis for $-Z$, whence
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z<\mathbb{E}Z-t\}=\mathbb{P}\{-Z>\mathbb{E}(-Z)+t\}\le e^{-t^2/(2v)},\qquad t>0.\ \blacksquare$$
