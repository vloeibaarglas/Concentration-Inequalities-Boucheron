**Central idea.** A Poisson$(\mu)$ law is the limit of Binomial$(n,\mu/n)$ (the law of rare events). Apply the tensorized Bernoulli modified log-Sobolev inequality (Lemma 6.18) to $f(S_n)$ where $S_n$ is a sum of $n$ Bernoulli$(\mu/n)$ variables: flipping one coordinate changes the sum by exactly $\pm 1$, so the Bernoulli gradient $\nabla_i$ becomes the discrete derivative $D$, and passing to the limit $n\to\infty$ yields Theorem 6.17.

**Solution.** Let $X$ be Poisson$(\mu)$, $\mu=\mathbb{E}X>0$, and let $f:\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)$. We first prove the inequalities for $f$ bounded with $f\ge c>0$ and extend by truncation at the end.

For $n>\mu$ let $X_{n,1},\dots,X_{n,n}$ be i.i.d. Bernoulli$(\mu/n)$, $S_n=\sum_{i=1}^n X_{n,i}$ and $T_i=S_n-X_{n,i}=\sum_{j\ne i}X_{n,j}$. Put $g_n(x_1,\dots,x_n)=f(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i)$ on $\{0,1\}^n$, so $g_n(X_{n,1},\dots,X_{n,n})=f(S_n)$. By tensorization of entropy,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(f(S_n))=\mathrm{Ent}(g_n)\le\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Ent}_i(g_n)],$$
where $\mathrm{Ent}_i$ is the entropy with respect to $X_{n,i}$ conditionally on $(X_{n,j})_{j\ne i}$.

Fix $i$ and condition on $(X_{n,j})_{j\ne i}$, so $T_i$ is fixed. As a function of $x_i\in\{0,1\}$, $g_n$ takes the values $f(T_i)$ at $0$ and $f(T_i+1)$ at $1$. With $\nabla_i$ denoting the coordinate flip as in Lemma 6.18, if $x_i=0$ then $\nabla_i g_n=f(T_i+1)-f(T_i)=Df(T_i)$, and if $x_i=1$ then $\nabla_i g_n=f(T_i)-f(T_i+1)=-Df(T_i)$; the same holds for $\log g_n$ with $D\log f$. Hence in both cases
$$\nabla_i g_n\cdot \nabla_i\log g_n = Df(T_i)\,D\log f(T_i),$$
the two factors always having the same sign. Applying the first inequality of Lemma 6.18 with $p=\mu/n$ gives
$$\mathrm{Ent}_i(g_n)\le \frac{\mu}{n}\Bigl(1-\frac{\mu}{n}\Bigr)\,\mathbb{E}_i\bigl[Df(T_i)\,D\log f(T_i)\bigr].$$
Summing over $i$ and using that the $T_i$ are identically distributed,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(f(S_n))\le \mu\Bigl(1-\frac{\mu}{n}\Bigr)\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[Df(T_1)\,D\log f(T_1)\bigr].\tag{1}$$

Now let $n\to\infty$. By the law of rare events, $\mathcal{L}(S_n)\to\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)$ in total variation, and likewise $\mathcal{L}(T_1)\to\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)$ since $T_1\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n-1,\mu/n)$. As $f$ is bounded with $f\ge c>0$, the functions $f$, $f\log f$ and $Df\cdot D\log f$ are all bounded, so
$$\mathbb{E}[f(S_n)]\to\mathbb{E}[f(X)],\qquad \mathbb{E}[(f\log f)(S_n)]\to\mathbb{E}[(f\log f)(X)],$$
hence $\mathrm{Ent}(f(S_n))\to\mathrm{Ent}(f(X))$, and $\mathbb{E}[Df(T_1)D\log f(T_1)]\to\mathbb{E}[Df(X)D\log f(X)]$. Passing to the limit in (1) yields the first inequality,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(f(X))\le (\mathbb{E}X)\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[Df(X)\,D\log f(X)\bigr].$$

For the second inequality, apply the second bound of Lemma 6.18 instead:
$$\mathrm{Ent}_i(g_n)\le \frac{\mu}{n}\Bigl(1-\frac{\mu}{n}\Bigr)\,\mathbb{E}_i\!\left[\frac{|\nabla_i g_n|^2}{g_n}\right],\qquad \frac{|\nabla_i g_n|^2}{g_n}=\frac{|Df(T_i)|^2}{f(T_i+X_{n,i})}.$$
Write the summand as $\frac{|Df(T_i)|^2}{f(T_i)}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{n,i}=0\}}+\frac{|Df(T_i)|^2}{f(T_i+1)}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{n,i}=1\}}$. With $M=\|f\|_\infty$, each term involving $\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{n,i}=1\}}$ is at most $\frac{4M^2}{c}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{n,i}=1\}}$, whose expectation is $O(1/n)$; summed over $i$ with the prefactor $\mu/n$ this contributes $O(1/n)\to0$. The main terms give $\mu(1-\mu/n)\,\mathbb{E}[|Df(T_1)|^2/f(T_1)]\to\mu\,\mathbb{E}[|Df(X)|^2/f(X)]$ by total-variation convergence as before. Hence
$$\mathrm{Ent}(f(X))\le (\mathbb{E}X)\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{|Df(X)|^2}{f(X)}\right].$$

Finally, for general $f:\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)$ apply the result to the truncations $f_k=\min(\max(f,1/k),k)$, which are bounded and bounded away from $0$. Since the truncation map $y\mapsto\min(\max(y,1/k),k)$ is nondecreasing and $1$-Lipschitz, one checks directly that $0\le Df_k\,D\log f_k\le Df\,D\log f$ pointwise (and similarly $|Df_k|^2/f_k$ is dominated), so dominated convergence passes the right-hand sides to the limit while monotone/dominated convergence passes $\mathrm{Ent}(f_k(X))\to\mathrm{Ent}(f(X))$. This extends both inequalities to all positive $f$, completing the proof of Theorem 6.17.
