**Central idea.** An Exp$(1)$ variable can be realized as $(X_1^2+X_2^2)/2$ for i.i.d. standard Gaussians $X_1,X_2$. Apply the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality to $g(x_1,x_2)=f((x_1^2+x_2^2)/2)$: the chain rule turns $\|\nabla g\|^2$ into $2X\,(f'(X))^2$, and the constant $2$ from the Gaussian LSI doubles to the claimed $4$.

**Solution.** Let $X_1,X_2$ be independent standard Gaussian variables and put $R=(X_1^2+X_2^2)/2$. Since $X_1^2+X_2^2\sim\chi^2_2$ has density $\tfrac12e^{-x/2}$ on $(0,\infty)$, i.e. it is exponential with mean $2$, the rescaled variable $R$ has density $e^{-x}$ on $(0,\infty)$: $R$ is exponentially distributed, $R\stackrel{d}{=}X$.

Let $f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$ be differentiable (we prove the claim for smooth $f$; the general case follows by approximation) and define $g(x_1,x_2)=f((x_1^2+x_2^2)/2)$. Then $g(X_1,X_2)=f(R)\stackrel{d}{=}f(X)$, so $\mathrm{Ent}(g(X_1,X_2)^2)=\mathrm{Ent}(f(X)^2)$, the entropy depending only on the law. The Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality applied to $g$ gives
$$\mathrm{Ent}(g(X_1,X_2)^2)\le 2\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla g(X_1,X_2)\|^2\bigr].$$
By the chain rule, with $r=(x_1^2+x_2^2)/2$,
$$\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_1}=f'(r)\,x_1,\qquad \frac{\partial g}{\partial x_2}=f'(r)\,x_2,$$
so that
$$\|\nabla g(x_1,x_2)\|^2=(f'(r))^2\,(x_1^2+x_2^2)=2r\,(f'(r))^2.$$
Evaluating at $(X_1,X_2)$ and writing $R=(X_1^2+X_2^2)/2$,
$$\|\nabla g(X_1,X_2)\|^2 = 2R\,(f'(R))^2 \stackrel{d}{=} 2X\,(f'(X))^2.$$
Substituting into the Gaussian log-Sobolev inequality,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(f(X)^2)=\mathrm{Ent}(g(X_1,X_2)^2)\le 2\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[2R\,(f'(R))^2\bigr]=4\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[X\,(f'(X))^2\bigr],$$
which is the claimed logarithmic Sobolev inequality for the exponential distribution. The right-hand side is interpreted as $+\infty$ when the expectation diverges, in which case the inequality is trivial.
