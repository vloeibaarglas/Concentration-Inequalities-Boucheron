**Central idea.** For (a), approximate the Poisson by Bernoulli sums $Z_n$ and apply Theorem 6.29 with the constant weight $W\equiv1$, since for a Bernoulli sum $V^+=(1-\mu/n)Z_n\le Z_n$; the uniform mgf bound passes to the Poisson limit. For (b), run the Herbst argument from the Poisson log-Sobolev inequality (Theorem 6.17) applied to $e^{\lambda\sqrt X}$: the discrete derivative $D\sqrt X=1/(\sqrt{X+1}+\sqrt X)$ satisfies $(D\sqrt X)^2\le1/(4X+1)$, and Chebyshev association separates the decreasing factor $1/(4X+1)$ from the increasing $e^{\lambda\sqrt X}$, giving $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{\psi(\lambda)}{\lambda}\le v\frac{e^\lambda-1}{\lambda}$. Part (c) is Chernoff with $\lambda=\log(1+t/(2v))$.

**Solution.** Let $X\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)$, $\mu=\mathbb{E}X$.

(a) Let $Z_n=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i$ with $X_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\mu/n)$ i.i.d. For $Z_n^{(i)}$ the leave-one-out copy, $Z_n-Z_n^{(i)}=X_i-X_i'$, and since the variables are $\{0,1\}$-valued, $\mathbb{E}'[(Z_n-Z_n^{(i)})_+^2]=\mathbb{P}'\{X_i=1,X_i'=0\}=X_i(1-\mu/n)$. Hence $V^+=(1-\mu/n)Z_n\le Z_n$, i.e. $V^+\le WZ_n$ with $W\equiv1$. Theorem 6.29 with $\theta=2$ gives, for $0<\lambda<1/2$,
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(\sqrt{Z_n}-\mathbb{E}\sqrt{Z_n})}\le\frac{2\lambda}{1-2\lambda}\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda/2}=\frac{\lambda^2}{1-2\lambda}.$$
By the law of rare events $Z_n\Rightarrow X$; $\{\sqrt{Z_n}\}$ is uniformly integrable (since $\mathbb{E}Z_n=\mu$), so $\mathbb{E}\sqrt{Z_n}\to\mathbb{E}\sqrt X$, and the uniform bound at any $\lambda'\in(\lambda,1/2)$ gives uniform integrability of $e^{\lambda\sqrt{Z_n}}$, so $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda\sqrt{Z_n}}\to\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda\sqrt X}$. The bound passes to the limit, proving the claim (both sides vanish at $\lambda=0$).

(b) Let $f=\sqrt X$, $\psi(\lambda)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(f-\mathbb{E}f)}$ for $\lambda\ge0$, $F(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f}$. Apply Theorem 6.17 to $e^{\lambda f}>0$. With $Df(x)=\sqrt{x+1}-\sqrt x=\frac1{\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt x}\in[0,1]$,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda f})\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda f}\,\lambda Df\,(e^{\lambda Df}-1)\big].$$
Since $u\mapsto(e^u-1)/u$ is increasing and $\lambda Df\le\lambda$, $e^{\lambda Df}-1\le Df\,(e^\lambda-1)$. Moreover $(Df(x))^2=\frac1{(\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt x)^2}\le\frac1{4x+1}$, because $(\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt x)^2=2x+1+2\sqrt{x^2+x}\ge4x+1$. Hence
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda f})\le\mu\lambda(e^\lambda-1)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{e^{\lambda f}}{4X+1}\right].$$
The map $x\mapsto1/(4x+1)$ is decreasing while $x\mapsto e^{\lambda\sqrt x}$ is increasing, so by Chebyshev's association inequality $\mathbb{E}[\frac{e^{\lambda f}}{4X+1}]\le\mathbb{E}[\frac1{4X+1}]\,\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f}$. With $v=\mu\mathbb{E}[\frac1{4X+1}]$,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda f})\le v\lambda(e^\lambda-1)F(\lambda).$$
Since $\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda f})=F(\lambda\psi'(\lambda)-\psi(\lambda))$, this gives $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{\psi(\lambda)}{\lambda}\le v\frac{e^\lambda-1}{\lambda}$. Integrating from $0$ (where $\psi(\lambda)/\lambda\to\psi'(0)=0$) and using $\frac{e^s-1}{s}\le\frac{e^\lambda-1}{\lambda}$ for $s\le\lambda$,
$$\frac{\psi(\lambda)}{\lambda}\le v\int_0^\lambda\frac{e^s-1}{s}\,ds\le v(e^\lambda-1),$$
i.e. $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(\sqrt X-\mathbb{E}\sqrt X)}\le v\lambda(e^\lambda-1)$.

(c) By Markov's inequality, for $\lambda\ge0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{\sqrt X-\mathbb{E}\sqrt X\ge t\}\le e^{-\lambda t}\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(\sqrt X-\mathbb{E}\sqrt X)}\le\exp\big(-\lambda t+v\lambda(e^\lambda-1)\big).$$
Taking $\lambda=\log(1+t/(2v))$, so $e^\lambda-1=t/(2v)$, the exponent becomes $-\lambda t+\lambda t/2=-\frac t2\log(1+\frac{t}{2v})$, which is the claimed bound.
