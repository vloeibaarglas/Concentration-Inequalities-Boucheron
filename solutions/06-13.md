**Central idea.** Apply the Poisson log-Sobolev inequality (Theorem 6.17, second form) to the density $f = p/\pi$ of $X$ with respect to $\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)$: the entropy $\mathrm{Ent}_\pi(f)$ is exactly the KL divergence, and the energy term $\mu\,\mathbb{E}_\pi[(Df)^2/f]$ becomes, via $\pi(k+1)/\pi(k) = \mu/(k+1)$, the scaled Fisher information $K(X)$. Subadditivity follows from a projection identity: the centered score $r_S(S)-1$ of the sum is the conditional expectation given $S$ of $A=\sum_i (p_i/\mu)(r_i(X_i)-1)$, a sum of independent centered terms. Conditional Jensen gives $\mathbb{E}(r_S-1)^2\le\mathbb{E}A^2=\sum_i(p_i/\mu)^2\,\mathbb{E}(r_i-1)^2$, and the squared weights produce exactly $\sum_i(p_i/\mu)K(X_i)$. For Bernoulli summands, $K(X_i) = p_i^2/(1-p_i)$. As printed, the statement is missing two hypotheses: supports without gaps (otherwise the first two claims fail), and Bernoulli summands for the final bound.

**Solution.** **Erratum: two hypotheses are missing from the printed statement.**

(a) *Support without gaps.* As printed, $X$ is any random variable with nonnegative integer values. The definition of $K$ only evaluates the ratio at points $X$ actually takes, so mass at $k+1$ is ignored when $p(k)=0$, and then the claims fail. For example, $\mathbb{P}\{X=0\}=0.9$, $\mathbb{P}\{X=10\}=0.1$ gives $\mu=1$ and $K(X)=1\cdot(0.9+0.1)=1$ (the ratio is $0$ at both support points), but $D(X\,\|\,\mathrm{Poisson}(1))=2.185\ldots>1$. Sub-additivity fails too: if $X_1,X_2$ are i.i.d. uniform on $\{0,2\}$, then $p_i=1$, $K(X_i)=1$, $S$ takes values $0,2,4$ with probabilities $\frac14,\frac12,\frac14$, and $K(S)=2>\sum_i\frac{p_i}{\mu}K(X_i)=1$. *Corrected hypothesis:* every variable $Y$ whose scaled Fisher information appears has support of the form $\{0,1,\ldots,N\}$ with $N\le\infty$, i.e. $\mathbb{P}\{Y=k\}>0$ implies $\mathbb{P}\{Y=k-1\}>0$ for $k\ge1$ (the setting of Kontoyiannis–Harremoës–Johnson). It holds for Bernoulli variables and is preserved under independent sums. We assume it throughout.

(b) *Bernoulli summands in the last claim.* The final bound $(1/\mu)\sum_ip_i^3/(1-p_i)$ uses $K(X_i)=p_i^2/(1-p_i)$, which holds for $X_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_i)$ but not for general $X_i$ with $\mathbb{E}X_i=p_i$, even gap-free ones. For example, $n=1$ and $\mathbb{P}\{X_1=0\}=0.9$, $\mathbb{P}\{X_1=1\}=0.01$, $\mathbb{P}\{X_1=2\}=0.09$ give $\mu=p_1=0.19$ and $D(X_1\,\|\,\mathrm{Poisson}(0.19))=0.2103\ldots>p_1^2/(1-p_1)=0.0446\ldots$. *Corrected statement:* the last claim is for $X_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_i)$, $0<p_i<1$ (the law of rare events). The first two claims are proved below for general gap-free variables.

Let $\pi$ be the $\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)$ mass function, $\pi(k) = e^{-\mu}\mu^k/k!$, and let $p$ be the mass function of $X$, $\mu = \mathbb{E}X \in (0,\infty)$. We may assume $K(X)<\infty$.

*KL divergence bounded by $K(X)$.* Put $f(k) = p(k)/\pi(k)$. Then $\mathbb{E}_\pi f = 1$ and
$$D(X\,\|\,\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)) = \sum_k p(k)\log\frac{p(k)}{\pi(k)} = \mathbb{E}_\pi[f\log f] = \mathrm{Ent}_\pi(f).$$
First assume $p(k) > 0$ for all $k$, so $f:\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)$. The second inequality of Theorem 6.17 gives, with $Df(k) = f(k+1)-f(k)$,
$$\mathrm{Ent}_\pi(f) \le \mu\,\mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[\frac{(Df)^2}{f}\right] = \mu\sum_k \pi(k)\,\frac{(f(k+1)-f(k))^2}{f(k)}.$$
Since $\pi(k+1) = \pi(k)\,\mu/(k+1)$,
$$f(k+1) = \frac{p(k+1)}{\pi(k+1)} = \frac{(k+1)p(k+1)}{\mu\,\pi(k)}, \qquad f(k) = \frac{p(k)}{\pi(k)},$$
so that
$$\frac{(f(k+1)-f(k))^2}{f(k)} = \frac{\pi(k)}{p(k)}\cdot\frac{1}{\pi(k)^2}\left(\frac{(k+1)p(k+1)}{\mu} - p(k)\right)^2 = \frac{1}{\pi(k)p(k)}\left(\frac{(k+1)p(k+1)}{\mu} - p(k)\right)^2.$$
Multiplying by $\pi(k)$ and summing,
$$\mu\,\mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[\frac{(Df)^2}{f}\right] = \mu\sum_k p(k)\left(\frac{(k+1)p(k+1)}{\mu\,p(k)} - 1\right)^2 = K(X),$$
which is $D(X\,\|\,\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)) \le K(X)$.

If instead the support is $\{0,\ldots,N\}$ with $N<\infty$, let $p_\varepsilon = (1-\varepsilon)p + \varepsilon\pi$, $0<\varepsilon<1$. It has full support and the same mean $\mu$, so $D(p_\varepsilon\,\|\,\pi)\le K(p_\varepsilon)$ by the case just proved. As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$: the terms of $K(p_\varepsilon)$ with $k<N$ converge to those of $K(X)$; the $k=N$ term is $p_\varepsilon(N)\big(\frac{(N+1)\varepsilon\pi(N+1)}{\mu p_\varepsilon(N)}-1\big)^2\to p(N)$, which is the $k=N$ term of $K(X)$ (there $p(N+1)=0$); for $k>N$, $p_\varepsilon(k)=\varepsilon\pi(k)$ and the ratio equals $\frac{(k+1)\pi(k+1)}{\mu\pi(k)}=1$, so these terms vanish. Hence $K(p_\varepsilon)\to K(X)$. Also $D(p_\varepsilon\,\|\,\pi)=\sum_{k\le N}p_\varepsilon(k)\log\frac{p_\varepsilon(k)}{\pi(k)}+\sum_{k>N}\varepsilon\pi(k)\log\varepsilon\to D(X\,\|\,\pi)$. Therefore $D(X\,\|\,\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu))\le K(X)$ in this case too.

*Subadditivity.* Let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ with the $X_i$ independent, $p_i=\mathbb{E}X_i$, $\mu=\sum_i p_i$. Let $m_i$ be the mass function of $X_i$, $q$ that of $S$, and $q^{(i)}$ that of $S^{(i)}=S-X_i$. Summands with $p_i=0$ vanish a.s. and may be dropped, so assume $p_i>0$ for all $i$; we may also assume every $K(X_i)<\infty$. Each $m_i$ has support $\{0,\ldots,N_i\}$, so $S$ has support $\{0,\ldots,\sum_iN_i\}$ and $q(s)>0$ there. Define the scores
$$r_S(s) = \frac{(s+1)q(s+1)}{\mu\,q(s)},\qquad r_i(k) = \frac{(k+1)m_i(k+1)}{p_i\,m_i(k)}\quad(m_i(k)>0),$$
so that $K(S)=\mu\,\mathbb{E}(r_S(S)-1)^2$ and $K(X_i)=p_i\,\mathbb{E}(r_i(X_i)-1)^2$.

*Centering.* By hypothesis (a), $m_i(j)>0$ implies $m_i(j-1)>0$ for $j\ge1$, so
$$\mathbb{E}\,r_i(X_i)=\sum_{k:\,m_i(k)>0}\frac{(k+1)m_i(k+1)}{p_i}=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{j\,m_i(j)}{p_i}=1.$$

*Projection identity.* On $\{S = s+1\}$ we have $\sum_i X_i = s+1$, hence
$$(s+1)q(s+1) = \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}\big[X_i\mathbf{1}_{\{S=s+1\}}\big] = \sum_{i=1}^n\sum_j j\,m_i(j)\,q^{(i)}(s+1-j).$$
For $q(s) > 0$, using $r_i(k)m_i(k) = (k+1)m_i(k+1)/p_i$, hypothesis (a) (the terms with $m_i(k)=0$ have $m_i(k+1)=0$), and the substitution $j = k+1$,
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[r_i(X_i)\mid S=s] &= \frac{1}{q(s)}\sum_{k:\,m_i(k)>0} r_i(k)\,m_i(k)\,q^{(i)}(s-k)
= \frac{1}{q(s)}\sum_{k\ge0} \frac{(k+1)m_i(k+1)}{p_i}\,q^{(i)}(s-k)\\
&= \frac{1}{p_i\,q(s)}\sum_{j\ge1} j\,m_i(j)\,q^{(i)}(s+1-j) = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X_i\mathbf{1}_{\{S=s+1\}}]}{p_i\,q(s)}.
\end{aligned}$$
Consequently
$$r_S(s) = \frac{(s+1)q(s+1)}{\mu\,q(s)} = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{p_i}{\mu}\,\mathbb{E}[r_i(X_i)\mid S=s],$$
and since $\sum_i p_i/\mu = 1$,
$$r_S(S) - 1 = \mathbb{E}[A\mid S],\qquad A=\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{p_i}{\mu}\,\big(r_i(X_i)-1\big).$$

*Variance bound.* By the conditional Jensen inequality, $\mathbb{E}\big[(\mathbb{E}[A\mid S])^2\big]\le\mathbb{E}A^2$. The summands of $A$ are independent (functions of different $X_i$), centered (by the centering step), and square-integrable (as $K(X_i)<\infty$), so all cross terms vanish:
$$\mathbb{E}(r_S(S)-1)^2\le\mathbb{E}A^2=\sum_{i=1}^n\Big(\frac{p_i}{\mu}\Big)^2\mathbb{E}(r_i(X_i)-1)^2=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{p_i^2}{\mu^2}\cdot\frac{K(X_i)}{p_i}=\frac1\mu\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{p_i}{\mu}K(X_i).$$
Multiplying by $\mu$ gives
$$K(S) \le \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{p_i}{\mu}\,K(X_i).$$
(The squared weights are essential: Jensen applied termwise to the convex combination would give only $\mathbb{E}(r_S-1)^2\le\sum_i\frac{p_i}{\mu}\mathbb{E}(r_i-1)^2$, i.e. $K(S)\le\sum_iK(X_i)$, which is weaker.)

*Bernoulli summands.* Let $X_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_i)$ with $0<p_i<1$ (the law of rare events); its support $\{0,1\}$ satisfies hypothesis (a). Then $m_i(0) = 1-p_i$, $m_i(1) = p_i$, $r_i(0)=\frac{p_i}{p_i(1-p_i)}=\frac{1}{1-p_i}$, $r_i(1)=0$, and
$$\mathbb{E}(r_i(X_i)-1)^2 = (1-p_i)\left(\frac{1}{1-p_i}-1\right)^2 + p_i(0-1)^2 = \frac{p_i^2}{1-p_i} + p_i = \frac{p_i}{1-p_i}.$$
Hence $K(X_i) = p_i\cdot\frac{p_i}{1-p_i} = \frac{p_i^2}{1-p_i}$. Combining with the first two parts,
$$D(S\,\|\,\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)) \le K(S) \le \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{p_i}{\mu}\,K(X_i) = \frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{p_i^3}{1-p_i}.$$
(Check, $p=(0.1,0.2)$: $K(S)=0.03348\ldots\le\frac{1}{0.3}\big(\frac{0.001}{0.9}+\frac{0.008}{0.8}\big)=0.03704\ldots$, and $D(S\,\|\,\mathrm{Poisson}(0.3))=0.01005\ldots$.) $\blacksquare$
