**Central idea.** Write $D = \max_{1\le i\le n}D_i$ with $D_i \sim \mathrm{Bin}(n-1,p)$ (dependent), $\sigma^2 = p(1-p)$, $x = \sqrt{2\sigma^2n\log n}$, $p \in (0,1)$ fixed. The upper tail holds at full strength by union bound plus Bernstein. The lower tail as stated is FALSE: a sharp binomial tail upper bound (Stirling plus a geometric ratio argument) gives $\mathbb{P}\{D \ge np+x-\delta\} \le nq \to 0$ whenever $\delta = a_n\sqrt{\sigma^2n/\log n}$ with $a_n = o(\log\log n)$ — the Gumbel $\log\log n$ correction is real and unavoidable. The correct lower bound, via the second-moment method, requires $a_n \gg \log\log n$. Lemma 2.4 with Theorem 6.12 only yields a much weaker $\omega(\sqrt n)$ window around $\mathbb{E}D$.

**Solution.** Let $D_i$ be the degree of vertex $i$, so $D = \max_i D_i$ and each $D_i \sim \mathrm{Bin}(n-1,p)$.

*Upper tail.* For $y > 0$, Bernstein's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}\{D_1-(n-1)p \ge y\} \le \exp(-y^2/(2(\sigma^2n+y/3)))$. With $y = x+\delta+p$ where $\delta = a_n\sqrt{\sigma^2n/\log n}$ and $a_n \to \infty$ (since the event $\{D \ge np+x+\delta\}$ shrinks as $a_n$ grows, we may replace $a_n$ by $\min(a_n,\log\log n)$, so that $\delta^2 = o(n)$ and $a_n\sqrt{\log n/n} \to 0$; this makes the $o(1)$ terms below legitimate),
$$\frac{y^2}{2(\sigma^2n+y/3)} = \frac{2\sigma^2n\log n + 2\sqrt2\,a_n\sigma^2n + o(n)}{2\sigma^2n(1+o(1))} = \log n + \sqrt2\,a_n + o(1),$$
since $2x\delta = 2\sqrt2\,a_n\sigma^2n$. By the union bound,
$$\mathbb{P}\{D \ge np+x+\delta\} \le n\exp(-\log n-\sqrt2\,a_n+o(1)) = e^{-\sqrt2\,a_n+o(1)} \to 0.$$
Thus the upper bound holds as claimed, for every $a_n \to \infty$.

*The lower bound as stated is false.* We prove a sharp binomial upper-tail lemma. Let $S_N \sim \mathrm{Bin}(N,p)$, $r_k = \mathbb{P}\{S_N=k\}$. By Stirling's formula, $r_k = \binom{N}{k}p^k(1-p)^{N-k} \le \frac{C_0}{\sqrt N}\exp(-ND(k/N\|p))$ uniformly for $k/N$ in a compact subinterval of $(0,1)$. Since $q \mapsto D(q\|p)$ satisfies $D(p\|p) = D'(p\|p) = 0$ and $D''(p\|p) = 1/\sigma^2$, Taylor's theorem gives $ND(k/N\|p) = \frac{y^2}{2\sigma^2N}(1+o(1)) = \frac{y^2}{2\sigma^2N} + o(1)$ uniformly for $k = Np+y$ with $y = o(N)$ (the relative error is $O(y/N)$ while $y^2/N = O(\log n)$ in the application below, so the absolute error is $o(1)$). For $k \ge Np+y$ ($1 \le y = o(N)$),
$$\frac{r_{k+1}}{r_k} = \frac{N-k}{k+1}\cdot\frac{p}{1-p} \le \frac{\sigma^2N-py}{\sigma^2N+y(1-p)} = 1-\frac{y}{\sigma^2N+y(1-p)} \le 1-\frac{y}{2\sigma^2N} =: \rho < 1$$
for large $N$. Hence, with $r_{\lceil Np+y\rceil} \le \frac{C_0}{\sqrt N}\exp(-\frac{y^2}{2\sigma^2N} + o(1))$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{S_N \ge Np+y\} \le r_{\lceil Np+y\rceil}\sum_{j\ge0}\rho^j \le \frac{C_0}{\sqrt N}e^{-y^2/(2\sigma^2N)+o(1)}\cdot\frac{2\sigma^2N}{y} = \frac{2C_0\sigma^2\sqrt N}{y}\,e^{-y^2/(2\sigma^2N)+o(1)}.\tag{*}$$
Now take $t_n = np+x-\delta_n$ with $\delta_n = a_n\sqrt{\sigma^2n/\log n}$ and $a_n = o(\log\log n)$, $a_n \to \infty$ (e.g.\ $a_n = \log\log\log n$). By the union bound, $\mathbb{P}\{D \ge t_n\} \le nq_n$ with $q_n = \mathbb{P}\{\mathrm{Bin}(n-1,p) \ge t_n\}$. Here $y_n = t_n-(n-1)p = x-\delta_n+O(1)$ satisfies $y_n^2/(2\sigma^2n) = \log n-\sqrt2\,a_n+o(1)$, so by $(*)$,
$$nq_n \le n\cdot\frac{C\sqrt n}{y_n}\exp(-\log n+\sqrt2\,a_n+o(1)) \le \frac{C'e^{\sqrt2\,a_n+o(1)}}{\sqrt{\log n}} \to 0,$$
since $a_n = o(\log\log n)$. Thus $\mathbb{P}\{D \ge np+x-\delta_n\} \to 0$, whereas the problem claims this probability tends to $1$. The stated lower bound is false; the obstruction is the Gumbel $\log\log n$ correction to the location of the maximum, which exceeds $a_n\sqrt{\sigma^2n/\log n}$ when $a_n = o(\log\log n)$. (The threshold is sharp, see below.)

*Correct lower bound.* Let now $\delta = b_n\sqrt{\sigma^2n/\log n}$ with $b_n/\log\log n \to \infty$ (the event $\{D \ge np+x-\delta\}$ grows with $b_n$, so we may assume $b_n \le (\log\log n)^2$, whence $\delta^2 = o(n)$ and $b_n^2/\log n \to 0$), and put $N = \sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf{1}_{\{D_i\ge t\}}$, $t = np+x-\delta$. By Paley–Zygmund, $\mathbb{P}\{D \ge t\} = \mathbb{P}\{N\ge1\} \ge (nq)^2/(nq+n(n-1)q_2)$ where $q = \mathbb{P}\{D_1\ge t\}$ and $q_2 = \mathbb{P}\{D_1\ge t,\,D_2\ge t\}$. Summing the Stirling lower bound $\mathbb{P}\{\mathrm{Bin}(N,p)=\lceil Np+y\rceil+j\} \ge c\,\exp(-\frac{y^2}{2\sigma^2N}(1+o(1)))/\sqrt N$ over $0 \le j \le c'\sigma^2N/y$ (the same Taylor expansion as above, relative error $O(y/N)$) gives $q \ge \frac{c''\sqrt N}{y}e^{-y^2/(2\sigma^2N)+o(1)}$, whence with $y^2/(2\sigma^2n) = \log n-\sqrt2\,b_n+o(1)$,
$$nq \ge \frac{c''e^{\sqrt2\,b_n+o(1)}}{\sqrt{\log n}} \to \infty \qquad (b_n \gg \log\log n).$$
Writing $D_1 = I_{12}+B_1$, $D_2 = I_{12}+B_2$ with $B_1,B_2$ i.i.d.\ $\mathrm{Bin}(n-2,p)$ independent of $I_{12}$ gives $q_2-q^2 = p(1-p)\,\mathbb{P}\{B_1=t-1\}^2 =: p(1-p)r^2$. Since $r \le \frac{C}{\sqrt n}e^{-y^2/(2\sigma^2n)}$ while $q \ge \frac{c}{\sqrt{\log n}}e^{-y^2/(2\sigma^2n)}$, $(r/q)^2 = O(\log n/n) \to 0$, so $q_2/q^2 \to 1$. Hence
$$\frac{(nq)^2}{nq+n(n-1)q_2} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{nq}+\frac{(n-1)q_2}{nq^2}} \to 1,$$
i.e.\ $\mathbb{P}\{D \ge np+x-b_n\sqrt{\sigma^2n/\log n}\} \to 1$ whenever $b_n/\log\log n \to \infty$. The $\log\log n$ threshold is sharp.

*What Lemma 2.4 and Theorem 6.12 give.* The centered degree $D_i-(n-1)p$ is a sum of $n-1$ independent centered variables $I-p\le1-p\le1$ with variance $\sigma^2$ each, so by the moment-generating-function bound behind Bernstein's inequality (Theorem 2.10, with $c=1/3$) $\psi_{D_i-(n-1)p}(\lambda)\le\frac{\lambda^2(n-1)\sigma^2}{2(1-\lambda/3)}$ for $0<\lambda<3$, i.e. $D_i-(n-1)p\in\Gamma_+((n-1)\sigma^2,1/3)$. (It is not sub-Gaussian with variance factor $(n-1)\sigma^2$ when $p\ne1/2$: for a single centered Bernoulli$(0.1)$ and $\lambda=1$, $\psi(1)=\log(0.9+0.1e)-0.1=0.0586\ldots>0.045=\lambda^2\sigma^2/2$. Hoeffding's lemma gives the valid sub-Gaussian factor $(n-1)/4$, which is weaker unless $p=1/2$.) Lemma 2.4 with Theorem 2.5, in the sub-gamma form of Corollary 2.6, yields
$$\mathbb{E}D=\mathbb{E}\max_i D_i\le(n-1)p+\sqrt{2(n-1)\sigma^2\log n}+\tfrac13\log n\le np+x+\tfrac13\log n,$$
while trivially $\mathbb{E}D\ge\mathbb{E}D_1=(n-1)p$. Theorem 6.12 ($D$ is self-bounding by Example 6.13) gives $\mathbb{P}\{|D-\mathbb{E}D| \ge t\} \le 2\exp(-t^2/(2\mathbb{E}D+2t/3))$, so, as $\mathbb{E}D\le n$, $|D-\mathbb{E}D| \le b_n\sqrt n$ with probability tending to $1$ for any $b_n \to \infty$. Combined: with probability tending to $1$, $(n-1)p-b_n\sqrt n\le D\le np+x+\frac13\log n+b_n\sqrt n$. The upper bound has the correct leading terms $np+x$ but an error $\omega(\sqrt n)$, far weaker than the sharp $a_n\sqrt{\sigma^2n/\log n}$ upper tail above, and there is no lower bound capturing the $\sqrt{2\sigma^2n\log n}$ term. The entropy method captures the correct order of $\mathbb{E}D$ but not the precise fluctuation scale. ∎
