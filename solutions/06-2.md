**Central idea.** (Courant–Fischer.) Diagonalize $A$ in an orthonormal eigenbasis; the Rayleigh quotient on a subspace spanned by eigenvectors is bounded by the extreme eigenvalues. Dimension counting forces any subspace of complementary dimension to meet these spans, giving both min-max formulas. For $B=PAP$, $R_B=R_A$ on $\mathrm{Ran}(P)$, so the min-max over the smaller family of subspaces in $\mathrm{Ran}(P)$ is squeezed between the global min-max values.

**Solution.** Let $Av_k=\alpha_kv_k$ with $\{v_k\}$ orthonormal, $\alpha_1\leq\cdots\leq\alpha_n$. For $x=\sum c_kv_k\neq0$, $R_A(x)=\sum\alpha_k|c_k|^2/\sum|c_k|^2$.

*Min-max formulas.* Let $\dim U=n-k+1$. With $W=\mathrm{span}\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\}$ ($\dim W=k$), $\dim(U\cap W)\geq(n-k+1)+k-n=1$, so some $0\neq x\in U\cap W$ has $R_A(x)\leq\alpha_k$ (as $x\in W$). Hence $\min_{x\in U}R_A(x)\leq\alpha_k$ for all such $U$, so $\max_{\dim U=n-k+1}\min_{x\in U}R_A(x)\leq\alpha_k$. With $U_0=\mathrm{span}\{v_k,\ldots,v_n\}$ ($\dim=n-k+1$), $R_A\geq\alpha_k$ on $U_0$, giving $\geq\alpha_k$. Thus
$$\alpha_k=\max\{\min\{R_A(x):x\in U,x\neq0\}:\dim U=n-k+1\}.$$
Similarly, for $\dim U=k$, $U$ meets $\mathrm{span}\{v_k,\ldots,v_n\}$ ($\dim=n-k+1$) nontrivially, giving $\max_{x\in U}R_A(x)\geq\alpha_k$; with $U=\mathrm{span}\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\}$ we get $\leq\alpha_k$. Hence
$$\alpha_k=\min\{\max\{R_A(x):x\in U,x\neq0\}:\dim U=k\}.$$

*Interlacing.* $P^*=P=P^2$, $\mathrm{rank}(P)=m$; $B^*=PAP=B$, and $B$ preserves $\mathrm{Ran}(P)$, with eigenvalues $\beta_1\leq\cdots\leq\beta_m$ (on $\mathrm{Ran}(P)$). For $0\neq x\in\mathrm{Ran}(P)$, $Px=x$, so $R_B(x)=x^*PAPx/x^*x=x^*Ax/x^*x=R_A(x)$.

For the lower bound, Courant–Fischer on $\mathrm{Ran}(P)$ gives $\beta_j=\min\{\max_{x\in V}R_B(x):V\subset\mathrm{Ran}(P),\dim V=j\}$. Since $R_B=R_A$ there,
$$\beta_j=\min_{\substack{V\subset\mathrm{Ran}(P)\\\dim V=j}}\max_{x\in V}R_A(x)\geq\min_{\dim V=j}\max_{x\in V}R_A(x)=\alpha_j,$$
as the minimum over the smaller family $\{V\subset\mathrm{Ran}(P)\}$ exceeds that over all $V$.

For the upper bound, $\beta_j=\max\{\min_{x\in V}R_B(x):V\subset\mathrm{Ran}(P),\dim V=m-j+1\}$, so
$$\beta_j=\max_{\substack{V\subset\mathrm{Ran}(P)\\\dim V=m-j+1}}\min_{x\in V}R_A(x)\leq\max_{\dim V=m-j+1}\min_{x\in V}R_A(x).$$
By the first min-max formula with $n-k+1=m-j+1$, i.e. $k=n-m+j$, the right side is $\alpha_{\,n-m+j}$. Hence $\alpha_j\leq\beta_j\leq\alpha_{\,n-m+j}$ for $j\leq m$. ∎
