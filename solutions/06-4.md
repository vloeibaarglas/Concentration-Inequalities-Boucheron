**Central idea.** $f$ is the distance to $A$, hence 1-Lipschitz. For $X\in\{0,1\}^n$, $f(X)=\sqrt{(\sum X_i-n/2)_+}$ (flipping $(\sum X_i-n/2)_+$ ones to zeros attains it), so $Mf(X)=0$ (as $P\{\sum X_i\leq n/2\}\geq1/2$) while $P\{f(X)>cn^{1/4}\}=P\{\sum X_i-n/2>c^2\sqrt{n}\}\to P\{N(0,1)>2c^2\}\geq1/4$ for small $c$ by the CLT — fluctuations of order $n^{1/4}$, not $O(1)$ as Theorem 6.10 would give.

**Erratum.** The set in the statement is wrong as printed. With $A=\{x\in[0,1]^n:\sum_i x_i\le n/2\}$, $A$ is convex, so $f=d(\cdot,A)$ is convex (not "not convex"), Theorem 6.10 applies, and the claim is false. Indeed, for $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ with $m=\sum_i x_i>n/2$ ones, the nearest point of this convex $A$ is $y$ with $y_i=1-\delta$ on the $m$ one-coordinates and $y_i=0$ elsewhere, $\delta=(m-n/2)/m\in(0,1/2]$ (KKT: on the ones the gradient $2(y_i-x_i)=-2\delta$ is balanced by the multiplier $\mu=2\delta\ge0$ of $\sum_iy_i\le n/2$; on the zeros $y_i=0$ is at its lower bound with multiplier $\mu\ge0$), so
$$f(x)=\frac{(\sum_i x_i-n/2)_+}{\sqrt{\sum_i x_i}}$$
(confirmed numerically against a constrained minimizer to $3\times10^{-15}$ on 200 random cases). Since $(S_n-n/2)/\sqrt{S_n}\approx(S_n-n/2)/\sqrt{n/2}$ is $O_P(1)$, $f(X)$ has $O(1)$ fluctuations: $Mf(X)=0$ and $\mathbb{P}\{f(X)>\tfrac12n^{1/4}\}=0.0060$ at $n=100$ and $1.1\times10^{-13}$ at $n=10^4$ (exact binomial sums), not $\ge1/4$. The example needs the discrete set $A=\{0,1\}^n\cap\{\sum_i x_i\le n/2\}$ (as in Ledoux–Talagrand 1991, p. 17), for which $f(X)=\sqrt{(\sum_iX_i-n/2)_+}$, $Mf(X)=0$, and $c=1/2$ works with limiting probability $\mathbb{P}\{N(0,1)>1/2\}\approx0.3085>1/4$. This corrected statement is what is proved below.

**Solution.** $f(x)=\inf_{y\in A}\|x-y\|$ is 1-Lipschitz: $|f(x)-f(x')|\leq\|x-x'\|$ by the triangle inequality.

Let $S_n=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,1/2)$. For $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ with $m=\sum x_i>n/2$, the closest $y\in A$ (with $A$ the discrete set, see the Erratum) is obtained by changing $m-n/2$ ones to zeros, giving $f(x)=\sqrt{m-n/2}$; if $m\leq n/2$ then $x\in A$ and $f(x)=0$. Hence
$$f(X)=\sqrt{(S_n-n/2)_+}\quad\text{a.s.}$$
Since $n$ is even, $P\{S_n\leq n/2\}\geq1/2$ (the median of $\mathrm{Binomial}(n,1/2)$ is $\geq n/2$), so $P\{f(X)=0\}\geq1/2$ and $Mf(X)=0$.

Now, for $c>0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{f(X)>cn^{1/4}\}=\mathbb{P}\{(S_n-n/2)_+>c^2\sqrt{n}\}=\mathbb{P}\{S_n-n/2>c^2\sqrt{n}\}.$$
By the CLT, $\frac{S_n-n/2}{\sqrt{n}/2}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1)$, so
$$\mathbb{P}\{S_n-n/2>c^2\sqrt{n}\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}\{N(0,1)>2c^2\}.$$
With $c=1/2$, $2c^2=1/2$ and $\mathbb{P}\{N(0,1)>1/2\}\approx0.309>1/4$. Hence for all sufficiently large (even) $n$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{f(X)>Mf(X)+cn^{1/4}\}\geq1/4,$$
with $c=1/2$. The fluctuations are of order $n^{1/4}\to\infty$, whereas Theorem 6.10 would give $P\{Z>\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\leq e^{-t^2/2}$ (sub-Gaussian, $O(1)$ fluctuations). Thus convexity cannot be dropped.

*Remark.* With the discrete $A$, $f$ is genuinely non-convex: $u=(1,\dots,1,0,\dots,0)$ ($n/2$ ones) and $\mathbf{1}-u$ both lie in $A$, so $f(u)=f(\mathbf{1}-u)=0$, while their midpoint $(\tfrac12,\dots,\tfrac12)$ is at distance $\sqrt n/2>0$ from every point of $\{0,1\}^n$. ∎
