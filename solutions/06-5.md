**Central idea.** This is Theorem 6.10 with $[0,1]^n$ replaced by $\mathcal{X}^n$. Apply Theorem 6.7 with $v = B^2$: the key is the vector analogue of the gradient estimate from Theorem 3.17. For $Z_i = \inf_{x'_i} f(X^{(i)}, x'_i)$ attained at $x_i^* \in \mathcal{X}$, separate convexity gives $Z - Z_i \le \langle \nabla_{x_i} f(X), X_i - x_i^*\rangle$, whence $(Z-Z_i)^2 \le B^2\|\nabla_{x_i}f(X)\|^2$ by Cauchy–Schwarz and $\|X_i - x_i^*\| \le B$. Summing over $i$ and using $\|\nabla f(X)\| \le 1$ (1-Lipschitz) yields $\sum_i (Z-Z_i)^2 \le B^2$.

**Solution.** We may assume $f$ is continuously differentiable on $\mathcal{X}^n$; otherwise approximate $f$ uniformly by smooth separately convex $1$-Lipschitz functions via mollification (which preserves separate convexity and does not increase the Lipschitz constant) and pass to the limit. Write $X = (X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ with $X_i \in \mathcal{X}$, and for each $i$ put $Z_i = \inf_{x'_i \in \mathcal{X}} f(X_1,\ldots,x'_i,\ldots,X_n)$. Since $\mathcal{X}$ is compact and $f$ continuous, the infimum is attained at some $x_i^* \in \mathcal{X}$.

Fix $X$ and $i$, and consider $g_i(x_i) = f(X_1,\ldots,x_i,\ldots,X_n)$ as a function of $x_i \in \mathcal{X}$. By separate convexity $g_i$ is convex, and it is $1$-Lipschitz since $f$ is $1$-Lipschitz on $\mathcal{X}^n \subset \mathbb{R}^{dn}$ and $x_i \mapsto (X_1,\ldots,x_i,\ldots,X_n)$ is an isometry. We have $Z - Z_i = g_i(X_i) - g_i(x_i^*) \ge 0$. By the first-order condition for convex differentiable functions,
$$g_i(X_i) - g_i(x_i^*) \le \langle \nabla g_i(X_i),\, X_i - x_i^*\rangle.$$
Note $\nabla g_i(X_i)$ is the $i$-th $d$-dimensional block of $\nabla f(X)$. By Cauchy–Schwarz and $\|X_i - x_i^*\| \le B$ (diameter of $\mathcal{X}$),
$$(Z - Z_i)^2 \le \|\nabla g_i(X_i)\|^2\,\|X_i - x_i^*\|^2 \le B^2\,\|\nabla g_i(X_i)\|^2.$$
Summing over $i$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n (Z - Z_i)^2 \le B^2 \sum_{i=1}^n \|\nabla g_i(X_i)\|^2 = B^2\,\|\nabla f(X)\|^2 \le B^2 \quad \text{a.s.},$$
where $\|\nabla f(X)\| \le 1$ follows from $f$ being $1$-Lipschitz (at every point of differentiability, hence a.e. by Rademacher's theorem, and the bound extends by continuity). Theorem 6.7 applied with $v = B^2$ therefore gives, for all $t > 0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z > \mathbb{E}Z + t\} \le e^{-t^2/(2B^2)}.$$
∎
