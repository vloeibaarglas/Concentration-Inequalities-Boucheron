**Central idea.** The largest singular value $f(X_1,\ldots,X_n) = \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \sup_{\|u\|=\|v\|=1} u^T A v$ is a supremum of functions affine in each column $X_i$ separately, hence separately convex; it is $1$-Lipschitz for the product Euclidean norm since $|\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} - \|A'\|_{\mathrm{op}}| \le \|A-A'\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \|A-A'\|_F$. Exercise 6.5 then applies directly.

**Solution.** Let $A = [X_1 \mid \cdots \mid X_n]$ and $f(X_1,\ldots,X_n) = \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \sup_{\|u\|_2 = \|v\|_2 = 1} u^T A v$, the largest singular value.

*Separate convexity.* Fix $X_j$ for $j \ne i$. Then $u^T A v = \sum_{j=1}^n v_j (u^T X_j) = v_i (u^T X_i) + C(u,v)$ where $C(u,v)$ does not depend on $X_i$. For each fixed $(u,v)$ this is affine in $X_i$, so $X_i \mapsto f(X_1,\ldots,X_n) = \sup_{\|u\|=\|v\|=1} [v_i (u^T X_i) + C(u,v)]$ is a supremum of affine functions, hence convex in $X_i$. Thus $f$ is separately convex on $\mathcal{X}^n$.

*$1$-Lipschitz.* By the reverse triangle inequality, $|\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} - \|A'\|_{\mathrm{op}}| \le \|A - A'\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. Since the operator norm is bounded by the Frobenius norm,
$$\|A - A'\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \|A - A'\|_F = \Big(\sum_{i=1}^n \|X_i - X'_i\|_2^2\Big)^{1/2},$$
which is exactly the Euclidean norm of $(X_1,\ldots,X_n) - (X'_1,\ldots,X'_n)$ in $\mathbb{R}^{dn}$. Hence $|f(x) - f(y)| \le \|x-y\|$ for all $x,y \in \mathcal{X}^n$.

Exercise 6.5 therefore applies and yields, for $Z = f(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ and all $t > 0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z > \mathbb{E}Z + t\} \le e^{-t^2/(2B^2)}.$$

*Comparison with Example 6.11.* Example 6.11 assumes the $mn$ *entries* $X_{i,j} \in [0,1]$ are independent and applies Theorem 6.10 on $[0,1]^{mn}$ to obtain $\mathbb{P}\{Z > \mathbb{E}Z + t\} \le e^{-t^2/2}$. Here only the $n$ *columns* are assumed independent — the entries within each column may be arbitrarily dependent — and the conclusion is $e^{-t^2/(2B^2)}$. For instance, if $\mathcal{X} = [0,1]^d$ then $B = \sqrt{d}$ and the bound is $e^{-t^2/(2d)}$, weaker than Example 6.11's $e^{-t^2/2}$ by the dimension factor $d$. The price of weakening entrywise independence to columnwise independence is the diameter $B$ of each independent block appearing in the exponent: each column contributes fluctuations on the scale of $\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{X}) = B$ rather than $1$. ∎
