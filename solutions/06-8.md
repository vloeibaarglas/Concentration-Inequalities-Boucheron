**Central idea.** The natural proof applies the modified log-Sobolev inequality (Theorem 6.6) with $Z_i = \inf_{x'_i}f$, giving $\lambda\psi'-\psi \le \frac{\lambda^2}{2}\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}V(X)]/\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}]$ where $V(X) = \sum_i(Z-Z_i)^2 \le v + C\mathbf{1}_{A^c}(X)$. To close the Herbst argument one needs $\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}\mathbf{1}_{A^c}] \le \mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}]\mathbb{P}(A^c)$, which via Harris (Theorem 2.15) requires $e^{\lambda Z}$ nondecreasing, i.e. $f$ nondecreasing — a hypothesis missing from the statement (compare Exercise 6.7, which assumes it explicitly). We document the gap and prove the corrected version.

**Erratum.** The statement is missing a hypothesis, and without it the stated bound is false: $f$ must be nondecreasing in each variable (the Harris step in the hint needs it; compare Exercise 6.7, which assumes it). Counterexample: $n=1$, $\mathbb{P}\{X=0\}=0.1=1-\mathbb{P}\{X=1\}$, $A=[1,\infty)$ (monotone), $f(x)=\mathbf{1}_{\{x<1\}}$. For $x\in A$, $f(x)-\inf_{x'}f(x')=0\le v$; for $x\notin A$, it equals $1\le C$; so the hypotheses hold with $v=10^{-9}$, $C=1$, and $\mathbb{P}\{X\notin A\}=0.1$. Here $Z=\mathbf{1}_{\{X=0\}}$, $\mathbb{E}Z=0.1$, and at $t=0.9-10^{-6}$, $\mathbb{P}\{Z>\mathbb{E}Z+t\}=\mathbb{P}\{Z=1\}=0.1$, whereas the claimed bound is $\exp\bigl(-t^2/(2(10^{-9}+0.1))\bigr)\approx0.0174$. With "$f$ nondecreasing in each variable" added, the stated bound holds; that corrected statement is proved below.

**Solution.** *Remark on the statement.* As written, the Harris step of the proof is not justified: with $V(X) \le v\mathbf{1}_A(X) + C\mathbf{1}_{A^c}(X)$, one needs $\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}\mathbf{1}_{A^c}(X)] \le \mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}]\,\mathbb{P}(X \notin A)$ for $\lambda > 0$, i.e. $\mathrm{Cov}(e^{\lambda Z}, \mathbf{1}_A) \ge 0$. By Harris this requires $e^{\lambda Z}$ nondecreasing, i.e. $f$ nondecreasing in each variable, which is not assumed. Counterexample to the correlation step: $n = 1$, $X_1 \in \{0,1\}$ with $\mathbb{P}\{X_1=0\}=\mathbb{P}\{X_1=1\}=1/2$, $A = [1,\infty)$ (monotone), $f(x) = 100\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{x<1\}}$, so $f(0) = 100$, $f(1) = 0$. Then $V(0) = 10000$, $V(1) = 0$, so the hypotheses hold with any $v > 0$ and $C = 10000$, but $\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}\mathbf{1}_{A^c}]/\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}] = e^{100\lambda}/(e^{100\lambda}+1) \to 1 > 1/2 = \mathbb{P}(A^c)$ as $\lambda \to \infty$. (This example breaks only the correlation step, not the final bound: here $\mathbb{E}Z=50$, $\mathbb{P}\{Z>\mathbb{E}Z+t\}=1/2$ for $t<50$ and $0$ for $t\ge50$, while the bound $e^{-t^2/10^4}$ exceeds $e^{-1/4}\approx0.78$ for $t<50$; the Erratum gives an example that violates the bound itself.) We prove the result under the additional (evidently intended) hypothesis that $f$ is nondecreasing in each variable.

Assume henceforth $f$ is nondecreasing in each variable, and w.l.o.g. $C \ge v$ (otherwise $V \le v$ everywhere and Theorem 6.7 gives the claim since $v \le v + C\mathbb{P}(A^c)$). For $\lambda > 0$, Theorem 6.6 with $Z_i = \inf_{x'_i} f(X_1,\ldots,x'_i,\ldots,X_n)$ gives
$$\lambda\frac{F'(\lambda)}{F(\lambda)} - \log F(\lambda) \le \frac{1}{F(\lambda)}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda Z}\,\phi(-\lambda(Z - Z_i))\big],$$
where $F(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}]$ and $\phi(x) = e^x - x - 1$. Since $Z \ge Z_i$ and $\lambda > 0$, $-\lambda(Z-Z_i) \le 0$, and $\phi(x) \le x^2/2$ for $x \le 0$; hence $\phi(-\lambda(Z-Z_i)) \le \frac{\lambda^2}{2}(Z-Z_i)^2$. With $V(X) = \sum_i (Z-Z_i)^2$ and $\psi(\lambda) = \log\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}]$,
$$\lambda\psi'(\lambda) - \psi(\lambda) \le \frac{\lambda^2}{2}\,\frac{\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}V(X)]}{F(\lambda)}, \qquad \lambda > 0.$$
By hypothesis $V(X) \le v\mathbf{1}_A(X) + C\mathbf{1}_{A^c}(X) \le v + C\mathbf{1}_{A^c}(X)$, so
$$\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}V(X)] \le vF(\lambda) + C\,\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}\mathbf{1}_{A^c}(X)].$$
Since $f$ is nondecreasing and $\lambda > 0$, $X \mapsto e^{\lambda f(X)}$ is nondecreasing, while $X \mapsto \mathbf{1}_{A^c}(X)$ is nonincreasing because $A$ is upward closed. Harris's inequality (Theorem 2.15, opposite-monotonicity case) yields $\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda Z}\mathbf{1}_{A^c}(X)] \le F(\lambda)\,\mathbb{P}(X \notin A)$. Consequently, with $p = \mathbb{P}(X \notin A)$,
$$\lambda\psi'(\lambda) - \psi(\lambda) \le \frac{\lambda^2}{2}\,(v + Cp), \qquad \lambda > 0.$$
The Herbst argument ($\frac{d}{d\lambda}[\psi(\lambda)/\lambda] \le (v+Cp)/2$, integrate $0$ to $\lambda$) gives $\psi(\lambda) \le \frac{\lambda^2}{2}(v+Cp)$, and Chernoff's bound $\mathbb{P}\{Z - \mathbb{E}Z \ge t\} \le \inf_{\lambda>0} e^{-\lambda t + \psi(\lambda)}$, optimized at $\lambda = t/(v+Cp)$, yields for all $t > 0$
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z > \mathbb{E}Z + t\} \le \exp\!\left(\frac{-t^2}{2\big(v + C\,\mathbb{P}\{X \notin A\}\big)}\right).$$
∎
