**Central idea.** Write $f(x)=d(x,B)$, which is $1$-Lipschitz. First show $\mathbb{P}\{f\ge t_0\}\le1/2$: otherwise $A=\{f\ge t_0\}$ has $\mathbb{P}(A)\ge1/2$, so $\mathbb{P}\{d(X,A)\ge t_0\}\le\alpha(t_0)$, but $B\subset\{d(X,A)\ge t_0\}$ by the triangle inequality, contradicting $\mathbb{P}(B)\ge\varepsilon>\alpha(t_0)$. Hence $A'=\{f\le t_0\}$ has $\mathbb{P}(A')\ge1/2$, and $\{f\ge t_0+t\}\subset\{d(X,A')\ge t\}$ (again by the triangle inequality), so the definition of $\alpha(t)$ gives the bound.

**Solution.** Let $f(x)=d(x,B)$, which satisfies $|f(x)-f(y)|\le d(x,y)$, i.e., $f$ is $1$-Lipschitz.

We first claim $\mathbb{P}\{f\ge t_0\}\le1/2$. Suppose not, and set $A=\{x:d(x,B)\ge t_0\}$, so $\mathbb{P}(A)>1/2\ge1/2$. By the definition of the concentration function, $\mathbb{P}\{d(X,A)\ge t_0\}\le\alpha(t_0)$. But $B\subset\{x:d(x,A)\ge t_0\}$: indeed, for $b\in B$ and $a\in A$, the triangle inequality gives $d(b,a)\ge d(a,B)-d(b,B)=d(a,B)\ge t_0$, so $d(b,A)=\inf_{a\in A}d(b,a)\ge t_0$. Hence $\mathbb{P}(B)\le\mathbb{P}\{d(X,A)\ge t_0\}\le\alpha(t_0)<\varepsilon\le\mathbb{P}(B)$, a contradiction. Therefore $\mathbb{P}\{f\ge t_0\}\le1/2$.

Consequently $A'=\{x:f(x)\le t_0\}$ satisfies $\mathbb{P}(A')=1-\mathbb{P}\{f>t_0\}\ge1-\mathbb{P}\{f\ge t_0\}\ge1/2$. We claim $\{f\ge t_0+t\}\subset\{d(X,A')\ge t\}$. Indeed, if $f(x)\ge t_0+t$ and $y\in A'$ (so $f(y)\le t_0$), then $d(x,y)\ge f(x)-f(y)\ge t$ by the $1$-Lipschitz property of $f$; hence $d(x,A')=\inf_{y\in A'}d(x,y)\ge t$. Therefore
$$\mathbb{P}\{d(X,B)\ge t_0+t\}=\mathbb{P}\{f\ge t_0+t\}\le\mathbb{P}\{d(X,A')\ge t\}\le\alpha(t),$$
the last inequality by the definition of $\alpha(t)$ since $\mathbb{P}(A')\ge1/2$. ∎
