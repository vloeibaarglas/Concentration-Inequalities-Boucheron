**Central idea.** We read $A^c_t$ as $(A_t)^c$, the complement of the $t$-blowup (for $(A^c)_t$ the stated upper bound would be false at large $t$, since $(A^c)_t\uparrow S$). The Section 7.2 argument generalizes: for $C,D\subset S$ with $\mathrm{dist}(C,D)\ge t$, Brunn–Minkowski applied to the cones $\tilde C,\tilde D$ gives $\mathrm{Vol}((\tilde C+\tilde D)/2)\ge\sqrt{\mu(C)\mu(D)}\,\mathrm{Vol}(B)$, while the modulus of convexity — refined to general convex combinations — forces $(\tilde C+\tilde D)/2\subset\max(\tfrac12,1-\delta(t))\,B$, whence $\mu(C)\mu(D)\le2e^{-2n\delta(t/2)}$. Apply this to $C=A$, $D=S\setminus A_t$.

**Solution.** Write $B=\{x:\|x\|\le1\}$, $S=\{x:\|x\|=1\}$, and for $E\subset S$ let $\tilde E=\bigcup_{a\in[0,1]}aE$ be the cone, so $\mu(E)=\mathrm{Vol}(\tilde E)/\mathrm{Vol}(B)$. The $t$-blowup is $A_t=\{x\in S:\mathrm{dist}(x,A)\le t\}$ (distance in $\|\cdot\|$), and we prove the bound for $(A_t)^c=S\setminus A_t$.

The modulus $\delta$ is nondecreasing (a larger $\varepsilon$ shrinks the constraint set), and $\delta(\varepsilon)\le\varepsilon/2$ for $\varepsilon\in[0,1]$: with $\|x\|=1$, $y=(1-\varepsilon)x$ gives $\|x-y\|=\varepsilon$ and $1-\|(x+y)/2\|=\varepsilon/2$. Since $\mathrm{dist}\le2$ on $S$, we use $\delta(t/2)\le1/2$ below.

*Convex-combination refinement.* Let $\|c\|,\|d\|\le1$ and $\alpha\in[0,1]$. Assuming $\alpha\le1/2$ (else swap),
$$\alpha c+(1-\alpha)d=(1-2\alpha)d+2\alpha\frac{c+d}{2},$$
so $\|\alpha c+(1-\alpha)d\|\le(1-2\alpha)+2\alpha(1-\delta(\|c-d\|))=1-2\alpha\,\delta(\|c-d\|)$. By symmetry,
$$\|\alpha c+(1-\alpha)d\|\le1-2\min(\alpha,1-\alpha)\,\delta(\|c-d\|).\tag{1}$$

*Two-set lemma.* Let $\emptyset\ne C,D\subset S$ be measurable with $\mathrm{dist}(C,D)\ge t$. For $\lambda c\in\tilde C$, $\mu d\in\tilde D$ ($\lambda,\mu\in[0,1]$, $c\in C$, $d\in D$), write $\frac{\lambda c+\mu d}{2}=s(\alpha c+(1-\alpha)d)$ with $s=\frac{\lambda+\mu}{2}$, $\alpha=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+\mu}$ (if $\lambda+\mu=0$ there is nothing to show). By (1) and $\|c-d\|\ge t$,
$$\left\|\frac{\lambda c+\mu d}{2}\right\|\le s\big(1-2\min(\alpha,1-\alpha)\delta(t)\big)=\frac{\lambda+\mu}{2}-\min(\lambda,\mu)\,\delta(t).$$
With $m=\min(\lambda,\mu)$, $M=\max(\lambda,\mu)$, the right side is $h(m):=\frac{M-m}{2}+m(1-\delta(t))$. This is linear in $m\in[0,M]$, $h(m)=M/2+m(\tfrac12-\delta(t))$, hence maximized at an endpoint: $h(m)\le\max(h(0),h(M))=\max(M/2,\,M(1-\delta(t)))\le\max(\tfrac12,1-\delta(t))$ (using $M\le1$). Hence
$$\frac{\tilde C+\tilde D}{2}\subset\max\!\left(\tfrac12,\,1-\delta(t)\right)B.\tag{2}$$
Brunn–Minkowski gives $\mathrm{Vol}((\tilde C+\tilde D)/2)\ge\sqrt{\mathrm{Vol}(\tilde C)\mathrm{Vol}(\tilde D)}=\sqrt{\mu(C)\mu(D)}\,\mathrm{Vol}(B)$. With (2):
- If $\delta(t)\ge1/2$: $\sqrt{\mu(C)\mu(D)}\le2^{-n}$, so $\mu(C)\mu(D)\le e^{-n\ln4}\le2e^{-n}\le2e^{-2n\delta(t/2)}$ (as $\delta(t/2)\le1/2$).
- If $\delta(t)\le1/2$: $\sqrt{\mu(C)\mu(D)}\le(1-\delta(t))^n\le e^{-n\delta(t)}\le e^{-n\delta(t/2)}$, so $\mu(C)\mu(D)\le e^{-2n\delta(t/2)}\le2e^{-2n\delta(t/2)}$.
Thus, for $\mathrm{dist}(C,D)\ge t$,
$$\mu(C)\mu(D)\le2e^{-2n\delta(t/2)}.\tag{3}$$

*Conclusion.* Let $D_0=S\setminus A_t=(A_t)^c$. For $a\in A$, $x\in D_0$: $\|a-x\|\ge\mathrm{dist}(x,A)>t$, so $\mathrm{dist}(A,D_0)\ge t$ (if $D_0=\emptyset$ there is nothing to prove). Applying (3) with $C=A$, $D=D_0$ (if $\mu(A)=0$ the claim is vacuous),
$$\mu\big((A_t)^c\big)=\mu(D_0)\le\frac{2}{\mu(A)}\,e^{-2n\delta(t/2)}.$$
∎
