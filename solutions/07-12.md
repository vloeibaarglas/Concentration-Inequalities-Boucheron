**Central idea.** Use an integer-valued potential that every nontrivial compression strictly decreases: $\Phi(A) = \sum_{x \in A} r(x)$, where $r(x)$ is the rank of $x$ in the simplicial ordering of $\{-1,1\}^n$. On each fiber $\{x_i = \sigma\}$, the initial segment $S^{(i,\sigma)}_N$ is the set of $N$ elements of smallest rank, and since ranks are distinct it is the *unique* minimizer of the rank sum among $N$-subsets of the fiber. Hence $C_i$ strictly decreases $\Phi$ whenever $C_i(A) \neq A$; with $|A|$ preserved, $\Phi$ is bounded below, so the process cannot continue forever.

**Solution.** Let $r(x) \in \{1, \ldots, 2^n\}$ be the position of $x$ in the simplicial ordering of $\{-1,1\}^n$ (so $r$ is injective), and define
$$\Phi(A) = \sum_{x \in A} r(x), \qquad A \subset \{-1,1\}^n.$$
Fix $i$ and $\sigma \in \{-1,1\}$, and let $F_i^\sigma = \{x : x_i = \sigma\}$. The "simplicial ordering of all vectors whose $i$-th component is $\sigma$" is the restriction of the global simplicial ordering to $F_i^\sigma$: indeed, for $x, y \in F_i^\sigma$ the weight comparison $\|x\| < \|y\|$ is unaffected by the fixed coordinate, and the tie-break (at the smallest index where $x$ and $y$ differ, which is never $i$) is likewise unchanged. Thus
$$S^{(i,\sigma)}_N = \{ \text{the $N$ elements of $F_i^\sigma$ of smallest $r$-value} \}.$$
Let $a_1 < a_2 < \cdots < a_{2^{n-1}}$ be the $r$-values on $F_i^\sigma$. For any $N$-subset with sorted $r$-values $b_1 < \cdots < b_N$, we have $b_j \ge a_j$ for each $j$ (at least $j$ fiber elements have $r$-value $\le b_j$), so $\sum b_j \ge \sum_{j=1}^N a_j$ with equality iff the subset is exactly $S^{(i,\sigma)}_N$. That is, the initial segment is the unique minimizer of the rank sum over $N$-subsets of the fiber.

Now $C_i(A)$ agrees with $A$ outside $F_i^- \cup F_i^+ = \{-1,1\}^n$, and on each fiber replaces $A \cap F_i^\sigma$ (of size $N_\sigma = |A^{(i\sigma)}|$) by $S^{(i,\sigma)}_{N_\sigma}$. Hence
$$\Phi(C_i(A)) \le \Phi(A),$$
with strict inequality iff $A \cap F_i^\sigma \neq S^{(i,\sigma)}_{N_\sigma}$ for some $\sigma$, i.e. iff $C_i(A) \neq A$.

By Exercise 7.11, $|C_i(A)| = |A|$, so every $A_k$ has the same cardinality $m = |A|$. Since the $r$-values are distinct positive integers, $\Phi(A_k) \ge 1 + 2 + \cdots + m$ for all $k$. Each step of the process applies $C_i$ with $C_i(A_k) \neq A_k$, strictly decreasing the positive integer $\Phi$. A strictly decreasing sequence of integers bounded below is finite, so the process terminates: for some $k$, $C_i(A_k) = A_k$ for every $i$. ∎
