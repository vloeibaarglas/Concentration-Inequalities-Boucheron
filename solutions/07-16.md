**Central idea.** Follow the proof of Theorem 7.12, but replace the sublevel sets $A_s=\{f\le s\}$ by $A_s\cap S$, which remain convex as intersections of convex sets. Since $f$ is $1$-Lipschitz on the convex set $S$, for $x\in S$ one still gets $f(x)\le s+D(x,A_s\cap S)\le s+d_T(x,A_s\cap S)$ via Lemma 7.11, so Talagrand's convex distance inequality (Theorem 7.9) applies to $A_s\cap S$. The mass $\mathbb{P}\{X\notin S\}$ outside $S$ — where no Lipschitz control is available — is accounted for separately, and the median properties $\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\le M\},\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge M\}\ge 1/2$ give $\mathbb{P}\{X\in A_s\cap S\}\ge 1/2-\mathbb{P}\{X\notin S\}$. Both tails come out as $p+\frac{1}{1/2-p}e^{-t^2/4}$ with $p=\mathbb{P}\{X\notin S\}$; the stated lower-tail form $2p+2e^{-t^2/4}$ follows by a case split: when $e^{-t^2/4}\le\frac12(\frac12-p)$ the first bound is smaller, and otherwise $2p+2e^{-t^2/4}>\frac12\ge\mathbb{P}\{f(X)<Mf(X)\}$.

**Solution.** Let $p=\mathbb{P}\{X\notin S\}<1/2$ and let $M=Mf(X)$ be a median of $f(X)$, so $\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\le M\}\ge 1/2$ and $\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge M\}\ge 1/2$. For $s\in\mathbb{R}$ put $A_s=\{x\in[0,1]^n:f(x)\le s\}$, convex by quasi-convexity of $f$, and $B_s=A_s\cap S$, convex as an intersection of convex sets.

*Key inequality.* Let $x\in S$. For any $y\in B_s\subset S$, the $1$-Lipschitz property on $S$ gives $f(x)\le f(y)+\|x-y\|\le s+\|x-y\|$. Taking $\inf_{y\in B_s}$ yields $f(x)\le s+D(x,B_s)$ for $x\in S$, where $D(x,B_s)=\inf_{y\in B_s}\|x-y\|$. By Lemma 7.11 ($B_s$ convex), $D(x,B_s)\le d_T(x,B_s)$. Hence, for all $x\in S$ and all $s\in\mathbb{R}$,
$$f(x)\le s+d_T(x,B_s).\tag{*}$$
In particular $\{f(X)\ge s+t\}\cap\{X\in S\}\subset\{d_T(X,B_s)\ge t\}$, so by Theorem 7.9 applied to the convex set $B_s$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge s+t,\,X\in S\}\cdot\mathbb{P}\{X\in B_s\}\le e^{-t^2/4}.\tag{**}$$
Consequently $\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge s+t\}\le p+e^{-t^2/4}/\mathbb{P}\{X\in B_s\}$.

*Upper tail.* Take $s=M$. Since $\mathbb{P}\{X\in B_M\}\ge\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\le M\}-p\ge 1/2-p>0$, (**) gives
$$\mathbb{P}\{f(X)>M+t\}\le\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge M+t\}\le p+\frac{1}{1/2-p}\,e^{-t^2/4}.$$

*Lower tail.* Take $s=M-t$, so $B_{M-t}=A_{M-t}\cap S$. From (**) with $s+t=M$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge M,\,X\in S\}\cdot\mathbb{P}\{X\in B_{M-t}\}\le e^{-t^2/4}.$$
Since $\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge M,\,X\in S\}\ge\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge M\}-p\ge 1/2-p$, we obtain $\mathbb{P}\{X\in B_{M-t}\}\le e^{-t^2/4}/(1/2-p)$. Hence
$$\mathbb{P}\{f(X)<M-t\}\le\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\le M-t\}\le p+\mathbb{P}\{X\in B_{M-t}\}\le p+\frac{1}{1/2-p}\,e^{-t^2/4}.$$

This is the bound $p+\frac{1}{1/2-p}e^{-t^2/4}$ for the lower tail; it remains to deduce the stated bound $2p+2e^{-t^2/4}$ from it. Write $e=e^{-t^2/4}$ and split into two cases.
- If $e\le\frac12\left(\frac12-p\right)$, then
$$\frac{e}{1/2-p}=\frac{2e\left(\frac12-p\right)+2pe}{1/2-p}=2e+\frac{2pe}{1/2-p}\le2e+p,$$
so $p+\frac{e}{1/2-p}\le2p+2e$ and the display above gives $\mathbb{P}\{f(X)<M-t\}\le2p+2e$.
- If $e>\frac12\left(\frac12-p\right)$, then $2p+2e>2p+\frac12-p=\frac12+p\ge\frac12$. On the other hand, since $\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge M\}\ge\frac12$, we have $\mathbb{P}\{f(X)<M-t\}\le\mathbb{P}\{f(X)<M\}\le\frac12<2p+2e$.

In both cases
$$\mathbb{P}\{f(X)<Mf(X)-t\}\le2\,\mathbb{P}\{X\notin S\}+2e^{-t^2/4}.$$

*Remark.* The $A_s\cap S$ argument actually gives the lower-tail bound $\min\!\left(p+\frac{1}{1/2-p}e^{-t^2/4},\,\frac12\right)$, which never exceeds the stated $2p+2e^{-t^2/4}$ (the case split above; a numerical grid over $p\in[0,0.4999]$, $t\in(0,10]$ confirms it). When $p=0$ both lower-tail bounds reduce to $2e^{-t^2/4}$, recovering Theorem 7.12. ∎
