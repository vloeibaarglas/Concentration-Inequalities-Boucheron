**Central idea.** Put $S = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2$ and $\mu = \mathbb{E}[S]$; the event is $\{S \ge 2\mu + t\} = \{S - \mu \ge \mu + t\}$. Since $X_i \in [0,1]$, the variables $Y_i := X_i^2 \in [0,1]$ are independent with $\mathrm{Var}(Y_i) \le \mathbb{E}[Y_i^2] \le \mathbb{E}[Y_i]$, so the total variance $v := \sum_i \mathrm{Var}(Y_i)$ satisfies $v \le \mu$. Bernstein's inequality applied to $S - \mu$ at deviation $u = \mu + t$ gives $\exp(-u^2/(2(v + u/3)))$, and the algebra $\frac{(\mu+t)^2}{2(\mu+(\mu+t)/3)} \ge \frac{3}{8}(\mu+t)$ reduces exactly to $t \ge 0$. That hypothesis is missing from the printed statement and cannot be dropped: a binomial counterexample shows the bound fails for some $t<0$.

**Solution.** Let $S = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2$ and $\mu = \mathbb{E}[S] \ge 0$. **Erratum.** As printed (with no restriction on $t$) the claim is false: it needs the hypothesis $t\ge0$; see the counterexample at the end. We prove it for $t\ge0$. If $\mu + t = 0$, i.e. $\mu = t = 0$, then $S = 0$ a.s.\ and both sides equal $1$; assume $\mu + t > 0$. Since both sides are nonnegative,
$$\left\{\sqrt{S} \ge \sqrt{2\mu + t}\right\} = \{S \ge 2\mu + t\} = \{S - \mu \ge \mu + t\}.$$
Put $Y_i = X_i^2 \in [0,1]$, independent, and $u = \mu + t > 0$. As $X_i \in [0,1]$,
$$\mathrm{Var}(Y_i) \le \mathbb{E}[Y_i^2] = \mathbb{E}[X_i^4] \le \mathbb{E}[X_i^2] = \mathbb{E}[Y_i],$$
so with $v := \sum_{i=1}^n \mathrm{Var}(Y_i)$ we have $v \le \mu$. If $\mu=0$ then $S=0$ a.s.\ and, as $u=t>0$, the probability is $0$; so assume $\mu>0$. Put $Z_i=Y_i-\mathbb{E}Y_i$, so $|Z_i|\le1$ and $\sum_i\mathbb{E}Z_i^2=v\le\mu$. For $q\ge3$, $\mathbb{E}(Z_i)_+^q\le\mathbb{E}Z_i^2\le\frac{q!}{2}3^{-(q-2)}\mathbb{E}Z_i^2$ because $q!/(2\cdot3^{q-2})\ge1$. Hence the hypotheses of Theorem 2.10 hold with the positive constant $\mu$ in place of $v$ and $c=1/3$, and Corollary 2.11 gives
$$\mathbb{P}\{S - \mu \ge u\} \le \exp\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{2(\mu + u/3)}\right) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\mu+t)^2}{2(\mu + (\mu+t)/3)}\right).$$
It remains to check $\frac{(\mu+t)^2}{2(\mu+(\mu+t)/3)} \ge \frac{3}{8}(\mu+t)$. Dividing by $\mu + t > 0$ and clearing denominators, this is equivalent to $8(\mu+t) \ge 6\mu + 2(\mu+t)$, i.e.\ $6t \ge 0$, which holds since $t \ge 0$. Hence
$$\mathbb{P}\left\{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2} \ge \sqrt{2\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2 + t}\right\} \le e^{-(3/8)(\mu+t)},$$
as claimed.

*Counterexample for $t<0$ (the printed claim is false without $t\ge0$).* The only place $t\ge0$ was used is the final step $6t\ge0$, and the claim genuinely fails for some negative $t$. Take $X_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0.02)$ i.i.d., $n=10^4$. Then $X_i^2=X_i$, $S=\sum_iX_i^2\sim\mathrm{Bin}(10^4,0.02)$ and $\mu=\mathbb{E}S=200$. For $t=-100$ the event is $\{\sqrt S\ge\sqrt{300}\}=\{S\ge300\}$, and an exact binomial tail computation gives
$$\mathbb{P}\{S\ge300\}\approx1.62\times10^{-11}>5.18\times10^{-17}\approx e^{-37.5}=e^{-(3/8)(200-100)}.$$
Likewise for $t=-25$: $\mathbb{P}\{S\ge375\}\approx3.85\times10^{-29}>3.16\times10^{-29}\approx e^{-(3/8)\cdot175}$. (For $t=-10$ and $t=-1$ the inequality happens to hold in this example, so the failure is not for every $t<0$; and for $t\le-\mu$ the right-hand side is $\ge1$, so the bound is trivially true there.) Hence the correct statement is: for all $t\ge0$, $\mathbb{P}\{\sqrt{\sum_iX_i^2}\ge\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}\sum_iX_i^2+t}\}\le e^{-(3/8)(\mathbb{E}\sum_iX_i^2+t)}$, which is what was proved above.
