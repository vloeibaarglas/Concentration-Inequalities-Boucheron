**Central idea.** The stated inequality $\beta(t)\ge\alpha(t/2)$ is false in general: on the two-point space the minimal valid $\beta$ is $(1/2)\mathbf{1}\{t\le1/2\}$ while $\alpha(1/2)=1/2$. The obstruction is that a one-sided mean-centered bound cannot see the median; the mean of $d(\cdot,A)$ may be large. What is true is $\alpha(2t)\le\beta(t)$: for $B=A_r^c$, the bounded $1$-Lipschitz function $g=\min(d(\cdot,B),r)$ equals $r$ on $A$, hence has mean at least $r/2$, and applying the hypothesis to $-g$ at level $s\le\mathbb{E}g(X)$ bounds $\mathbb{P}(B)$ by $\beta(s)$. (Truncating at $r$ guarantees that $\mathbb{E}g(X)$ is finite, which $d(\cdot,B)$ alone need not satisfy.)

**Solution.** The claim as stated is false. Consider $\mathcal{X}=\{0,1\}$ with $d(0,1)=1$ and $\mathbb{P}$ uniform. For $A=\{0\}$ (so $\mathbb{P}(A)=1/2$), $\mathbb{P}\{d(X,A)\ge t\}=(1/2)\mathbf{1}\{t\le1\}$, and the same for $A=\{1\}$; for $A=\{0,1\}$ it is $0$. Hence $\alpha(t)=(1/2)\mathbf{1}\{t\le1\}$, in particular $\alpha(1/2)=1/2$. A function $f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}$ is $1$-Lipschitz iff $|f(0)-f(1)|\le1$. For such $f$, $\max f-\mathbb{E}f=|f(0)-f(1)|/2\le1/2$, so $\{f\ge\mathbb{E}f+t\}\ne\emptyset$ requires $t\le1/2$, and $\mathbb{P}\{f\ge\mathbb{E}f+t\}\in\{0,1/2\}$ (it cannot be $1$ for $t>0$). Thus $\beta(t)=(1/2)\mathbf{1}\{t\le1/2\}$ satisfies the hypothesis, but $\beta(1)=0\not\ge1/2=\alpha(1/2)$. So $\beta(t)\ge\alpha(t/2)$ fails in general.

The correct statement is $\alpha(2t)\le\beta(t)$ for all $t>0$ (equivalently, $\alpha(r)\le\inf_{0<s\le r/2}\beta(s)$ for all $r>0$). To see this, fix $r>0$ and $A\subset\mathcal{X}$ with $\mathbb{P}(A)\ge1/2$, and set $B=\{x:d(x,A)\ge r\}=A_r^c$. If $B=\emptyset$ then $\mathbb{P}(B)=0$ and there is nothing to prove, so assume $B\ne\emptyset$; then $d(\cdot,B)$ is finite and $1$-Lipschitz. Let
$$g(x)=\min\{d(x,B),\,r\}.$$
As a minimum of a $1$-Lipschitz function and a constant, $g$ is $1$-Lipschitz; it is continuous (hence measurable) and takes values in $[0,r]$, so $\mathbb{E}g(X)$ is finite and the hypothesis may be applied to $-g$. (Without the truncation, $\mathbb{E}\,d(X,B)$ could be $+\infty$ on an unbounded space, and then $-d(\cdot,B)$ would have no finite mean.) For $a\in A$ and $b\in B$, $d(a,b)\ge d(b,A)-d(a,A)\ge r-0=r$, so $d(a,B)\ge r$ and hence $g=r$ on $A$. Since $g\ge0$, $\mathbb{E}g(X)\ge r\,\mathbb{P}(A)\ge r/2$. Applying the hypothesis to the $1$-Lipschitz function $-g$ gives, for every $s>0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{g(X)\le\mathbb{E}g(X)-s\}\le\beta(s).$$
Since $g=0$ on $B$, we have $\mathbb{P}(B)\le\mathbb{P}\{g(X)=0\}$. Moreover $\{g(X)=0\}\subset\{g(X)\le\mathbb{E}g(X)-s\}$ whenever $0<s\le\mathbb{E}g(X)$. Hence $\mathbb{P}(B)\le\beta(s)$ for all $0<s\le\mathbb{E}g(X)$, i.e.,
$$\mathbb{P}(B)\le\inf_{0<s\le\mathbb{E}g(X)}\beta(s)\le\inf_{0<s\le r/2}\beta(s),$$
using $\mathbb{E}g(X)\ge r/2$. Taking the supremum over $A$ with $\mathbb{P}(A)\ge1/2$ yields $\alpha(r)\le\inf_{0<s\le r/2}\beta(s)$. In particular, with $r=2t$,
$$\alpha(2t)\le\inf_{0<s\le t}\beta(s)\le\beta(t),\qquad t>0.$$
∎
