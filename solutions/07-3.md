**Central idea.** Chernoff's bound turns the Laplace functional into a one-sided deviation inequality: for $1$-Lipschitz $f$ with $\mathbb{E}f(X)=0$, $\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge s\}\le e^{-\lambda s}\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f(X)}\le e^{-\lambda s}L(\lambda)$ for every $\lambda>0$. Hence $\beta(s)=\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda s}L(\lambda)$ satisfies the hypothesis of Exercise 7.2 (applied to $g-\mathbb{E}g(X)$ for general $1$-Lipschitz $g$), and the corrected Exercise 7.2 gives $\alpha(t)\le\beta(t/2)=\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda t/2}L(\lambda)$.

**Solution.** Let $f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}$ be $1$-Lipschitz with $\mathbb{E}f(X)=0$ (so $\mathbb{E}|f(X)|<\infty$). For $s>0$ and $\lambda>0$, by Markov's inequality applied to the nonnegative variable $e^{\lambda f(X)}$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge s\}=\mathbb{P}\{e^{\lambda f(X)}\ge e^{\lambda s}\}\le e^{-\lambda s}\,\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f(X)}\le e^{-\lambda s}L(\lambda),$$
where $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f(X)}\in[0,\infty]$ is well defined and bounded by $L(\lambda)$ by definition of the supremum. Taking the infimum over $\lambda>0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge s\}\le\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda s}L(\lambda)=:\beta(s),\qquad s>0.$$
Now let $g:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}$ be $1$-Lipschitz with $\mathbb{E}g(X)$ existing and finite. Then $f=g-\mathbb{E}g(X)$ is $1$-Lipschitz with $\mathbb{E}f(X)=0$, and $\mathbb{P}\{g(X)\ge\mathbb{E}g(X)+s\}=\mathbb{P}\{f(X)\ge s\}\le\beta(s)$. Thus $\beta$ satisfies the hypothesis of Exercise 7.2. By the corrected form of Exercise 7.2 proved above, $\alpha(2t)\le\beta(t)$ for all $t>0$, i.e., $\alpha(u)\le\beta(u/2)$ for all $u>0$. Hence
$$\alpha(t)\le\beta(t/2)=\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda(t/2)}L(\lambda)=\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda t/2}L(\lambda),\qquad t>0.$$
∎
