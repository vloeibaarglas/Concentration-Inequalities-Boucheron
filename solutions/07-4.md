**Central idea.** A $1$-Lipschitz $f$ on a space of diameter $D$ takes values in an interval $[a,b]$ with $b-a\le D$ (since $|f(x)-f(y)|\le d(x,y)\le D$). Hoeffding's lemma (Lemma 2.2) then gives $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(f-\mathbb{E}f)}\le\exp(\lambda^2(b-a)^2/8)\le\exp(\lambda^2D^2/8)$; with $\mathbb{E}f(X)=0$ and taking the supremum over $f$, this is $L(\lambda)\le e^{D^2\lambda^2/8}$.

**Solution.** Let $f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}$ be $1$-Lipschitz with $\mathbb{E}f(X)=0$. Since $f$ is continuous, it is Borel measurable. Fix $x_0\in\mathcal{X}$; then $|f(x)|\le|f(x_0)|+d(x,x_0)\le|f(x_0)|+D$ for all $x$, so $f$ is bounded and all expectations below exist. Let $a=\inf_{\mathcal{X}}f$ and $b=\sup_{\mathcal{X}}f$, both finite. For any $x,y\in\mathcal{X}$, $f(x)-f(y)\le|f(x)-f(y)|\le d(x,y)\le D$, so taking suprema gives $b-a\le D$. Moreover $f(X)\in[a,b]$ almost surely.

Hoeffding's lemma (Lemma 2.2) states that a random variable taking values in $[a,b]$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}\le\exp(\lambda^2(b-a)^2/8)$ for all $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$. Applying this to $Z=f(X)$ and using $\mathbb{E}f(X)=0$,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f(X)}\le\exp\!\left(\frac{\lambda^2(b-a)^2}{8}\right)\le\exp\!\left(\frac{D^2\lambda^2}{8}\right),\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.$$
Taking the supremum over all $1$-Lipschitz $f$ with $\mathbb{E}f(X)=0$ yields $L(\lambda)\le e^{D^2\lambda^2/8}$ for all $\lambda$. ∎
