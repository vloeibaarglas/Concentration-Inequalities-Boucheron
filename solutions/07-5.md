**Central idea.** Induction on $n$ via conditional centering (tensorization). For $1$-Lipschitz $f$ on the $\ell^1$-product, freeze the first $n-1$ coordinates: $x_n\mapsto f(x',x_n)$ is $1$-Lipschitz on $\mathcal{X}_n$, so centering it by its $\mathbb{P}_n$-mean $m(x')$ and applying $L_n$ gives $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_n}e^{\lambda f}\le e^{\lambda m(x')}L_n(\lambda)$; the conditional mean $m$ is $1$-Lipschitz on the $(n-1)$-fold product with mean zero, so induction applies. A truncation plus Fatou's lemma reduces to bounded $f$, where all conditional expectations are everywhere defined.

**Solution.** We prove $L(\lambda)\le\prod_{i=1}^nL_i(\lambda)$ by induction on $n$, the case $n=1$ being trivial. Let $f$ be $1$-Lipschitz on $(\mathcal{X},d)$, $d(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^nd_i(x_i,y_i)$, with $\mathbb{E}f(X)=0$ under $\mathbb{P}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n\mathbb{P}_i$ (so $\mathbb{E}|f(X)|<\infty$).

We first reduce to bounded $f$. Put $f_k=(f\wedge k)\vee(-k)$, which is $1$-Lipschitz (composition of $1$-Lipschitz maps) and bounded, and $g_k=f_k-\mathbb{E}f_k(X)$, which is $1$-Lipschitz, bounded, with $\mathbb{E}g_k(X)=0$. If the claim holds for bounded functions, $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda g_k(X)}\le\prod_{i=1}^nL_i(\lambda)$ for all $k$. Since $f_k\to f$ pointwise and $|f_k|\le|f|\in L^1$, dominated convergence gives $\mathbb{E}f_k(X)\to0$, so $g_k\to f$ pointwise; Fatou's lemma yields $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f(X)}\le\liminf_k\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda g_k(X)}\le\prod_{i=1}^nL_i(\lambda)$. Hence it suffices to prove $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda f(X)}\le\prod_{i=1}^nL_i(\lambda)$ for bounded $f$.

Assume now $f$ is bounded. Write $\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}'\times\mathcal{X}_n$ with $\mathcal{X}'=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathcal{X}_i$, $\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}'\otimes\mathbb{P}_n$ ($\mathbb{P}'=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}_i$), and $d=d'+d_n$. For fixed $x'\in\mathcal{X}'$, the map $f_{x'}(x_n)=f(x',x_n)$ is $1$-Lipschitz on $(\mathcal{X}_n,d_n)$, since $d((x',x_n),(x',y_n))=d_n(x_n,y_n)$, and bounded. Let $m(x')=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_n}[f(x',X_n)]$, defined for every $x'$. Then $f_{x'}-m(x')$ is $1$-Lipschitz on $\mathcal{X}_n$ with $\mathbb{P}_n$-mean zero, so
$$\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_n}\!\left[e^{\lambda(f(x',X_n)-m(x'))}\right]\le L_n(\lambda),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_n}\!\left[e^{\lambda f(x',X_n)}\right]\le e^{\lambda m(x')}L_n(\lambda).$$
Moreover $m$ is $1$-Lipschitz on $(\mathcal{X}',d')$: indeed,
$$|m(x')-m(y')|\le\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_n}|f(x',X_n)-f(y',X_n)|\le\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_n}[d((x',X_n),(y',X_n))]=d'(x',y'),$$
and $m$ is bounded with $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}'}[m(X')]=\mathbb{E}[f(X)]=0$ by Fubini. By the induction hypothesis applied to $m$ on the $(n-1)$-fold product,
$$\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}'}[e^{\lambda m(X')}]\le\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}L_i(\lambda).$$
Therefore, by Fubini,
$$\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda f(X)}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}'}\!\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_n}[e^{\lambda f(X',X_n)}]\right]\le L_n(\lambda)\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}'}[e^{\lambda m(X')}]\le\prod_{i=1}^nL_i(\lambda).$$
Taking the supremum over all $1$-Lipschitz $f$ with $\mathbb{E}f(X)=0$ gives $L(\lambda)\le\prod_{i=1}^nL_i(\lambda)$. ∎
