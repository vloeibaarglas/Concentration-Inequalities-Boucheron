**Central idea.** Apply the divergence theorem to the vector field $F(x)=x$: $\mathrm{div}\,F=n$ on $B$, while on $\partial B$ the outward unit normal is $\nu(x)=x$ itself, so $\langle F,\nu\rangle=1$ and the boundary flux is exactly the surface area.

**Solution.** Let $B=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n:\|x\|_2\le1\}$ and $\partial B=S^{n-1}$. By $\mathrm{Vol}(\partial B)$ we mean the $(n-1)$-dimensional surface (Hausdorff) measure $\sigma(\partial B)$, and by $\mathrm{Vol}(B)$ the $n$-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

Consider the vector field $F(x)=x$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$. Then $\mathrm{div}\,F=\sum_{i=1}^n\partial_{x_i}x_i=n$. By the divergence theorem,
$$\int_B \mathrm{div}\,F\,dx=\int_{\partial B}\langle F,\nu\rangle\,d\sigma,$$
where $\nu$ is the outward unit normal. On $\partial B=S^{n-1}$ we have $\nu(x)=x$ (the outward normal to the unit sphere at $x$ is $x$ itself), so $\langle F(x),\nu(x)\rangle=\langle x,x\rangle=\|x\|_2^2=1$ for $x\in\partial B$. Hence
$$n\,\mathrm{Vol}(B)=\int_B n\,dx=\int_{\partial B}1\,d\sigma=\sigma(\partial B)=\mathrm{Vol}(\partial B).$$
That is, $\mathrm{Vol}(\partial B)=n\,\mathrm{Vol}(B)$. ∎
