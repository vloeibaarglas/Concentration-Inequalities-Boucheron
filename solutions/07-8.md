**Central idea.** A single integration by parts, $1-\Phi(t)=\int_t^\infty\phi=\phi(t)/t-\int_t^\infty\phi(x)/x^2\,dx$ (taking $u=1/x$, $dv=x\phi(x)\,dx$): dropping the subtracted integral gives the upper bound $\phi(t)/t$, while $1/x^2\le1/t^2$ on $[t,\infty)$ bounds the subtracted integral by $(1-\Phi(t))/t^2$, and rearranging yields the lower bound.

**Solution.** For $t>0$,
$$1-\Phi(t)=\int_t^\infty\phi(x)\,dx=\int_t^\infty\frac1x\cdot x\phi(x)\,dx.$$
Integrating by parts with $u=1/x$, $dv=x\phi(x)\,dx$ (so $du=-dx/x^2$, $v=-\phi(x)$, since $\phi'(x)=-x\phi(x)$):
$$1-\Phi(t)=\left[-\frac{\phi(x)}{x}\right]_t^\infty-\int_t^\infty\frac{\phi(x)}{x^2}\,dx=\frac{\phi(t)}{t}-\int_t^\infty\frac{\phi(x)}{x^2}\,dx.\tag{*}$$
Since the subtracted integral is nonnegative, $1-\Phi(t)\le\phi(t)/t$, i.e.
$$\frac{1-\Phi(t)}{\phi(t)}\le\frac1t.$$
For the lower bound, on $[t,\infty)$ we have $1/x^2\le1/t^2$, so
$$\int_t^\infty\frac{\phi(x)}{x^2}\,dx\le\frac1{t^2}\int_t^\infty\phi(x)\,dx=\frac{1-\Phi(t)}{t^2}.$$
Substituting in $(*)$,
$$1-\Phi(t)\ge\frac{\phi(t)}{t}-\frac{1-\Phi(t)}{t^2}.$$
Rearranging, $(1-\Phi(t))\left(1+\frac1{t^2}\right)\ge\frac{\phi(t)}{t}$, i.e.
$$\frac{1-\Phi(t)}{\phi(t)}\ge\frac{1/t}{1+1/t^2}=\frac{t}{t^2+1}.$$
This holds for all $t>0$. ∎
