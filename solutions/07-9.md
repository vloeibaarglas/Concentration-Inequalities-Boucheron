**Central idea.** By rotation invariance take $u=e_1$; then $\mu(C(u,s))=c_n\int_s^1(1-t^2)^{(n-3)/2}\,dt$ with $c_n=\sigma(S^{n-2})/\sigma(S^{n-1})$. Writing the integrand via $\frac{d}{dt}(1-t^2)^{(n-1)/2}=-(n-1)t(1-t^2)^{(n-3)/2}$ turns the integral into $(1-s^2)^{(n-1)/2}/(n-1)$ times $\mathbb{E}[1/t]$ under an explicit probability law on $[s,1]$. The upper bound uses $1/t\le1/s$; the lower bound uses Jensen ($\mathbb{E}[s/t]\ge s/\mathbb{E}[t]$) together with an exponential tail estimate giving $\mathbb{E}[t]\le2s$ (this is where $s^2\ge2/n$ enters). Log-convexity of $\Gamma$ controls $c_n$ from both sides.

**Solution.** By rotation invariance let $u=e_1$, so $C(u,s)=\{x\in S^{n-1}:x_1\ge s\}$. Parametrize $x_1=t\in(-1,1)$; by the coarea formula the surface element is $d\sigma_{n-1}=(1-t^2)^{(n-3)/2}\,dt\,d\sigma_{n-2}$, so with $\mu$ normalized,
$$\mu(C(u,s))=c_n\int_s^1(1-t^2)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\,dt,\qquad c_n=\frac{\sigma(S^{n-2})}{\sigma(S^{n-1})}=\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((n-1)/2)}.$$
For $n=2$, $\sqrt{2/n}\le s\le1$ forces $s=1$, and both bounds are $0=0$; assume $n\ge3$.

Since $\frac{d}{dt}(1-t^2)^{(n-1)/2}=-(n-1)t(1-t^2)^{(n-3)/2}$,
$$(1-t^2)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}=\frac{-\frac{d}{dt}(1-t^2)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{(n-1)t},$$
and with $I:=\int_s^1(1-t^2)^{(n-3)/2}\,dt$,
$$I=\frac{(1-s^2)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{n-1}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac1t\right],\tag{1}$$
where $\mathbb{E}$ is expectation under the probability density $p(t)=-\frac{d}{dt}(1-t^2)^{(n-1)/2}/(1-s^2)^{(n-1)/2}$ on $[s,1]$.

*Gamma bounds.* Log-convexity of $\Gamma$ gives $\Gamma(x+\tfrac12)^2\le\Gamma(x)\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)^2$, so with $x=(n-1)/2$,
$$c_n=\frac{\Gamma(x+\frac12)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(x)}\le\frac{\sqrt{(n-1)/2}}{\sqrt\pi}.$$
Also $\Gamma(x+1)^2\le\Gamma(x+\tfrac12)\Gamma(x+\tfrac32)=(x+\tfrac12)\Gamma(x+\tfrac12)^2$ gives $\Gamma(x+\tfrac12)/\Gamma(x)\ge x/\sqrt{x+\tfrac12}$, so
$$c_n\ge\frac{(n-1)/2}{\sqrt\pi\sqrt{n/2}}=\frac{n-1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}.\tag{2}$$

*Upper bound.* On $[s,1]$, $1/t\le1/s$, so from (1), $I\le(1-s^2)^{(n-1)/2}/((n-1)s)$. Hence
$$\mu(C(u,s))\le\frac{(1-s^2)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{s}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{(n-1)/2}}{\sqrt\pi\,(n-1)}=\frac{(1-s^2)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{s\sqrt n}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{n}{2\pi(n-1)}}.$$
Since $\sqrt{n/(2\pi(n-1))}\le1/2\iff2n\le\pi(n-1)\iff n\ge\pi/(\pi-2)\approx2.75$, for $n\ge3$,
$$\mu(C(u,s))\le\frac{1}{2s\sqrt n}(1-s^2)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.$$

*Lower bound.* By Jensen ($1/t$ convex), with $J:=\mathbb{E}[s/t]$,
$$J=s\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac1t\right]\ge\frac{s}{\mathbb{E}[t]}.$$
Moreover $\mathbb{E}[t-s]=\int_0^{1-s}\mathbb{P}\{t-s>u\}\,du$ with, for $u\in[0,1-s]$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{t>s+u\}=\left(\frac{1-(s+u)^2}{1-s^2}\right)^{\!\frac{n-1}{2}}\le\exp\!\left(-\frac{(n-1)su}{1-s^2}\right),$$
using $1-\frac{(s+u)^2-s^2}{1-s^2}\le\exp(-\frac{2su}{1-s^2})$. Thus $\mathbb{E}[t-s]\le\frac{1-s^2}{(n-1)s}$, and
$$\mathbb{E}[t]\le s+\frac{1-s^2}{(n-1)s}\le2s,$$
since $\frac{1-s^2}{(n-1)s}\le s\iff1-s^2\le(n-1)s^2\iff1\le ns^2$, which holds as $s^2\ge2/n$. Hence $J\ge1/2$. From (1),
$$\mu(C(u,s))=c_n\frac{(1-s^2)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{n-1}\,\frac{J}{s}=\frac{(1-s^2)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{s\sqrt n}\left(c_n\frac{\sqrt n}{\,n-1\,}J\right)\ge\frac{(1-s^2)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{s\sqrt n}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdot\frac12,$$
using (2). Since $2\sqrt{2\pi}\approx5.01<6$,
$$\mu(C(u,s))\ge\frac{1}{6s\sqrt n}(1-s^2)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.$$
This completes the proof. ∎
