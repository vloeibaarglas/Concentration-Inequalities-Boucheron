**Central idea.** Apply Marton's transportation inequality (Theorem 8.2) to the conditional measures $\mathbb{Q}_A = \mathbb{P}(\cdot \mid A)$ and $\mathbb{Q}_B = \mathbb{P}(\cdot \mid B)$, for which $D(\mathbb{Q}_A\|\mathbb{P}) = \log(1/\mathbb{P}(A))$. Glue the two optimal couplings through a common $\mathbb{P}$-marginal $X$; then $d_H(A,B) \le \mathbb{E}\,d_H(X,Y) + \mathbb{E}\,d_H(X,Z)$ by the triangle inequality, and Cauchy–Schwarz turns each expected Hamming distance into $\sqrt{n}$ times the root of Marton's cost. Taking $B = A_t^c$ gives $d_H(A,B) > t$, which rearranges into Marton's blowup lemma.

**Solution.** Assume $\mathbb{P}(A), \mathbb{P}(B) > 0$, else the right-hand side is $+\infty$. Set $\mathbb{Q}_A(\cdot) = \mathbb{P}(\cdot \mid A)$ and $\mathbb{Q}_B(\cdot) = \mathbb{P}(\cdot \mid B)$. Then
$$D(\mathbb{Q}_A\|\mathbb{P}) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_A}\left[\log\frac{\mathbb{1}_A}{\mathbb{P}(A)}\right] = \log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(A)},$$
and similarly $D(\mathbb{Q}_B\|\mathbb{P}) = \log(1/\mathbb{P}(B))$.

Let $P_A \in \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q}_A)$ and $P_B \in \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q}_B)$ attain the minima in Theorem 8.2. Glue them: let $(X,Y,Z)$ with $X \sim \mathbb{P}$ and, conditionally on $X$, $Y \sim P_A(\cdot \mid X)$ and $Z \sim P_B(\cdot \mid X)$ independent. Then $(X,Y) \sim P_A$, $(X,Z) \sim P_B$, so $Y \sim \mathbb{Q}_A$ (hence $Y \in A$ a.s.) and $Z \sim \mathbb{Q}_B$ (hence $Z \in B$ a.s.).

By the triangle inequality for Hamming distance, a.s.
$$d_H(A,B) \le d_H(Y,Z) \le d_H(X,Y) + d_H(X,Z).$$
Taking expectations and writing $d_H(X,Y) = \sum_i \mathbb{1}_{\{X_i \ne Y_i\}}$,
$$d_H(A,B) \le \sum_{i=1}^n P_A\{X_i \ne Y_i\} + \sum_{i=1}^n P_B\{X_i \ne Z_i\}.$$
By Cauchy–Schwarz, $\sum_i P_A\{X_i \ne Y_i\} \le \sqrt{n\sum_i P_A\{X_i \ne Y_i\}^2}$, and Theorem 8.2 gives $\sum_i P_A\{X_i \ne Y_i\}^2 \le \frac12 D(\mathbb{Q}_A\|\mathbb{P}) = \frac12\log(1/\mathbb{P}(A))$. Hence
$$\sum_{i=1}^n P_A\{X_i \ne Y_i\} \le \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(A)}},$$
and likewise for $B$. Adding yields
$$d_H(A,B) \le \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(A)}} + \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(B)}}.$$

Now take $B = A_t^c$, where $A_t = \{x : d_H(x,A) \le t\}$ is the $t$-blowup of $A$. For $x \in A$ and $y \in B$, $d_H(x,y) > t$ (else $y \in A_t$), so $d_H(A,B) > t$. If $\mathbb{P}(B) = 0$ then $\mathbb{P}(A_t) = 1$ and there is nothing to prove; else, with $a = \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}\log(1/\mathbb{P}(A))}$,
$$t < a + \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(B)}}.$$
For $t > a$ this gives $\frac{n}{2}\log(1/\mathbb{P}(B)) > (t-a)^2$, i.e. $\mathbb{P}(B) < \exp(-\frac{2}{n}(t-a)^2)$. Since $\mathbb{P}(B) = 1 - \mathbb{P}(A_t)$,
$$\mathbb{P}(A_t) > 1 - \exp\left(-\frac{2}{n}\left(t - \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(A)}}\right)^2\right), \qquad t > \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(A)}},$$
which is Marton's blowup lemma. In particular, if $\mathbb{P}(A) \ge 1/2$ then $\mathbb{P}(A_t) \ge 1 - \exp(-\frac{2}{n}(t-\sqrt{\frac{n}{2}\log 2})^2)$ for $t > \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}\log 2}$. ∎
