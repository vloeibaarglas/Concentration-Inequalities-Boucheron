**Central idea.** Apply the layer-cake identity $|a-b|=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathbf{1}_{\{a>x\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{b>x\}}|\,dx$ to $a=F^{-1}(t)$, $b=G^{-1}(t)$, swap the integrals by Tonelli, and use the generalized-inverse duality $F^{-1}(t)>x\iff t>F(x)$ to turn the inner $t$-integral into the measure of $t$ strictly between $F(x)$ and $G(x)$, namely $|F(x)-G(x)|$.

**Solution.** For real $a,b$,
$$|a-b|=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big|\mathbf{1}_{\{a>x\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{b>x\}}\big|\,dx,$$
the integrand being $1$ exactly on the interval between $a$ and $b$. Hence, with $F^{-1},G^{-1}$ the generalized inverses,
$$\int_0^1\big|F^{-1}(t)-G^{-1}(t)\big|\,dt=\int_0^1\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big|\mathbf{1}_{\{F^{-1}(t)>x\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{G^{-1}(t)>x\}}\big|\,dx\,dt.$$
By Tonelli's theorem (nonnegative integrand; both sides lie in $[0,\infty]$),
$$=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_0^1\big|\mathbf{1}_{\{F^{-1}(t)>x\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{G^{-1}(t)>x\}}\big|\,dt\,dx.$$
For the generalized inverse $F^{-1}(t)=\inf\{y:F(y)\ge t\}$ and fixed $x$,
$$F^{-1}(t)>x\iff t>F(x):$$
indeed, if $\inf\{y:F(y)\ge t\}>x$ then $x\notin\{y:F(y)\ge t\}$, so $F(x)<t$; conversely, if $F(x)<t$, right-continuity of $F$ gives $\delta>0$ with $F<t$ on $[x,x+\delta)$, so the infimum exceeds $x$. Thus $\mathbf{1}_{\{F^{-1}(t)>x\}}=\mathbf{1}_{\{t>F(x)\}}$, and similarly for $G$. The inner integral becomes, for fixed $x$ with $F(x),G(x)\in[0,1]$,
$$\int_0^1\big|\mathbf{1}_{\{t>F(x)\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{t>G(x)\}}\big|\,dt,$$
whose integrand is $1$ exactly when $t$ lies strictly between $F(x)$ and $G(x)$ — a subset of $[0,1]$ of Lebesgue measure $|F(x)-G(x)|$. Therefore
$$\int_0^1\big|F^{-1}(t)-G^{-1}(t)\big|\,dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|F(x)-G(x)|\,dx.\ ∎$$
