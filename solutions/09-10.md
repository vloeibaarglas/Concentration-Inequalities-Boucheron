**Central idea.** Here $p_t$ is the critical parameter: $P_{p_t}(A)=t$. By Russo's lemma, $\frac12=P_{p_{3/4}}(A)-P_{p_{1/4}}(A)=\int_{p_{1/4}}^{p_{3/4}}I^p(A)\,dp$. So it suffices to bound the total influence from above on the window. For monotone $A$, $I_i^p=\mathrm{Cov}_p(\mathbf 1_A,X_i)/(2p(1-p))$, and Cauchy–Schwarz then gives $I^p(A)\le\frac12\sqrt{n/(p(1-p))}$. A union-of-copies argument shows that the window $[p_{1/4},p_{3/4}]$ lies inside $[\frac16,\frac56]$ when $p_{1/2}=\frac12$, so $p(1-p)\ge\frac5{36}$ there. Together these give $p_{3/4}-p_{1/4}\ge\frac{\sqrt5}{6\sqrt n}$. Majority shows that the order $1/\sqrt n$ cannot be improved.

**Solution.** We use the notation of Section 9.5. $P_p$ is the product measure on $\{-1,1\}^n$ with $P_p\{X_i=1\}=p$, and $q=1-p$. $A$ is monotone, i.e. an up-set, and $f=\mathbf 1_A$. $I_i^p=P_p\{i\text{ is pivotal}\}$ and $I^p=\sum_iI_i^p$. Since $p\mapsto P_p(A)$ is a polynomial with derivative $I^p(A)$ (Russo's lemma), and $P_{1/2}(A)=\frac12$ means $A$ is nontrivial, $I^p(A)>0$ on $(0,1)$. So $P_p(A)$ increases strictly from $0$ to $1$, and $p_t$ is well defined for every $t\in(0,1)$.

*Correction to the previous version.* That version read $p_q$ as $P_q(A)$ and "proved" the $n$-free bound $p_{3/4}-p_{1/4}\ge1-e^{-1/2}\approx0.3935$. With the correct reading this is false. Majority on $n=101$ voters, $A=\{\sum_ix_i>0\}$, has $p_{1/2}=\frac12$ by symmetry, yet $p_{3/4}-p_{1/4}=0.0669$. The statement of the exercise itself is correct.

*Step 1: covariance identity.* Fix $i$ and write $f(x)=f(x^{(i,-1)})+\mathbf 1\{x_i=1\}D_if(x)$, where $D_if=f(x^{(i,1)})-f(x^{(i,-1)})$. By monotonicity $D_if\in\{0,1\}$, and $D_if=\mathbf 1\{i\text{ pivotal}\}$. Neither $f(x^{(i,-1)})$ nor $D_if$ depends on $x_i$, and $X_i$ is independent of $X_{-i}$. Hence
$$\mathrm{Cov}_p(f,X_i)=\mathbb{E}_p[D_if]\,\mathrm{Cov}_p(\mathbf 1\{X_i=1\},X_i)=I_i^p\cdot2pq,$$
because $X_i=2\cdot\mathbf 1\{X_i=1\}-1$ gives $\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf 1\{X_i=1\},X_i)=2\,\mathrm{Var}(\mathbf 1\{X_i=1\})=2pq$.

*Step 2: upper bound on total influence.* Sum Step 1 over $i$ and apply Cauchy–Schwarz. Since $\mathrm{Var}_p(f)\le\frac14$ and $\mathrm{Var}_p(\sum_iX_i)=4npq$,
$$I^p(A)=\frac{\mathrm{Cov}_p\big(f,\sum_iX_i\big)}{2pq}\le\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_p(f)\cdot4npq}}{2pq}\le\frac12\sqrt{\frac{n}{pq}}.$$

*Step 3: the window lies in $[\frac16,\frac56]$.* Let $X^{(1)},X^{(2)},X^{(3)}$ be independent with law $P_p$, and let $U$ be their coordinatewise maximum. Then $U\sim P_{p'}$ with $p'=1-q^3\le3p$. Since $A$ is an up-set, $U\in A$ whenever some $X^{(k)}\in A$, so
$$P_{p'}(A)\ge1-(1-P_p(A))^3.$$
Take $p=p_{1/4}$. Then $P_{p'}(A)\ge1-(\frac34)^3=\frac{37}{64}>\frac12$, so $\frac12=p_{1/2}<p'\le3p_{1/4}$, i.e. $p_{1/4}>\frac16$.

For the other end, the dual set $A^*=\{x:-x\notin A\}$ is also an up-set. It satisfies $P_p(A^*)=1-P_{1-p}(A)$, so its critical values are $p^*_t=1-p_{1-t}$, and in particular $p^*_{1/2}=\frac12$. Applying the same argument to $A^*$ gives $1-p_{3/4}=p^*_{1/4}>\frac16$.

Hence $[p_{1/4},p_{3/4}]\subset(\frac16,\frac56)$. On this interval $pq\ge\frac16\cdot\frac56=\frac5{36}$, because $pq$ is concave and symmetric about $\frac12$.

*Step 4: conclusion.* By Russo's lemma, Step 2 and Step 3,
$$\frac12=\int_{p_{1/4}}^{p_{3/4}}I^p(A)\,dp\le(p_{3/4}-p_{1/4})\cdot\frac12\sqrt{\frac{36\,n}{5}},$$
so
$$p_{3/4}-p_{1/4}\ \ge\ \frac{\sqrt5}{6}\cdot\frac1{\sqrt n}\approx\frac{0.373}{\sqrt n}.$$
This proves the claim with the universal constant $c=\sqrt5/6$. Using only the trivial window $[0,1]$ would not work, because $\sqrt{n/(pq)}$ blows up at the ends. That is why Step 3 is needed.

*Checks and sharpness.* Numerically, Step 1, Step 2 and the three-copy inequality of Step 3 hold on 300 random monotone sets with $n\le6$ and random $p$. For majority, $\sqrt n\,(p_{3/4}-p_{1/4})$ is $0.50$, $0.60$, $0.65$, $0.67$, $0.674$ for $n=1,3,11,101,10001$. These values are always above $\sqrt5/6\approx0.373$. They also stay bounded, so the order $1/\sqrt n$ is best possible. ∎
