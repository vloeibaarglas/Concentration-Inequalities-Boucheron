**Central idea.** $\mathbb{E}f(X)$ is a polynomial in $p$ (a finite sum of products of linear factors), hence differentiable; differentiating the product $\prod_{j=1}^n \mu_p(x_j)$ with $\mu_p(1) = p$, $\mu_p(-1) = 1-p$ term-by-term, the derivative of the $i$-th factor is $x_i = \pm 1$, and summing over $x_i \in \{-1,1\}$ against the frozen marginal of $X_{-i}$ produces exactly the difference $f(X_{-i},1) - f(X_{-i},-1)$.

**Solution.** For $p \in (0,1)$ write $\mu_p(1) = p$, $\mu_p(-1) = 1-p$, so that
$$\mathbb{E}_p f(X) := \mathbb{E}f(X) = \sum_{x \in \{-1,1\}^n} f(x) \prod_{j=1}^n \mu_p(x_j).$$
This is a polynomial in $p$ of degree at most $n$ (each coordinate contributes a factor linear in $p$), hence $C^\infty$ in $p$; we may differentiate term-by-term. Since $\frac{d}{dp}\mu_p(1) = 1$ and $\frac{d}{dp}\mu_p(-1) = -1$, i.e. $\frac{d}{dp}\mu_p(x_i) = x_i$ for $x_i \in \{-1,1\}$, the product rule gives
$$\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{E}_p f(X) = \sum_{x \in \{-1,1\}^n} f(x) \sum_{i=1}^n x_i \prod_{j \ne i} \mu_p(x_j).$$
Interchanging the sums and writing $x = (x_{-i}, x_i)$ with $x_{-i} \in \{-1,1\}^{n-1}$,
$$\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{E}_p f(X) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{x_{-i}} \sum_{x_i \in \{-1,1\}} f(x_{-i}, x_i)\, x_i \prod_{j \ne i} \mu_p(x_j).$$
For fixed $i$ and $x_{-i}$, the inner sum over $x_i$ is
$$f(x_{-i}, 1)\cdot 1 + f(x_{-i}, -1)\cdot(-1) = f(x_{-i}, 1) - f(x_{-i}, -1),$$
while $\prod_{j \ne i}\mu_p(x_j) = \mathbb{P}_p\{X_{-i} = x_{-i}\}$ is the marginal of the remaining coordinates. Hence the $i$-th summand equals
$$\sum_{x_{-i}} \big(f(x_{-i}, 1) - f(x_{-i}, -1)\big)\,\mathbb{P}_p\{X_{-i} = x_{-i}\} = \mathbb{E}_p\big[f(X_1,\ldots,X_{i-1},1,X_{i+1},\ldots,X_n) - f(X_1,\ldots,X_{i-1},-1,X_{i+1},\ldots,X_n)\big],$$
completing the proof. For $f = \mathbf{1}_A$ with $A$ increasing, the bracketed difference is $\mathbf{1}\{i\text{ is pivotal for }A\}$, recovering the classical Russo's lemma. ∎
