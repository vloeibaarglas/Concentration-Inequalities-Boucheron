**Central idea.** Induct on the dimension by splitting on the last coordinate. If $A^\pm\subseteq\{-1,1\}^{n-1}$ are the two sections of $A$, the total influence decomposes exactly as $I(A)=\frac12\big(I(A^+)+I(A^-)\big)+\mu(A^+\triangle A^-)$, and the distance obeys $d((x',\pm1),A)=\min\big(d(x',A^\pm),\,1+d(x',A^\mp)\big)$. Keeping the minimum (via $\mathbb{E}\min\le\min\mathbb{E}$) rather than averaging the two sections is what closes the induction: the leftover term reduces to $\frac{(b-a)\delta}{2}\le|a-b|$ with $\delta\le1$. (No greedy paths or injections are used; the earlier injectivity route was false and is abandoned.)

**Solution.** Throughout, $I_i(A)=\mathbb{P}\{\mathbb{1}_A(X)\ne\mathbb{1}_A(X^{(i)})\}$ and $I(A)=\sum_{i=1}^nI_i(A)$, as defined in Section 9.1 (recalled from Chapter 4), where $X^{(i)}$ is $X$ with its $i$-th coordinate flipped. The statement only makes sense for $A\ne\emptyset$, which we assume. We prove, by induction on $n\ge0$, that for every nonempty $A\subseteq\{-1,1\}^n$,
$$2\,\mathbb{P}(A)\,\mathbb{E}d(X,A)\le I(A).\tag{1}$$
Dividing by $2\mathbb{P}(A)>0$ gives the claim.

*Base case $n=0$.* The cube is a single point, $A$ is the whole space, $d\equiv0$ and both sides of (1) vanish.

*Induction step.* Let $n\ge1$ and assume (1) in dimension $n-1$. Write $x=(x',x_n)$ with $x'\in\{-1,1\}^{n-1}$, let $\mu$ be the uniform probability on $\{-1,1\}^{n-1}$, and let $X'$ be uniform on $\{-1,1\}^{n-1}$. Define the sections
$$A^+=\{x':(x',1)\in A\},\qquad A^-=\{x':(x',-1)\in A\},\qquad a=\mu(A^+),\ b=\mu(A^-),$$
so $\mathbb{P}(A)=\frac{a+b}{2}$.

*Influences.* For $i<n$, conditioning on $X_n$,
$$I_i(A)=\tfrac12\,\mu\{\mathbb{1}_{A^+}(x')\ne\mathbb{1}_{A^+}(x'^{(i)})\}+\tfrac12\,\mu\{\mathbb{1}_{A^-}(x')\ne\mathbb{1}_{A^-}(x'^{(i)})\}=\tfrac12\big(I_i(A^+)+I_i(A^-)\big),$$
and $I_n(A)=\mu\{x':\mathbb{1}_{A^+}(x')\ne\mathbb{1}_{A^-}(x')\}=\mu(A^+\triangle A^-)$. Hence
$$I(A)=\tfrac12\big(I(A^+)+I(A^-)\big)+\mu(A^+\triangle A^-),\tag{2}$$
with the convention $I(\emptyset)=0$. Note $\mu(A^+\triangle A^-)\ge|\mu(A^+)-\mu(A^-)|=|a-b|$.

*Distances.* A point $y\in A$ is either $(y',1)$ with $y'\in A^+$, at distance $d(x',y')+\mathbb{1}\{x_n\ne1\}$ from $x$, or $(y',-1)$ with $y'\in A^-$, at distance $d(x',y')+\mathbb{1}\{x_n\ne-1\}$. Therefore, with $d(\cdot,\emptyset)=+\infty$,
$$d((x',1),A)=\min\big(d(x',A^+),\,1+d(x',A^-)\big),\qquad d((x',-1),A)=\min\big(d(x',A^-),\,1+d(x',A^+)\big).\tag{3}$$

Since $A\ne\emptyset$, at least one section is nonempty. The map $(x',x_n)\mapsto(x',-x_n)$ preserves the uniform measure, distances and influences and swaps $A^+$ and $A^-$, so we may relabel freely.

**Case 1: both sections nonempty.** Put $\Phi_\pm=\mathbb{E}d(X',A^\pm)<\infty$ and, after relabeling, assume $\Phi_+\ge\Phi_-$. Let $\delta=\min(\Phi_+-\Phi_-,1)\in[0,1]$. By (3) and $\mathbb{E}\min(U,V)\le\min(\mathbb{E}U,\mathbb{E}V)$,
$$\mathbb{E}d(X,A)=\tfrac12\,\mathbb{E}\min\big(d(X',A^+),1+d(X',A^-)\big)+\tfrac12\,\mathbb{E}\min\big(d(X',A^-),1+d(X',A^+)\big)\le\tfrac12\big[\min(\Phi_+,1+\Phi_-)+\Phi_-\big]=\Phi_-+\tfrac\delta2.$$
By the induction hypothesis, $I(A^+)\ge2a\Phi_+$ and $I(A^-)\ge2b\Phi_-$. With (2), $\Phi_+\ge\Phi_-+\delta$ and $\mu(A^+\triangle A^-)\ge|a-b|$,
$$I(A)\ge a\Phi_++b\Phi_-+|a-b|\ge(a+b)\Phi_-+a\delta+|a-b|.$$
Meanwhile $2\mathbb{P}(A)\,\mathbb{E}d(X,A)\le(a+b)\Phi_-+\frac{(a+b)\delta}{2}$. So (1) follows once
$$\frac{(a+b)\delta}{2}\le a\delta+|a-b|,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\frac{(b-a)\delta}{2}\le|a-b|.$$
If $b\le a$ the left side is $\le0$. If $b>a$, it is $\frac{(b-a)\delta}{2}\le\frac{b-a}{2}\le|a-b|$, because $\delta\le1$.

**Case 2: one section empty.** After relabeling, $A^+=\emptyset$ and $A^-\ne\emptyset$, so $a=0$ and $b>0$. By (3), $d((x',1),A)=1+d(x',A^-)$ and $d((x',-1),A)=d(x',A^-)$. Hence, with $\Phi_-=\mathbb{E}d(X',A^-)$,
$$\mathbb{E}d(X,A)=\Phi_-+\tfrac12,\qquad 2\mathbb{P}(A)=b,\qquad I(A)=\tfrac12I(A^-)+\mu(A^-)=\tfrac12I(A^-)+b$$
by (2). The induction hypothesis gives $b\Phi_-\le\frac12I(A^-)$, so
$$2\mathbb{P}(A)\,\mathbb{E}d(X,A)=b\Phi_-+\tfrac b2\le\tfrac12I(A^-)+b=I(A).$$

This completes the induction, so for every nonempty $A\subseteq\{-1,1\}^n$,
$$\mathbb{E}d(X,A)\le\frac{I(A)}{2\mathbb{P}(A)}.$$

*Checks.* For a single point $A=\{a\}$: $\mathbb{E}d=\frac n2$, $I_i(A)=2\cdot2^{-n}$ and $\mathbb{P}(A)=2^{-n}$, so the bound is $n$. For the half-cube $A=\{x_1=1\}$: $\mathbb{E}d=\frac12$ and the bound is $\frac{1}{2\cdot\frac12}=1$. An exhaustive computer check of (1) over all nonempty $A\subseteq\{-1,1\}^n$ for $n\le4$ gives a largest ratio $\mathbb{E}d(X,A)\big/\frac{I(A)}{2\mathbb{P}(A)}$ of exactly $\frac12$ for each $n\le4$; on 3,000 uniformly random sets for $n=6$ the ratio stays below $\frac12$ (largest $\approx0.23$). $\blacksquare$
