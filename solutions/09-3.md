**Central idea.** This is the Benjamini–Kalai–Schramm proof of Talagrand's $L_1$–$L_2$ inequality. Write $\mathrm{Var}(f)$ via the Fourier coefficients of the discrete derivatives $g_i = (f-f^{(i)})/2$, use $\frac{1}{|S|} \leq \frac{3}{2|S|+1}$ to introduce the integral $\int_0^1 \gamma^{2|S|}d\gamma$, recognize the noise operator and apply Bonami–Beckner (Corollary 5.17) to get $\|g_i\|_{1+\gamma^2}^2$, then interpolate with Hölder and evaluate the resulting calculus integral. (Note: the hint's $\log\frac{\|g_i\|_1}{\|g_i\|_2}$ is a typo — see the remark at the end.)

**Solution.** Let $g_i(x) = \frac{f(x)-f(x^{(i)})}{2}$, so $\hat{g}_i(S) = \hat{f}(S)\mathbb{1}_{\{i \in S\}}$ and
$$\mathrm{Var}(f) = \sum_{S \neq \emptyset}\hat{f}(S)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{S \neq \emptyset}\frac{\hat{g}_i(S)^2}{|S|}.$$
For $|S| \geq 1$, $\frac{1}{|S|} \leq \frac{3}{2|S|+1}$, and $\int_0^1 \gamma^{2|S|}d\gamma = \frac{1}{2|S|+1}$, so for each $i$,
$$\sum_{S \neq \emptyset}\frac{\hat{g}_i(S)^2}{|S|} \leq 3\sum_{S}\frac{\hat{g}_i(S)^2}{2|S|+1} = 3\int_0^1\sum_{S}\hat{g}_i(S)^2\gamma^{2|S|}d\gamma.$$
Since $\sum_S \hat{g}_i(S)^2\gamma^{2|S|} = \|T_\gamma g_i\|_2^2$ where $T_\gamma$ is the noise operator, the Bonami–Beckner inequality (Corollary 5.17) gives $\|T_\gamma g_i\|_2 \leq \|g_i\|_{1+\gamma^2}$, hence
$$\mathrm{Var}(f) \leq 3\sum_{i=1}^n\int_0^1 \|g_i\|_{1+\gamma^2}^2\,d\gamma. \tag{1}$$

Fix $i$ with $\|g_i\|_2 > 0$ (otherwise the summand is $0$; note $\|g_i\|_2 > 0$ implies $\|g_i\|_1 > 0$). By Hölder (log-convexity of $L^p$ norms), for $p = 1+\gamma^2$,
$$\|g_i\|_{1+\gamma^2} \leq \|g_i\|_1^{\frac{1-\gamma^2}{1+\gamma^2}}\|g_i\|_2^{\frac{2\gamma^2}{1+\gamma^2}},$$
so with $r_i = \|g_i\|_1/\|g_i\|_2 \in (0,1]$,
$$\int_0^1\|g_i\|_{1+\gamma^2}^2d\gamma \leq \|g_i\|_2^2\int_0^1 r_i^{\frac{2(1-\gamma^2)}{1+\gamma^2}}d\gamma. \tag{2}$$

*Calculus lemma.* For $t \geq 0$, $\int_0^1 e^{-2t\frac{1-\gamma^2}{1+\gamma^2}}d\gamma \leq \frac{6}{5(1+t)}$.

*Proof.* Since $\frac{1-\gamma^2}{1+\gamma^2} - (1-\gamma) = \frac{\gamma(1-\gamma)^2}{1+\gamma^2} \geq 0$ on $[0,1]$, the integral is at most $\int_0^1 e^{-2t(1-\gamma)}d\gamma = \frac{1-e^{-2t}}{2t}$ (and equals $1$ at $t=0$). For $t \leq 1/5$, $1 \leq \frac{6}{5(1+t)}$. For $t \geq 1/5$, $\frac{1-e^{-2t}}{2t} \leq \frac{6}{5(1+t)}$ is equivalent to $k(t) := 1+t-e^{2t}(1-1.4t) \geq 0$; $k'(t) = 1-e^{2t}(0.6-2.8t) > 0.4 > 0$ since $e^{2t}(0.6-2.8t)$ is strictly decreasing from $0.6$, and $k(1/5) = 1.2-0.72e^{0.4} > 1.2-0.72\cdot1.5 > 0$. Hence $k \geq 0$ on $[1/5,\infty)$. ∎

With $t_i = \log(1/r_i) = \log(\|g_i\|_2/\|g_i\|_1) \geq 0$, the lemma gives
$$\int_0^1 r_i^{\frac{2(1-\gamma^2)}{1+\gamma^2}}d\gamma \leq \frac{6}{5\big(1+\log\frac{\|g_i\|_2}{\|g_i\|_1}\big)}.$$
Combined with (1)–(2),
$$\mathrm{Var}(f) \leq \frac{18}{5}\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\|g_i\|_2^2}{1+\log\frac{\|g_i\|_2}{\|g_i\|_1}}.$$
Since $\|g_i\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}[(f(X)-f(X^{(i)}))^2]$, this is Theorem 9.9 with $C = \frac{18}{5}\cdot\frac14 = \frac{9}{10}$. ∎

*Remark (typo in the hint).* The hint writes the final denominator as $1+\log\frac{\|g_i\|_1}{\|g_i\|_2}$. Since $\|g_i\|_1 \leq \|g_i\|_2$, this logarithm is $\leq 0$, and for $\|g_i\|_1/\|g_i\|_2 < 1/e$ the right-hand side would be negative while the left is positive — the inequality would be false. The correct form, consistent with the calculus above and with Theorem 9.9's proof, is $1+\log\frac{\|g_i\|_2}{\|g_i\|_1}$.
