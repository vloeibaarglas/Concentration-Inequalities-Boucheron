**Central idea.** Write $f=\mathbf{1}_A$ and $\phi(p)=\mathbb{P}_p(A)$. Couple two independent $\mu_p$ samples $x,y$ by resampling coordinates one at a time from $x$ to $y$: if $f(x)\ne f(y)$ then $f$ must change value at some resampling step, and the change probability at step $i$ is exactly $2p(1-p)I_i^p$. The union bound plus Russo's lemma (Theorem 9.15) yields the log-odds differential inequality $\frac{d}{dp}\log\frac{\phi}{1-\phi}\ge\frac1{p(1-p)}$; integrating from $p_{1/2}$ to $p_{1-\varepsilon}$ bounds $p_{1-\varepsilon}$ by a constant multiple of $p_{1/2}$. The case $p_{1/2}>1/2$ reduces to this via the symmetry $g(x)=1-f(-x)$.

**Solution.** Let $f=\mathbf{1}_A:\{-1,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$, monotone and non-constant (otherwise $p_\alpha$ is undefined), and $\phi(p)=\mathbb{P}_p(A)=\mathbb{E}_p[f]$, a polynomial in $p$. By Theorem 9.15 (Russo's lemma), $\phi'(p)=\sum_{i=1}^n I_i^p$ where $I_i^p=\mathbb{P}_p(\{x:f(x)\ne f(\bar x^{(i)})\})$. Monotonicity and non-constancy give $f(-1,\ldots,-1)=0$, $f(1,\ldots,1)=1$, so $\phi(0+)=0$, $\phi(1-)=1$; since some influence is strictly positive on $(0,1)$, $\phi'>0$ there and $\phi$ is strictly increasing. Hence for each $\alpha\in(0,1)$ there is a unique $p_\alpha\in(0,1)$ with $\phi(p_\alpha)=\alpha$.

*Reduction to $p_{1/2}\le1/2$.* Put $g(x)=1-f(-x)$, monotone and non-constant. Since $-x\sim\mu_{1-p}$ under $x\sim\mu_p$, $\mathbb{E}_p[g]=1-\phi(1-p)$, whence $p_\alpha(g)=1-p_{1-\alpha}(f)$. Thus $p_{1/2}(g)=1-p_{1/2}(f)$, $\min(p_{1/2}(g),1-p_{1/2}(g))=\min(p_{1/2}(f),1-p_{1/2}(f))$, and $p_{1-\varepsilon}(g)-p_\varepsilon(g)=p_{1-\varepsilon}(f)-p_\varepsilon(f)$. It suffices to prove the claim under $p_{1/2}\le1/2$.

*Differential inequality.* Let $x,y$ be independent with law $\mu_p$. Set $z^{(0)}=x$ and let $z^{(i)}$ be $z^{(i-1)}$ with its $i$-th coordinate replaced by $y_i$, so $z^{(n)}=y$. If $f(x)\ne f(y)$ then $f(z^{(i-1)})\ne f(z^{(i)})$ for some $i$, hence
$$\mathbb{P}_p(f(x)\ne f(y))\le\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{P}_p(f(z^{(i-1)})\ne f(z^{(i)})).\tag{1}$$
By independence the left side is $2\phi(p)(1-\phi(p))$. For the right side, $z^{(i-1)}$ and $z^{(i)}$ share all coordinates but the $i$-th ($x_i$ versus $y_i$); writing $w$ for the shared part, $w\sim\mu_p^{\,n-1}$ is independent of the iid pair $(x_i,y_i)$. Then $f$ differs at step $i$ iff $x_i\ne y_i$ (probability $2p(1-p)$) and $i$ is pivotal for $w$, so
$$\mathbb{P}_p(f(z^{(i-1)})\ne f(z^{(i)}))=2p(1-p)\,I_i^p.$$
Summing over $i$ and applying Russo's lemma to (1) gives, for all $p\in(0,1)$,
$$2\phi(p)(1-\phi(p))\le2p(1-p)\phi'(p),\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\frac{d}{dp}\log\frac{\phi(p)}{1-\phi(p)}=\frac{\phi'(p)}{\phi(p)(1-\phi(p))}\ge\frac1{p(1-p)}.\tag{2}$$
(Monotonicity is not used in (2), only for $p_\alpha$ to be well defined.)

*Integration.* Assume $p_{1/2}\le1/2$. On $[p_{1/2},p_{1-\varepsilon}]$ (nonempty since $1-\varepsilon>1/2$) we have $\phi\in[1/2,1-\varepsilon]\subset(0,1)$; integrating (2),
$$\log\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}=\left[\log\frac{\phi}{1-\phi}\right]_{p_{1/2}}^{p_{1-\varepsilon}}\ge\int_{p_{1/2}}^{p_{1-\varepsilon}}\frac{dq}{q(1-q)}=\log\frac{p_{1-\varepsilon}}{1-p_{1-\varepsilon}}-\log\frac{p_{1/2}}{1-p_{1/2}}.$$
Thus, with $K=(1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon$,
$$\frac{p_{1-\varepsilon}}{1-p_{1-\varepsilon}}\le K\,\frac{p_{1/2}}{1-p_{1/2}}\le2K\,p_{1/2},$$
using $p_{1/2}\le1/2$. Since $\frac{q}{1-q}\ge q$ for $q\in[0,1)$,
$$p_{1-\varepsilon}-p_\varepsilon\le p_{1-\varepsilon}\le\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\,p_{1/2}=\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\min(p_{1/2},1-p_{1/2}),$$
the claim with $c=2(1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon$ depending only on $\varepsilon$. ∎
