**Central idea.** This is the $\mathbb{P}_p$-generalization of the book's $p=1/2$ computation that the total influence $I(A) = 2^{-n}\sum_{x \in A}(2\|x\|-n)$ is maximized by the Hamming ball. Differentiating $\mathbb{P}_p(A) = \sum_{x \in A}p^{\|x\|}(1-p)^{n-\|x\|}$ gives $\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{P}_p(A) = \frac{1}{p(1-p)}\sum_{x \in A}(\|x\|-np)\mathbb{P}_p(x)$, and at any $p \in (0,1)$ with $\mathbb{P}_p(A) = \mathbb{P}_p(B)$, the Hamming ball $B = \{x : \|x\| \ge k\}$ strictly maximizes this derivative among all sets of the same $\mathbb{P}_p$-measure (unless $A = B$). Since $\mathbb{P}_q(A) = 1/2 \le \mathbb{P}_q(B)$ at $q = p_{1/2}(A) \ge p_{1/2}(B)$, a last-crossing argument shows $A$'s curve can never overtake $B$'s.

**Solution.** Write $\|x\|$ for the number of $+1$ coordinates of $x \in \{-1,1\}^n$, and $f(p) = \mathbb{P}_p(A)$, $g(p) = \mathbb{P}_p(B)$, where $\mathbb{P}_p$ is the product measure with $\mathbb{P}_p\{x_i = +1\} = p$. Both $f$ and $g$ are polynomials in $p$, hence $C^\infty$ on $[0,1]$, and nondecreasing since $A$ and $B$ are monotone. Let $q = p_{1/2}(A)$ and $r = p_{1/2}(B)$, so $f(q) = 1/2 = g(r)$ and $q \ge r$ by hypothesis. Since $k \ge 1$, $\mathbb{P}_0(B) = 0 < 1/2$, so $r > 0$ and hence $q > 0$. Moreover $f(q) = 1/2 \le g(q)$, as $g$ is nondecreasing and $q \ge r$.

For any $S \subset \{-1,1\}^n$ and $p \in (0,1)$, differentiating termwise,
$$\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{P}_p(S) = \sum_{x \in S}\frac{d}{dp}\Big[p^{\|x\|}(1-p)^{n-\|x\|}\Big] = \frac{1}{p(1-p)}\sum_{x \in S}(\|x\|-np)\,\mathbb{P}_p(x), \tag{*}$$
since $\frac{d}{dp}[p^j(1-p)^{n-j}] = p^j(1-p)^{n-j}\left(\frac{j}{p}-\frac{n-j}{1-p}\right)$.

*Key estimate.* Let $p \in (0,1)$ with $\mathbb{P}_p(A) = \mathbb{P}_p(B) =: \alpha$. Then, with $\delta := \mathbb{P}_p(A \setminus B) = \mathbb{P}_p(B \setminus A)$,
\begin{align*}
&\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{P}_p(A) - \frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{P}_p(B) \\
&\qquad = \frac{1}{p(1-p)}\left[\sum_{x \in A \setminus B}(\|x\|-np)\mathbb{P}_p(x) - \sum_{x \in B \setminus A}(\|x\|-np)\mathbb{P}_p(x)\right].
\end{align*}
For $x \in A \setminus B$, $\|x\| \le k-1$ (as $x \notin B$), so the first sum is at most $(k-1-np)\,\delta$; for $x \in B \setminus A$, $\|x\| \ge k$, so the subtracted sum is at least $(k-np)\,\delta$. Hence
$$\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{P}_p(A) - \frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{P}_p(B) \le \frac{(k-1-np)\delta - (k-np)\delta}{p(1-p)} = \frac{-\delta}{p(1-p)} \le 0,$$
with strict inequality iff $\delta > 0$. Since $\mathbb{P}_p$ charges every $x \in \{-1,1\}^n$ for $p \in (0,1)$, $\delta = 0$ iff $A = B$ as sets. Thus $f'(p) < g'(p)$ whenever $f(p) = g(p)$ and $A \ne B$; if $A = B$ then $f \equiv g$.

Now suppose, for contradiction, that $f(p_0) > g(p_0)$ for some $p_0 > q$. Let
$$p_1 = \sup\{p \in [q, p_0] : f(p) \le g(p)\},$$
nonempty since $f(q) \le g(q)$. By continuity $f(p_1) = g(p_1)$, and $p_1 < p_0 \le 1$ since $f > g$ on $(p_1, p_0]$. If $p_1 = 1$ then $p_0 = 1$ and $f(1) > g(1) = 1$ (as $B \ne \emptyset$), impossible; so $p_1 \in (0,1)$, using $p_1 \ge q > 0$. Since $f(p_1) = g(p_1)$ and $f > g$ immediately to the right of $p_1$, $f'(p_1) \ge g'(p_1)$. But the key estimate gives $f'(p_1) < g'(p_1)$ unless $A = B$, in which case $f \equiv g$, contradicting $f(p_0) > g(p_0)$. This contradiction shows no such $p_0$ exists.

Therefore $f(p) \le g(p)$ for all $p \ge q$, i.e. $\mathbb{P}_p(A) \le \mathbb{P}_p(B)$ for all $p \ge p_{1/2}$. ∎
