**Central idea.** In the proof of Theorem 9.4, replace the uniform log-Sobolev inequality by the $p$-biased one (Theorem 5.2: $\mathrm{Ent}_p(g^2)\le c(p)\,\mathcal{E}(g)$ with $\mathcal{E}(g)=p(1-p)\sum_j\mathbb{E}_p(g-g^{(j)})^2$). Apply it to each martingale difference $\Delta_i$ of $f=\mathbf 1_A$, and use $\mathbb{E}_p|\Delta_i|\le 2p(1-p)I_i^p$ and $\sum_i\mathcal{E}(\Delta_i)=\mathcal{E}(f)=p(1-p)I^p$. This gives the $p$-biased (9.1): $\sum_i (I_i^p)^2\ge \frac{V}{4p^2q^2}e^{-\beta I^p/V}$, where $\beta=c(p)pq\le\frac12$. A fixed-point argument, used instead of the two-case argument, then gives $\max_i I_i^p\ge \frac{y_n}{\beta}\frac Vn$ and $\sum_i (I_i^p)^2\ge (\frac{y_n}{\beta})^2\frac{V^2}{n}$, where $y_n^2e^{y_n}=c(p)^2n/(4V)$.

For $p\neq\frac12$ the inequality $\beta<\frac12$ is strict. That leaves enough room to absorb the printed $\varepsilon$ once $n\ge n_0(p)$. At $p=\frac12$ we can only prove the bound with a larger $\varepsilon_n=O(\log\log n/\log n)$. Corollary 9.18 follows from the max-influence bound and Russo's lemma.

*Notation.*
- $q=1-p$, $f=\mathbf 1_A$, and $V=P_p(A)(1-P_p(A))$, assumed $>0$ (otherwise $\varepsilon$ is undefined).
- For $h:\{-1,1\}^n\to\mathbb R$, let $D_jh(x)=h(x^{(j,1)})-h(x^{(j,-1)})$, where $x^{(j,\pm1)}$ is $x$ with coordinate $j$ set to $\pm1$. Then $(h(x)-h(\bar x^{(j)}))^2=(D_jh(x))^2$.
- So the book's Dirichlet form (pp. 120 and 283) is $\mathcal{E}(h)=pq\sum_j\mathbb{E}_p(D_jh)^2$, and Theorem 5.2 reads $\mathrm{Ent}_p(h^2)\le c(p)\mathcal{E}(h)$.
- For binary $f$, $(D_if)^2=\mathbf 1\{i\text{ pivotal}\}$. Hence $I_i:=I_i^p(A)=\mathbb{E}_p(D_if)^2=\mathbb{E}_p|D_if|$, and $\mathcal{E}(f)=pqI$ with $I=\sum_iI_i$.
- Put $\beta=c(p)pq$.

*$\beta\le\frac12$, with equality only at $p=\frac12$.* For $p<\frac12$ put $t=q/p>1$. Then $\beta=pq\log t/(q-p)$, and
$$\beta<\tfrac12\iff\log t<\frac{q-p}{2pq}=\tfrac12(t-t^{-1}).$$
The function $g(t)=\frac12(t-t^{-1})-\log t$ has $g(1)=0$ and $g'(t)=\frac12(1-t^{-1})^2>0$, so $g>0$ on $(1,\infty)$. The case $p>\frac12$ follows from $c(p)=c(1-p)$. At $p=\frac12$, $c(\frac12)=2$ and $\beta=\frac12$. Numerically, $\max_{p\ne1/2}\beta<\frac12$ on a fine grid.

*Step 1: the $p$-biased (9.1).* Let $\mathcal F_i=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_i)$ and $\Delta_i=\mathbb{E}_p[f\mid\mathcal F_i]-\mathbb{E}_p[f\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]$. Then $f-\mathbb{E}_pf=\sum_i\Delta_i$ and $\sum_i\mathbb{E}_p\Delta_i^2=V$.

(a) We have $f(x)=f(x^{(i,-1)})+\mathbf 1\{x_i=1\}D_if(x)$, where $D_if$ does not depend on $x_i$, and $X_i$ is independent of the other coordinates. Hence
$$\Delta_i=(\mathbf 1\{X_i=1\}-p)\,\mathbb{E}_p[D_if\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}],\qquad\text{so}\qquad \mathbb{E}_p|\Delta_i|=2pq\,\mathbb{E}_p\big|\mathbb{E}_p[D_if\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\big|\le 2pq\,I_i .$$

(b) We show $\sum_i\mathcal{E}(\Delta_i)=\mathcal{E}(f)=pqI$. Fix $j$.
- For $i<j$, $\Delta_i$ does not depend on $x_j$, so $D_j\Delta_i=0$.
- For $i\ge j$, $D_j$ acts on a coordinate that the conditioning keeps, so it commutes with $\mathbb{E}_p[\cdot\mid\mathcal F_i]$.
- For $i=j$: $\mathbb{E}_p[f\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]$ does not depend on $x_j$, and $D_jf$ does not depend on $x_j$. Hence $D_j\Delta_j=\mathbb{E}_p[D_jf\mid\mathcal F_j]=\mathbb{E}_p[D_jf\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]$.
- For $i>j$: $D_j\Delta_i=\mathbb{E}_p[D_jf\mid\mathcal F_i]-\mathbb{E}_p[D_jf\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]$.

So the terms $D_j\Delta_i$, $i\ge j$, are an $\mathcal F_{j-1}$-measurable term followed by the later martingale differences of $D_jf$. They are mutually orthogonal and sum to $D_jf$. Hence $\sum_i\mathbb{E}_p(D_j\Delta_i)^2=\mathbb{E}_p(D_jf)^2=I_j$. Summing over $j$ and multiplying by $pq$ gives the claim. The same computation gives $\sum_i\mathcal{E}(\Delta_i(h))=\mathcal{E}(h)$ for every real-valued $h$; this was checked numerically on random functions.

(c) For any $g$, $\mathrm{Ent}_p(g^2)\ge\mathbb{E}_pg^2\log\frac{\mathbb{E}_pg^2}{(\mathbb{E}_p|g|)^2}$. To see this, let $dQ=g^2\,dP_p/\mathbb{E}_pg^2$. Jensen's inequality gives
$$\mathbb{E}_Q\log|g|\ge-\log\mathbb{E}_Q|g|^{-1}=\log\frac{\mathbb{E}_pg^2}{\mathbb{E}_p|g|},$$
so $\mathbb{E}_p[g^2\log g^2]\ge2\mathbb{E}_pg^2\log\frac{\mathbb{E}_pg^2}{\mathbb{E}_p|g|}$. Subtracting $\mathbb{E}_pg^2\log\mathbb{E}_pg^2$ gives the claim.

Now apply Theorem 5.2 to each nonzero $\Delta_i$ and use (c). Sum over $i$, then use (b), the log-sum inequality, and (a):
$$c(p)\,pq\,I=\sum_ic(p)\mathcal{E}(\Delta_i)\ \ge\ \sum_i\mathbb{E}_p\Delta_i^2\log\frac{\mathbb{E}_p\Delta_i^2}{(\mathbb{E}_p|\Delta_i|)^2}\ \ge\ V\log\frac{V}{\sum_i(\mathbb{E}_p|\Delta_i|)^2}\ \ge\ V\log\frac{V}{4p^2q^2\sum_iI_i^2}.$$
Hence
$$\sum_{i=1}^nI_i^2\ \ge\ \frac{V}{4p^2q^2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\beta I}{V}\Big).\tag{9.1$_p$}$$
At $p=\frac12$ this is exactly the book's (9.1). We verified (9.1$_p$) numerically, together with (a), (b) and Theorem 5.2 for each $\Delta_i$, on 400 random sets with $2\le n\le6$ and random $p$.

*Step 2: fixed-point bounds.* Let $y=y_n>0$ be the unique solution of $y^2e^y=\frac{c(p)^2n}{4V}$. Equivalently, $y+2\log y=s$ with $s:=\log\frac{c(p)^2n}{4V}$; the solution is unique because $y\mapsto y+2\log y$ increases from $-\infty$ to $\infty$. Note that
$$\frac{V}{4p^2q^2}e^{-y}=\frac{V}{4p^2q^2}\cdot\frac{4Vy^2}{c(p)^2n}=\frac{V^2y^2}{\beta^2n}.$$

*Max influence.* Suppose $M:=\max_iI_i<\frac{yV}{\beta n}$. Then $I\le nM<\frac{yV}{\beta}$, so (9.1$_p$) gives $\sum_iI_i^2>\frac{V^2y^2}{\beta^2n}$. But also $\sum_iI_i^2\le MI<\frac{yV}{\beta n}\cdot\frac{yV}{\beta}=\frac{V^2y^2}{\beta^2n}$, a contradiction.

*Sum of squares.* Suppose $\sum_iI_i^2<\frac{V^2y^2}{\beta^2n}$. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $I\le\sqrt{n\sum_iI_i^2}<\frac{Vy}{\beta}$, and then (9.1$_p$) gives $\sum_iI_i^2>\frac{V^2y^2}{\beta^2n}$, a contradiction.

Therefore, for every $n$, every $p$, and every $A$ with $V>0$,
$$\max_iI_i^p(A)\ \ge\ \frac{y_n}{\beta}\cdot\frac{V}{n},\qquad \sum_iI_i^p(A)^2\ \ge\ \Big(\frac{y_n}{\beta}\Big)^2\frac{V^2}{n}.\tag{$\ast$}$$
Both bounds were checked numerically on the same random sets.

Since $c(p)\ge2$ and $V\le\frac14$, we have $s\ge\log(4n)>1$. If $y<1$ then $y+2\log y<1<s$; hence $y\ge1$, and therefore $y\le s$. So $(\ast)$ has the required form, with $2-\varepsilon$ replaced by $y_n/(\beta\log n)$.

To make this explicit, use $1/\beta\ge2$ and $y_n=s-2\log y_n$. This gives $y_n/\beta\ge2y_n=(2-\varepsilon_n')\log n$ with
$$\varepsilon_n'=\frac{2\log(4Vy_n^2/c(p)^2)}{\log n}\le\frac{2\log(s^2/4)}{\log n}.$$
This is $O\big(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\big)$ for fixed $p$ and $V$ bounded below.

*Step 3: the printed $\varepsilon$ for $p\neq\frac12$, $n\ge n_0(p)$.* Write $L=\log n$, $v=\log(1/V)\ge\log4$, and $C=2\log(c(p)/2)\ge0$. Then $s=L+v+C$ and
$$\varepsilon=\frac{\log(Vpq\,L^2)}{c(p)L}=\frac{-v+\log(pq)+2\log L}{c(p)L}.$$
The numerator is at most $2\log L-2\log4$ and $c(p)L\ge2L$. So $\varepsilon\le\max\big(0,\frac{\log L-\log4}{L}\big)<1$, hence $2-\varepsilon>1>0$. It follows that both printed inequalities follow from $(\ast)$ once $y/\beta\ge(2-\varepsilon)L$.

Multiplying by $\beta=c(p)pq$, the condition $y/\beta\ge(2-\varepsilon)L$ becomes
$$y\ge2\beta L+pq\,v-pq\log(pq)-2pq\log L.$$
Substituting $y=L+v+C-2\log y$, this is equivalent to
$$D:=(1-2\beta)L+(1-pq)v+C+pq\log(pq)+2pq\log L-2\log y\ \ge\ 0 .$$
Now bound the terms of $D$:
- $pq\log(pq)\ge\frac14\log\frac14$, because $x\log x$ is decreasing on $(0,\frac14]\subset(0,e^{-1})$;
- $2pq\log L\ge0$ for $n\ge3$;
- $1-pq\ge\frac34$;
- $\log y\le\log s$.

Let $a=1-2\beta>0$ and $m=\min(a,\frac34)\le1$. Since $C\ge0$, $aL+\frac34v+C\ge m(L+v+C)=ms$. Hence
$$D\ \ge\ ms-2\log s-\tfrac{\log4}{4}.$$
The map $s\mapsto ms-2\log s$ is increasing for $s\ge2/m$, and $s\ge L$. Choose $L_0(p)\ge\max(2/m,1)$ such that $mL_0-2\log L_0\ge\frac{\log 4}{4}$. If $L\ge L_0(p)$, then $D\ge0$ for **every** $A$, uniformly in $V$.

Hence, for $p\ne\frac12$ and $n\ge n_0(p)=e^{L_0(p)}$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^nI_i^p(A)^2\ \ge\ \frac{(2-\varepsilon)^2\big(P_p(A)(1-P_p(A))\big)^2\log^2n}{n},\qquad \max_iI_i^p(A)\ \ge\ \frac{(2-\varepsilon)P_p(A)(1-P_p(A))\log n}{n}.$$
The threshold $n_0(p)$ is huge near $p=\frac12$:
- For $p=0.3$, $a\approx0.110$ and $L_0\approx83.3$.
- A numerical minimisation of the exact $D$ over $v$ is negative at $L=40$ and positive at $L=80$ for $p=0.3$.
- For $p=0.45$ and $p=0.49$ it is still negative at $L=160$, consistent with $a\to0$.

*Step 4: what fails at $p=\frac12$.* Here $a=0$, $C=0$ and $pq=\frac14$, so
$$D=\tfrac34v-\tfrac14\log4+\tfrac12\log L-2\log y,$$
with $y=L+v-2\log y\sim L$. For fixed $V$ and $L\to\infty$, $D\approx-\frac32\log L\to-\infty$. So $(\ast)$ does not imply the printed bound at $p=\frac12$; it gives only $\varepsilon_n'$ from Step 2.

The book's two-case argument does not help either. In the case $I<(2-\varepsilon)V\log n$, (9.1) gives $\sum_i I_i^2\ge4Vn^{-1+\varepsilon/2}$. With the printed $\varepsilon$, $n^{\varepsilon/2}=z^{1/4}$, where $z=V\log^2n/4$. The target is $(2-\varepsilon)^2V^2\log^2n/n=4(2-\varepsilon)^2Vz/n$. So this case yields the target only if $z^{1/4}\ge(2-\varepsilon)^2z$, which fails as soon as $(2-\varepsilon)^2z^{3/4}>1$, for example for fixed $V$ and large $n$.

With the book's $4\times$ larger $\varepsilon$, $n^{\varepsilon/2}=z$. The case then closes only for the weaker conclusion $\mathrm{Var}(f)^2\log^2n/n$ of Theorem 9.5, using $\varepsilon<1$ in the other case. Whether the printed inequality holds at $p=\frac12$ is left open here.

*Step 5: Corollary 9.18.* We rename the corollary's $\varepsilon$ to $\eta\in(0,\frac12)$, to avoid a clash with the $\varepsilon$ above. We prove it for all $p$ from $(\ast)$, that is, from the second inequality in the form proved above.

Let $A$ be monotone and nontrivial, with $I_1^p(A)=\dots=I_n^p(A)$ for all $p\in(0,1)$, and let $n>16$. Put $u(p)=P_p(A)\in(0,1)$. By Russo's lemma (Theorem 9.15), $u'(p)=I^p(A)=n\max_iI_i^p(A)$. So by $(\ast)$ and $1/\beta\ge2$,
$$u'(p)\ \ge\ 2y_n(p)\,u(1-u).$$
Since $V\le\frac14$ and $c(p)\ge2$, we have $s\ge t:=\log(4n)$. Let $y^*$ solve $y^*+2\log y^*=t$. Because $y\mapsto y+2\log y$ is increasing, $y_n(p)\ge y^*$. As in Step 2, $1\le y^*\le t$, hence $y^*\ge t-2\log t$.

Also $y^*\ge w:=\frac14\log\frac n{16}>0$:
- if $w\le1$, then $w+2\log w\le w<t$;
- if $w>1$, then $w+2\log w<3w<t$.

Therefore $\frac{d}{dp}\log\frac{u}{1-u}=\frac{u'}{u(1-u)}\ge2y^*$. Integrating over $[p_\eta,p_{1-\eta}]$,
$$2\log\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}\ \ge\ 2y^*(p_{1-\eta}-p_\eta),\qquad p_{1-\eta}-p_\eta\ \le\ \frac{\log\frac{1-\eta}\eta}{y^*}\ \le\ \frac{\log\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}}{\log(4n)-2\log\log(4n)} .$$
Finally, $\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}\le\frac1{4\eta^2}$, which is equivalent to $4\eta(1-\eta)\le1$. This gives $\log\frac{1-\eta}\eta\le2\log\frac1{2\eta}$. Combined with $y^*\ge\frac14\log\frac n{16}$,
$$p_{1-\eta}-p_\eta\ \le\ \frac{8\log\frac1{2\eta}}{\log\frac n{16}},$$
which is Corollary 9.18. The middle bound sharpens it to $(1+o(1))\log\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}/\log n$. ∎
