**Central idea.** Run Friedgut's proof of Theorem 9.7 with the $p$-biased log-Sobolev inequality (Theorem 5.2). It reads $\mathrm{Ent}_p(h^2)\le c(p)\mathcal{E}(h)$, where $\mathcal{E}(h)=p(1-p)\sum_j\mathbb{E}_p(h-h^{(j)})^2$ is the book's Dirichlet form, *not* the variance. Order the variables by decreasing influence, and let $g$ be the martingale truncated after the $m=\lfloor I/\varepsilon\rfloor$ most influential ones. Apply the log-Sobolev inequality to each discarded martingale difference $\Delta_i$, and use three facts:
- $\sum_i\mathcal{E}(\Delta_i)=\mathcal{E}(f)=p(1-p)I$;
- $\mathbb{E}_p|\Delta_i|\le2p(1-p)I_i$;
- $I_i<\varepsilon$ for every discarded $i$.

These give $W\log(W/S)\le c(p)p(1-p)I$ for $W=\mathbb{E}_p(f-g)^2$ and $S\le4p^2(1-p)^2\varepsilon I$. Inverting $x\log x\le y$ yields the bound. For a coarse threshold, Russo's lemma gives a point $p^*$ in the window where $I^{p^*}\le 1/(2K_1)$. $K_2$ keeps $p^*$ away from $0$ and $1$, so the bound at $p^*$ tends to $0$ as $\varepsilon\to0$. Rounding $g$ then gives a junta approximation of $A$.

**Solution.** Throughout, $f=\mathbf 1_A:\{-1,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$ and $q=1-p$, and $\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{E}_p$ unless indicated. $I_i=I_i^p(f)=P_p\{f(X)\ne f(\bar X^{(i)})\}$ and $I=I(f)=\sum_iI_i$. For real $h$, $D_jh(x)=h(x^{(j,1)})-h(x^{(j,-1)})$, so $(h-h^{(j)})^2=(D_jh)^2$ and $\mathcal{E}(h)=pq\sum_j\mathbb{E}(D_jh)^2$ (book, pp. 120 and 283). For binary $f$, $\mathbb{E}(D_jf)^2=I_j$, hence $\mathcal{E}(f)=pqI$. We also use $c(p)\ge2$ and $\beta:=c(p)pq\le\frac12$; both are proved in solution 09-8.

*Correction to the previous version.* It set "$\mathcal{E}(f)=\sum_i\mathbb{E}[\Delta_i^2]$", which is $\mathrm{Var}_p(f)$, and used $\mathrm{Ent}_p(h^2)\le c(p)\mathcal{E}(h)$ with that $\mathcal{E}$. This is false. Take $n=3$, $p=\frac12$ and $f=\mathbf 1\{x_1=x_2=x_3=1\}$: then $\mathrm{Ent}(f^2)=\frac18\log8\approx0.260$, but $c\cdot\mathrm{Var}(f)=2\cdot\frac{7}{64}\approx0.219$. (The book's $c\,\mathcal{E}(f)=0.375$.) Inequality (6) below remains true, by the argument given here.

*Martingale facts.* Fix an ordering $1,\dots,n$ of the coordinates. Let $\mathcal F_i=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_i)$ and $\Delta_i=\mathbb{E}[f\mid\mathcal F_i]-\mathbb{E}[f\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]$. Steps 1(a) and 1(b) of solution 09-8 hold for any ordering:
$$\mathbb{E}|\Delta_i|\le2pq\,I_i,\tag{1}$$
$$\sum_{i=1}^n\mathcal{E}(\Delta_i)=\mathcal{E}(f)=pq\,I,\qquad\mathcal{E}(\Delta_i)\ge0.\tag{2}$$
Also, for every $g$ (09-8, Step 1(c)),
$$\mathrm{Ent}(g^2)\ \ge\ \mathbb{E}g^2\log\frac{\mathbb{E}g^2}{(\mathbb{E}|g|)^2}.\tag{3}$$

*Junta construction.* Order the coordinates so that $I_1\ge I_2\ge\dots\ge I_n$, and put $m=\lfloor I/\varepsilon\rfloor$. If $m\ge n$, take $g=f$. Otherwise $I_i<\varepsilon$ for every $i>m$: if $I_{m+1}\ge\varepsilon$, then $I\ge(m+1)\varepsilon>I$, since $m+1>I/\varepsilon$. Hence
$$\sum_{i>m}I_i^2\ \le\ \varepsilon\sum_{i>m}I_i\ \le\ \varepsilon I.\tag{4}$$
Let $g=\mathbb{E}[f\mid\mathcal F_m]$. It depends only on the $m$ most influential variables. Then $f-g=\sum_{i>m}\Delta_i$ has mean $0$, and
$$W:=\mathbb{E}(f-g)^2=\sum_{i>m}\mathbb{E}\Delta_i^2,\qquad S:=\sum_{i>m}(\mathbb{E}|\Delta_i|)^2\ \le\ 4p^2q^2\varepsilon I\tag{5}$$
by orthogonality, (1) and (4). If $S=0$, every $\Delta_i$ with $i>m$ vanishes, so $W=0$ and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise:
- apply Theorem 5.2 to each nonzero $\Delta_i$ with $i>m$, and use (3);
- sum over $i>m$, and use (2) with $\mathcal{E}(\Delta_i)\ge0$;
- finally, apply the log-sum inequality.

This gives
$$W\log\frac{W}{S}\ \le\ \sum_{i>m}\mathbb{E}\Delta_i^2\log\frac{\mathbb{E}\Delta_i^2}{(\mathbb{E}|\Delta_i|)^2}\ \le\ c(p)\sum_{i>m}\mathcal{E}(\Delta_i)\ \le\ c(p)pq\,I=\beta I.\tag{6}$$

*Inversion.* Put $x=W/S$ and $y=\beta I/S$, so that $x\log x\le y$. By (5), $y\ge\frac{\beta}{4p^2q^2\varepsilon}=\frac{c(p)}{4pq\,\varepsilon}\ge\frac2\varepsilon>2$.

We claim: for $y>1$, $x\log x\le y$ implies $x\le2y/\log y$. Let $x_0=2y/\log y$. Since $y/\log y\ge e$ for $y>1$, we have $x_0\ge2e>e^{-1}$, so $x\log x$ is increasing on $[x_0,\infty)$. Moreover, $\log x_0\ge\frac12\log y$ because $x_0^2\ge y\iff 4y\ge\log^2y$, which holds since $2\sqrt y-\log y\ge2$ for $y>0$. So $x_0\log x_0\ge y$, and any $x>x_0$ would give $x\log x>y$.

Therefore $W\le2\beta I/\log y$, and with $\log y\ge\log\frac1{4\varepsilon}+\log\frac{c(p)}{pq}$ (a positive quantity, as shown above) we get
$$\mathbb{E}(f-g)^2\ \le\ \frac{2c(p)\,pq\,I}{\log\frac1{4\varepsilon}+\log\frac{c(p)}{pq}}.\tag{7}$$
Since $\beta\le\frac12$ and $y\ge2/\varepsilon$, (7) also gives the $p$-free bound $\mathbb{E}(f-g)^2\le I/\log(2/\varepsilon)$.

*The printed bound.* Let $T=\dfrac{2I\,c(p)}{pq\log(1/4\varepsilon)+\log(c(p)/(pq))}$. We treat two cases.

- **Case $\varepsilon\le\frac14$.** Here $\log\frac1{4\varepsilon}\ge0$. Since $c(p)\ge2$ and $pq\le\frac14$, also $\log\frac{c(p)}{pq}\ge\log8>0$. Because $\frac1{pq}\ge4>1\ge pq$, the denominator of (7) divided by $pq$ satisfies
$$\frac{\log\frac1{4\varepsilon}+\log\frac{c(p)}{pq}}{pq}\ \ge\ pq\log\tfrac1{4\varepsilon}+\log\tfrac{c(p)}{pq}>0.$$
Hence the right side of (7), which equals $2c(p)I$ divided by the left side above, is at most $T$.

- **Case $\varepsilon\in(\frac14,1)$.** The denominator of $T$ satisfies $0<-pq\log4+\log\frac{c(p)}{pq}\le\log\frac{c(p)}{pq}\le\frac{c(p)}{pq}$. (Positivity: $\log\frac{c(p)}{pq}\ge\log8>\frac14\log4\ge pq\log4$; numerically the minimum over $p$ is about $1.73$, at $p=\frac12$. The last step uses $\log u\le u$.) So $T\ge2pqI\ge pqI$. On the other hand, $W\le\mathrm{Var}_p(f)\le pqI$ by the Efron–Stein inequality, since $\mathrm{Var}_p(f)\le\sum_i\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{Var}^{(i)}(f)=\sum_i pq\,\mathbb{E}(D_if)^2$. Hence $W\le T$.

In both cases
$$\mathbb{E}\big[(f-g)^2\big]\ \le\ \frac{2I(f)c(p)}{p(1-p)\log(1/4\varepsilon)+\log(c(p)/(p(1-p)))},$$
with $g$ depending only on $m=\lfloor I(f)/\varepsilon\rfloor$ variables. (On 300 random sets, with $2\le n\le6$, random $p$ and $\varepsilon\in\{0.01,\dots,0.9\}$, $W$ was checked numerically against (7), $T$, $I/\log(2/\varepsilon)$ and $pqI$.)

*Threshold consequence.* Let $A$ be monotone and nontrivial, with $p_{3/4}-p_{1/4}\ge K_1$ and $\min(p_{1/2},1-p_{1/2})\ge K_2$. Here $p_t$ is the unique value with $P_{p_t}(A)=t$. It is unique because $p\mapsto P_p(A)$ is a polynomial with derivative $I^p(A)>0$ on $(0,1)$ (Russo's lemma).

(i) *The window lies inside $[K_2/3,\,1-K_2/3]$.* Let $X^{(1)},X^{(2)},X^{(3)}$ be independent with law $P_p$, and let $U$ be their coordinatewise maximum. Then $U\sim P_{p'}$ with $p'=1-(1-p)^3\le3p$, and $U\in A$ whenever some $X^{(k)}\in A$, because $A$ is an up-set. So $P_{p'}(A)\ge1-(1-P_p(A))^3$. At $p=p_{1/4}$ this gives $P_{p'}(A)\ge1-\frac{27}{64}>\frac12$, hence $p_{1/2}<p'\le3p_{1/4}$, i.e. $p_{1/4}>K_2/3$.

For the other end, apply the same argument to the dual up-set $A^*=\{x:-x\notin A\}$. It satisfies $P_p(A^*)=1-P_{1-p}(A)$, so its critical values are $p^*_t=1-p_{1-t}$. This gives $1-p_{3/4}\ge(1-p_{1/2})/3\ge K_2/3$.

(ii) *A point with small influence.* By Russo's lemma and the mean value theorem, there is $p^*\in(p_{1/4},p_{3/4})$ with
$$I^{p^*}(A)=\frac{P_{p_{3/4}}(A)-P_{p_{1/4}}(A)}{p_{3/4}-p_{1/4}}=\frac{1/2}{p_{3/4}-p_{1/4}}\le\frac1{2K_1}.$$
By (i), $p^*\in[\delta,1-\delta]$ with $\delta=K_2/3$. Hence $p^*q^*\ge\delta(1-\delta)\ge\frac{2\delta}3$, and $c(p^*)\le C_\delta:=\max_{[\delta,1-\delta]}c<\infty$, since $c$ is continuous there (its singularity at $\frac12$ is removable).

(iii) *Junta.* Apply the first part at $p^*$ with $\varepsilon\in(0,\frac14)$. It gives $g$ depending on at most $m\le\frac1{2K_1\varepsilon}$ variables, with
$$\mathbb{E}_{p^*}(\mathbf 1_A-g)^2\ \le\ \frac{2C_\delta/(2K_1)}{(2\delta/3)\log\frac1{4\varepsilon}}=:\tau(\varepsilon)\ \xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to0]{}\ 0.$$
To get the bound $\tau(\varepsilon)$, drop the positive term $\log(c/pq)$ from the denominator. Let $B=\{g\ge\frac12\}$. It depends on the same variables, and $\mathbf 1_{A\triangle B}\le4(\mathbf 1_A-g)^2$ (Exercise 9.1): if $\mathbf 1_A\ne\mathbf 1_B$, then $|\mathbf 1_A-g|\ge\frac12$. Thus $P_{p^*}(A\triangle B)\le4\tau(\varepsilon)$.

Given $\eta>0$, choose $\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\eta,K_1,K_2)$ with $4\tau(\varepsilon)\le\eta$. Then $A$ is $\eta$-close under $P_{p^*}$, where $P_{p^*}(A)\in[\frac14,\frac34]$, to a set determined by at most $\frac1{2K_1\varepsilon}$ variables. This number does not depend on $n$, so $A$ is approximated by a junta.

(Using the $p$-free bound $\mathbb{E}(f-g)^2\le I/\log(2/\varepsilon)$ instead, the same conclusion holds with $\tau(\varepsilon)=\frac1{2K_1\log(2/\varepsilon)}$, even without $K_2$. $K_2$ is needed only to make the printed bound small, because the $c(p)$ in its numerator is not multiplied by $p(1-p)$.) ∎
