**Central idea.** Write $f(p)=P_p(A)$. By Russo's lemma $f'(p)=I^p(A)$ (total influence), and the mean value theorem gives $p^*\in(p_{1/2},p_{1-\varepsilon})$ with $f'(p^*)=(1/2-\varepsilon)/(p_{1-\varepsilon}-p_{1/2})$. Corollary 10.10 (isoperimetric inequality for monotone sets) lower-bounds the total influence by $\gamma(f(p^*))^2/(2p^*P_{p^*}(\partial_VA)\log(1/p^*))$. Solving for the threshold width and using $\gamma(f(p^*))\ge\gamma(1-\varepsilon)\gtrsim\varepsilon\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}$ (Lemma below, with the sharp constant $c_0=2/(\pi\log2)$) plus the global bound $p\log(1/p)\le1/e$ yields the width bound. The lower tail $p_{1/2}-p_\varepsilon$ follows by applying the same argument to $p\mapsto1-P_{1-p}(A)$.

**Erratum.** The statement is false as printed: the bound uses $\inf_{q\in(1/2,1-\varepsilon)}P_q(\partial_V(A))$, but the critical window $(p_{1/2},p_{1-\varepsilon})$ need not lie in $(1/2,1-\varepsilon)$, and an infimum goes the wrong way in any case. Counterexample: the OR set $A=\{x:\text{some }x_i=1\}$ with $n=20$, $\varepsilon=1/4$. Here $P_p(A)=1-(1-p)^{20}$, so $p_a=1-(1-a)^{1/20}$, $p_{1/2}\approx0.03406$, $p_{3/4}\approx0.06697$, and the width is $p_{3/4}-p_{1/2}\approx0.0329$. The prefactor is $\frac{(\log2)(1/4)}{(1/16)\log4}=2$, and $P_q(\partial_V(A))$ is decreasing on $(1/2,3/4)$, so the printed right side is $2\cdot20\cdot\tfrac34\cdot(\tfrac14)^{19}\approx1.1\times10^{-10}$ for the inner vertex boundary ($P_q=nq(1-q)^{n-1}$) and $2\cdot(\tfrac14)^{20}\approx1.8\times10^{-12}$ for the outer one ($P_q=(1-q)^n$). (Even a $\sup$ over the printed interval $(1/2,3/4)$ gives only $2\cdot20\cdot2^{-20}\approx3.8\times10^{-5}$.) Corrected statement (proved below, via Corollary 10.10):
$$p_{1-\varepsilon}-p_{1/2}\le\frac{\pi\log2}{e}\cdot\frac{1/2-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2\log(1/\varepsilon)}\sup_{q\in(p_{1/2},\,p_{1-\varepsilon})}P_q(\partial_V(A)),$$
and analogously $p_{1/2}-p_\varepsilon$ with the $\sup$ over $(p_\varepsilon,p_{1/2})$. In the example the corrected right side is $\approx0.87$ (inner) or $\approx1.16$ (outer), consistent with the width $0.0329$. Whether the printed constant $\log2$ suffices together with the $\sup$ is open: it is neither proved nor refuted here (the Corollary 10.10 route gives $\pi\log2/e$; see the final remark).

**Solution.** Let $f(p)=P_p(A)$ for $p\in[0,1]$, where $P_p$ is the product of Bernoulli$(p)$ measures on $\{-1,1\}^n$ (with $P\{X_i=1\}=p$). Since $A$ is nonempty and monotone, $(1,\dots,1)\in A$; if also $(-1,\dots,-1)\in A$ then $A=\{-1,1\}^n$ and $P_p(A)\equiv1$, contradicting the uniqueness of $p_a$. Hence $(-1,\dots,-1)\notin A$, so $f$ is continuous and strictly increasing with $f(0)=0$, $f(1)=1$. For each $a\in[0,1]$ there is therefore a unique $p_a$ with $f(p_a)=a$.

By Russo's lemma (Section 9.6), $f$ is differentiable on $(0,1)$ with
$$f'(p)=I^p(A):=\sum_{i=1}^n I^p_i(A),$$
the total influence of $A$ under $P_p$.

Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)$. Applying the mean value theorem to $f$ on $[p_{1/2},p_{1-\varepsilon}]$, there exists $p^*\in(p_{1/2},p_{1-\varepsilon})$ such that
$$f'(p^*)=\frac{f(p_{1-\varepsilon})-f(p_{1/2})}{p_{1-\varepsilon}-p_{1/2}}=\frac{1/2-\varepsilon}{\,p_{1-\varepsilon}-p_{1/2}\,}.\tag{1}$$

Corollary 10.10, in its "in particular" form, asserts that for every $p\in(0,1)$ and monotone $A$,
$$2\,P_p(\partial_V(A))\cdot I^p(A)\cdot p\log\frac1p\;\ge\;\gamma(P_p(A))^2,\tag{2}$$
where $\gamma(s)=\phi(\Phi^{-1}(s))$ is the Gaussian isoperimetric function and $\partial_V(A)$ is the vertex boundary. Since $I^{p^*}(A)=f'(p^*)$, (2) at $p=p^*$ gives
$$f'(p^*)\;\ge\;\frac{\gamma(f(p^*))^2}{2\,p^*\,P_{p^*}(\partial_V(A))\,\log(1/p^*)}.\tag{3}$$

Combining (1) and (3) and solving for $p_{1-\varepsilon}-p_{1/2}$:
$$p_{1-\varepsilon}-p_{1/2}\;\le\;\frac{2(1/2-\varepsilon)\;p^*\log(1/p^*)}{\gamma(f(p^*))^2}\;P_{p^*}(\partial_V(A)).\tag{4}$$

We now bound the three factors. First, $f(p^*)\in(1/2,1-\varepsilon)$ because $p^*\in(p_{1/2},p_{1-\varepsilon})$ and $f$ is strictly increasing. The function $\gamma$ is symmetric about $1/2$ and strictly decreasing on $[1/2,1]$, hence $\gamma(f(p^*))\ge\gamma(1-\varepsilon)$.

Second, the function $p\mapsto p\log(1/p)$ attains its global maximum $1/e$ at $p=1/e$ on $(0,1)$, so for every $p^*\in(0,1)$,
$$p^*\log(1/p^*)\le\frac1e.\tag{5}$$
(Note that $p^*$ need not lie in $[1/2,1)$: e.g. for $A=\{x:\text{some }x_i=1\}\subset\{-1,1\}^2$, $f(p)=1-(1-p)^2$ and $p_{1/2}=1-1/\sqrt2\approx0.293<1/2$, so the bound $\frac12\log2$ valid only on $[1/2,1)$ does not apply; $1/e$ is the sharp uniform bound.)

Third, we use the corrected Gaussian tail estimate
$$\gamma(1-\varepsilon)^2\;\ge\;c_0\,\varepsilon^2\log\frac1\varepsilon\qquad(0<\varepsilon<1/2),\tag{6}$$
with the sharp constant $c_0=\frac{2}{\pi\log2}\approx0.9184$. (The estimate with $c_0=1$ is false: $\gamma(1-\varepsilon)^2/(\varepsilon^2\log(1/\varepsilon))\to2/(\pi\log2)$ as $\varepsilon\to\frac12^-$, and e.g. at $\varepsilon=0.45$ the left side is $0.1567<0.1617$. Nor does it follow from Mill's ratio $\phi(t)\ge t(1-\Phi(t))$, which would require $t^2\ge-\log(1-\Phi(t))$, false as $t\to0$.)

*Lemma.* For $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, $\gamma(1-\varepsilon)^2\ge c_0\varepsilon^2\log(1/\varepsilon)$ with $c_0=2/(\pi\log2)$, best possible.
*Proof.* Write $t=\Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon)>0$, so $\gamma(1-\varepsilon)=\phi(t)$, $\varepsilon=1-\Phi(t)$. Put $L(t)=-\log(1-\Phi(t))$, $\lambda(t)=\phi(t)/(1-\Phi(t))$, and
$$F(t)=\phi(t)^2-c_0(1-\Phi(t))^2L(t),\qquad t\ge0.$$
Then $F(0)=\frac1{2\pi}-c_0\frac{\log2}{4}=0$. Differentiating, with $\lambda'(t)=\lambda(t)(\lambda(t)-t)$,
$$F'(t)=\phi(t)(1-\Phi(t))\,G(t),\qquad G(t)=2c_0L(t)-2t\lambda(t)-c_0.$$
Now $G(0)=c_0(2\log2-1)>0$; $G'(t)=2(c_0-1)\lambda(t)-2t\lambda(t)(\lambda(t)-t)<0$ for $t>0$ since $c_0<1$ and $\lambda(t)>t>0$ (Mill's inequality $1-\Phi(t)<\phi(t)/t$); and $G(t)\to-\infty$ as $t\to\infty$ (from $1-\Phi(t)\ge t\phi(t)/(1+t^2)$ we get $L(t)\le\frac12\log(2\pi)+\log(1+t^2)-\log t+t^2/2$, while $-2t\lambda(t)\le-2t^2$, so $G(t)\le(c_0-2)t^2+O(\log t)$ with $c_0<2$). Hence $G$ has exactly one zero $t^*>0$: $F$ strictly increases on $[0,t^*]$ and strictly decreases on $[t^*,\infty)$. Since $F(0)=0$ and $F(t)\to0$ (as $\phi(t)^2\to0$ and $(1-\Phi(t))^2L(t)\le(\phi(t)/t)^2\cdot O(t^2)\to0$), we obtain $F(t)\ge0$ for all $t\ge0$, i.e. (6). Sharpness follows from $\phi(t)^2/((1-\Phi(t))^2L(t))\to\frac{1/(2\pi)}{(\log2)/4}=c_0$ as $t\to0^+$. ∎

Inserting $\gamma(f(p^*))\ge\gamma(1-\varepsilon)$, (5), and (6) into (4), and using $\frac{2}{ec_0}=\frac{\pi\log2}{e}$:
$$p_{1-\varepsilon}-p_{1/2}\;\le\;\frac{\pi\log2}{e}\cdot\frac{1/2-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2\log(1/\varepsilon)}\;P_{p^*}(\partial_V(A)).\tag{7}$$

*Remark on $\inf$ vs.\ $\sup$ and on the constant.* The estimate (7) bounds the width by the vertex boundary at the mean-value point $p^*\in(p_{1/2},p_{1-\varepsilon})$, so
$$p_{1-\varepsilon}-p_{1/2}\;\le\;\frac{\pi\log2}{e}\cdot\frac{1/2-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2\log(1/\varepsilon)}\;\sup_{q\in(p_{1/2},\,p_{1-\varepsilon})}P_q(\partial_V(A)).$$
The problem as written has $\inf_{q\in(1/2,1-\varepsilon)}P_q(\partial_V(A))$ with constant $\log2$; neither follows from (7): $\inf\le P_{p^*}(\partial_V(A))$ goes the wrong way, the interval $(p_{1/2},p_{1-\varepsilon})$ need not lie in $(1/2,1-\varepsilon)$, and the constant $\log2$ would require the false estimate (6) with $c_0=1$ together with $p^*\ge1/2$. The corrected constant $\pi\log2/e\approx0.801$ exceeds the stated $\log2\approx0.693$ by the factor $\pi/e\approx1.156$ (from $1/c_0=\pi\log2/2$ and $(1/e)/(\frac12\log2)$). The $\inf$/interval defect alone makes the printed statement false (see the Erratum); the constant shortfall is a limitation of this proof route, not a demonstrated falsity.

*Lower tail.* For $p_{1/2}-p_\varepsilon$, apply the same argument to $g(p):=1-f(1-p)$. Then $g$ is continuous strictly increasing with $g(0)=0$, $g(1)=1$; writing $g(p)=P_p(B)$ where $B=\{x:-x\notin A\}$ (nonempty and monotone, since $A$ is, and $(-1,\dots,-1)\notin B$ since $(1,\dots,1)\in A$), one checks $p^{(g)}_a=1-p_{1-a}$ (with $p_{\cdot}$ for $f$) and $P_q(\partial_V(B))=P_{1-q}(\partial_V(A))$ (pivotality is preserved under $x\mapsto -x$, which maps $P_q$ to $P_{1-q}$). Hence $p^{(g)}_{1-\varepsilon}-p^{(g)}_{1/2}=p_{1/2}-p_\varepsilon$, and (7) applied to $B$ gives, with $r=1-q$,
$$p_{1/2}-p_\varepsilon\;\le\;\frac{\pi\log2}{e}\cdot\frac{1/2-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2\log(1/\varepsilon)}\;\sup_{r\in(p_\varepsilon,\,p_{1/2})}P_r(\partial_V(A)),$$
the analogous upper bound for the lower tail (with the same $\inf$/$\sup$ and constant remarks as above). ∎
