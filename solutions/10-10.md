**Central idea.** Apply Bobkov's Gaussian inequality (Theorem 10.13) to the rescaled function $g=\varepsilon f^2\in[0,1]$ and let $\varepsilon\to0$. Since $\gamma(u)=u\sqrt{2\log(1/u)}(1+o(1))$ as $u\to0^+$, both sides of Bobkov expand as $\varepsilon\sqrt{2\log(1/\varepsilon)}\,\mathbb{E}f^2$ plus a correction of order $\varepsilon/\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}$; the leading terms cancel, and the correction equals $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(-\mathrm{Ent}(f^2)+2\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f\|^2\big)\ge0$, using the chain rule $\nabla f^2=2f\nabla f$. The same $\varepsilon$-scaling on the hypercube does not give Theorem 5.1, because the discrete gradient has no chain rule.

**Solution.** (a) Let $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ be smooth with bounded derivatives and $0<c\le f\le C$; the general case follows by approximation (see below). For $0<\varepsilon<1/C^2$, $g=\varepsilon f^2$ maps $\mathbb{R}^n$ into $[0,1]$, so Theorem 10.13 gives
$$\gamma(\varepsilon\mathbb{E}f^2)\le\mathbb{E}\sqrt{\gamma(\varepsilon f^2)^2+\varepsilon^2\|\nabla f^2\|^2},\tag{*}$$
where $\gamma=\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}$. By Lemma 10.3, $\gamma(u)=u\sqrt{2\log(1/u)}(1+\eta(u))$ with $\eta(u)\to0$ as $u\to0^+$.

Write $L=\log(1/\varepsilon)$. For fixed $x$ and $u=\varepsilon f^2(x)$,
$$\gamma(\varepsilon f^2)=\varepsilon f^2\sqrt{2L+2\log(1/f^2)}\,(1+\eta(\varepsilon f^2))=\varepsilon f^2\sqrt{2L}+\varepsilon\frac{f^2\log(1/f^2)}{\sqrt{2L}}+o(\varepsilon/\sqrt L),$$
using $\sqrt{1+y}=1+y/2+o(y)$. Since $f$ and $\nabla f$ are bounded and $\eta(\varepsilon f^2)\to0$ uniformly in $x$, every $o(\cdot)$ below is uniform in $x$, justifying passage of the limit $\varepsilon\to0$ through $\mathbb{E}$.

Squaring and adding the gradient term,
$$\gamma(\varepsilon f^2)^2+\varepsilon^2\|\nabla f^2\|^2=2L\varepsilon^2f^4+2\varepsilon^2f^4\log(1/f^2)+\varepsilon^2\|\nabla f^2\|^2+o(\varepsilon^2),$$
and, since $f^2\ge c^2>0$,
$$\sqrt{\gamma(\varepsilon f^2)^2+\varepsilon^2\|\nabla f^2\|^2}=\varepsilon f^2\sqrt{2L}+\varepsilon\frac{f^2\log(1/f^2)}{\sqrt{2L}}+\varepsilon\frac{\|\nabla f^2\|^2}{2f^2\sqrt{2L}}+o(\varepsilon/\sqrt L).$$
The identical expansion with the constant $\mathbb{E}f^2$ in place of $f^2$ gives
$$\gamma(\varepsilon\mathbb{E}f^2)=\varepsilon\mathbb{E}f^2\sqrt{2L}+\varepsilon\frac{\mathbb{E}f^2\log(1/\mathbb{E}f^2)}{\sqrt{2L}}+o(\varepsilon/\sqrt L).$$
Inserting these into $(*)$, the leading terms $\varepsilon\sqrt{2L}\,\mathbb{E}f^2$ cancel. Multiplying by $\sqrt L/\varepsilon$ and letting $\varepsilon\to0$ yields
$$0\le\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(\mathbb{E}[f^2\log(1/f^2)]-\mathbb{E}f^2\log(1/\mathbb{E}f^2)+\mathbb{E}\frac{\|\nabla f^2\|^2}{2f^2}\right).$$
Now $\mathbb{E}[f^2\log(1/f^2)]-\mathbb{E}f^2\log(1/\mathbb{E}f^2)=-\mathrm{Ent}(f^2)$, and by the chain rule $\nabla f^2=2f\nabla f$, so $\|\nabla f^2\|^2/(2f^2)=2\|\nabla f\|^2$. Hence $\mathrm{Ent}(f^2)\le2\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f\|^2$, Theorem 5.4. For general $C^1$ $f$, apply this to $f_\delta=\sqrt{f^2+\delta}$ (bounded case first, then remove boundedness by truncation) and let $\delta\to0$: $\mathrm{Ent}(f^2+\delta)\to\mathrm{Ent}(f^2)$ while $\|\nabla f_\delta\|^2=\frac{f^2}{f^2+\delta}\|\nabla f\|^2\uparrow\|\nabla f\|^2$.

(b) No. On $\{-1,1\}^n$ the discrete gradient $\nabla_i f(x)=(f(x)-f(x^{(i)}))/2$ is linear, so $\nabla_i(\varepsilon f^2)=\varepsilon\nabla_i(f^2)$, but it satisfies no chain rule:
$$\nabla_i(f^2)=\frac{f(x)^2-f(x^{(i)})^2}{2}=2\nabla_i f\cdot\frac{f(x)+f(x^{(i)})}{2}.$$
The identical $\varepsilon$-expansion therefore produces, in place of $2\|\nabla f\|^2$,
$$\frac{\|\nabla f^2\|^2}{2f^2}=\sum_{i=1}^n(\nabla_i f)^2\,\frac{(f(x)+f(x^{(i)}))^2}{2f(x)^2},$$
and the limit $\varepsilon\to0$ yields only $\mathrm{Ent}(f^2)\le\mathbb{E}\sum_i(\nabla_i f)^2\frac{(f(x)+f(x^{(i)}))^2}{2f(x)^2}$, which is not Theorem 5.1. It is genuinely weaker: for $n=1$, $f(1)=2$, $f(-1)=1$, one has $\mathrm{Ent}(f^2)\approx0.48$, Theorem 5.1 gives the bound $2\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f\|^2=0.50$, but the scaled Bobkov inequality gives only $\mathrm{Ent}(f^2)\le0.70$. The chain rule $\nabla(f^2)=2f\nabla f$ is essential to the computation in (a), and it has no discrete analogue.
