**Central idea.** For closed convex $A$, enlargements are Minkowski sums, $A_s=A+sB_2^n$, and $\lambda A+(1-\lambda)A_s=A_{(1-\lambda)s}$; feeding this into Ehrhard's Gaussian Brunn–Minkowski inequality (10.4) makes $\varphi(s)=\Phi^{-1}(\gamma_n(A_s))$ concave. Since $\psi(t):=\varphi(t)-t$ is concave and bounded below on $[0,\infty)$ — boundedness below for large $t$ follows from Gaussian concentration via a fixed ball $B(x_0,t)\subset A_t$ (using $\Phi^{-1}(1-e^{-u^2/2})-u$ bounded below on $[1,\infty)$, not the reversed estimate $\Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon)\ge\sqrt{2\log(1/\varepsilon)}$), and on compacta by continuity — concavity forces $\psi$ to be nondecreasing, which is exactly $\Phi^{-1}(\gamma_n(A_t))\ge\Phi^{-1}(\gamma_n(A))+t$.

**Solution.** Let $P=\gamma_n$ be the canonical Gaussian measure on $\mathbb{R}^n$. The Gaussian isoperimetric theorem states that for Borel $A$ with $P(A)=\Phi(a)$,
$$P(A_t)\ge\Phi(a+t),\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad\Phi^{-1}(P(A_t))\ge\Phi^{-1}(P(A))+t,$$
with the conventions $\Phi^{-1}(0)=-\infty$, $\Phi^{-1}(1)=+\infty$. Let $A$ be convex. If $P(A)\in\{0,1\}$ or $A=\varnothing$ the claim is trivial, so assume $A\ne\varnothing$ and $0<P(A)<1$. Replacing $A$ by its closure does not change $P(A)$ (the boundary of a convex set is Lebesgue-null, hence $P$-null) nor the enlargements, since $d(\cdot,A)=d(\cdot,\bar A)$; assume henceforth that $A$ is closed and convex.

For $s\ge0$, $A_s=\{x:d(x,A)\le s\}=A+sB_2^n$, where $B_2^n$ is the closed Euclidean unit ball (the infimum defining $d$ is attained since $A$ is closed). Put $\varphi(s)=\Phi^{-1}(P(A_s))$, well-defined and finite: $0<P(A)\le P(A_s)$, and $P(A_s)<1$ because $\{d(\cdot,A)>s\}=d^{-1}((s,\infty))$ is a nonempty open set ($d$ is continuous; e.g. for $y\notin A$ with projection $a$ and $v=(y-a)/\|y-a\|$, convexity gives $d(y+rv,A)\ge\|y-a\|+r\to\infty$), and $P$ charges every nonempty open set.

For $\lambda\in[0,1]$ and $s\ge0$, convexity gives $\lambda A+(1-\lambda)A=A$, so
$$\lambda A+(1-\lambda)A_s=\lambda A+(1-\lambda)(A+sB_2^n)=A+(1-\lambda)sB_2^n=A_{(1-\lambda)s}.$$
Apply the Gaussian Brunn–Minkowski inequality (10.4) to the convex sets $A$ and $A_s$:
$$\varphi((1-\lambda)s)=\Phi^{-1}(P(A_{(1-\lambda)s}))\ge\lambda\Phi^{-1}(P(A))+(1-\lambda)\Phi^{-1}(P(A_s))=\lambda\varphi(0)+(1-\lambda)\varphi(s).$$
Writing $u=(1-\lambda)s\in[0,s]$, i.e. $\lambda=1-u/s$, this is $\varphi(u)\ge(1-u/s)\varphi(0)+(u/s)\varphi(s)$: $\varphi$ is concave on $[0,\infty)$.

Fix $x_0\in A$; then $B(x_0,t)\subset A_t$. With $Z\sim P$,
$$P(A_t)\ge\mathbb{P}(\|Z+x_0\|\le t)\ge\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|\le t-\|x_0\|).$$
Gaussian concentration for the $1$-Lipschitz norm gives $\mathbb{P}(\|Z\|\ge\mathbb{E}\|Z\|+u)\le e^{-u^2/2}$, and $\mathbb{E}\|Z\|\le(\mathbb{E}\|Z\|^2)^{1/2}=\sqrt n$, so for $u:=t-\|x_0\|-\sqrt n\ge0$,
$$P(A_t)\ge1-e^{-u^2/2},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\psi(t):=\varphi(t)-t\ge\big[\Phi^{-1}(1-e^{-u^2/2})-u\big]-\|x_0\|-\sqrt n.$$
We show $u\mapsto\Phi^{-1}(1-e^{-u^2/2})-u$ is bounded below on $[1,\infty)$. It is continuous on $(0,\infty)$. For $u\ge3$, Gordon's lower bound $1-\Phi(v)\ge v\phi(v)/(1+v^2)$ at $v=u-1\ge2$ gives $1-\Phi(u-1)\ge e^{-u^2/2}$: indeed this is $\frac{u-1}{1+(u-1)^2}e^{\,u-1/2}\ge\sqrt{2\pi}$, and $w\mapsto\frac{w}{1+w^2}e^{w+1/2}$ is increasing for $w\ge2$ with value $\frac25e^{2.5}\approx4.87\ge\sqrt{2\pi}$ at $w=2$. Hence $\Phi^{-1}(1-e^{-u^2/2})\ge u-1$ for $u\ge3$. On the compact $[1,3]$ continuity gives a finite minimum. Thus $\psi(t)\ge m_0-\|x_0\|-\sqrt n$ for $t\ge\|x_0\|+\sqrt n+1$ (so $u\ge1$), with $m_0>-\infty$. On $[0,\|x_0\|+\sqrt n+1]$, $\psi$ is continuous — $t\mapsto P(A_t)$ is: right-continuous by continuity of measure from above ($\{d\le t_n\}\downarrow\{d\le t\}$), left-continuous since $A_t\setminus\bigcup_{s<t}A_s=\{d(\cdot,A)=t\}\subset\partial A_t$ is $P$-null (the boundary of the convex set $A_t$ is Lebesgue-null) — and $\Phi^{-1}$ is continuous on $(0,1)$, so $\psi$ attains a finite minimum there. Hence $\psi$ is bounded below on $[0,\infty)$. (The estimate $\Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon)\ge\sqrt{2\log(1/\varepsilon)}$ is false — the correct implication of $1-\Phi(u)\le e^{-u^2/2}$ is $\Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon)\le\sqrt{2\log(1/\varepsilon)}$ — and is not needed.)

But $\psi$ is concave. If $\psi(s_1)>\psi(s_2)$ for some $s_1<s_2$, concavity would give $\psi(t)\le\psi(s_2)+m(t-s_2)$ for $t>s_2$ with $m=(\psi(s_2)-\psi(s_1))/(s_2-s_1)<0$, forcing $\psi(t)\to-\infty$, a contradiction. Hence $\psi$ is nondecreasing, so $\psi(t)\ge\psi(0)$ for all $t\ge0$, i.e.
$$\Phi^{-1}(P(A_t))\ge\Phi^{-1}(P(A))+t,\qquad t\ge0,$$
which is the Gaussian isoperimetric inequality for the convex set $A$. ∎
