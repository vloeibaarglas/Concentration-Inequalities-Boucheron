**Central idea.** In one dimension half-lines are isoperimetrically extremal. Testing the definition of $\alpha_P$ on $A=(-\infty,x]$ gives $\kappa(P)\le\operatorname*{ess\,inf}f/\min(F,1-F)$. For the reverse, the pointwise bound $f\ge c\min(F,1-F)$ (with $c$ the essinf) implies the $L^1$ Poincaré inequality $\mathbb{E}_P|u'|\ge c\,\mathbb{E}_P|u-m|$ for Lipschitz $u$ with median $m$: by the coarea formula this reduces to a level-set estimate, and since $m$ is a median each component interval of $\{u\ge s\}$ carries $P$-mass at most $1/2$, where the elementary bound $\min(\alpha,1-\alpha)+\min(\beta,1-\beta)\ge\beta-\alpha$ applies. Transferring to sets through the Lipschitz approximants $u_t(x)=\min(1,(t-d(x,A))_+/t)$ of $\mathbf{1}_{\bar A}$ (with $\mathbb{E}|u_t'|\le P(A_t\setminus A)/t$) yields $\alpha_P(p)\ge c\min(p,1-p)$.

**Solution.** Let $P$ be absolutely continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ with density $f$ and distribution function $F$, and put
$$c=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{a<x<b}\frac{f(x)}{\min(F(x),1-F(x))},\qquad \psi(x)=\min(F(x),1-F(x)),$$
with $a=\inf\{x:F(x)>0\}$, $b=\sup\{x:F(x)<1\}$. For $x\in(a,b)$ one has $0<F(x)<1$. Since $f\ge c\psi$ a.e. on $(a,b)$ and $f=\psi=0$ a.e. outside $[a,b]$, we have $f\ge c\psi$ a.e. on $\mathbb{R}$. Below $\liminf_{t\to0}$ means $t\downarrow0$ (the $t$-enlargement).

*Upper bound.* Let $x\in(a,b)$ be a point of differentiability of $F$ (a.e. $x$). For $A=(-\infty,x]$, $P(A)=F(x)\in(0,1)$ and $A_t=(-\infty,x+t)$, so
$$\liminf_{t\downarrow0}\frac{P(A_t\setminus A)}{t}\le\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{F(x+t)-F(x)}{t}=f(x).$$
Hence $\alpha_P(F(x))\le f(x)$ for a.e. $x\in(a,b)$, and therefore
$$\kappa(P)=\inf_{p\in(0,1)}\frac{\alpha_P(p)}{\min(p,1-p)}\le\frac{f(x)}{\min(F(x),1-F(x))}\quad\text{for a.e. }x,$$
so $\kappa(P)\le c$.

*Functional inequality.* We claim that for every Lipschitz $u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ with median $m$,
$$\int |u'|\,dP\ge c\int |u-m|\,dP.\tag{*}$$
Translating, assume $m=0$. By the coarea formula,
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u'(x)|\psi(x)\,dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{u(x)=t}\psi(x)\Big)\,dt,$$
and since $f\ge c\psi$ a.e., it suffices to show $\int|u'|\psi\,dx\ge\int|u|\,dP=\int_0^\infty P(|u|>s)\,ds$.

Fix $s>0$. The closed set $\{u\ge s\}$ is a countable disjoint union of maximal closed intervals $[a_j,b_j]$ (points and rays allowed), with distinct finite endpoints: if e.g. $b_j=a_k$ for $j\ne k$, the union would be a strictly larger interval in $\{u\ge s\}$. By continuity and maximality, $u=s$ at each finite endpoint. Since $0$ is a median, $P(u\ge s)\le P(u>0)\le1/2$, so each component has $P$-mass at most $1/2$. We claim
$$\psi(a_j)\mathbf{1}_{\{a_j>-\infty\}}+\psi(b_j)\mathbf{1}_{\{b_j<\infty\}}\ge P([a_j,b_j]).\tag{1}$$
If $[a_j,b_j]$ is bounded, $P([a_j,b_j])=F(b_j)-F(a_j)\le1/2$ by continuity of $F$; writing $\alpha=F(a_j)$, $\beta=F(b_j)$, the estimate $\min(\alpha,1-\alpha)+\min(\beta,1-\beta)\ge\beta-\alpha$ holds in all three cases: $\alpha+\beta\ge\beta-\alpha$ if $\beta\le1/2$; $(1-\alpha)+(1-\beta)\ge\beta-\alpha$ if $\alpha\ge1/2$; and $\alpha+(1-\beta)\ge\beta-\alpha\iff\beta-\alpha\le1/2$ if $\alpha<1/2<\beta$. If $[a_j,\infty)$, then $P=1-F(a_j)\le1/2$ forces $F(a_j)\ge1/2$, so $\psi(a_j)=1-F(a_j)=P$; likewise for $(-\infty,b_j]$. The case $(-\infty,\infty)$ is impossible since $P(u\ge s)\le1/2$.

Summing (1) over $j$ gives $\sum_{u(x)=s}\psi(x)\ge P(u\ge s)\ge P(u>s)$. Applying the same argument to $\{u\le -s\}$ (using $P(u\le -s)\le P(u<0)\le1/2$) gives $\sum_{u(x)=-s}\psi(x)\ge P(u\le -s)\ge P(u<-s)$. Hence
$$\int|u'|\psi\,dx=\int_0^\infty\Big[\sum_{u=s}\psi+\sum_{u=-s}\psi\Big]\,ds\ge\int_0^\infty\big[P(u>s)+P(u<-s)\big]\,ds=\mathbb{E}|u|,$$
proving $(*)$.

*From functions to sets.* Let $A$ be measurable with $P(A)=p\in(0,1)$ and put $\bar p=P(\bar A)\ge p$. If $\bar p>p$, then $\bar A\subseteq A_t$ for all $t>0$ gives $P(A_t\setminus A)\ge\bar p-p>0$, so $\liminf_{t\downarrow0}P(A_t\setminus A)/t=+\infty$. Assume $\bar p=p$ and set $u_t(x)=\min\{1,(t-d(x,A))_+/t\}$ with $d(x,A)$ the distance to $A$. Then $u_t$ is $1/t$-Lipschitz, $u_t\to\mathbf{1}_{\bar A}$ pointwise, and $u_t'=0$ a.e. outside $\{0<d(\cdot,A)<t\}\subseteq A_t\setminus A$, so $\mathbb{E}|u_t'|\le P(A_t\setminus A)/t$. Applying $(*)$ with medians $m_t$,
$$\frac{P(A_t\setminus A)}{t}\ge c\,\mathbb{E}|u_t-m_t|=c\min_m\mathbb{E}|u_t-m|\xrightarrow[t\downarrow0]{}c\min_m\mathbb{E}|\mathbf{1}_{\bar A}-m|=c\min(p,1-p),$$
since $|\min_m\mathbb{E}|u_t-m|-\min_m\mathbb{E}|\mathbf{1}_{\bar A}-m||\le\mathbb{E}|u_t-\mathbf{1}_{\bar A}|\to0$ by dominated convergence. Thus $\alpha_P(p)\ge c\min(p,1-p)$ for every $p$, i.e. $\kappa(P)\ge c$. With the upper bound, $\kappa(P)=c$.

*Standard Gaussian.* Here $f=\phi$, $F=\Phi$, and by symmetry $f(x)/\min(\Phi(x),1-\Phi(x))$ takes exactly the values $g(x)=\phi(x)/\Phi(-x)$ for $x\ge0$. Since $(\log g)'(x)=-x+g(x)>0$ for $x>0$ by Mill's inequality $\Phi(-x)=\int_x^\infty\phi(t)\,dt\le\int_x^\infty\frac{t}{x}\phi(t)\,dt=\phi(x)/x$, $g$ is strictly increasing, so $\kappa(P)=g(0)=\phi(0)/\Phi(0)=\sqrt{2/\pi}$.

*Laplace.* With $f(x)=\frac12e^{-|x|}$ we have $F(x)=\frac12e^{x}$ for $x\le0$ and $F(x)=1-\frac12e^{-x}$ for $x\ge0$, whence $\min(F(x),1-F(x))=f(x)$ for all $x$. The ratio is identically $1$, so $\kappa(P)=1$. ∎
