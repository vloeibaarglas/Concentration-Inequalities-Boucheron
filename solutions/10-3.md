**Central idea.** Monotone transport from the Laplace law $\nu$ (density $\frac12e^{-|y|}$) to $P$ is $(1/\kappa(P))$-Lipschitz, because by Exercise 10-2 the density of $P$ is at least $\kappa(P)\min(F,1-F)$ while $\nu$'s density equals $\min(F_\nu,1-F_\nu)$. The coordinatewise product map is then $(1/\kappa(P))$-Lipschitz on $\mathbb{R}^n$, and Lipschitz images lose at most the Lipschitz constant in the Cheeger constant, so $\kappa(P^n)\ge\kappa(P)\,\kappa(\nu^n)$. For $\kappa(\nu^n)$ we use Ledoux's semigroup (Buser-type) argument, which avoids any one-dimensional functional inequality. Smooth the convex potential $\sum_i|x_i|$ to $V_\varepsilon=\sum_i\sqrt{x_i^2+\varepsilon^2}$, which costs factors $e^{O(n\varepsilon)}$. For a diffusion with convex potential:
- reverse Poincaré gives $|\nabla P_sg|\le\|g\|_\infty/\sqrt{2s}$, hence $\|\mathbb{1}_A-P_t\mathbb{1}_A\|_1\le\sqrt{2t}\,\mu^+(A)$;
- the spectral gap $\lambda\ge\frac14$ (Laplace Poincaré constant $4$, tensorized) gives $\|\mathbb{1}_A-P_t\mathbb{1}_A\|_1\ge2p(1-p)(1-e^{-\lambda t})$.

Optimizing in $t$ yields $\kappa(\nu^n)\ge0.2256>\frac1{2\sqrt6}=0.2041$.

**Solution.** We use the notation of Exercise 10-2: $P$ is an absolutely continuous law on $\mathbb{R}$ with density $f$, distribution function $F$ and support endpoints $a<b$. For a law $\mu$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ and measurable $A$, put $\mu^+(A)=\liminf_{t\downarrow0}\mu(A_t\setminus A)/t$, where $A_t=\{x:d(x,A)<t\}$. Then $\kappa(\mu)=\inf\{\mu^+(A)/\min(\mu(A),1-\mu(A)):0<\mu(A)<1\}$. Let $k=\kappa(P)$; if $k=0$ there is nothing to prove, so assume $k>0$. Let $\nu$ be the Laplace law with distribution function $F_\nu$, and write $\varphi(u)=\min(u,1-u)$. As computed in Exercise 10-2, $f_\nu=\varphi\circ F_\nu$ everywhere, and $f\ge k\,\varphi\circ F$ a.e. on $(a,b)$. In particular $F$ is continuous and strictly increasing on $(a,b)$.

**Step 1: $\kappa(P^n)\ge k\,\kappa(\nu^n)$.** Let $G=(F|_{(a,b)})^{-1}:(0,1)\to(a,b)$ and $T=G\circ F_\nu:\mathbb{R}\to(a,b)$. Since $F_\nu(Y)$ is uniform on $(0,1)$ for $Y\sim\nu$, we have $T_\#\nu=P$.

*$T$ is $(1/k)$-Lipschitz.* Let $y_1<y_2$, $x_j=T(y_j)$ and $u_j=F(x_j)=F_\nu(y_j)$. For an absolutely continuous nondecreasing $H$ and Borel $h\ge0$, $\int_{s_1}^{s_2}h(H(s))H'(s)\,ds=\int_{H(s_1)}^{H(s_2)}h(u)\,du$. This holds for indicators of intervals by the fundamental theorem of calculus and extends by monotone class. Applying it to $H=F_\nu$ and to $H=F$ with $h=1/\varphi$ gives
$$y_2-y_1=\int_{y_1}^{y_2}\frac{f_\nu(y)}{\varphi(F_\nu(y))}\,dy=\int_{u_1}^{u_2}\frac{du}{\varphi(u)}=\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\frac{f(x)}{\varphi(F(x))}\,dx\ge k\,(x_2-x_1).$$
Hence $T^{\otimes n}(y)=(T(y_1),\dots,T(y_n))$ satisfies $|T^{\otimes n}y-T^{\otimes n}y'|^2=\sum_i|T(y_i)-T(y_i')|^2\le k^{-2}|y-y'|^2$, and $T^{\otimes n}_\#\nu^n=P^n$.

*Lipschitz images.* Let $S$ be $L$-Lipschitz with $S_\#\mu=\mu'$, and let $A$ satisfy $\mu'(A)=p\in(0,1)$. Put $B=S^{-1}A$, so $\mu(B)=p$. If $d(y,B)<t/L$, pick $y'\in B$ with $|y-y'|<t/L$; then $|Sy-Sy'|<t$, so $Sy\in A_t$. Thus $B_{t/L}\subseteq S^{-1}(A_t)$ and $\mu'(A_t\setminus A)\ge\mu(B_{t/L}\setminus B)$, giving $\mu'^+(A)\ge\mu^+(B)/L\ge\kappa(\mu)\min(p,1-p)/L$. Hence $\kappa(\mu')\ge\kappa(\mu)/L$. With $S=T^{\otimes n}$ and $L=1/k$,
$$\kappa(P^n)\ge k\,\kappa(\nu^n).$$

**Step 2: reduction to closed sets and to a smooth potential.** Let $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ with $\nu^n(A)=p\in(0,1)$.
- If $\nu^n(\bar A)>p$, then $A_t\supseteq\bar A$ gives $\nu^n(A_t\setminus A)\ge\nu^n(\bar A)-p>0$ for all $t$, so $(\nu^n)^+(A)=\infty$.
- Otherwise $\nu^n(\bar A)=p$ and $A_t=\bar A_t$, so $(\nu^n)^+(A)=(\nu^n)^+(\bar A)$.

So it suffices to treat closed $A$.

Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, let $V(x)=\sum_i|x_i|$ and $V_\varepsilon(x)=\sum_i\sqrt{x_i^2+\varepsilon^2}$, and let $\mu=\mu_\varepsilon=Z_\varepsilon^{-1}e^{-V_\varepsilon}dx$. Since $V\le V_\varepsilon\le V+n\varepsilon$, we have $Z_\varepsilon\in[2^ne^{-n\varepsilon},2^n]$, and the density $d\mu/d\nu^n=2^ne^{-(V_\varepsilon-V)}/Z_\varepsilon$ lies in $[e^{-n\varepsilon},e^{n\varepsilon}]$. Hence for closed $A$ with $\nu^n(A)=p$ and $q=\mu(A)$:
$$(\nu^n)^+(A)\ge e^{-n\varepsilon}\mu^+(A),\qquad\min(q,1-q)\ge e^{-n\varepsilon}\min(p,1-p)>0.\tag{1}$$

*Poincaré inequality.* First take $n=1$, $g$ Lipschitz on $[0,\infty)$ with $g(0)=0$. Then $g^2e^{-x}\to0$ at $\infty$, and integrating by parts and applying Cauchy–Schwarz,
$$\int_0^\infty g^2e^{-x}dx=2\int_0^\infty gg'e^{-x}dx\le2\Big(\int_0^\infty g^2e^{-x}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_0^\infty g'^2e^{-x}\Big)^{1/2},$$
so $\int_0^\infty g^2e^{-x}\le4\int_0^\infty g'^2e^{-x}$. The same holds on $(-\infty,0]$. With $g=h-h(0)$ this gives $\mathrm{Var}_\nu(h)\le\int(h-h(0))^2d\nu\le4\int h'^2d\nu$ for Lipschitz $h$.

By the Efron–Stein inequality (Theorem 3.1) applied coordinatewise, $\mathrm{Var}_{\nu^n}(h)\le4\int|\nabla h|^2d\nu^n$ for Lipschitz $h$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$. Transferring to $\mu$ with the density bounds,
$$\mathrm{Var}_\mu(h)\le\int\Big(h-\int h\,d\nu^n\Big)^2d\mu\le e^{n\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{Var}_{\nu^n}(h)\le4e^{n\varepsilon}\int|\nabla h|^2d\nu^n\le4e^{2n\varepsilon}\int|\nabla h|^2d\mu.$$
By density of Lipschitz functions in $H^1(\mu)$, the spectral gap of $\mu$ satisfies
$$\lambda\ge\tfrac14e^{-2n\varepsilon}.\tag{2}$$

**Step 3: the semigroup bound for $\mu$.** The potential $V_\varepsilon$ is $C^\infty$ and convex. Every derivative of $s\mapsto\sqrt{s^2+\varepsilon^2}$ of order $\ge1$ is bounded; in particular $|\nabla V_\varepsilon|\le\sqrt n$ and $0\le\mathrm{Hess}\,V_\varepsilon\le\varepsilon^{-1}I$. We use the following standard facts (Bakry, Gentil and Ledoux, *Analysis and Geometry of Markov Diffusion Operators*, 2014, Chapters 1–4). The SDE $dX_t=\sqrt2\,dB_t-\nabla V_\varepsilon(X_t)\,dt$ has unique non-exploding strong solutions $X_t^x$. The semigroup $P_tg(x)=\mathbb{E}g(X^x_t)$ is Markov and symmetric in $L^2(\mu)$. Its $L^2$ generator $L$ (equal to $\Delta-\nabla V_\varepsilon\cdot\nabla$ on $C_c^\infty$) is the self-adjoint operator of the Dirichlet form $\mathcal{E}(h,h)=\int|\nabla h|^2d\mu$ on $H^1(\mu)$, which contains all bounded Lipschitz functions. Finally, $P_tg$ is $C^1$ for $g\in C_c^\infty$.

*(a) Gradient bound.* For $g\in C_c^\infty$ with $|g|\le1$ and $s>0$,
$$|\nabla P_sg|\le\frac1{\sqrt{2s}}.\tag{3}$$
*Proof.* Drive $X^x$ and $X^y$ by the same $B$. The noise cancels, so $\frac{d}{dt}|X^x_t-X^y_t|^2=-2\langle\nabla V_\varepsilon(X^x_t)-\nabla V_\varepsilon(X^y_t),X^x_t-X^y_t\rangle\le0$ by convexity, and $|X^x_t-X^y_t|\le|x-y|$. For $h\in C^1$ with bounded gradient, the quotient $|h(X^x_s)-h(X^y_s)|/|x-y|$ is at most the supremum of $|\nabla h|$ on the segment $[X^y_s,X^x_s]$, which shrinks to $X^x_s$ as $y\to x$. By dominated convergence (the quotients are bounded by $\|\nabla h\|_\infty$), $|\nabla P_sh|\le P_s|\nabla h|$, hence $|\nabla P_sh|^2\le P_s(|\nabla h|^2)$ by Jensen. Now fix $t>0$ and let $\Psi(r)=P_r\big((P_{t-r}g)^2\big)$ for $r\in[0,t]$. The standard computation (BGL §4.7, the local Poincaré inequalities under $\mathrm{CD}(0,\infty)$) gives
$$\Psi'(r)=P_r\big(L(h^2)-2hLh\big)=2P_r\big(|\nabla h|^2\big)\ge2|\nabla P_rh|^2=2|\nabla P_tg|^2,\qquad h=P_{t-r}g.$$
Integrating over $[0,t]$: $2t|\nabla P_tg|^2\le P_t(g^2)-(P_tg)^2\le1$. $\square$

*(b) Upper bound.* For bounded Lipschitz $f$ and $t>0$,
$$\|f-P_tf\|_{L^1(\mu)}\le\sqrt{2t}\,\|\nabla f\|_{L^1(\mu)}.\tag{4}$$
*Proof.* Let $g\in C_c^\infty$ with $|g|\le1$. For $0<\delta<t$, the map $s\mapsto P_sg$ is $C^1$ into $L^2(\mu)$ on $(0,\infty)$ with derivative $LP_sg$ (spectral theorem). Using symmetry, then $\int f(-Lw)\,d\mu=\mathcal{E}(f,w)$, then (3):
$$\int f\,(P_\delta g-P_tg)\,d\mu=\int_\delta^t\mathcal{E}(f,P_sg)\,ds=\int_\delta^t\!\!\int\langle\nabla f,\nabla P_sg\rangle\,d\mu\,ds\le\|\nabla f\|_1\int_0^t\frac{ds}{\sqrt{2s}}=\sqrt{2t}\,\|\nabla f\|_1.$$
Let $\delta\downarrow0$, using $P_\delta g\to g$ in $L^2$, and then use symmetry: $\int(f-P_tf)g\,d\mu=\int f(g-P_tg)\,d\mu\le\sqrt{2t}\|\nabla f\|_1$. Taking the supremum over such $g$ (which computes the $L^1(\mu)$ norm) gives (4). $\square$

Now let $A$ be closed with $\mu(A)=q\in(0,1)$, and let $f_r=(1-d(\cdot,A)/r)_+$. This function is $(1/r)$-Lipschitz, equals $1$ on $A$ and $0$ off $A_r$. Since a Lipschitz function has zero gradient a.e. on any set where it is constant, $|\nabla f_r|\le r^{-1}\mathbb{1}_{A_r\setminus A}$ a.e., and so $\|\nabla f_r\|_1\le\mu(A_r\setminus A)/r$. As $r\downarrow0$, $f_r\to\mathbb{1}_A$ pointwise (as $A$ is closed) and in $L^1(\mu)$, and $P_t$ is an $L^1$ contraction. So $\|f_r-P_tf_r\|_1\to\|\mathbb{1}_A-P_t\mathbb{1}_A\|_1$. Taking $r$ along a sequence realizing the $\liminf$ in $\mu^+(A)$, (4) gives
$$\|\mathbb{1}_A-P_t\mathbb{1}_A\|_1\le\sqrt{2t}\,\mu^+(A).\tag{5}$$

*(c) Lower bound.* Since $0\le P_t\mathbb{1}_A\le1$ and $\int P_t\mathbb{1}_A\,d\mu=q$ (invariance),
$$\|\mathbb{1}_A-P_t\mathbb{1}_A\|_1=\int_A(1-P_t\mathbb{1}_A)\,d\mu+\int_{A^c}P_t\mathbb{1}_A\,d\mu=2\Big(q-\int\mathbb{1}_AP_t\mathbb{1}_A\,d\mu\Big)=2\big(q-\|P_{t/2}\mathbb{1}_A\|_2^2\big),$$
using symmetry and the semigroup property. By the spectral gap (spectral theorem), $\|P_{t/2}(\mathbb{1}_A-q)\|_2^2\le e^{-\lambda t}\|\mathbb{1}_A-q\|_2^2=e^{-\lambda t}q(1-q)$. Since $P_{t/2}$ fixes constants and preserves means, $\|P_{t/2}\mathbb{1}_A\|_2^2=q^2+\|P_{t/2}(\mathbb{1}_A-q)\|_2^2$. Hence
$$\|\mathbb{1}_A-P_t\mathbb{1}_A\|_1\ge2q(1-q)\big(1-e^{-\lambda t}\big).\tag{6}$$

*(d) Optimization.* Combining (5) and (6) with $t=s/\lambda$:
$$\mu^+(A)\ge2q(1-q)\sqrt\lambda\,\frac{1-e^{-s}}{\sqrt{2s}}\qquad(s>0).$$
Take $s=\frac54$; numerically $c_0:=\frac{1-e^{-5/4}}{\sqrt{5/2}}=0.451254$. (The maximum over $s$ is $0.451256$, at $s\approx1.2564$.) By (2), $\sqrt\lambda\ge\frac12e^{-n\varepsilon}$, and $q(1-q)\ge\frac12\min(q,1-q)$. So
$$\mu^+(A)\ge\tfrac{c_0}{2}\,e^{-n\varepsilon}\min(q,1-q).$$

**Step 4: conclusion.** By (1), for every closed $A$ with $\nu^n(A)=p\in(0,1)$,
$$(\nu^n)^+(A)\ge e^{-n\varepsilon}\mu^+(A)\ge\tfrac{c_0}{2}e^{-2n\varepsilon}\min(q,1-q)\ge\tfrac{c_0}{2}e^{-3n\varepsilon}\min(p,1-p).$$
Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ ($n$ fixed); with Step 2 this gives $\kappa(\nu^n)\ge c_0/2=0.225627$. By Step 1,
$$\kappa(P^n)\ge0.2256\,\kappa(P)\ge\frac{\kappa(P)}{2\sqrt6}\qquad\Big(\frac1{2\sqrt6}=0.204124\Big).$$

*Remarks.* The constants $c_0=0.451254$, $\max_s(1-e^{-s})/\sqrt{2s}=0.451256$ and $1/(2\sqrt6)=0.204124$ were checked numerically. Bobkov and Houdré obtain $2\sqrt6$ from a one-dimensional square-root functional inequality that they tensorize. An earlier version of this file followed that route but left its one-dimensional base case unproved. The argument above does not use that inequality, nor the false variant with $\Phi(u)=\min(u,1-u)^2$. $\blacksquare$
