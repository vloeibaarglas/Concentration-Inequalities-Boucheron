**Central idea.** The upper bound is Lemma 10.9 (the $n=1$ Tillich–Zémor inequality) applied to the indicator $f=\mathbf{1}_{\{X=1\}}$, which collapses the right-hand side to $p\sqrt{2\log(1/p)}$. For the lower bound, write $x=1-\Phi(t)$, $\gamma(x)=\varphi(t)$ with $t=-\Phi^{-1}(x)\ge 0$; Gordon's inequality gives $\gamma(x)\ge tx$, and the $\sqrt{\frac12\log(1/x)}$ factor is obtained by a case analysis on $t$ combining Gordon's two-sided Mills-ratio bounds with elementary estimates (the product $(1-\Phi(t))\sqrt{\frac12\log(1/(1-\Phi(t)))}$ is decreasing, which localizes the hard regime to compact intervals). The limit follows from Gordon's inequality by squeeze, giving sharp Mills-ratio asymptotics.

**Solution.** Recall $\gamma(x)=\varphi(\Phi^{-1}(x))$ for $x\in(0,1)$, $\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)=0$, where $\varphi$ is the standard Gaussian density and $\Phi$ its cdf. Gordon's inequality (Exercise 7.8) states that for $t>0$,
$$\frac{t}{t^2+1}\le \frac{1-\Phi(t)}{\varphi(t)}\le\frac1t.\tag{G}$$
We use the numerical bounds $\pi\le 3.15$, $\pi\ge 3.125$, $e\ge 2.7083$ (from $e\ge\sum_{k=0}^4 1/k!$), $e\le 2.7183$, $e^{-u}\ge 1-u$ and $e^{-u}\le 1-u+u^2/2$ for $u\ge 0$, whence $1/\sqrt{2\pi}\le 0.4$ and $1/\sqrt{2\pi}\ge 1/2.51$.

*Upper bound.* Apply Lemma 10.9 to the monotone $f:\{-1,1\}\to[0,1\}$, $f(1)=1$, $f(-1)=0$, with $\mathbb{P}\{X=1\}=p$. Since $\mathbb{E}f(X)=p$,
$$\gamma(p)\le \mathbb{E}\sqrt{\gamma(f(X))^2+2\log(1/p)\,(f(X)-f(-X))_+^2}.$$
At $X=1$ the radicand is $0+2\log(1/p)$ (using $\gamma(1)=0$); at $X=-1$ it is $0$ (using $\gamma(0)=0$ and $(-1)_+=0$). Hence $\gamma(p)\le p\sqrt{2\log(1/p)}$ for all $p\in(0,1)$, in particular $\gamma(x)\le x\sqrt{2\log(1/x)}$ for $x\in(0,1/2]$.

*Lower bound.* Let $x\in(0,1/2]$, $t=-\Phi^{-1}(x)\ge 0$, so $x=1-\Phi(t)$ and $\gamma(x)=\varphi(t)$. Put $S(t)=\sqrt{\frac12\log\frac1{1-\Phi(t)}}$; we prove $\varphi(t)\ge (1-\Phi(t))S(t)$. Note $p(t):=(1-\Phi(t))S(t)$ is decreasing: $p'(t)=\varphi(t)\frac{1-4S(t)^2}{4S(t)}\le 0$ since $1-\Phi(t)\le 1/2<e^{-1/2}$ gives $S(t)^2\ge 1/4$. Also $\varphi$ is decreasing on $[0,\infty)$ and concave on $[0,1]$ (as $\varphi''(t)=(t^2-1)\varphi(t)$), so Hermite–Hadamard gives midpoint upper and trapezoidal lower bounds for its integrals.

First, $1-\Phi(t)\le \frac12e^{-t^2/2}$ for $t\ge 0$: indeed $M(t):=\frac{1-\Phi(t)}{e^{-t^2/2}}$ satisfies $M(0)=1/2$ and $M'(t)=\frac{-\varphi(t)+t(1-\Phi(t))}{e^{-t^2/2}}\le 0$ by (G).

Case $t\in[0,1/2]$: $S$ is increasing, and $S(1/2)\le\sqrt{2/\pi}$ because $1-\Phi(1/2)\ge \frac12(1-\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}})\ge 0.3\ge e^{-4/\pi}$ (here $e^{-4/\pi}\le e^{-1.2698}\le 1/3.417\le 0.3$ since $e^{1.2698}\ge 3.417$). Hence $(1-\Phi(t))S(t)\le \frac12e^{-t^2/2}\sqrt{2/\pi}=\varphi(t)$.

Case $t\in[1/2,3/4]$: $\varphi(t)\ge\varphi(3/4)\ge\frac{1-0.28125}{2.51}\ge 0.286$, while $p(t)\le p(1/2)\le 0.3133\cdot 0.782\le 0.246$, using $1-\Phi(1/2)\le \frac12-\frac14(\varphi(0)+\varphi(1/2))\le 0.3133$ (trapezoidal lower bound for the concave $\varphi$, with $\varphi(0)\ge 1/2.51$, $\varphi(1/2)\ge 0.875/2.51$) and $S(1/2)\le\sqrt{\frac12\log\frac1{0.3}}\le\sqrt{0.61}\le 0.782$ (using $1-\Phi(1/2)\ge 0.3$ and $\log 3.34\le 1.22$ as $e^{1.22}\ge 3.359$).

Case $t\in[3/4,1]$: $\varphi(t)\ge\varphi(1)\ge\frac{0.606}{2.51}\ge 0.241$ (using $e^{1/2}\le 1.65$, so $e^{-1/2}\ge 0.606$), while $p(t)\le p(3/4)\le 0.2433\cdot 0.9\le 0.219$, using $1-\Phi(3/4)\le \frac12-\frac38(\varphi(0)+\varphi(3/4))\le 0.2433$ and $S(3/4)\le\sqrt{\frac12\log 5}\le 0.9$ (using $1-\Phi(3/4)\ge \frac12-\frac34\varphi(0)\ge 0.2$ and $\log 5\le 1.62$ as $e^{1.62}\ge 5.01$).

Case $t\ge 1$: by (G), $\varphi(t)\ge t(1-\Phi(t))$, so it suffices that $K(t):=t^2-\frac12\log\frac1{1-\Phi(t)}\ge 0$. Now $K'(t)=2t-\frac{\varphi(t)}{2(1-\Phi(t))}\ge 2t-\frac{t^2+1}{2t}=\frac{3t^2-1}{2t}>0$ for $t\ge 1$, using $\frac{1-\Phi(t)}{\varphi(t)}\ge\frac{t}{t^2+1}$ from (G). Moreover $K(1)\ge 0$ since $1-\Phi(1)=\frac12-\int_0^1\varphi\ge \frac12-\frac12(\varphi(1/4)+\varphi(3/4))\ge 0.148\ge e^{-2}$ (midpoint upper bound for concave $\varphi$, with $\varphi(1/4)\le 0.4$, $\varphi(3/4)\le 0.7584/2.5$, and $e^{-2}\le 1/7.33$). Hence $K(t)\ge 0$ for $t\ge 1$, giving $\varphi(t)\ge t(1-\Phi(t))\ge S(t)(1-\Phi(t))$.

Thus $\gamma(x)\ge x\sqrt{\frac12\log(1/x)}$ for all $x\in(0,1/2]$, and by continuity at $x=0$.

*Limit.* From (G), $\frac{t^2}{t^2+1}\le\frac{t(1-\Phi(t))}{\varphi(t)}\le 1$, so with $t=-\Phi^{-1}(x)\to\infty$ as $x\to 0$, $\frac{\gamma(x)}{x}=\frac{\varphi(t)}{1-\Phi(t)}=t(1+o(1))$. Taking logs gives $-\log(1-\Phi(t))=\frac{t^2}{2}+\log t+\frac12\log(2\pi)+o(1)$, so $\frac{\log(1/x)}{t^2/2}\to 1$. Hence
$$\frac{\gamma(x)}{x\sqrt{2\log(1/x)}}=\frac{\varphi(t)/(1-\Phi(t))}{\sqrt{2\log(1/(1-\Phi(t)))}}=\frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt{1+o(1)}}\to 1,$$
using also the upper bound for the $\le 1$ direction. This completes the proof. ∎
