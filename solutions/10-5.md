**Central idea.** By rotation invariance of the Gaussian law it suffices to treat the one-dimensional half-space $\{x_1 < \mu\}$; the distance from $x$ to this half-space is $(\langle x, v\rangle - \mu)_+$, so enlarging by $t$ just shifts the threshold to $\mu + t$, and $\Phi^{-1}$ converts the Gaussian tail back to the threshold.

**Solution.** Let $A_t = \{x : d(x,A) \le t\}$. If $u = 0$, then $A = \emptyset$ for $\lambda \le 0$ (so $A_t = \emptyset$, $P(A_t) = 0$) and $A = \mathbb{R}^n$ for $\lambda > 0$ (so $A_t = \mathbb{R}^n$, $P(A_t) = 1$); with $\Phi^{-1}(0) = -\infty$, $\Phi^{-1}(1) = +\infty$, the identity holds trivially in both cases. Assume $u \ne 0$ and set $v = u/|u|$, $\mu = \lambda/|u|$, so $A = \{x : \langle x,v\rangle < \mu\}$.

Since $P$ is rotation invariant and $v$ is a unit vector, $\langle X,v\rangle \sim N(0,1)$ for $X \sim P$, hence $P(A) = \Phi(\mu)$ and $\Phi^{-1}(P(A)) = \mu$. Moreover $d(x,A) = d(x,\bar A)$ where $\bar A = \{y : \langle y,v\rangle \le \mu\}$; for $\langle x,v\rangle \ge \mu$ the nearest point is the orthogonal projection onto the boundary hyperplane, giving $d(x,A) = \langle x,v\rangle - \mu$, and $d(x,A) = 0$ for $x \in A$. Thus, up to a $P$-null boundary,
$$A_t = \{x : \langle x,v\rangle \le \mu + t\},$$
so $P(A_t) = \Phi(\mu+t)$ and $\Phi^{-1}(P(A_t)) = \mu + t = \Phi^{-1}(P(A)) + t$. ∎
