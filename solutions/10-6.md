**Central idea.** The statement is false as written, in two ways: absolute continuity alone does not suffice (a 1D kink when $A_t$ exits the support), and even a smooth Gaussian density fails in dimension one (boundary points annihilate pairwise when balls merge, jumping the surface integral). The sharp picture: for $n\ge 2$ with a continuous density the claim is true — coarea writes $P(A_t)$ as an integral of surface integrals over $\partial A_u$, and each sphere's contribution is continuous in $u$ by spherical parametrization plus dominated convergence (the "active" patch is cut out by inequalities with continuous coefficients, so in $n\ge 2$ its indicator converges a.e.). The book applies it to Gaussian $P$, where the $n=1$ gap is a harmless erratum (1D Gaussian isoperimetry is elementary).

**Solution.** The claim is false for general absolutely continuous $P$. Counterexample 1: on $\mathbb{R}$, let $P$ be uniform on $[0,1]$ and $A=(-1/2,1/2)$. Then $A_t=(-1/2-t,1/2+t)$ and $P(A_t)=\min(1/2+t,1)$, not differentiable at $t=1/2$ (left derivative $1$, right derivative $0$).

Even a smooth density does not save it in one dimension. Counterexample 2: on $\mathbb{R}$ with $P$ standard Gaussian, $A=(-1,1)\cup(3,5)$. For $t<1$, $A_t=(-1-t,1+t)\cup(3-t,5+t)$ (disjoint); for $t>1$, $A_t=(-1-t,5+t)$ (merged). With $F(t)=P(A_t)$,
$$F'(1^-)=2\varphi(2)+\varphi(-2)+\varphi(6)\neq\varphi(-2)+\varphi(6)=F'(1^+),$$
so $F$ is not differentiable at $t=1$: the two boundary points $1+t$, $3-t$ collide and annihilate, jumping the surface sum.

What is true. Let $n\ge 2$, let $P$ have a continuous density $p$ (in particular Gaussian), and let $A=\bigcup_{i=1}^mB(c_i,r_i)$ be a finite union of distinct open balls ($m\ge1$; $m=0$ is trivial). Put $d(x)=d(x,A)$ and $F(t)=P(A_t)$. Since $P$ charges no finite union of spheres, $F(t)=\int_{\{d<t\}}p$. By the coarea formula applied to the $1$-Lipschitz $d$ (with $|\nabla d|=1$ a.e. on $\{d>0\}$), for $0\le s<t$,
$$F(t)-F(s)=\int_{\{s<d<t\}}p\,dx=\int_s^tG(u)\,du,\qquad G(u):=\int_{\{d=u\}}p\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},$$
and $\{d=u\}=\partial A_u=\bigsqcup_iS_i(u)$ up to $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-null sets, where $S_i(u)=\partial B(c_i,r_i+u)\setminus\bigcup_{j\ne i}B(c_j,r_j+u)$. Parametrize $\partial B(c_i,r_i+u)$ by $x=c_i+(r_i+u)\theta$, $\theta\in S^{n-1}$; then with $\alpha_{ij}=c_i-c_j$,
$$G_i(u):=\int_{S_i(u)}p\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=(r_i+u)^{n-1}\int_{E_i(u)}p(c_i+(r_i+u)\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta),$$
where $E_i(u)=\{\theta:\langle\alpha_{ij},\theta\rangle\ge\beta_{ij}(u)\ \forall j\ne i\}$ and $\beta_{ij}(u)=\frac{(r_j+u)^2-(r_i+u)^2-|\alpha_{ij}|^2}{2(r_i+u)}$ is continuous in $u>0$ (for $\alpha_{ij}=0$ the constraint is $u$-independent: it is vacuous or forces $G_i\equiv0$). Fix $u_0>0$. For $\sigma$-a.e. $\theta$ (all but a finite union of great subspheres $\{\langle\alpha_{ij},\theta\rangle=\beta_{ij}(u_0)\}$, null since $n\ge2$), $\mathbf{1}_{E_i(u)}(\theta)\to\mathbf{1}_{E_i(u_0)}(\theta)$ as $u\to u_0$; with $p$ uniformly continuous on compacta, dominated convergence gives continuity of $u\mapsto\int_{E_i(u)}p(c_i+(r_i+u)\theta)d\sigma$, hence of $G_i$. Thus $G=\sum_iG_i$ is continuous on $(0,\infty)$, and $F(t)=F(0)+\int_0^tG$ is $C^1$ there with $F'(t)=\int_{\partial A_t}p\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$. ∎
