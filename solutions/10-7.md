**Central idea.** Apply the infinitesimal hypothesis to the enlarged sets $A_t$ themselves (using $(A_t)_s = A_{t+s}$) to get a differential inequality for $p(t) = P(A_t)$; a first-crossing argument with $\psi(t) = F^{-1}(p(t))$ then forces $\psi(t) \ge \psi(0) + ct$, for if $\psi$ dipped below the line $\alpha + (c-\delta)t$ at a first time $T$, the difference quotient along a sequence $t_n \searrow T$ of "bad" points would contradict the differential inequality at $T$.

**Solution.** Take closed enlargements $A_t = \{x : d(x,A) \le t\}$, so $A_0 = \bar A \supseteq A$ and $p(t) := P(A_t)$ is nondecreasing and right-continuous. Since $d(x,A_t) = \max(0,d(x,A)-t)$ (nearest-point projection), $(A_t)_s = A_{t+s}$ for $s,t \ge 0$. Assume $F:\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)$ is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism with $f = F' > 0$ (as for $\Phi$), extended by $F^{-1}(0) = -\infty$, $F^{-1}(1) = +\infty$.

If $P(A) \in \{0,1\}$ the claim is trivial ($p(t) \ge P(A)$ and $F(F^{-1}(P(A))+ct) = P(A)$). Assume $0 < P(A) \le p(0) \le 1$; if $p(0) = 1$ then $p \equiv 1$ and both sides equal $1$. Let $p(0) \in (0,1)$, $\alpha = F^{-1}(p(0)) \ge F^{-1}(P(A))$; it suffices to show $p(t) \ge F(\alpha+ct)$.

Applying the hypothesis to the Borel set $A_t$ and using $(A_t)_s = A_{t+s}$,
$$\liminf_{s\searrow 0}\frac{p(t+s)-p(t)}{s} \ge cf(F^{-1}(p(t))) \tag{*}$$
whenever $p(t) \in (0,1)$. Put $\psi(t) = F^{-1}(p(t))$ (nondecreasing, right-continuous where finite). Fix $\delta > 0$ and let $S = \{t \ge 0 : \psi(t) < \alpha+(c-\delta)t\}$, $T = \inf S$ (with $\inf\emptyset = +\infty$). Suppose $T < \infty$. Choose $t_n \in S$ with $t_n \searrow T$. Right-continuity gives $\psi(T) = \lim_n\psi(t_n) \le \alpha+(c-\delta)T$ (the limit is finite, so $p(T) \in (0,1)$); monotonicity gives $\psi(T) \ge \psi(t) \ge \alpha+(c-\delta)t$ for $t < T$, whence $\psi(T) = \alpha+(c-\delta)T$.

Now $(*)$ at $t = T$ and the sequence $r_n = t_n-T \searrow 0$ give, since $\psi(t_n) < \alpha+(c-\delta)t_n$ and $F$ is increasing,
$$cf(\psi(T)) \le \liminf_{s\searrow0}\frac{p(T+s)-p(T)}{s} \le \liminf_n\frac{F(\alpha+(c-\delta)t_n)-F(\alpha+(c-\delta)T)}{t_n-T} = (c-\delta)f(\psi(T)).$$
As $f(\psi(T)) > 0$, this yields $c \le c-\delta$, a contradiction. Hence $S = \emptyset$, i.e. $\psi(t) \ge \alpha+(c-\delta)t$ for all $t \ge 0$; letting $\delta \to 0$ gives $p(t) \ge F(\alpha+ct) \ge F(F^{-1}(P(A))+ct)$. ∎
