**Central idea.** The error set $E$ is increasing, so Theorem 10.11 applies once every point of a vertex boundary has $\ge\Delta$ edges leaving the set. For codes this count is provable on the *outer* side (correctly decoded words adjacent to an error word), not on the inner side that Theorem 10.11 uses for $E$. Complementing coordinates turns the outer count into an inner count for an increasing set, but at parameter $1-p$; this proves the threshold bound with $\log(1-p)$ in place of $\log p$. The printed $\log p$ form survives every brute-force test but is not proved here.

**Solution.** **Status: incomplete**. Fully proved below: the outer-boundary lemma and the variant bound $(\ast)$ with $-\log(1-p)$. The printed bound with $-\log p$ is **not** proved; the exact missing step is inequality (D) in part 3. Part 4 documents why the natural routes fail (counterexamples re-verified numerically).

**0. Setup.** Write $|y|$ for Hamming weight, $y\cap c$ for the common support, and $\delta_c(y)=d(y,c)-d(y,0)=|c|-2|y\cap c|$. ML decoding leaves ties open; take either translation-invariant convention: ties count as errors, $A_0=\{y:\delta_c(y)>0\ \forall c\ne0\}$, or ties count as correct, $A_0=\{y:\delta_c(y)\ge0\ \forall c\ne0\}$. Since $d(v\oplus x,c)=d(x,c\oplus v)$ and $c\mapsto c\oplus v$ is a bijection of $\mathcal C$ sending $v$ to $0$, $A_v=A_0$, so $\mathrm{err}_v=\mathrm{err}_0=\mathbb P_p(E)$ with $E=A_0^c$. Flipping a $0$ to $1$ at $j$ changes $\delta_c$ by $-2\cdot\mathbf 1\{j\in c\}$, so every $\delta_c$ is nonincreasing upward: $E$ is increasing and $A_0$ decreasing. Also $0\notin E$ and $1^n\in E$ ($\delta_c(1^n)=-|c|<0$). By Russo's lemma $\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb P_p(E)=I_p(E)>0$ (some edge of the cube crosses $\partial E$ and $\mu_p$ has full support), so $\mathrm{err}_0$ increases continuously and strictly from $0$ to $1$ and $p_c$ is unique. Assume $\mathcal C\ne\{0\}$ (otherwise $\mathrm{err}_v\equiv0$ and $p_c$ is undefined) and $\Delta\ge1$ (if $\Delta=0$ both claims read $\Phi(0)=1/2$ and follow from monotonicity).

**1. Lemma (outer count).** If $y\in A_0$, $y_i=0$ and $y\oplus e_i\in E$, then $g(y):=|\{j:y_j=0,\ y\oplus e_j\in E\}|\ge\Delta$.

*Proof.* Ties as errors: some $c\ne0$ has $\delta_c(y)-2\cdot\mathbf 1\{i\in c\}\le0<\delta_c(y)$, forcing $i\in c$ and $\delta_c(y)\in\{1,2\}$. For every $j\in c\setminus y$, $\delta_c(y\oplus e_j)=\delta_c(y)-2\le0$, so $y\oplus e_j\in E$. Hence $g(y)\ge|c\setminus y|=|c|-|y\cap c|=\tfrac12(|c|+\delta_c(y))\ge\tfrac12(2\Delta+1)$, i.e. $g(y)\ge\Delta+1$. Ties as correct: likewise $\delta_c(y)\in\{0,1\}$, every $j\in c\setminus y$ gives $\delta_c(y\oplus e_j)<0$, and $g(y)\ge\tfrac12|c|\ge\Delta$. ∎

**2. Proved variant.** For $0<p<p_c$,
$$\mathrm{err}_v(p)\le\Phi\Big(\sqrt{2\Delta}\big(\sqrt{-\log(1-p)}-\sqrt{-\log(1-p_c)}\big)\Big),\qquad(\ast)$$
and the reverse inequality holds for $p_c<p<1$.

*Proof.* Let $\tau(y)=1^n\oplus y$ and $B=\tau(A_0)$; $B$ is increasing because $A_0$ is decreasing. If $X$ has i.i.d. Bernoulli$(p)$ coordinates, $\tau(X)$ has i.i.d. Bernoulli$(q)$ coordinates, $q=1-p$, so $\mathbb P_q(B)=1-\mathrm{err}_0(p)$ and $q_{1/2}:=1-p_c$ satisfies $\mathbb P_{q_{1/2}}(B)=1/2$. For $z=\tau(y)\in B$ with a neighbour outside $B$, the edges leaving $B$ from $z$ (flips $z_j=1\to0$) are exactly the flips $y_j=0\to1$ entering $E$, so $h_B(z)=g(y)\ge\Delta$ by the Lemma: $h_B\ge\Delta$ on $\partial_V(B)$. Theorem 10.11 with $k=\Delta$ at parameter $q$ gives, for $q>q_{1/2}$ (i.e. $p<p_c$),
$$1-\mathrm{err}_0(p)\ge\Phi\big(\sqrt{2\Delta}(\sqrt{-\log(1-p_c)}-\sqrt{-\log(1-p)})\big),$$
and $1-\Phi(t)=\Phi(-t)$ yields $(\ast)$; the case $p>p_c$ is identical with inequalities reversed. (Under ties-as-errors, $\Delta$ may be replaced by $\Delta+1$.) ∎

Hassani–Kudekar–Ordentlich–Polyanskiy–Urbanke (arXiv:1801.09481, Thm. 1) restate Tillich–Zémor's BSC result with $\sqrt{d_{\min}}=\sqrt{2\Delta}$ and $\log(1-\varepsilon)$, not $\log\varepsilon$, consistent with $(\ast)$ (original paper not consulted). The printed bound is not a consequence of $(\ast)$: for $\Delta=2$, $p_c=0.1$, $p=0.01$ the printed right-hand side is $0.1044$, that of $(\ast)$ is $0.3268$; for $p,p_c<1/2$ the printed form is the stronger one.

**3. What would finish the printed bound.** Let $P(p)=\mathbb P_p(E)\in(0,1)$ and $\gamma=\varphi\circ\Phi^{-1}$. It suffices to prove
$$p\,I_p(E)=\mathbb E_p h_E(X)\ \ge\ \frac{\sqrt\Delta\,\gamma(P(p))}{\sqrt{2\log(1/p)}}\qquad\text{for all }p\in(0,1).\qquad\text{(D)}$$
Indeed, by Russo's lemma (D) says $\frac{d}{dp}\Phi^{-1}(P(p))=P'(p)/\gamma(P(p))\ge\sqrt{2\Delta}\,\frac{d}{dp}\big(-\sqrt{-\log p}\big)$; integrating from $p$ to $p_c$ (where $\Phi^{-1}(1/2)=0$) gives $\Phi^{-1}(P(p))\le\sqrt{2\Delta}(\sqrt{-\log p_c}-\sqrt{-\log p})$ for $p<p_c$, and integrating from $p_c$ to $p$ gives the reverse for $p>p_c$ — exactly the printed statement. (D) is precisely what the proof of Theorem 10.11 extracts from "$h\ge k$ on $\partial_V$". **Missing step: a proof of (D) for the error set of a linear code.** (D) held in all 216 test cases below.

**4. Why the natural routes fail** (all re-checked numerically; scripts in `external_audit_2026-10-30/compare/repair_checks/10-9_*.py`).

(a) *Inner count $h_E\ge\Delta$ on $\partial_V(E)$ is false.* $n=7$, $c=\mathbf 1_{\{0,1,2,3\}}$, $c'=\mathbf 1_{\{3,4,5,6\}}$; the code $\{0,c,c',c\oplus c'\}$ has $d_{\min}=4$, $\Delta=2$. Ties as errors, $x=\mathbf 1_{\{0,3,4\}}$: $d(x,0)=d(x,c)=d(x,c')=3$, so $x\in E$; removing coordinate 0 leaves a tie with $c'$, removing 4 a tie with $c$, and only removing 3 leaves $E$: $h_E(x)=1<2$. Ties as correct also fails (random $n=8$ code, generators $209,92,107$ with bit $i$ = coordinate $i$: minimum boundary count $1<\Delta=2$). The step "every $j\in S_1$ is pivotal" in the previous version of this solution is therefore false.

(b) *Complementing does not preserve $\log p$.* Part 2 is the correct use of the complement and gives $\log(1-p)$. Applying Theorem 10.11 verbatim to a decreasing set at parameter $p$ is invalid: $A=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{40}:|x|\le1\}$ has $39$ outward edges at each boundary point, $p_{1/2}=0.04160$, and at $p=0.001$, $\Phi^{-1}(\mathbb P_p(A))=3.171<7.464=\sqrt{78}(\sqrt{-\log p}-\sqrt{-\log p_{1/2}})$.

(c) *Averaged count $\mathbb E_p h_E\ge\sqrt\Delta\,\mathbb E_p\sqrt{h_E}$ is false.* Combined with Corollary 10.10 ($\mathbb E_p\sqrt{h_E}\ge\gamma(P)/\sqrt{2\log(1/p)}$) it would imply (D), but: $n=9$, generators $326,115,314$ (bit $i$ = coordinate $i$), $d_{\min}=4$, $\Delta=2$, ties as correct, $p=0.695$: $\mathbb E h_E=0.2232<0.2376=\sqrt2\,\mathbb E\sqrt{h_E}$. (It failed in 7 of 216 cases.) So any proof of (D) must go beyond Corollary 10.10 combined with this averaged count.

**5. Numerical support for the printed statement.** For 216 (code, tie-convention) cases — random linear codes with $5\le n\le11$, dimension $\le4$, $\Delta\ge1$, exact probabilities on a 1999-point grid of $p$ — the printed bound, $(\ast)$ and (D) all held (minimum margins $\ge0$); the outer-count Lemma held in every case, the inner count failed in 11. The independent audit found the same on $\{0,c,c',c\oplus c'\}$ codes and 60 further random codes. The printed statement is thus plausibly true but unproved here.
