**Central idea.** The wimpy variance sees only marginal variances, while $\mathrm{Var}(\sup_s \sum_i X_{i,s})$ also depends on the dependence across $s$. With $n=1$, $\mathcal{T}=\{1,2\}$, take $Y_1,Y_2$ Bernoulli$(1/4)$ with disjoint supports: each has variance $3/16$ (so $\sigma^2=3/16$), but $\max(Y_1,Y_2)\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ has variance $1/4>3/16$.

**Solution.** Let $n=1$, $\mathcal{T}=\{1,2\}$, and write $Y_s=X_{1,s}$. The wimpy variance is $\sigma^2=\max_{s=1,2}\mathrm{Var}(Y_s)$, and $Z=\max(Y_1,Y_2)$.

Define the joint law of $(Y_1,Y_2)$ on $\{0,1\}^2$ by
$$\mathbb{P}(Y_1=1,Y_2=0)=\mathbb{P}(Y_1=0,Y_2=1)=\tfrac14,\quad \mathbb{P}(Y_1=0,Y_2=0)=\tfrac12,\quad \mathbb{P}(Y_1=1,Y_2=1)=0.$$
Each marginal is Bernoulli$(1/4)$, so $\mathrm{Var}(Y_1)=\mathrm{Var}(Y_2)=\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac34=\tfrac{3}{16}$, hence $\sigma^2=3/16$.

But $Z=\max(Y_1,Y_2)$ equals $1$ iff at least one of $Y_1,Y_2$ is $1$, so $\mathbb{P}(Z=1)=1/4+1/4=1/2$; i.e., $Z\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ and
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac14>\tfrac{3}{16}=\sigma^2.$$
Thus $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sigma^2$ fails in general. ∎
