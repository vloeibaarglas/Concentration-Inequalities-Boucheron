**Central idea.** Condition on the $X$'s to reduce to fixed vectors, then derive the Gaussian contraction from the Rademacher contraction: write $Y_i = \varepsilon_i |Y_i|$ with $\varepsilon_i$ Rademacher independent of $|Y_i|$, and absorb the random amplitudes into new contractions $\psi_i(t) = |Y_i|\varphi_i(t/|Y_i|)$, so that $\sum_i Y_i\varphi_i(X_{i,s})$ becomes a Rademacher sum to which the contraction principle applies conditionally on $|Y|$.

**Solution.** *Convention.* "Lipschitz" means $1$-Lipschitz here, by the book's own convention: p. 216 states "Recall that a function $f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}$ is Lipschitz if for all $x,y\in\mathcal{X}$, $|f(x)-f(y)|\le d(x,y)$", and Theorem 11.6 (the contraction principle) uses the word in the same sense. The convention matters, since the dimension-free constants cannot hold for an arbitrary Lipschitz constant: with $n=1$, $|\mathcal{T}|=1$, $X_1=\pm1$ Rademacher, $\varphi_1(t)=5t$ and $\Psi(x)=x$, the left side would be $\tfrac52\,\mathbb{E}|Y_1|\approx1.995$ and the right side $2\,\mathbb{E}|Y_1|\approx1.596$. The $Y_i$ are taken independent of the $X_i$, as is implicit in the statement. Condition on $X$; it suffices to prove the inequality for fixed vectors $x_i = (x_{i,s})_{s \in \mathcal{T}}$, then integrate over $X$.

Write $Y_i = \varepsilon_i A_i$ with $A_i = |Y_i| \ge 0$ and $\varepsilon_i = \operatorname{sign}(Y_i)$ Rademacher, $\varepsilon_i$ independent of $A_i$, the pairs $(\varepsilon_i, A_i)$ independent across $i$. For fixed $a_i \ge 0$ define $\psi_{i,a_i}(t) = a_i \varphi_i(t/a_i)$ (and $\psi_{i,0} \equiv 0$). Since $\varphi_i$ is $1$-Lipschitz, so is $\psi_{i,a_i}$: $|\psi_{i,a_i}(t) - \psi_{i,a_i}(s)| = a_i|\varphi_i(t/a_i) - \varphi_i(s/a_i)| \le |t-s|$; moreover $\psi_{i,a_i}(0) = 0$ and $\psi_{i,A_i}(A_i x_{i,s}) = A_i \varphi_i(x_{i,s})$.

Hence, with $A = (A_i)$ fixed,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i \varphi_i(x_{i,s}) = \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i A_i \varphi_i(x_{i,s}) = \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i \,\psi_{i,A_i}(A_i x_{i,s}).$$
By the Rademacher contraction principle (Ledoux--Talagrand, Theorem 4.12) applied conditionally on $A$ to the fixed vectors $(A_1 x_{1,s}, \dots, A_n x_{n,s})$ and the contractions $\psi_{i,A_i}$ (with $\Psi - \Psi(0)$ in place of $\Psi$ to ensure vanishing at $0$, the constant cancelling),
$$\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big[\Psi\Big(\sup_{s}\Big|\sum_i \varepsilon_i \psi_{i,A_i}(A_i x_{i,s})\Big|\Big)\Big] \le \mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big[\Psi\Big(2\sup_{s}\Big|\sum_i \varepsilon_i A_i x_{i,s}\Big|\Big)\Big].$$
The left side equals $\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}[\Psi(\sup_s |\sum_i Y_i \varphi_i(x_{i,s})|)]$ given $A$, and the right side equals $\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}[\Psi(2\sup_s |\sum_i Y_i x_{i,s}|)]$ given $A$. Taking expectation over $A$,
$$\mathbb{E}_Y\Big[\Psi\Big(\sup_s\Big|\sum_i Y_i \varphi_i(x_{i,s})\Big|\Big)\Big] \le \mathbb{E}_Y\Big[\Psi\Big(2\sup_s\Big|\sum_i Y_i x_{i,s}\Big|\Big)\Big].$$
Since $\Psi$ is nondecreasing, $\Psi(u/2) \le \Psi(u)$, so the left side dominates $\mathbb{E}_Y[\Psi(\tfrac12 \sup_s |\sum_i Y_i \varphi_i(x_{i,s})|)]$. Integrating over $X$ gives the claim. ∎
