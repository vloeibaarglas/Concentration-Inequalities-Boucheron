**Central idea.** By homogeneity assume $|\lambda_i| \le 1$. Symmetrize with independent Rademachers, then replace the coefficients $\lambda_i$ by $1$ one coordinate at a time: for fixed $y$, the map $a \mapsto \tfrac12\|y+ax\|_{\mathbb{B}}^p + \tfrac12\|y-ax\|_{\mathbb{B}}^p$ is convex and even in $a$, hence maximized on $[-1,1]$ at $a = \pm 1$.

**Solution.** If $\|\lambda\|_{\infty} = 0$ there is nothing to prove; dividing by $\|\lambda\|_{\infty}$ we assume $|\lambda_i| \le 1$ for all $i$. By symmetry, $(X_1,\dots,X_n) \stackrel{d}{=} (\varepsilon_1 X_1, \dots, \varepsilon_n X_n)$ for independent Rademachers $\varepsilon_i$ independent of $X$, so
$$\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i X_i\Big\|^p = \mathbb{E}_{X,\varepsilon}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i \lambda_i X_i\Big\|^p.$$
Fix deterministic $x_1,\dots,x_n \in \mathbb{B}$ and $|a_i| \le 1$. We claim $\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i a_i x_i\|^p \le \mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i x_i\|^p$. Indeed, for fixed $y, x \in \mathbb{B}$ the function $f(a) = \tfrac12\|y+ax\|^p + \tfrac12\|y-ax\|^p$ is convex in $a$ (as $\|\cdot\|^p \circ$ affine is convex for $p \ge 1$) and even, so $f(a) \le f(1)$ for $|a| \le 1$. Writing $S(a_1,\dots,a_n) = \mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i a_i x_i\|^p$ and conditioning on $\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_{n-1}$,
$$S(a_1,\dots,a_n) = \mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon_{<n}}[f_n(a_n)] \le \mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon_{<n}}[f_n(1)] = S(a_1,\dots,a_{n-1},1),$$
where $f_n(a) = \mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon_n}\|y + \varepsilon_n a x_n\|^p$ with $y = \sum_{i<n}\varepsilon_i a_i x_i$. Iterating over the coordinates gives $S(a_1,\dots,a_n) \le S(1,\dots,1)$.

Applying this with $a_i = \lambda_i$, $x_i = X_i$ conditionally on $X$,
$$\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i \lambda_i X_i\Big\|^p \le \mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i X_i\Big\|^p \quad \text{a.s.}$$
Taking $\mathbb{E}_X$ and using symmetry once more, $\mathbb{E}\|\sum_i \lambda_i X_i\|^p \le \mathbb{E}\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i X_i\|^p = \mathbb{E}\|\sum_i X_i\|^p$. For general $\lambda$, apply to $\lambda_i/\|\lambda\|_{\infty}$ and multiply by $\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^p$. ∎
