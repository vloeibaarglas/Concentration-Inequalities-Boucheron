**Central idea.** Symmetrize: $X_i \stackrel{d}{=} \varepsilon_i|X_i|$, $Y_i \stackrel{d}{=} \varepsilon_i|Y_i|$ with $\varepsilon_i$ Rademacher independent of the moduli. Condition on the moduli; with $0 \le |X_i| \le |Y_i|$ write $|X_i| = \lambda_i|Y_i|$ for $\lambda_i \in [0,1]$, and apply the deterministic contraction from Exercise 11-12 to the vectors $|Y_i|v_i$.

**Solution.** (``$|X_n| \le |Y_n|$ for all $n$'' is read as $|X_i| \le |Y_i|$ for all $i$ a.s.) By symmetry and independence, with $\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_n$ iid Rademacher independent of all $X_j, Y_j$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n X_i v_i \stackrel{d}{=} \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i |X_i| v_i, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i v_i \stackrel{d}{=} \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i |Y_i| v_i,$$
so with $a_i = |X_i|$, $b_i = |Y_i|$ (hence $0 \le a_i \le b_i$ a.s.),
$$\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_i X_i v_i\Big\|^p = \mathbb{E}_{a,b}\,\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i a_i v_i\Big\|^p,$$
and likewise for $Y$. Fix $a_i, b_i$ with $0 \le a_i \le b_i$ and set $\lambda_i = a_i/b_i \in [0,1]$ ($\lambda_i = 0$ if $b_i = 0$). Then $\sum_i \varepsilon_i a_i v_i = \sum_i \varepsilon_i \lambda_i (b_i v_i)$, and by the deterministic contraction inequality proved in Exercise 11-12 applied to the fixed vectors $x_i = b_i v_i \in \mathbb{B}$,
$$\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i \lambda_i (b_i v_i)\Big\|^p \le \mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i (b_i v_i)\Big\|^p = \mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Big\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i b_i v_i\Big\|^p.$$
Integrating over $(a,b) = (|X|,|Y|)$ yields $\mathbb{E}\|\sum_i X_i v_i\|^p \le \mathbb{E}\|\sum_i Y_i v_i\|^p$. ∎
