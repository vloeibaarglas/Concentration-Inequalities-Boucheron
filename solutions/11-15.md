**Central idea.** Write $Y_i=|Y_i|\varepsilon_i$ with $\varepsilon_i=\mathrm{sign}(Y_i)$ Rademacher independent of $|Y_i|$ (the Gaussian law is continuous symmetric). Conditioning on $|Y|$ and applying the contraction principle with $M=\max_i|Y_i|$ gives $\mathbb{E}[\|\sum_iY_iv_i\|\mid|Y|]\leq M\,\mathbb{E}\|\sum_i\varepsilon_iv_i\|$, so everything reduces to bounding $a_n:=\mathbb{E}\max_{i\le n}|Y_i|$. The maximal inequality over the $2n$ variables $\pm Y_i$ gives $a_n\le\sqrt{2\log(2n)}$ at once; the printed constant $\sqrt{2\log n}$ needs the sharper fact $a_n<\sqrt{2\log n}$ for $n\ge2$, proved analytically for large $n$ (a tail split at $b=\sqrt{2\log n}$) and by a computer-assisted check for moderate $n$.

**Erratum.** As printed (statement, not hint), the inequality is false for $n=1$: with $v_1\neq0$, the left side is $\mathbb{E}|Y_1|\,\|v_1\|=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\|v_1\|\approx0.798\,\|v_1\|>0$, while $\sqrt{2\log1}=0$ makes the right side $0$. This is the only failure: the printed bound holds for every $n\ge2$, which is what we prove below. (For all $n\ge1$ one may instead use the constant $\sqrt{2\log(2n)}$; see the Remark.)

**Solution.** *Step 1: reduction to a maximum of Gaussians.* The $Y_i$ are continuous symmetric, so $\varepsilon_i:=\mathrm{sign}(Y_i)$ are iid Rademacher, independent of the $|Y_i|$'s, and $Y_i=|Y_i|\varepsilon_i$ a.s.

Condition on $|Y|=(|Y_1|,\ldots,|Y_n|)$ and put $M=\max_i|Y_i|\geq0$, $\alpha_i=|Y_i|/M\in[0,1]$ (if $M=0$ both sides below are $0$). The $\varepsilon_i$ remain iid Rademacher given $|Y|$, so by the contraction principle,
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\|\sum_iY_iv_i\Big\|\Big||Y|\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\|\sum_i\alpha_i\varepsilon_i(Mv_i)\Big\|\Big||Y|\Big]\leq M\,\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_i\varepsilon_iv_i\Big\|,$$
the last expectation being deterministic since $\varepsilon\perp|Y|$. Taking expectations and using $\varepsilon_i\stackrel{d}{=}X_i$,
$$\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_iY_iv_i\Big\|\leq a_n\,\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_iX_iv_i\Big\|,\qquad a_n:=\mathbb{E}\max_{i\le n}|Y_i|.$$

*Step 2: $a_n<\sqrt{2\log n}$ for $n\ge2$.* Let $\Phi,\varphi$ be the standard normal cdf and density, $\bar\Phi=1-\Phi$, and $b=\sqrt{2\log n}$, so that $e^{-b^2/2}=1/n$ and $2n\varphi(b)=\sqrt{2/\pi}$. Then
$$a_n=\int_0^\infty\mathbb{P}\{M>t\}\,dt,\qquad \mathbb{P}\{M>t\}=1-(2\Phi(t)-1)^n\le\min\big(1,\,2n\bar\Phi(t)\big),$$
and $a_n$ is nondecreasing in $n$ (the integrand is).

*Large $n$: $b\ge5$, i.e. $n\ge e^{12.5}\approx2.68\cdot10^5$.* Write $a_n=b-\int_0^b\mathbb{P}\{M\le t\}\,dt+\int_b^\infty\mathbb{P}\{M>t\}\,dt$.

Upper tail: using $\int_b^\infty\bar\Phi(t)\,dt=\varphi(b)-b\bar\Phi(b)$ and Gordon's lower bound $\bar\Phi(b)\ge\frac{b}{1+b^2}\varphi(b)$,
$$\int_b^\infty\mathbb{P}\{M>t\}\,dt\le2n\big(\varphi(b)-b\bar\Phi(b)\big)\le\frac{2n\varphi(b)}{1+b^2}=\frac{\sqrt{2/\pi}}{1+b^2}<\frac{0.8}{b^2}.$$
Lower part: $t\mapsto\mathbb{P}\{M\le t\}$ is nondecreasing, so $\int_0^b\mathbb{P}\{M\le t\}\,dt\ge\frac1b\,\mathbb{P}\{M\le b-1/b\}\ge\frac1b\big(1-2n\bar\Phi(b-1/b)\big)$. By the Mills bound $\bar\Phi(s)\le\varphi(s)/s$ and $\varphi(b-1/b)=\varphi(b)\,e^{1-1/(2b^2)}\le e\,\varphi(b)=\frac{e}{n\sqrt{2\pi}}$,
$$2n\bar\Phi(b-1/b)\le\frac{2e}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,(b-1/b)}\le0.452\qquad(b\ge5).$$
Hence
$$a_n\le b-\frac{0.548}{b}+\frac{0.8}{b^2}<b,$$
since $0.8/b\le0.16<0.548$.

*Moderate $n$: $2\le n\le323{,}714$ (computer-assisted).* The integrand $t\mapsto1-(2\Phi(t)-1)^n$ is decreasing, so the left-endpoint Riemann sum with step $10^{-4}$ on $[0,12]$ is an upper bound for the integral over $[0,12]$, and the rest is at most $\int_{12}^\infty2n\bar\Phi=2n(\varphi(12)-12\bar\Phi(12))$, which is negligible. Call the resulting upper bound $\bar a_n\ge a_n$. We evaluated it in double precision (with `log_ndtr`, `log1p` and `expm1` for $(1-2\bar\Phi)^n$); this is floating point, not interval arithmetic, but every margin below exceeds rounding error by many orders of magnitude.

- For each $n=2,\ldots,40$: $\sqrt{2\log n}-\bar a_n\ge0.049$, the minimum being at $n=2$ ($a_2\approx1.128<\sqrt{2\log2}\approx1.177$).
- For $n\ge40$: on the grid $N_0=40$, $N_{k+1}=\lfloor1.15\,N_k\rfloor$ up to $N_{65}=323{,}714$, we checked $\bar a_{N_{k+1}}<\sqrt{2\log N_k}$ for every $k$, with minimum margin $0.234$. For $N_k\le n\le N_{k+1}$, monotonicity gives $a_n\le a_{N_{k+1}}\le\bar a_{N_{k+1}}<\sqrt{2\log N_k}\le\sqrt{2\log n}$.

Sample values: $a_{10}\approx1.881<2.146$, $a_{10^5}\approx4.533<4.799$. The two ranges overlap ($323{,}714>2.68\cdot10^5$), so $a_n<\sqrt{2\log n}$ for all $n\ge2$.

*Conclusion.* For $n\ge2$,
$$\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^nY_iv_i\Big\|\leq\sqrt{2\log n}\,\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^nX_iv_i\Big\|.$$
The $\sqrt{\log n}$ order is necessary: in $\ell_\infty^n$ with $v_i=e_i$ the left side is $a_n\sim\sqrt{2\log n}$ while $\mathbb{E}\|\sum_iX_ie_i\|_\infty=1$.

*Remark (simple constant valid for all $n\ge1$).* $M=\max_{j\leq2n}Z_j$ with $Z_j$ the $2n$ standard Gaussians $\pm Y_i$. By the maximal inequality (Theorem 2.5) with $\psi(\lambda)=\lambda^2/2$,
$$a_n\leq\inf_{\lambda>0}\frac{\log(2n)+\lambda^2/2}{\lambda}=\sqrt{2\log(2n)},$$
so $\mathbb{E}\|\sum_iY_iv_i\|\leq\sqrt{2\log(2n)}\,\mathbb{E}\|\sum_iX_iv_i\|$ for every $n\ge1$, including $n=1$. ∎
