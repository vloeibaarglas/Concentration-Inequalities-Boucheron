**Central idea.** Follow the Efron–Stein + self-bounding pattern of Theorem 11.10 (via Lemma 11.11), but center the leave-one-out approximators: use $\tilde Z = Z - nE$ and $\tilde Z_i = Z_i - (n-1)E$ with $E = \mathbb{E}Z/n$, so that $\tilde Z - \tilde Z_i = (Z-Z_i) - E$. The $\phi$-argument of Lemma 11.11 then runs with linear coefficient $2(1-E)$ instead of $2$, and the $E^2$ terms cancel, saving $(\mathbb{E}Z)^2/n$.

**Solution.** Use the notation of Theorem 11.10: $Z = \sup_{s\in T}\sum_{i=1}^n X_{i,s}$ with $X_i$ iid, $|X_{i,s}|\le 1$, $\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}=0$; $Z_i = \sup_{s\in T}\sum_{j\ne i}X_{j,s}$; $W_i = Z-Z_i$. Take $T$ countable (the separable case follows by approximation). Fix $\varepsilon > 0$ and, enumerating $T = \{s_1, s_2, \dots\}$, choose $\hat s_i \in T$ $X^{(i)}$-measurably with $\sum_{j\ne i}X_{j,\hat s_i} \ge Z_i - \varepsilon$ (least such index); put $Y_i = X_{i,\hat s_i}$. Then $W_i \le 1$, $Y_i \le 1$, $\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Y_i = 0$ (with $\mathbb{E}^{(i)}$ conditional on $X^{(i)}$, as $X_i \perp X^{(i)}$), $Y_i \le W_i + \varepsilon$ (since $W_i = Z - Z_i \ge \sum_j X_{j,\hat s_i} - Z_i \ge Y_i - \varepsilon$), and $\sum_{i=1}^n W_i \le Z$. Let $\sigma^2 = \sup_{s\in T}\mathbb{E}X_{1,s}^2$ and $E = \mathbb{E}Z/n \ge 0$.

Put $\tilde Z = Z - nE$ and $\tilde Z_i = Z_i - (n-1)E$. Then $\mathrm{Var}(Z) = \mathrm{Var}(\tilde Z)$, $\tilde Z_i$ is $X^{(i)}$-measurable, and
$$\tilde Z - \tilde Z_i = (Z-Z_i) - E = W_i - E.$$
By the Efron–Stein inequality (Theorem 3.1) and the $L^2$-projection property,
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}\big[(\tilde Z-\mathbb{E}^{(i)}\tilde Z)^2\big] \le \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}^{(i)}\big[(\tilde Z-\tilde Z_i)^2\big] = \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}\big[(W_i-E)^2\big].$$

Write $\tilde W_i = W_i - E$ and $\tilde Y_i = Y_i - E - \varepsilon$, so $\tilde Y_i \le \tilde W_i$. With $\phi(x) = x^2 - 2(1-E)x$,
$$\phi(\tilde W_i)-\phi(\tilde Y_i) = (\tilde W_i-\tilde Y_i)\big(\tilde W_i+\tilde Y_i-2(1-E)\big) \le 0,$$
since $\tilde W_i-\tilde Y_i = W_i-Y_i+\varepsilon \ge 0$ and $\tilde W_i+\tilde Y_i-2(1-E) = W_i+Y_i-2-\varepsilon \le 0$ (as $W_i\le 1$, $Y_i\le 1$). Hence, taking $\mathbb{E}^{(i)}$,
$$\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[\tilde W_i^2] \le 2(1-E)\,\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[\tilde W_i] + \mathbb{E}^{(i)}[\tilde Y_i^2] - 2(1-E)\,\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[\tilde Y_i].$$
Now $\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[\tilde W_i] = \mathbb{E}^{(i)}[W_i]-E$, $\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[\tilde Y_i] = -E-\varepsilon$ (since $\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Y_i=0$), and $\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[\tilde Y_i^2] = \mathbb{E}^{(i)}[Y_i^2]+(E+\varepsilon)^2$. Thus
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[(W_i-E)^2] &\le 2(1-E)\big(\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[W_i]-E\big) + \mathbb{E}^{(i)}[Y_i^2] + (E+\varepsilon)^2 + 2(1-E)(E+\varepsilon) \\
&= 2(1-E)\,\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[W_i] + \mathbb{E}^{(i)}[Y_i^2] + (E+\varepsilon)^2 + 2(1-E)\varepsilon,
\end{align*}
the $-2(1-E)E$ and $+2(1-E)E$ cancelling. Summing over $i$ and taking expectations, with $\sum_i W_i \le Z$ and $\mathbb{E}[Y_i^2] = \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}[X_{i,s}^2]_{s=\hat s_i}\big] \le \sigma^2$,
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le 2(1-E)\,\mathbb{E}Z + n\sigma^2 + n(E+\varepsilon)^2 + 2n(1-E)\varepsilon.$$
Letting $\varepsilon \to 0$ gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le 2(1-E)\mathbb{E}Z + n\sigma^2 + nE^2$. Since $nE^2 = (\mathbb{E}Z)^2/n = E\,\mathbb{E}Z$, this is $n\sigma^2 + (2-E)\mathbb{E}Z$. ∎
