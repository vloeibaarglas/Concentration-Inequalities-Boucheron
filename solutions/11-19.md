**Central idea.** Apply Theorem 11.1 (second part) to the empirical process $X_{i,s} = \alpha_{i,s}X_i$: since the $X_i$ are centered, $\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le \Sigma^2 + \sigma^2$ where the weak variance is exactly $\mathbb{E}\sup_s \sum_i \alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2$ and the wimpy variance is at most $\sigma^2 \sup_s \sum_i \alpha_{i,s}^2/n$ (using $\mathbb{E}X_i^2 \le \sigma^2/n$ from the moment condition at $q=2$). The crude bound $\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le 2\sigma^2$ follows by dropping the $\alpha_{i,s}^2 \le 1$ weights. For the third inequality, symmetrize the weak-variance term (Lemma 11.9, the opening step of Theorem 11.17's proof): $\mathbb{E}\sup_s \sum_i \alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2 \le \sigma^2 \sup_s \sum_i \alpha_{i,s}^2/n + 2\mathbb{E}\sup_s \sum_i \varepsilon_i \alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2$.

**Solution.** Put $X_{i,s} = \alpha_{i,s}X_i$ for $i = 1,\ldots,n$, $s \in \mathcal{T}$, so $Z = \sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n X_{i,s}$. Since the $X_i$ are independent and centered, $\mathbb{E}X_{i,s} = \alpha_{i,s}\mathbb{E}X_i = 0$ for all $i,s$. (As in the book, we take $\mathcal{T}$ countable, or the process separable, so all suprema are measurable.) The moment hypothesis at $q=2$ gives
$$\mathbb{E}X_i^2 = \mathrm{Var}(X_i) \le 2!\,\frac{c^{0}\sigma^2/n}{2} = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}, \qquad i = 1,\ldots,n.$$

By the second part of Theorem 11.1,
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le \Sigma^2 + \sigma^2,$$
with weak variance $\Sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n X_{i,s}^2$ and wimpy variance $\sigma^2 = \sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}X_{i,s}^2$. Here
$$\Sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2, \qquad \sigma^2 = \sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2\,\mathbb{E}X_i^2 \le \sigma^2 \sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\alpha_{i,s}^2}{n}.$$
This is the second claimed inequality:
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le \sigma^2 \sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2/n + \mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2.$$

For the first inequality, use $|\alpha_{i,s}| \le 1$: $\sup_{s} \sum_i \alpha_{i,s}^2/n \le 1$, and
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2 \le \mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2 = \mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}X_i^2 \le \sigma^2,$$
since $\sum_i X_i^2$ does not depend on $s$. Hence $\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le \sigma^2 + \sigma^2 = 2\sigma^2$.

For the third inequality, bound the weak-variance term by symmetrization. Write $A = \sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2/n$. Then
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2 \le \mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2 (X_i^2 - \mathbb{E}X_i^2) + \sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2\,\mathbb{E}X_i^2 \le \mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2 (X_i^2 - \mathbb{E}X_i^2) + \sigma^2 A.$$
Let $\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n$ be independent Rademacher variables, independent of the $X_i$. By the symmetrization inequality (Lemma 11.4), applied to the independent summands $Y_{i,s} = \alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2$,
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2 (X_i^2 - \mathbb{E}X_i^2) \le 2\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i\,\alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2.$$
(This is Lemma 11.9, the symmetrization step with which the proof of Theorem 11.17 opens; the full strength of Theorem 11.17, which further bounds the Rademacher term via contraction under a symmetry hypothesis, is not needed here.) Thus
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2 \le \sigma^2 A + 2\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i\,\alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2.$$
Substituting into the second inequality gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le \sigma^2 A + \sigma^2 A + 2\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i\,\alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2 = 2\sigma^2 \sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_{i,s}^2/n + 2\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i\,\alpha_{i,s}^2 X_i^2,$$
as claimed. ∎
