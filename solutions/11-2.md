**Central idea.** Read the process diagonally, $X_{i,s}=Y_i\mathbb{1}\{i=s\}$ with $Y_i$ i.i.d.\ Exp$(1)$, so $Z=\max_sY_s$ (the case the hint describes). The Rényi representation gives $\mathbb{E}Z=H_n$ and $\mathrm{Var}(Z)=H_n^{(2)}\le\pi^2/6$. Theorem 11.1 bounds the variance by the strong variance $V$ ($=2n$, or $n$ after centering) or, after centering, by $\Sigma^2+\sigma^2$. Both are much too large: $V$ grows linearly in $n$, and $\Sigma^2+\sigma^2\ge1+H_n^{(2)}+(H_n-1)^2\sim\log^2n$, because the weak variance is driven by the typical size of the maximum rather than its fluctuations. The leave-one-out Efron–Stein bound $\sum_i\mathbb{E}(Z-Z_i)^2$ equals exactly $2$, within a bounded factor of the truth.

**Solution.** *Interpretation.* If all $X_{i,s}$ were literally i.i.d.\ Exp$(1)$, each $\sum_{i=1}^nX_{i,s}$ would be Gamma$(n,1)$, and $Z$ would not be "the supremum of $n$ i.i.d.\ exponentials" with mean $H_n$. We therefore take the diagonal process $X_{i,s}=Y_i\mathbb{1}\{i=s\}$, $1\le i,s\le n$, with $Y_1,\dots,Y_n$ i.i.d.\ Exp$(1)$. The vectors $X_i=(X_{i,s})_s$ are independent, $\sum_iX_{i,s}=Y_s$, and $Z=\max_{1\le s\le n}Y_s$.

*Mean and variance.* $\mathbb{P}(Z>t)=1-(1-e^{-t})^n$, so with $u=1-e^{-t}$,
$$\mathbb{E}Z=\int_0^\infty\!\big(1-(1-e^{-t})^n\big)dt=\int_0^1\!\frac{1-u^n}{1-u}\,du=\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1k=H_n.$$
By the Rényi representation, the spacings of the order statistics are independent with $Y_{(n-k+1)}-Y_{(n-k)}\stackrel{d}{=}E_k/k$ ($Y_{(0)}=0$, $E_k$ i.i.d.\ Exp$(1)$). So $Z\stackrel{d}{=}\sum_{k=1}^nE_k/k$ and
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)=\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{k^2}=:H_n^{(2)}\le\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{k^2}=\frac{\pi^2}6\approx1.645.$$

*The bounds of Theorem 11.1.* Recall $V=\sum_i\mathbb{E}\sup_sX_{i,s}^2$, $\Sigma^2=\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_iX_{i,s}^2$ and $\sigma^2=\sup_s\sum_i\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}^2$. Theorem 11.1 gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le V$ always, and $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\Sigma^2+\sigma^2$ when $\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}=0$ for all $i,s$.

(a) For the process as given (not centered), $\sup_sX_{i,s}^2=Y_i^2$, so
$$V=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}Y_i^2=2n.$$
The second inequality does not apply, because $\mathbb{E}X_{i,i}=1\ne0$.

(b) Centering. Put $\tilde X_{i,s}=(Y_i-1)\mathbb{1}\{i=s\}$. These vectors are independent, $\mathbb{E}\tilde X_{i,s}=0$ for all $i,s$, and $\tilde Z=\sup_s\sum_i\tilde X_{i,s}=\max_s(Y_s-1)=Z-1$, so $\mathrm{Var}(\tilde Z)=\mathrm{Var}(Z)$. For this process
$$\tilde V=\sum_i\mathbb{E}(Y_i-1)^2=n,\qquad \sigma^2=\max_s\mathrm{Var}(Y_s)=1,\qquad \Sigma^2=\mathbb{E}\max_s(Y_s-1)^2 .$$
Taking $s$ to be an index achieving $\max_sY_s$ gives $\max_s(Y_s-1)^2\ge(Z-1)^2$, so
$$\Sigma^2\ge\mathbb{E}(Z-1)^2=\mathrm{Var}(Z)+(\mathbb{E}Z-1)^2=H_n^{(2)}+(H_n-1)^2 .$$
Conversely, fix $s$. If $Y_s\ge1$, then $0\le Y_s-1\le Z-1$. If $0\le Y_s<1$, then $(Y_s-1)^2\le1$. Hence $\max_s(Y_s-1)^2\le(Z-1)^2+1$, and $\Sigma^2\le1+H_n^{(2)}+(H_n-1)^2$. Therefore
$$1+H_n^{(2)}+(H_n-1)^2\ \le\ \Sigma^2+\sigma^2\ \le\ 2+H_n^{(2)}+(H_n-1)^2 .$$

*Comparison.* Since $H_n=\log n+O(1)$, the bound $\Sigma^2+\sigma^2$ is of exact order $\log^2n$. The bound $V$ is of order $n$ ($2n$ uncentered, $n$ centered). Meanwhile $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\pi^2/6$ stays bounded. Both bounds of Theorem 11.1 are off by an unbounded factor: $\Sigma^2$ is dominated by $(\mathbb{E}Z-1)^2$, the squared typical size of the maximum, not by its fluctuations.

*The leave-one-out bound.* Here $Z_i=\sup_s\sum_{j\ne i}X_{j,s}$. For $s\ne i$ the inner sum is $Y_s$; for $s=i$ it is $0$.

- If $n=1$, then $Z_1=0$ and $\mathbb{E}(Z-Z_1)^2=\mathbb{E}Y_1^2=2$.
- If $n\ge2$, then $Z_i=\max(M_i,0)=M_i$ with $M_i=\max_{j\ne i}Y_j\ge0$. So $Z-Z_i=(Y_i-M_i)_+$, where $M_i$ is independent of $Y_i$. For $m\ge0$,
$$\mathbb{E}[(Y_i-m)_+^2]=\int_m^\infty(y-m)^2e^{-y}\,dy=2e^{-m},$$
so $\mathbb{E}[(Z-Z_i)^2]=2\,\mathbb{E}[e^{-M_i}]$. Now $e^{-M_i}=\min_{j\ne i}e^{-Y_j}$, and the $e^{-Y_j}$ are i.i.d.\ Uniform$[0,1]$, because $\mathbb{P}(e^{-Y_j}\le t)=\mathbb{P}(Y_j\ge-\log t)=t$. Hence $\mathbb{E}[e^{-M_i}]=\int_0^1(1-t)^{n-1}dt=1/n$.

In both cases,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big[(Z-Z_i)^2\big]=n\cdot\frac2n=2 .$$
Equivalently, only the index of the maximum contributes, with $(Y_{(n)}-Y_{(n-1)})^2$, and the top spacing is Exp$(1)$, whose second moment is $2$. Since $Z_i$ is a function of $X^{(i)}$, the Efron–Stein inequality gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le2$.

*Comparison with the truth.* $\mathrm{Var}(Z)=H_n^{(2)}\in[1,\pi^2/6)$, while the leave-one-out bound is $2$. The ratio $2/H_n^{(2)}$ decreases to $12/\pi^2\approx1.216$, so this bound is sharp up to a constant factor. The bounds of Theorem 11.1 ($2n$ or $n$, and $\gtrsim\log^2n$) are not. The loss in the bound $V$ of Theorem 11.1 comes from the step $(Z-Z_i)^2\le\sup_sX_{i,s}^2$ in its proof: it charges every coordinate its full size $Y_i^2$, instead of only the gap above the runner-up. ∎
