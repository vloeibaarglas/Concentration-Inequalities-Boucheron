**Central idea.** Write $Z = \|X\|_2 = \sup_{\theta \in S^{n-1}} \sum_{i=1}^n \theta_i X_i$ as the supremum of a separable empirical process with centered summands $X_{i,\theta} = \theta_i X_i$. Jensen gives $\mathbb{E}Z \le \sqrt{n/3}$; the lower bound follows from $\mathrm{Var}(Z) = n/3 - (\mathbb{E}Z)^2$ together with Theorem 11.1, which yields $\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le \Sigma^2 + \sigma^2 \le 1 + 1/3 = 4/3$ (weak variance $\le 1$ since $X_i^2 \le 1$; wimpy variance $= 1/3$ since $\mathbb{E}[X_i^2] = 1/3$). The tail bound is Theorem 6.10 applied to $Z/2$, viewed as a separately convex $1$-Lipschitz function of $Y_i = (X_i+1)/2 \in [0,1]$.

**Solution.** Since $\|X\|_2 = \sup_{\theta \in S^{n-1}} \langle\theta, X\rangle$ (attained at $\theta = X/\|X\|$ a.s.), and $\theta \mapsto \langle\theta, X\rangle$ is continuous, with $T \subset S^{n-1}$ countable dense,
$$Z = \sup_{\theta \in T} \sum_{i=1}^n \theta_i X_i \qquad \text{a.s.}$$
Put $X_{i,\theta} = \theta_i X_i$. For fixed $\theta$ the $X_{i,\theta}$ are independent and centered ($\mathbb{E}X_i = 0$), so $Z$ is the supremum of a separable centered empirical process, and the book's results for countable $T$ apply.

Since $\mathbb{E}[X_i^2] = \int_{-1}^1 \frac{x^2}{2}\,dx = 1/3$, Jensen's inequality gives
$$\mathbb{E}Z = \mathbb{E}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2} \le \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[X_i^2]} = \sqrt{n/3}.$$

The wimpy variance is $\sigma^2 = \sup_{\theta \in S^{n-1}} \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[(\theta_i X_i)^2] = \frac13 \sup_\theta \|\theta\|_2^2 = 1/3$, and the weak variance is $\Sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}[\sup_\theta \sum_i \theta_i^2 X_i^2] \le 1$, since $X_i^2 \le 1$ a.s.\ and $\sum_i \theta_i^2 = 1$. Theorem 11.1 (centered case) gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le \Sigma^2 + \sigma^2 \le 1 + \frac13 = \frac43.$$
As $\mathbb{E}[Z^2] = \sum_i \mathbb{E}[X_i^2] = n/3$,
$$(\mathbb{E}Z)^2 = \mathbb{E}[Z^2] - \mathrm{Var}(Z) \ge \frac{n}{3} - \frac43 = \frac{n-4}{3}.$$
For $n \ge 5$, $\sqrt{(n-4)/3} \ge \sqrt{n/3} - 1$: both sides are nonnegative and squaring gives the equivalent $2\sqrt{n/3} \ge 7/3$, i.e.\ $n \ge 49/12$. Hence $\mathbb{E}Z \ge \sqrt{n/3} - 1$. For $n \le 4$, $Z \ge |X_1|$ gives $\mathbb{E}Z \ge \mathbb{E}|X_1| = 1/2 > \sqrt{4/3} - 1 \ge \sqrt{n/3} - 1$.

For the tail bound, let $Y_i = (X_i+1)/2$, iid uniform on $[0,1]$, and $g(y) = \|2y - \mathbf{1}\|_2$ on $[0,1]^n$, so $Z = g(Y)$. For fixed $y_{-i}$, $y_i \mapsto g(y) = \sqrt{(2y_i-1)^2 + C}$ with $C = \sum_{j \ne i}(2y_j-1)^2 \ge 0$ has second derivative $4C/((2y_i-1)^2+C)^{3/2} \ge 0$, so $g$ is separately convex; by the reverse triangle inequality $|g(y) - g(y')| \le 2\|y - y'\|_2$. Hence $f = g/2$ is separately convex and $1$-Lipschitz on $[0,1]^n$. Theorem 6.10 applied to $\tilde Z = f(Y) = Z/2$ gives $\mathbb{P}\{\tilde Z > \mathbb{E}\tilde Z + t\} \le e^{-t^2/2}$ for $t > 0$, i.e.\ $\mathbb{P}\{Z > \mathbb{E}Z + s\} \le e^{-s^2/8}$ for $s = 2t > 0$. With $s = t\sqrt{n}$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z \ge \mathbb{E}Z + t\sqrt{n}\} \le e^{-nt^2/8}, \qquad t > 0.$$

*Remark.* Here $\Sigma^2 \le 1$ and $\sigma^2 = 1/3$ are both $O(1)$ while $\mathbb{E}Z \approx \sqrt{n/3}$; Theorem 11.8's estimate $\Sigma^2 \le \sigma^2 + 8\mathbb{E}Z$ is far from sharp in this case.
