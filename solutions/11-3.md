**Central idea.** Separability makes every relevant supremum over $\mathcal{T}$ equal almost surely to the same supremum over a countable separant $\mathcal{S}=\{s_1,s_2,\dots\}$. Theorem 11.1 applies to the finite index sets $\{s_1,\dots,s_m\}$, and the finite-set quantities $V_m,\Sigma_m^2,\sigma_m^2$ are dominated by $V,\Sigma^2,\sigma^2$. The finite maxima $Z_m=\max_{k\le m}\sum_iX_{i,s_k}$ increase to $Z$. Whenever the relevant bound ($V$, or $\Sigma^2+\sigma^2$) is finite, $|Z_m|$ is dominated by a single square-integrable variable, so $\mathrm{Var}(Z_m)\to\mathrm{Var}(Z)$ and both inequalities pass to the limit.

**Solution.** *Setting.* $X_1,\dots,X_n$ are independent, $X_i=(X_{i,t})_{t\in\mathcal{T}}$, and $Z=\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^nX_{i,t}$. As in the text,
$$V=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}X_{i,t}^2,\qquad\Sigma^2=\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^nX_{i,t}^2,\qquad\sigma^2=\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}X_{i,t}^2 .$$
Separability means there is a countable $\mathcal{S}=\{s_1,s_2,\dots\}\subseteq\mathcal{T}$ and an event $\Omega_0$ with $\mathbb{P}(\Omega_0)=1$ such that on $\Omega_0$, for every $t\in\mathcal{T}$, some sequence $(t_k)\subseteq\mathcal{S}$ satisfies $X_{i,t_k}\to X_{i,t}$ for all $i=1,\dots,n$ (density of $\mathcal{S}$ for the topology of pointwise convergence). We must show: $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le V$, and $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\Sigma^2+\sigma^2$ if $\mathbb{E}X_{i,t}=0$ for all $i,t$. We use Theorem 11.1 only for finite index sets.

*Step 1: reduction to $\mathcal{S}$.* Fix $\omega\in\Omega_0$ and $t\in\mathcal{T}$, and take $t_k\in\mathcal{S}$ as above. Then $\sum_iX_{i,t_k}\to\sum_iX_{i,t}$, $\sum_iX_{i,t_k}^2\to\sum_iX_{i,t}^2$, and $X_{i,t_k}^2\to X_{i,t}^2$ for each $i$. So each value at $t$ is a limit of values at points of $\mathcal{S}$, hence at most the supremum over $\mathcal{S}$. The reverse inequalities are trivial because $\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}$. Hence, on $\Omega_0$,
$$Z=Y:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_iX_{i,s},\qquad\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_iX_{i,t}^2=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_iX_{i,s}^2,\qquad\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}X_{i,t}^2=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}X_{i,s}^2\ \ (i\le n).$$
The right-hand sides are countable suprema, hence measurable. So $Z$ coincides a.s.\ with the random variable $Y$; $\mathrm{Var}(Z)$ means $\mathrm{Var}(Y)$; and $V$ and $\Sigma^2$ are unchanged when $\mathcal{T}$ is replaced by $\mathcal{S}$. Also $\sigma^2_{\mathcal{S}}:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_i\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}^2\le\sigma^2$, and the centering hypothesis is inherited by $\mathcal{S}$.

*Step 2: finite approximants.* For $m\ge1$ let $Z_m=\max_{k\le m}\sum_iX_{i,s_k}$, and let $V_m,\Sigma_m^2,\sigma_m^2$ be the three quantities for the index set $\{s_1,\dots,s_m\}$. The vectors $(X_{i,s_k})_{k\le m}$, $i=1,\dots,n$, are independent, so Theorem 11.1 for a finite index set gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z_m)\le V_m,\qquad\text{and, if centered,}\qquad\mathrm{Var}(Z_m)\le\Sigma_m^2+\sigma_m^2 .$$
Maxima over subsets of $\mathcal{S}$ are smaller, so by Step 1 $V_m\le V$, $\Sigma_m^2\le\Sigma^2$, and $\sigma_m^2\le\sigma^2_{\mathcal{S}}\le\sigma^2$. Let $B$ denote the relevant bound ($B=V$, or $B=\Sigma^2+\sigma^2$ in the centered case). Then
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z_m)\le B\qquad\text{for all }m. \tag{1}$$
If $B=\infty$ there is nothing to prove, so assume $B<\infty$. Moreover $Z_m\uparrow Y$ pointwise, since $Y$ is the supremum over $\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_m\{s_1,\dots,s_m\}$.

*Step 3: a square-integrable envelope.* We find $U\ge0$ with $\mathbb{E}U^2<\infty$ and $|Z_m|\le U$ for all $m$; then also $|Y|\le U$.

- Case $B=V<\infty$. Put $U=\sum_{i=1}^n\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}|X_{i,s}|$. For every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, $|\sum_iX_{i,s}|\le U$, so $|Z_m|\le U$. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $U^2\le n\sum_i\sup_sX_{i,s}^2$, so $\mathbb{E}U^2\le nV<\infty$.
- Case $B=\Sigma^2+\sigma^2<\infty$. Put $U=\sqrt n\,\big(\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_iX_{i,s}^2\big)^{1/2}$. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $|\sum_iX_{i,s}|\le\sqrt n(\sum_iX_{i,s}^2)^{1/2}\le U$ for every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, so $|Z_m|\le U$, and $\mathbb{E}U^2=n\Sigma^2<\infty$.

*Step 4: passage to the limit.* $Z_m\to Y$ and $Z_m^2\to Y^2$ pointwise, with $|Z_m|\le U$ and $Z_m^2\le U^2$, where $U$ and $U^2$ are integrable. Dominated convergence gives $\mathbb{E}Z_m\to\mathbb{E}Y$ and $\mathbb{E}Z_m^2\to\mathbb{E}Y^2$, both finite. Hence $\mathrm{Var}(Z_m)\to\mathrm{Var}(Y)$. (Equivalently, as in the hint, monotone convergence applies to $0\le Z_m-Z_1\uparrow Y-Z_1$ and to its square; the envelope only guarantees finite limits.) Letting $m\to\infty$ in (1),
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)=\mathrm{Var}(Y)\le V,\qquad\text{and, if }\mathbb{E}X_{i,t}=0\ \text{for all } i,t,\qquad\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\Sigma^2+\sigma^2 .$$
So both conclusions of Theorem 11.1 hold for separable processes indexed by an arbitrary set $\mathcal{T}$. As a by-product, $Z\in L^2$ whenever the relevant bound is finite; no integrability of $Z$ had to be assumed. ∎
