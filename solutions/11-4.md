**Central idea.** The $-n$ spikes of $X_{i,s}$ flip to $+n$ under negative Rademachers. On $\{m \ge n/4\}$ (high probability, $m = \#\{i:\varepsilon_i=-1\}$), each coordinate's flipped-spike count $B_s \sim \mathrm{Bin}(m,1/n)$ is independent across $s$, and $\mathbb{E}\max_s B_s \gtrsim \log(n/4)/\log\log n$ (binomial-max lemma); each spike contributes $n$. Maximizing over $s$ via $s^* = \arg\max B_s$ keeps the competing $-n$ spikes $C_{s^*}$ an independent $\mathrm{Bin}(n-m,1/n)$ of mean $<1$. The non-symmetrized process is $\le n^2/(n-1) = O(n)$ pointwise, so for large $n$ half the symmetrized expectation exceeds it: the absolute values in Lemma 11.4's left symmetrization inequality cannot be dropped.

**Solution.** $\mathbb{E}X_{i,s} = \frac{n}{n-1}\cdot\frac{n-1}{n} - n\cdot\frac1n = 0$. Let $m = \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf{1}_{\{\varepsilon_i=-1\}} \sim \mathrm{Bin}(n,1/2)$; by Hoeffding, $\mathbb{P}\{m < n/4\} \le e^{-n/8}$. Fix $\varepsilon$ with $m \ge n/4$ and write $I_- = \{i:\varepsilon_i=-1\}$, $I_+ = \{i:\varepsilon_i=+1\}$. For $s \in \mathcal{T}$ let $B_s = \#\{i \in I_-: X_{i,s}=-n\}$ and $C_s = \#\{i \in I_+: X_{i,s}=-n\}$; conditional on $\varepsilon$, the $B_s$ are iid $\mathrm{Bin}(m,1/n)$, the $C_s$ are iid $\mathrm{Bin}(n-m,1/n)$, and the two families are independent.

Pointwise, $\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}$ equals $n$ on the $B_s$ good spikes, $-n$ on the $C_s$ bad spikes, and is $\ge -n/(n-1)$ otherwise, so
$$\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_iX_{i,s} \;\ge\; nB_s - nC_s - \frac{n^2}{\,n-1\,} \;\ge\; nB_s - nC_s - 2n.$$
Let $s^* \in \arg\max_s B_s$, measurable w.r.t.\ the $I_-$-variables, hence independent of the $I_+$-variables; thus $C_{s^*} \sim \mathrm{Bin}(n-m,1/n)$ still, with $\mathbb{E}[C_{s^*}] = (n-m)/n < 1$. Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_s\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}\;\Big|\;\varepsilon\Big] \;\ge\; n\,\mathbb{E}[\max_s B_s \mid \varepsilon] - 3n.$$

*Binomial-max lemma.* If $Z_1,\dots,Z_n$ are iid $\mathrm{Bin}(N,1/n)$ with $N = \lfloor n/4\rfloor$, then $\mathbb{E}\max_s Z_s \ge (1-o(1))\frac{\log(n/4)}{\log\log n}$. Indeed, for integer $k \ge 1$, $\mathbb{P}\{Z_1 \ge k\} \ge \binom{N}{k}n^{-k}(1-1/n)^N \ge \frac12(5k)^{-k}$ (for large $n$), and with $k_n = \lfloor \log(n/4)/\log\log n\rfloor$ one checks $n\mathbb{P}\{Z_1 \ge k_n\} \to \infty$, so $\mathbb{P}\{\max_s Z_s \ge k_n\} \to 1$. Since $\mathrm{Bin}(m,1/n)$ dominates $\mathrm{Bin}(N,1/n)$ for $m \ge n/4$, $\mathbb{E}[\max_s B_s \mid \varepsilon] \ge (1-o(1))\frac{\log(n/4)}{\log\log n}$.

Assembling, with $L_n = \frac{\log(n/4)}{\log\log n} \to \infty$,
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s} \;\ge\; (1-e^{-n/8})\,n\big((1-o(1))L_n - 3\big) - n^2e^{-n/8} \;\ge\; \frac{n}{2}L_n$$
for $n$ large.

For the non-symmetrized process, $\sum_i X_{i,s} = \frac{n}{n-1}(n-C'_s) - nC'_s = \frac{n^2}{n-1}(1-C'_s)$ with $C'_s \sim \mathrm{Bin}(n,1/n)$, so $\sup_s\sum_iX_{i,s} \le \frac{n^2}{n-1}$ pointwise and $\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_iX_{i,s} \le \frac{n^2}{n-1}$. Since $L_n \to \infty$, for large $n$,
$$\frac12\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s}\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_iX_{i,s} \;\ge\; \frac{n}{4}L_n \;>\; \frac{n^2}{n-1} \;\ge\; \mathbb{E}\sup_{s}\sum_{i=1}^n X_{i,s}.$$

*Comparison with Lemma 11.4.* Lemma 11.4's left symmetrization inequality, $\tfrac12\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s}|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}| \le \mathbb{E}\sup_{s}|\sum_iX_{i,s}|$, genuinely needs the absolute values: this example shows the one-sided analogue $\tfrac12\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s}\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s} \le \mathbb{E}\sup_{s}\sum_iX_{i,s}$ is false — here the left side exceeds the right by an unbounded factor (at least $\tfrac{n-1}{4n}L_n \to \infty$). The heavy left tail ($-n$ spikes) keeps $\sup_s\sum_iX_{i,s} \le n^2/(n-1)$ pointwise, but the Rademachers flip those spikes to $+n$, so the symmetrized process sees spikes the original process never sees. ∎
