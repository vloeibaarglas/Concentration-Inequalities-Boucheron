**Central idea.** The book's "Theorem 11.4" is Lemma 11.4 (the symmetrization inequalities) — no Theorem 11.4 exists in the numbering. If $S \subset T$ is a countable separant, the suprema over $T$ agree a.s. with those over $S$ (the same separant works for the Rademacher-symmetrized process, since multiplication by fixed signs is continuous), so Lemma 11.4 applied to the finite sets $S_m = \{s_1,\dots,s_m\} \uparrow S$ passes to the limit by monotone convergence, exactly as in Exercise 11.3.

**Solution.** (Remark on numbering: the chapter contains no Theorem 11.4; the symmetrization inequalities are Lemma 11.4, and Exercises 11.4 and 11.6 refer to it.) Let $X_1,\dots,X_n$ be independent centered random vectors $X_i = (X_{i,s})_{s\in T}$ with each $X_{i,s}$ integrable, and let $\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_n$ be independent Rademacher variables independent of the $X_i$. Assume $T$ is separable with countable separant $S$: a.s., for every $t \in T$ there is a sequence in $S$ along which $(X_{1,t_n},\dots,X_{n,t_n}) \to (X_{1,t},\dots,X_{n,t})$ (the topology of pointwise convergence on $\mathbb{R}^n$). For fixed signs $\eta_i \in \{-1,1\}$ the same sequence gives $\eta_i X_{i,t_n} \to \eta_i X_{i,t}$, so $S$ is also a separant for every Rademacher-symmetrized process $(\varepsilon_i X_{i,s})_{s\in T}$. By continuity of the sum and of $|\cdot|$, a.s.
$$\sup_{s\in T}\sum_{i=1}^n X_{i,s} = \sup_{s\in S}\sum_{i=1}^n X_{i,s}, \qquad \sup_{s\in T}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^n X_{i,s}\Big| = \sup_{s\in S}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^n X_{i,s}\Big|,$$
and likewise with $\varepsilon_i X_{i,s}$ in place of $X_{i,s}$; this is the consequence of separability displayed in Section 11.1. In particular all suprema are a.s. equal to measurable (countable) suprema.

Enumerate $S = \{s_1,s_2,\dots\}$ and set $S_m = \{s_1,\dots,s_m\}$. Lemma 11.4 applied to the finite (hence countable) index set $S_m$ gives
$$\tfrac12\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in S_m}\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}\Big| \le \mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in S_m}\Big|\sum_iX_{i,s}\Big| \le 2\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in S_m}\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}\Big|$$
and
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in S_m}\sum_iX_{i,s} \le 2\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in S_m}\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}.$$
As $m\to\infty$, $\sup_{S_m} \uparrow \sup_S$ pointwise in each case. For the absolute-value suprema the terms are nonnegative, so monotone convergence gives convergence of the expectations to those over $S$, hence over $T$ by the a.s. equalities above. For the signed supremum, $\sup_{S_m}\sum_iX_{i,s} \ge -\sum_i|X_{i,s_1}| \in L^1$, so monotone convergence applied to $\sup_{S_m}\sum_iX_{i,s} + \sum_i|X_{i,s_1}| \ge 0$ yields $\mathbb{E}\sup_{S_m}\sum_iX_{i,s} \to \mathbb{E}\sup_S\sum_iX_{i,s} = \mathbb{E}\sup_T\sum_iX_{i,s}$ (and similarly for the symmetrized version). Passing to the limit in the finite-index inequalities gives, for separable $T$,
$$\tfrac12\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in T}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}\Big| \le \mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in T}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^nX_{i,s}\Big| \le 2\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in T}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}\Big|$$
and
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in T}\sum_{i=1}^nX_{i,s} \le 2\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in T}\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_iX_{i,s},$$
i.e. Lemma 11.4 holds for separable index sets. ∎
