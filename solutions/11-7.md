**Central idea.** Write $u = \Phi^{-1}(1-1/t)$, so that $\phi(u) = \gamma(1/t)$ where $\gamma(x) = \phi(\Phi^{-1}(x))$ is the Gaussian isoperimetric function (using $\Phi^{-1}(1/t) = -\Phi^{-1}(1-1/t)$ and evenness of $\phi$). The two-sided bound of Lemma 10.3 on $\gamma$ at $x = 1/t$ translates directly into two-sided bounds on $u^2 = 2\log t \pm \log\log t \pm \log(\text{const})$. (The hint's "Lemma 10.1" refers to the isoperimetric-function material; Lemma 10.3 is its directly usable form.)

**Solution.** Let $u = \Phi^{-1}(1-1/t)$, so $\Phi(u) = 1-1/t$. By symmetry $\Phi^{-1}(1/t) = -\Phi^{-1}(1-1/t) = -u$, and since $\phi$ is even,
$$\phi(u) = \phi(\Phi^{-1}(1/t)) = \gamma(1/t),$$
where $\gamma$ is the Gaussian isoperimetric function. For $t \ge 2$ we have $1/t \in (0,1/2]$, so Lemma 10.3 applies:
$$\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{\tfrac12\log t} \le \gamma(1/t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^2/2} \le \frac{1}{t}\sqrt{2\log t}.$$
Also $u = \Phi^{-1}(1-1/t) \ge \Phi^{-1}(1/2) = 0$ for $t \ge 2$.

*Lower bound.* From the right-hand inequality, $e^{u^2/2} \ge t/\sqrt{4\pi\log t}$, i.e.
$$u^2 \ge 2\log t - \log(4\pi) - \log\log t.$$
At $t = 5$, $2\log 5 - \log\log 5 - \log(4\pi) \approx 0.21 > 0$, and the right-hand side is increasing for $t \ge 5$ (derivative $2/t - 1/(t\log t) > 0$), so it is positive for all $t \ge 5$. Since $u \ge 0$,
$$\Phi^{-1}(1-1/t) = u \ge \sqrt{2\log t - \log\log t - \log(4\pi)}, \qquad t \ge 5.$$

*Upper bound.* From the left-hand inequality, $e^{u^2/2} \le t/\sqrt{\pi\log t}$, i.e.
$$u^2 \le 2\log t - \log\pi - \log\log t.$$
The right-hand side is positive for $t \ge 2$ (at $t=2$ it equals $2\log 2 - \log\log 2 - \log\pi \approx 0.61 > 0$ and it is increasing), so with $u \ge 0$,
$$\Phi^{-1}(1-1/t) = u \le \sqrt{2\log t - \log\log t - \log\pi}, \qquad t \ge 2.$$
∎
