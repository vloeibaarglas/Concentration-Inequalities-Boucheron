**Central idea.** Split $\mathbb{E}\|S_n\|_\infty^2 = \mathrm{Var}(\|S_n\|_\infty) + (\mathbb{E}\|S_n\|_\infty)^2$ as in Theorem 11.2. The variance term needs no symmetry: Theorem 11.1 (centered case) gives $\mathrm{Var}(\|S_n\|_\infty) \le \Sigma^2 + \sigma^2 \le 2\Sigma^2$. Only the mean term needs symmetrization, via Lemma 11.4 ($\mathbb{E}\sup \le 2\,\mathbb{E}\sup$ of the Rademacher version), which contributes the factor $4$ in front of $\log(2d)$ after the conditional sub-Gaussian maximal inequality (Theorem 2.5).

**Solution.** If $\Sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}\max_{j\le d}\sum_{i=1}^n X_{i,j}^2 = \infty$ there is nothing to prove; assume $\Sigma^2 < \infty$. Write $\|\cdot\|$ for $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ and
$$\mathbb{E}\|S_n\|^2 = \mathrm{Var}(\|S_n\|) + (\mathbb{E}\|S_n\|)^2.$$
Since $\|S_n\| = \max_{j\le d,\,b\in\{-1,1\}}\sum_{i=1}^n bX_{i,j}$, it is the supremum of the empirical process $(bX_{i,j})$ over the finite index set $\{(j,b)\}$. The variables $bX_{i,j}$ are centered (the $X_i$ are centered) and no symmetry is assumed.

*Variance term.* By Theorem 11.1 (centered case), $\mathrm{Var}(\|S_n\|) \le \Sigma^2 + \sigma^2$ with $\sigma^2 = \max_{j,b}\sum_i\mathbb{E}[(bX_{i,j})^2] = \max_j\sum_i\mathbb{E}[X_{i,j}^2] \le \mathbb{E}\max_j\sum_iX_{i,j}^2 = \Sigma^2$. Hence
$$\mathrm{Var}(\|S_n\|) \le 2\Sigma^2.$$

*Mean term.* By Lemma 11.4 (symmetrization, finite index set),
$$\mathbb{E}\|S_n\| = \mathbb{E}\max_{j,b}\sum_i bX_{i,j} \le 2\,\mathbb{E}\max_{j,b}\sum_i\varepsilon_i bX_{i,j} = 2\,\mathbb{E}\max_{j\le d}\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,j}\Big|,$$
with $\varepsilon_i$ independent Rademacher variables independent of the $X_i$. Conditionally on the $X_i$, each $\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,j}$ is a centered Rademacher sum, hence sub-Gaussian with variance factor $\sum_iX_{i,j}^2$. Theorem 2.5 (maximal inequality) gives, conditionally,
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\max_{j\le d}\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,j}\Big| \le \sqrt{2\log(2d)\,\max_{j\le d}\sum_iX_{i,j}^2}.$$
Taking expectation over the $X_i$ and using Jensen to bring $\mathbb{E}$ under the square root,
$$\mathbb{E}\max_{j\le d}\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,j}\Big| \le \sqrt{2\log(2d)\,\Sigma^2}.$$
Therefore $\mathbb{E}\|S_n\| \le 2\sqrt{2\log(2d)}\,\Sigma$ and
$$(\mathbb{E}\|S_n\|)^2 \le 8\log(2d)\,\Sigma^2.$$

Combining,
$$\mathbb{E}\|S_n\|_\infty^2 \le 2\Sigma^2 + 8\log(2d)\,\Sigma^2 = 2\big(1 + 4\log(2d)\big)\Sigma^2.$$
∎
