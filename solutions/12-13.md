**Central idea.** A quantile coupling lets us replace the $X_i$ by independent majorants $Y_i$ with exactly the sub-gamma tail, so $Y_i\stackrel{d}{=}g(T_i)$ with $g(s) = \mu+\sqrt{2vs}+cs$ and $T_i$ i.i.d. Exp$(1)$. Then $\max_iY_i = g(T_{(n)})$, and Rényi's representation $T_{(n)}\stackrel{d}{=}\sum_{j=1}^nE_j/j$ gives exact moments: the mean bound follows by Jensen. The printed second-moment bound is false because it involves $M^2$, which sees the lower tail that upper-tail hypotheses do not control. It holds for $M_+$ (with $\mu\ge0$), or for $(M-\mu)_+$. For the mgf, linearizing the concave $g$ at $H_n$ and using Rényi gives the printed shape with an extra factor $\sum_j1/j^2\le\pi^2/6$ in the quadratic term. Without that factor, the printed mgf bound fails under the tail-form reading of the hypothesis (which is what the exercise's parenthetical and hint use). Under the literal mgf sub-gamma hypothesis we leave it unresolved.

**Solution.** *Reduction.* As the hint allows (it also follows from a quantile coupling, since $\mathbb{P}\{X_i\ge x\}\le\mathbb{P}\{Y_i\ge x\}$ for every $x$), take independent $Y_1,\dots,Y_n$ with $X_i\le Y_i$ a.s. and $\mathbb{P}\{Y_i\ge g(t)\} = e^{-t}$ for $t\ge0$, where $g(s) = \mu+\sqrt{2vs}+cs$. Since $g$ is continuous and strictly increasing on $[0,\infty)$ (if $v = c = 0$ then $Y_i\equiv\mu$ and everything below is trivial), $Y_i\stackrel{d}{=}g(T_i)$ with $T_i$ i.i.d. Exp$(1)$. Also, $g$ is concave. Put $\tilde M = \max_iY_i$. Then $M\le\tilde M$ a.s. and, $g$ being increasing,
$$\tilde M = g(T_{(n)}) = \mu+\sqrt{2vT_{(n)}}+cT_{(n)},\qquad T_{(n)} = \max_{1\le i\le n}T_i.$$
By Rényi's representation of exponential order statistics (de Haan–Ferreira, Ch. 2), $T_{(n)}\stackrel{d}{=}\sum_{j=1}^nE_j/j$ with $E_j$ i.i.d. Exp$(1)$. Hence $\mathbb{E}T_{(n)} = H_n := \sum_{j=1}^n1/j$ and $\mathrm{Var}(T_{(n)}) = \zeta_n(2) := \sum_{j=1}^n1/j^2<\pi^2/6$.

*Mean (true as stated).* By Jensen's inequality and the concavity of $g$, $\mathbb{E}M\le\mathbb{E}g(T_{(n)})\le g(H_n) = \mu+\sqrt{2vH_n}+cH_n$. This is the Theorem 2.6 bound.

*Second moment: false as stated.* Take $n = 2$, $X_1 = X_2\equiv-100$, $\mu = 0$, $v = c = 1$. Then $\mathbb{E}X_i = -100\le\mu$ and $\mathbb{P}\{X_i\ge\sqrt{2t}+t\} = 0\le e^{-t}$ for all $t\ge0$. Constants are also sub-gamma in the mgf sense, so the hypotheses hold under every reading. But $M\equiv-100$ gives $\mathbb{E}M^2 = 10000$, whereas the claimed bound is $(\sqrt3+3/2)^2+10/\log2+\pi^2/3\approx28.16$. The reason: $\mathbb{E}M^2$ depends on the lower tail of $M$, which upper-tail hypotheses do not control.

*Corrected second-moment bounds ($n\ge2$).* Let $W := \sqrt{2vT_{(n)}}+cT_{(n)}\ge0$, so $\tilde M = \mu+W$. First, $\mathbb{E}W\le\sqrt{2vH_n}+cH_n$ by Jensen. Second, $\mathrm{Var}(W)\le2\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt{2vT_{(n)}})+2c^2\mathrm{Var}(T_{(n)})$, where
$$\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt{T_{(n)}})\le\mathbb{E}\big[(\sqrt{T_{(n)}}-\sqrt{H_n})^2\big] = \mathbb{E}\frac{(T_{(n)}-H_n)^2}{(\sqrt{T_{(n)}}+\sqrt{H_n})^2}\le\frac{\mathrm{Var}(T_{(n)})}{H_n}\le\frac{\pi^2}{6H_n}.$$
So $2\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt{2vT_{(n)}}) = 4v\,\mathrm{Var}(\sqrt{T_{(n)}})\le\frac{2\pi^2v}{3H_n}\le\frac{10v}{\log n}$, using $H_n\ge\log n$ and $2\pi^2/3<10$. Also $2c^2\mathrm{Var}(T_{(n)})\le c^2\pi^2/3$. Hence
$$\mathrm{Var}(W)\le\frac{10v}{\log n}+\frac{c^2\pi^2}3.\tag{V}$$
Two corrected statements follow.

(a) Since $(M-\mu)_+\le(\tilde M-\mu)_+ = W$,
$$\mathbb{E}[(M-\mu)_+^2]\le\mathbb{E}W^2 = (\mathbb{E}W)^2+\mathrm{Var}(W)\le(\sqrt{2vH_n}+cH_n)^2+\frac{10v}{\log n}+\frac{c^2\pi^2}{3}.$$

(b) (The form used by the independent audit.) If $\mu\ge0$, then $\tilde M = \mu+W\ge0$ and $M_+ := \max(M,0)\le\tilde M$, so
$$\mathbb{E}M_+^2\le\mathbb{E}\tilde M^2 = (\mu+\mathbb{E}W)^2+\mathrm{Var}(W)\le\big(\mu+\sqrt{2vH_n}+cH_n\big)^2+\frac{10v}{\log n}+\frac{c^2\pi^2}{3}.$$
This is the printed bound with $M^2$ replaced by $M_+^2$. If $\mu<0$, the hypotheses still hold with $\mu$ replaced by $0$, so (b) holds with $\mu_+$ in place of $\mu$. The two forms are consistent: (b) is (a) shifted by the constant $\mu\ge0$, because $\tilde M - \mu = W$ in both. In particular, if $X_i\ge0$ a.s. and $\mu\ge0$ (e.g. gamma $X_i$), then $M = M_+$ and the printed $\mathbb{E}M^2$ bound holds.

*Corrected mgf bound.* Let $B_1 := c+\sqrt{v/(2H_n)}$ and $a := g(H_n) = \mu+\sqrt{2vH_n}+cH_n$. By concavity of $\sqrt{\cdot}$ at $H_n$, $\sqrt{2vT_{(n)}}\le\sqrt{2vH_n}+\sqrt{v/(2H_n)}\,(T_{(n)}-H_n)$. Hence $\tilde M\le a+B_1(T_{(n)}-H_n)$. For $\lambda>0$, $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda M}\le\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda\tilde M}$. By Rényi, for $0<\lambda<1/B_1$,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda B_1(T_{(n)}-H_n)} = \prod_{j=1}^n\frac{e^{-\lambda B_1/j}}{1-\lambda B_1/j}.$$
For $x\in[0,1)$, $-x-\log(1-x) = \sum_{k\ge2}x^k/k\le\frac{x^2}2\sum_{k\ge0}x^k = \frac{x^2}{2(1-x)}$. Applying this with $x = \lambda B_1/j\le\lambda B_1$,
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda B_1(T_{(n)}-H_n)} = \sum_{j=1}^n\Big[-\tfrac{\lambda B_1}{j}-\log\Big(1-\tfrac{\lambda B_1}{j}\Big)\Big]\le\frac{\lambda^2B_1^2}{2(1-\lambda B_1)}\,\zeta_n(2).$$
Therefore, for $0<\lambda<1/B_1$,
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda M}\le\lambda\big(\mu+\sqrt{2vH_n}+cH_n\big)+\zeta_n(2)\,\frac{\lambda^2B_1^2}{2(1-\lambda B_1)}\le\lambda\big(\mu+\sqrt{2vH_n}+cH_n\big)+\frac{\pi^2}{6}\cdot\frac{\lambda^2B_1^2}{2(1-\lambda B_1)}.$$
*Comparison with the printed shape.* Let $B := c+\sqrt{v/H_n}\ge B_1$. On $\lambda B<1$, the map $b\mapsto\frac{\lambda^2b^2}{2(1-\lambda b)}$ is increasing in $b\in[0,B]$. Also $\sqrt{2vH_n}\le2\sqrt{vH_n}$ and $\lambda>0$. So, for $0<\lambda<1/B$,
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda M}\le\lambda\big(\mu+2\sqrt{vH_n}+cH_n\big)+\frac{\pi^2}6\cdot\frac{\lambda^2(c+\sqrt{v/H_n})^2}{2(1-\lambda(c+\sqrt{v/H_n}))}.$$
This is the printed bound with the extra factor $\pi^2/6$ (more precisely $\zeta_n(2)$) in the quadratic term.

*Is the factor $\pi^2/6$ needed? Under the tail-form hypothesis, yes.* The parenthetical "thus, for $t\ge0$, $\mathbb{P}\{X_i\ge\mu+\sqrt{2vt}+ct\}\le e^{-t}$" and the hint use only the tail form. Under that reading, the printed mgf bound (without the factor) is false, i.e. it cannot be derived from the tail assumptions. Take $v = 0$, $c = 1$, $\mu = 0$, $n = 2$, so the claim reads $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda M}\le\frac32\lambda+\frac{\lambda^2}{2(1-\lambda)}$. Let $Y_i\sim$ Exp$(1)$ be independent and $X_i^{(K)} = Y_i\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_i\le K\}}-C_K\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_i>K\}}$, where $C_K = e^K-K-1>0$ makes $\mathbb{E}X_i^{(K)} = 0$. For $t\ge0$, $\mathbb{P}\{X_i^{(K)}\ge t\}\le\mathbb{P}\{Y_i\ge t\}= e^{-t}$, so the tail-form hypotheses hold. Also $M^{(K)} = \max_iX_i^{(K)}\to T_{(2)} := \max(Y_1,Y_2)$ a.s., and $e^{\lambda M^{(K)}}\le e^{\lambda T_{(2)}}\in L^1$ for $0<\lambda<1$. By dominated convergence, $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda M^{(K)}}\to\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda T_{(2)}} = -\log(1-\lambda)-\log(1-\lambda/2)$. At $\lambda = 0.01$ this limit is $0.0150629>0.0150505 = \frac32(0.01)+\frac{0.01^2}{2(0.99)}$, so the printed bound is violated for large $K$. By continuity it is also violated for small $v>0$: with $v = 10^{-8}$ the printed right-hand side is $0.0150530$. The limit does satisfy the corrected bound, $0.0150629\le0.0150831$. (All numbers checked in Python.)

*Scope (unresolved case).* The $X_i^{(K)}$ have variance of order $e^K$, so they are not sub-gamma in the mgf sense, $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(X_i-\mathbb{E}X_i)}\le\frac{\lambda^2v}{2(1-c\lambda)}$, with $v = 0$ or small $v$. Under that literal mgf hypothesis, the counterexample is not admissible. Whether the printed bound without the factor $\pi^2/6$ then holds is **not resolved here**. Our corrected bound holds under both readings, because mgf sub-gamma with $\mathbb{E}X_i\le\mu$ implies the tail form (Section 2.4). (An earlier version of this solution called the printed mgf bound "false as stated" and argued that $B^2\to c^2$ contradicts the Gumbel variance $\pi^2c^2/6$. That argument is invalid. The printed linear term exceeds $\mathbb{E}M$ by a margin of order $\lambda(\mu+\sqrt{vH_n})$, so for small $\lambda$ no comparison of $\lambda^2$-coefficients follows. For instance, for i.i.d. Exp$(1)$ variables, where $\mu = v = c = 1$, the printed bound holds numerically for $n\in\{2,3,5,10,10^2,10^3,10^5\}$ on a grid of $\lambda\in(0,1/B)$.)

*Tightness (gamma case).* If $X_i\sim\Gamma(\alpha,\beta)$ i.i.d. (shape $\alpha$, rate $\beta$), then $-\alpha\log(1-\lambda/\beta)-\alpha\lambda/\beta\le\frac{(\alpha/\beta^2)\lambda^2}{2(1-\lambda/\beta)}$. So the $X_i$ are sub-gamma in the mgf sense, and hence in the tail sense, with $v = \alpha/\beta^2$, $c = 1/\beta$, $\mu = \alpha/\beta\ge0$, and $X_i\ge0$. Classical extreme-value theory gives $\beta\max_iX_i-\log n-(\alpha-1)\log\log n+\log\Gamma(\alpha)\Rightarrow$ Gumbel, with convergence of the variance, so $\mathrm{Var}(M)\to\pi^2/(6\beta^2) = \pi^2c^2/6$. The excess $\frac{10v}{\log n}+\frac{c^2\pi^2}{3}$ in the corrected second-moment bound tends to $\frac{\pi^2}{3}c^2$, within a factor $2$ of this. For the mgf, consider the exponential case $\alpha = 1$ and $\beta = 1/c$. Then $M\stackrel{d}{=}cT_{(n)}$ and $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(M-\mathbb{E}M)} = \sum_{j=1}^n[-\lambda c/j-\log(1-\lambda c/j)]\ge\frac{\lambda^2c^2}{2}\zeta_n(2)$. So the factor $\zeta_n(2)$ in the quadratic term cannot be improved for the centered maximum. Also $B_1 = c+c/\sqrt{2H_n}\to c$ as $n\to\infty$. Thus the right tail of the maximum is no heavier than a Gumbel tail with scale $c$, up to the constants above.
