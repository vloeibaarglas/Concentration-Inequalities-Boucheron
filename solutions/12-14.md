**Central idea.** The quantile coupling $|X_i|\le\sqrt{2vY_i}+cY_i$ with $Y_i$ i.i.d.\ Exp$(1)$ turns the truncation $\{|X_i|\ge\tau_n\}$ into $\{Y_i\ge\log n\}$ (by monotonicity of $s\mapsto\sqrt{2vs}+cs$); by memorylessness, $Y_i=\log n+E_i$ on this event with $E_i\sim$ Exp$(1)$, reducing every computation to a single exponential variable. A convexity estimate $\sqrt{2vY}+cY\le\tau_n(1+(Y-\log n)/\log n)$ for $Y\ge\log n$ gives the mgf in closed form, and Chernoff with $\lambda=1/(4\tau_n)$ yields the tail.

**Erratum.** Two defects, one in the statement and one in the hint. (i) *Statement:* the range "$0\le\lambda\le\tau_n/2$" should read $0\le\lambda\le1/(2\tau_n)$. As printed, whenever $\tau_n>\sqrt2$ we have $\tau_n/2>1/\tau_n$, so the range contains values $\lambda\ge1/\tau_n$ at which the denominator $1-\lambda\tau_n$ of the bound vanishes or is negative, so the printed inequality is undefined or false there. For instance, with standard Gaussian $X_i$ ($v=1$, $c=0$) and $n\ge3$, $\tau_n=\sqrt{2\log n}>\sqrt2$ and $Z$ is symmetric, so $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}\ge\lambda\mathbb{E}Z=0$ by Jensen, whereas for $1/\tau_n<\lambda\le\tau_n/2$ the printed right side $\frac{e^{\lambda\tau_n}+\lambda\tau_n-1}{1-\lambda\tau_n}$ is negative. (ii) *Hint:* the indicator must sit inside the exponential, $n\log\mathbb{E}\exp\big(\lambda(\sqrt{2vY_1}+cY_1)\mathbb{1}_{\{Y_1\ge\log n\}}\big)$. As written, $n\log\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda(\sqrt{2vY_1}+cY_1)}\mathbb{1}_{\{Y_1\ge\log n\}}\big]\to n\log\mathbb{P}\{Y_1\ge\log n\}=n\log(1/n)<0$ as $\lambda\to0$, so it cannot bound $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}\to0$. We prove the mgf bound for $0\le\lambda<1/\tau_n$ (indeed for $0\le\lambda<\log n/\tau_n$ in the sharper form), which contains the corrected range, and the mean and tail bounds as printed.

**Solution.** Put $L=\log n$ (assume $n\ge3$ so $L>1$) and $\tau_n=\sqrt{2vL}+cL$. By the hint's quantile coupling there are independent $Y_i\sim$ Exp$(1)$ with $|X_i|\le\sqrt{2vY_i}+cY_i$ a.s.\ (this uses the two-sided sub-gamma tail for $|X_i|$). If $|X_i|\ge\tau_n$ then $\sqrt{2vY_i}+cY_i\ge\sqrt{2vL}+cL$, whence $Y_i\ge L$ by strict monotonicity. Thus with $V_i:=X_i\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_i|\ge\tau_n\}}$ and $W_i:=(\sqrt{2vY_i}+cY_i)\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_i\ge L\}}\ge0$,
$$V_i\le W_i\quad\text{a.s.,}\qquad Z=\sum_{i=1}^nV_i.$$

*Mean.* $\mathbb{E}Z\le n\mathbb{E}W_1=n\mathbb{E}[(\sqrt{2vY}+cY)\mathbf{1}_{\{Y\ge L\}}]$. By memorylessness, on $\{Y\ge L\}$, $Y\stackrel{d}{=}L+E$ with $E\sim$ Exp$(1)$ independent, and $\mathbb{P}\{Y\ge L\}=1/n$, so
$$\mathbb{E}Z\le\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{2vL+2vE}]+cL+c.$$
Since $\sqrt{2vL+2vE}-\sqrt{2vL}=\frac{2vE}{\sqrt{2vL+2vE}+\sqrt{2vL}}\le\frac{2vE}{2\sqrt{2vL}}$, taking expectations gives $\mathbb{E}\sqrt{2vL+2vE}\le\sqrt{2vL}+\sqrt{v/(2L)}$. Hence $\mathbb{E}Z\le\tau_n+c+\sqrt{v/(2\log n)}$.

*Moment-generating function.* For $\lambda\ge0$, $e^{\lambda V_i}\le e^{\lambda W_i}$ pointwise, so by independence $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}=\prod_i\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda V_i}\le(\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda W_1})^n$, i.e.\
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}\le n\log\mathbb{E}\exp\!\big(\lambda(\sqrt{2vY}+cY)\mathbf{1}_{\{Y\ge L\}}\big)$$
(the indicator inside the exponential, as corrected in the Erratum). For $Y\ge L$,
$$\sqrt{2vY}+cY\le\tau_n\Big(1+\frac{Y-L}{L}\Big):$$
indeed $h(Y):=\tau_n(1+(Y-L)/L)-(\sqrt{2vY}+cY)$ satisfies $h(L)=0$, $h'(L)=\sqrt{v/(2L)}\ge0$, $h''(Y)=\sqrt{2v}/(4Y^{3/2})>0$, so $h$ is convex and nondecreasing on $[L,\infty)$. Hence, with $x:=\lambda\tau_n$ and memorylessness,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda(\sqrt{2vY}+cY)}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y\ge L\}}\big]
&\le e^{x}\,\mathbb{E}\big[e^{x(Y-L)/L}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y\ge L\}}\big]
= \frac{e^{x}}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{1-x/L},\qquad x<L.
\end{align*}
Thus $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda W_1}\le1+\frac1n\big(\frac{e^{x}}{1-x/L}-1\big)$ and, with $\log(1+u)\le u$,
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}\le\frac{e^{\lambda\tau_n}}{1-\lambda\tau_n/\log n}-1,\qquad 0\le\lambda<\frac{\log n}{\tau_n}.$$
For $L\ge1$ this implies the stated bound $\frac{e^{\lambda\tau_n}+\lambda\tau_n-1}{1-\lambda\tau_n}$, since $\frac{e^{x}}{1-x/L}-1\le\frac{e^{x}}{1-x}-1$. This covers the corrected range $0\le\lambda\le1/(2\tau_n)$ of the Erratum.

*Tail.* By Chernoff, for $0\le\lambda<(\log n)/\tau_n$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge t\}\le\exp\!\Big(-\lambda t+\frac{e^{\lambda\tau_n}}{1-\lambda\tau_n/L}-1\Big).$$
Take $\lambda=1/(4\tau_n)$ (so $x=1/4$): $\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge t\}\le e^{-t/(4\tau_n)}\exp\!\big(\frac{e^{1/4}}{1-1/(4L)}-1\big)$. Since $\frac{e^{1/4}}{1-1/(4L)}-1\le\log2$ for $L\ge1.0347$ (in particular for $n\ge3$),
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge t\}\le2\exp\!\big(-t/(4\tau_n)\big),\qquad t\ge0.$$
Note the $2$ genuinely needs the $L$-dependent mgf above: the loosened stated mgf $\frac{e^{x}+x-1}{1-x}$ only gives $e^{0.712}e^{-t/(4\tau_n)}\approx2.04\,e^{-t/(4\tau_n)}$.

*Remark.* For small $\lambda$, $\frac{e^{\lambda\tau_n}+\lambda\tau_n-1}{1-\lambda\tau_n}=2\lambda\tau_n+O(\lambda^2)$, while Exercise 12.13's (corrected) mgf is $\lambda(\mu+\sqrt{2vH_n}+cH_n)+O(\lambda^2)$ with $H_n\sim\log n$ — both of order $\lambda\tau_n$, as expected since, with overwhelming probability, at most one $|X_i|$ exceeds $\tau_n$ and $Z$ is then essentially $\max_iX_i$ (the setting of Lemma 11.16).
