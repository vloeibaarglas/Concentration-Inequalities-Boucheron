**Central idea.** By symmetry, $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\stackrel{d}{=}(\varepsilon_1|X_1|,\ldots,\varepsilon_n|X_n|)$ with $\varepsilon_i$ i.i.d. Rademacher independent of the $|X_i|$'s. Conditioning on the $|X_i|$'s turns the ratio into a Rademacher tail; the Chernoff bound with $\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon e^{\lambda\sum_i\varepsilon_i a_i}=\prod_i\cosh(\lambda a_i)\le e^{\lambda^2\sum_i a_i^2/2}$ (from $\cosh x\le e^{x^2/2}$) is optimized at $\lambda=t/\sqrt{\sum_i a_i^2}$, giving $e^{-t^2/2}$ uniformly.

**Solution.** Let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ and $V=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2$. Since each $X_i$ is symmetric, $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ has the same law as $(\varepsilon_1|X_1|,\ldots,\varepsilon_n|X_n|)$ where $\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n$ are i.i.d. Rademacher, independent of $(|X_1|,\ldots,|X_n|)$. Define $S/\sqrt{V}=0$ on $\{V=0\}$ (then $S=0$ too, so $\{S/\sqrt{V}\ge t\}=\varnothing$ for $t>0$).

Condition on $\mathcal{F}=\sigma(|X_1|,\ldots,|X_n|)$ and work on $\{V>0\}$, writing $a_i=|X_i|$. For $\lambda>0$, by Markov's inequality,
$$\mathbb{P}_\varepsilon\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i a_i\ge t\sqrt{\textstyle\sum_i a_i^2}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}\Big\}\le \frac{\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\exp(\lambda\sum_i\varepsilon_i a_i)}{\exp(\lambda t\sqrt{\sum_i a_i^2})}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^n\cosh(\lambda a_i)}{\exp(\lambda t\sqrt{\sum_i a_i^2})}.$$
Since $\cosh x=\sum_{k\ge 0}x^{2k}/(2k)!\le\sum_{k\ge 0}x^{2k}/(2^k k!)=e^{x^2/2}$ (as $(2k)!\ge 2^k k!$),
$$\mathbb{P}_\varepsilon\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i a_i\ge t\sqrt{\textstyle\sum_i a_i^2}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}\Big\}\le \exp\!\Big(\tfrac{\lambda^2}{2}\sum_i a_i^2-\lambda t\sqrt{\textstyle\sum_i a_i^2}\Big).$$
With $s=\sqrt{\sum_i a_i^2}>0$, the exponent $\lambda^2s^2/2-\lambda ts$ is minimized at $\lambda=t/s$, giving $-t^2/2$. Hence, on $\{V>0\}$,
$$\mathbb{P}\Big\{\frac{S}{\sqrt{V}}\ge t\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}\Big\}=\mathbb{P}_\varepsilon\Big\{\frac{\sum_i\varepsilon_i a_i}{\sqrt{\sum_i a_i^2}}\ge t\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}\Big\}\le e^{-t^2/2}.$$
The same bound holds trivially on $\{V=0\}$. Taking expectations over $\mathcal{F}$,
$$\mathbb{P}\Big\{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n X_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2}}\ge t\Big\}\le e^{-t^2/2},\qquad t>0.$$
∎
