**Central idea.** Apply the entropy method via the modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality (Theorem 6.6), as in the proof of Theorem 12.5, but replace the boundedness-dependent Lemma 12.6 by a direct estimate using the sub-gamma moment hypothesis. Writing $e^{\lambda Z}\phi(-\lambda(Z-Z_i))=e^{\lambda Z_i}\psi(\lambda(Z-Z_i))$ with $\psi(u)=e^u\phi(-u)$, the bound $|Z-Z_i|\le|X_i|$ and monotonicity of $\psi$ give $\psi(\lambda(Z-Z_i))\le\psi(\lambda|X_i|)$; the power series $\psi(u)=\sum_{q\ge2}\frac{q-1}{q!}u^q$ together with $\mathbb{E}|X_i|^q\le q!c^{q-2}\sigma^2/(2n)$ yields $\mathbb{E}\psi(\lambda|X_i|)\le\sigma^2\lambda^2/(2n(1-c\lambda)^2)$. This gives $\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})\le\frac{\sigma^2\lambda^2}{2(1-c\lambda)^2}\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}$, and the Herbst argument produces $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}\le\sigma^2\lambda^2/(2(1-c\lambda))\le v\lambda^2/(2(1-c\lambda))$ since $v=\sigma^2+2\mathbb{E}Z\ge\sigma^2$.

**Solution.** Let $\tilde X_{i,s}=\alpha_{i,s}X_i$, so $Z=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^n\tilde X_{i,s}$ with $|\alpha_{i,s}|\le1$, and let $Z_i=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{j\ne i}\tilde X_{j,s}$. Put $W_i=Z-Z_i$ and $\phi(u)=e^u-u-1$. The modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality (Theorem 6.6) gives, for $\lambda\ge0$,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})\le\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda Z}\phi(-\lambda W_i)\right],$$
where $\mathrm{Ent}(Y)=\mathbb{E}[Y\log Y]-\mathbb{E}Y\log\mathbb{E}Y$ (all expectations finite for $0\le\lambda<1/c$ since $Z\le\sum_i|X_i|$ and each $|X_i|$ has an exponential moment).

Write $e^{\lambda Z}\phi(-\lambda W_i)=e^{\lambda Z_i}\psi(\lambda W_i)$ with $\psi(u)=e^u\phi(-u)=1-e^u+ue^u$. If $\hat s$ attains the supremum defining $Z$ then $Z_i\ge\sum_{j\ne i}\tilde X_{j,\hat s}$, so $W_i\le\tilde X_{i,\hat s}\le|X_i|$; if $\hat s_i$ attains $Z_i$ then $Z\ge\tilde X_{i,\hat s_i}+Z_i$, so $W_i\ge\tilde X_{i,\hat s_i}\ge-|X_i|$. Hence $|W_i|\le|X_i|$. Since $\psi'(u)=ue^u$, $\psi$ is increasing on $[0,\infty)$, decreasing on $(-\infty,0]$, and $\psi(-u)\le\psi(u)$ for $u\ge0$ (as $\psi(u)-\psi(-u)$ has nonnegative derivative $u(e^u-e^{-u})$ and vanishes at $0$). Thus $\psi(\lambda W_i)\le\psi(\lambda|X_i|)$ pointwise, and, $X_i$ being independent of $X^{(i)}=(X_j)_{j\ne i}$,
$$\mathbb{E}^{(i)}\left[e^{\lambda Z}\phi(-\lambda W_i)\right]=e^{\lambda Z_i}\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[\psi(\lambda W_i)]\le e^{\lambda Z_i}\mathbb{E}[\psi(\lambda|X_i|)],$$
where $\mathbb{E}^{(i)}$ conditions on $X^{(i)}$.

For $u\ge0$, $\psi(u)=\sum_{q\ge2}\frac{q-1}{q!}u^q$ (from $1-e^u+ue^u$). By monotone convergence and the moment hypothesis,
$$\mathbb{E}[\psi(\lambda|X_i|)]=\sum_{q\ge2}\frac{q-1}{q!}\lambda^q\mathbb{E}|X_i|^q\le\frac{\sigma^2}{2n}\sum_{q\ge2}(q-1)(c\lambda)^{q-2}\lambda^2=\frac{\sigma^2\lambda^2}{2n(1-c\lambda)^2},\qquad 0\le\lambda<1/c,$$
using $\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)r^k=(1-r)^{-2}$. Since $\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[W_i]\ge\mathbb{E}^{(i)}[\tilde X_{i,\hat s_i}]=\alpha_{i,\hat s_i}\mathbb{E}X_i=0$, Jensen gives $e^{\lambda Z_i}\le e^{\lambda\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Z}\le\mathbb{E}^{(i)}e^{\lambda Z}$, whence $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z_i}\le\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}$. Therefore
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})\le\frac{\sigma^2\lambda^2}{2(1-c\lambda)^2}\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z},\qquad 0\le\lambda<1/c.$$

Put $G(\lambda)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}$. Then $\frac{\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z})}{\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}}=\lambda G'(\lambda)-G(\lambda)$, so
$$\lambda G'(\lambda)-G(\lambda)\le\frac{\sigma^2\lambda^2}{2(1-c\lambda)^2},\qquad G(0)=G'(0)=0.$$
Hence $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{G(\lambda)}{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda G'-G}{\lambda^2}\le\frac{\sigma^2}{2(1-c\lambda)^2}$, and integrating,
$$\frac{G(\lambda)}{\lambda}\le\int_0^\lambda\frac{\sigma^2}{2(1-cs)^2}\,ds=\frac{\sigma^2\lambda}{2(1-c\lambda)},$$
i.e. $G(\lambda)\le\frac{\sigma^2\lambda^2}{2(1-c\lambda)}$. Since $\mathbb{E}Z\ge\sup_s\sum_i\alpha_{i,s}\mathbb{E}X_i=0$, $v=\sigma^2+2\mathbb{E}Z\ge\sigma^2$, and
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}\le\frac{v\lambda^2}{2(1-c\lambda)},\qquad 0\le\lambda<1/c,$$
which is the sub-gamma bound with variance factor $v$ and scale $c$ (Bousquet 2002b, Theorem 2.12). The estimate is vacuous for $\lambda\ge1/c$, where the right-hand side is nonpositive while the left-hand side is nonnegative.
