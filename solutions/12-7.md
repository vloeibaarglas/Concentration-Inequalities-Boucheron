**Central idea.** Rio's trick is to apply the modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality (Theorem 6.6) not to the leave-one-out suprema $Z_i$ but to constant shifts $\tilde Z_i = Z_i + c_i$, with $c_i$ chosen so that $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda\tilde Z_i} = \mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}$. Then the terms $\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda\tilde Z_i} - e^{\lambda Z}]$ in Theorem 6.6 cancel, and the self-bounding property $\sum_i(Z-Z_i)\le Z$ cancels $\lambda\mathbb{E}[Ze^{\lambda Z}]$ against the entropy, leaving $\sum_i\log(\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}/\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z_i})\le\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}$. Since the $X_i$ are i.i.d., every $Z_i$ has the law of $Z_n$, which gives the claim for every $\lambda\ge0$. For the cancellation to work the shift must be $c_i = \lambda^{-1}\log(\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}/\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z_i})$: the hint as printed is missing the factor $1/\lambda$.

**Solution.** *Erratum in the hint.* The hint's shift $\tilde Z_i = Z_i + \log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z} - \log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z_i}$ does not normalize the exponential moment unless $\lambda = 1$. Write $M = \mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}$ and $M_i = \mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z_i}$. With the printed shift,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda\tilde Z_i} = \Big(\frac{M}{M_i}\Big)^{\lambda}M_i = M^{\lambda}M_i^{1-\lambda},$$
which differs from $M$ whenever $\lambda\ne1$ and $M_i\ne M$. For example, $M = 2$, $M_i = 1.5$, $\lambda = 0.5$ gives $\sqrt{3}\approx1.732\ne2$. The correct shift is
$$\tilde Z_i = Z_i + c_i,\qquad c_i = \frac1\lambda\log\frac{M}{M_i},$$
for which $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda\tilde Z_i} = e^{\lambda c_i}M_i = M$. (An earlier version of this solution used the printed shift, wrote $e^{\lambda c_i}M_i = M$ for all $\lambda$, and then could only conclude for $\lambda\ge1$. Both the identity and that restriction were artifacts of the missing $1/\lambda$. The proof below holds for every $\lambda\ge0$.)

*Setting.* As in Theorem 12.5, $X_1,\dots,X_n$ are i.i.d., $\mathbb{E}X_{i,s} = 0$ and $X_{i,s}\le1$ for all $s\in\mathcal{T}$. Let $\hat s$ attain $Z$ and $\hat s_i$ attain $Z_i$ (take $\mathcal T$ finite; the countable case follows by monotone convergence along finite $\mathcal T_k\uparrow\mathcal T$). As shown before Theorem 12.5, $Z\ge\sum_jX_{j,\hat s_i}$, and $Z_i\ge\sum_{j\ne i}X_{j,\hat s}$ gives $Z - Z_i\le X_{i,\hat s}$. Summing,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n(Z - Z_i)\le\sum_{i=1}^nX_{i,\hat s} = Z.\tag{1}$$
Since $Z\le n$ and $Z\ge\sum_jX_{j,s_0}$ for any fixed $s_0$, all quantities below are finite for $\lambda\ge0$. Because the $X_i$ are i.i.d., each $Z_i$ has the same distribution as $Z_n$, so $M_i = M_n := \mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z_n}$ for all $i$.

*Proof for $\lambda>0$.* (For $\lambda = 0$ both sides are $0$.) Each $\tilde Z_i = Z_i + c_i$ is a function of $X^{(i)} = (X_j)_{j\ne i}$, so Theorem 6.6 applies with $Z_i$ replaced by $\tilde Z_i$. With $\phi(u) = e^u - u - 1$,
$$\mathrm{Ent}(e^{\lambda Z}) = \lambda\mathbb{E}[Ze^{\lambda Z}] - M\log M\le\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda Z}\phi(-\lambda(Z - \tilde Z_i))\big].$$
Since $e^{\lambda Z}\phi(-\lambda(Z-\tilde Z_i)) = e^{\lambda\tilde Z_i} - e^{\lambda Z} + \lambda(Z - \tilde Z_i)e^{\lambda Z}$, the right-hand side equals
$$\sum_{i=1}^n\big(\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda\tilde Z_i} - M\big) + \lambda\,\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\lambda Z}\sum_{i=1}^n(Z - Z_i)\Big] - \lambda M\sum_{i=1}^nc_i.$$
The first sum is $0$ by the choice of $c_i$. By (1) and $\lambda e^{\lambda Z}\ge0$, the middle term is at most $\lambda\mathbb{E}[Ze^{\lambda Z}]$. Therefore
$$\lambda\mathbb{E}[Ze^{\lambda Z}] - M\log M\le\lambda\mathbb{E}[Ze^{\lambda Z}] - \lambda M\sum_{i=1}^nc_i.$$
Cancel $\lambda\mathbb{E}[Ze^{\lambda Z}]$, divide by $M>0$, and use $\lambda c_i = \log M - \log M_i$:
$$\sum_{i=1}^n\big(\log M - \log M_i\big)\le\log M,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad(n-1)\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}\le\sum_{i=1}^n\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z_i}.\tag{2}$$
(Inequality (2) does not use identical distribution.) In the i.i.d. case the right-hand side of (2) is $n\log M_n$, so
$$\frac{\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}}{n}\le\frac{\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z_n}}{n-1},\qquad\lambda\ge0.$$

*Pollard's inequality.* Taking expectations in (1) gives $n\mathbb{E}Z - \sum_i\mathbb{E}Z_i\le\mathbb{E}Z$, i.e. $(n-1)\mathbb{E}Z\le n\mathbb{E}Z_n$, which is $\mathbb{E}Z/n\le\mathbb{E}Z_n/(n-1)$. It also follows from the result: divide by $\lambda$ and let $\lambda\downarrow0$, using $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z} = \lambda\mathbb{E}Z + o(\lambda)$.

*Remark on $\lambda<0$.* The restriction $\lambda\ge0$ (the range of Theorem 12.5) is needed: the step using (1) multiplies by $\lambda e^{\lambda Z}$, and the inequality itself can fail for $\lambda<0$. Take $\mathcal T = \{1,2\}$ and $X_i$ uniform on $\{(1,-\tfrac12),(-\tfrac12,1),(-\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)\}$, which is centered with coordinates $\le1$. For $n = 2$ and $\lambda = -0.1$, exact computation gives $\tfrac12\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}\approx-0.03153$, which is larger than $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z_2}\approx-0.04746$. For the same distribution, exact enumeration confirms the inequality for $n\in\{3,4,5\}$ and $\lambda\in\{0.1,0.5,1,2,5\}$. $\blacksquare$
