**Central idea.** Since $\theta(y)$ is chosen so that equality holds at $x = y$, the inequality is equivalent to the ratio $\psi(t) = e^t\phi(-t)/(y\phi(t)+t^2/2)$ being nondecreasing in $t$. Differentiating, the numerator of $\psi'$ splits as $yA(t) + B(t)$ with $A(t) = e^{2t}-(t^2+2)e^t+1$ and $B(t) = t^3e^t/2 - th(t)$ (where $h(t) = e^t\phi(-t)$); both $A$ and $B$ are nonnegative for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$ (each is monotone on either side of a global minimum of $0$ at $t=0$), so $\psi'\ge 0$ for every $y\ge 0$.

**Solution.** Let $\phi(u) = e^u-u-1 \ge 0$, $h(x) = e^x\phi(-x) = 1-(1-x)e^x$, and $k_y(x) = y\phi(x)+x^2/2$. Then $\theta(y) = h(y)/k_y(y)$ for $y>0$, and we show $h(x)\le\theta(y)k_y(x)$ for $x\le y$.

First, $k_y(x)\ge 0$ for all $x$ (as $\phi\ge 0$, $y\ge 0$), and for $y>0$, $k_y(x)>0$ when $x\ne 0$ since $\phi(x)>0$ for $x\ne 0$. Also $h(0)=0$, $h'(x)=xe^x$ (from $h(x)=1-(1-x)e^x$), so $h$ is strictly increasing on $[0,\infty)$ with $h(0)=0$, hence $h(y)>0$ and $\theta(y)>0$ for $y>0$.

*Case $y=0$.* Here $\theta$ extends continuously with $\theta(0)=1$ (since $h(y) = y^2/2+O(y^3)$ and $k_y(y)=y^2/2+O(y^3)$), and for $x\le 0$ we need $h(x)\le x^2/2$. With $d(x)=x^2/2-h(x)$, $d(0)=0$ and $d'(x)=x-xe^x=x(1-e^x)\le 0$ for $x\le 0$ (as $e^x\le 1$), so $d$ is decreasing on $(-\infty,0]$ and $d(x)\ge 0$ for $x\le 0$.

*Case $y>0$.* For $x=0$ both sides equal $0$. For $x\ne 0$ put $\psi(t)=h(t)/k_y(t)$, continuously extended at $t=0$ by $\psi(0)=\lim_{t\to0}h(t)/k_y(t)=1/(y+1)$ (using $h(t)=t^2/2+O(t^3)$, $k_y(t)=(y+1)t^2/2+O(t^3)$). Then
$$\psi'(t)=\frac{h'(t)k_y(t)-h(t)k_y'(t)}{k_y(t)^2},\qquad k_y'(t)=y(e^t-1)+t.$$
The numerator is $N(t)=te^t\big(y\phi(t)+t^2/2\big)-h(t)\big(y(e^t-1)+t\big)=yA(t)+B(t)$ where
$$A(t)=te^t\phi(t)-h(t)(e^t-1),\qquad B(t)=\frac{t^3e^t}{2}-th(t).$$
Expanding $A$: since $h(t)=1-(1-t)e^t$ and $\phi(t)=e^t-t-1$,
\begin{align*}
A(t)&=te^t(e^t-t-1)-\big(1-(1-t)e^t\big)(e^t-1)\\
&=e^{2t}-(t^2+2)e^t+1.
\end{align*}
Now $A(0)=0$ and $A'(t)=2e^{2t}-(t^2+2t+2)e^t=e^t\big(2e^t-(t^2+2t+2)\big)$. The bracket $n(t)=2e^t-(t^2+2t+2)$ satisfies $n(0)=0$ and $n'(t)=2(e^t-t-1)=2\phi(t)\ge 0$, so $n(t)\le 0$ for $t\le 0$ and $n(t)\ge 0$ for $t\ge 0$. Hence $A$ decreases on $(-\infty,0]$, increases on $[0,\infty)$, with minimum $A(0)=0$; thus $A(t)\ge 0$ for all $t$.

For $B$: $B(t)=t\big(\tfrac{t^2e^t}{2}-h(t)\big)=:t\,m(t)$ with $m(t)=\tfrac{t^2e^t}{2}-1+(1-t)e^t$. Then $m(0)=0$ and $m'(t)=te^t+\tfrac{t^2e^t}{2}-te^t=\tfrac{t^2e^t}{2}\ge 0$, so $m$ is nondecreasing with $m(0)=0$, i.e. $m(t)$ has the sign of $t$. Consequently $B(t)=t\,m(t)\ge 0$ for all $t$.

Therefore $N(t)=yA(t)+B(t)\ge 0$ for every $y\ge 0$ and all $t$, so $\psi'(t)\ge 0$ on $(-\infty,0)$ and $(0,\infty)$. With continuity at $0$, $\psi$ is nondecreasing on all of $\mathbb{R}$. For $x\le y$ (and $y>0$) we get $\psi(x)\le\psi(y)$, i.e. $\frac{h(x)}{k_y(x)}\le\frac{h(y)}{k_y(y)}=\theta(y)$ for $x\ne 0$ (where $k_y(x),k_y(y)>0$), and hence $h(x)\le\theta(y)k_y(x)$. For $x=0$ this is $0=0$. In original notation,
$$e^x\phi(-x)\le\theta(y)\left(y\phi(x)+\frac{x^2}{2}\right)\qquad\text{for }y\ge 0,\ x\le y,$$
as claimed. ∎
