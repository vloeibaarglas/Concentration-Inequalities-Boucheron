**Central idea.** Each increment $X^T(A-B)X$ of the chaos, with $A-B$ symmetric of zero trace, is sub-gamma with variance factor $2\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$ and scale $2\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}$, so it has a mixed Gaussian/exponential tail in the two norms. Chaining along partitions generated by packing nets for both norms (as in the hint), with level-$m$ thresholds of order $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HS},m}\sqrt{u2^m}+u\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{op},m}2^m$ and a union bound over the $e^{O(2^m)}$ links at level $m$, gives $Z\le K uE$ outside an event of probability at most $\kappa e^{-u}$, after comparing the dyadic sums with the entropy integrals. However, the bound **as printed (with $K=1$) is false**: a two-point index set has a tail decaying like $e^{-0.763u}$ only. A universal factor $K$ in front of $E$ is necessary; we prove the statement with $K=137$, $\kappa=1.42$.

**Erratum.** The printed claim $\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge uE\}\le\kappa e^{-u}$ for all $u>1$, with $\kappa$ universal, is false. Take $n=2$, $M=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, $\mathcal{T}=\{0,M\}$, $A_0=0$. Then $\delta_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|M\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\sqrt2$, $\delta_{\mathrm{op}}=\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1$, and (with the book's packing entropy, $N(t)$ the maximal number of points with pairwise distances $>t$) $H_\nu(t)=\log2$ for $t<\delta_\nu$. Hence
$$E=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\sqrt{2\log2}+\frac12\cdot2\log2=\sqrt{\log2}+\log2=1.52570\ldots$$
Now $Z=\max(0,2X_1X_2)$, and with $W_1=(X_1+X_2)/\sqrt2$, $W_2=(X_1-X_2)/\sqrt2$ (independent standard Gaussians) $2X_1X_2=W_1^2-W_2^2$. Therefore, with $t=\sqrt{uE+1}$ and the Mills-ratio lower bound $\mathbb{P}\{|g|\ge t\}\ge\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\frac{t}{1+t^2}e^{-t^2/2}$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge uE\}\ge\mathbb{P}\{W_2^2\le1\}\,\mathbb{P}\{W_1^2\ge uE+1\}\ge0.68\cdot\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\frac{t}{1+t^2}\,e^{-1/2}e^{-uE/2}.$$
Since $E/2=0.7629<1$, $e^{u}\,\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge uE\}\to\infty$ as $u\to\infty$, so no universal (indeed no finite) $\kappa$ exists. Numerically (exact law $\mathbb{P}\{X_1X_2\ge a\}=\pi^{-1}\int_a^\infty K_0$), $e^u\,\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge uE\}=0.588,\ 1.44,\ 11.3,\ 933,\ 8.8\times10^6$ at $u=5,10,20,40,80$. The same example shows that in a corrected statement $\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge KuE\}\le\kappa e^{-u}$ one needs $KE/2\ge1$, i.e. $K\ge2/E=1.31$.

**Corrected statement.** There are universal constants $K,\kappa$ (one may take $K=137$, $\kappa=1.42$) such that, whenever $\mathcal{T}$ has at least two elements, for all $u>1$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge KuE\}\le\kappa e^{-u}.$$
Equivalently $\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge uE\}\le1.42\,e^{-u/137}$ for $u>137$. (If $|\mathcal{T}|=1$ then $Z=0=E$ and $\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge uE\}=1$, so $|\mathcal{T}|\ge2$ is needed in any version.)

**Solution.** Throughout, $\nu\in\{\mathrm{HS},\mathrm{op}\}$, $N_\nu(t)$ is the maximal cardinality of a subset of $\mathcal{T}$ with pairwise $\nu$-distances $>t$, $H_\nu(t)=\log N_\nu(t)$ (nonincreasing in $t$), $\delta_\nu$ is the $\nu$-diameter, and
$$I_{\mathrm{HS}}=\int_0^{\delta_{\mathrm{HS}}/2}\sqrt{2H_{\mathrm{HS}}(t)}\,dt,\qquad I_{\mathrm{op}}=\int_0^{\delta_{\mathrm{op}}/2}2H_{\mathrm{op}}(t)\,dt,\qquad E=I_{\mathrm{HS}}+I_{\mathrm{op}}.$$
We may assume $\mathcal{T}$ bounded and $E<\infty$ (otherwise there is nothing to prove, $Z$ being finite when $\mathcal{T}$ is bounded since $|X^TAX|\le\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}|X|^2$).

*Step 1 (increments).* Let $M$ be symmetric with zero diagonal, eigenvalues $\lambda_j$, so $\sum_j\lambda_j=\mathrm{tr}M=0$ and, by rotation invariance, $X^TMX\overset{d}{=}\sum_j\lambda_j(g_j^2-1)$ with $g_j$ i.i.d. $N(0,1)$. For $y<\tfrac12$, $\log\mathbb{E}e^{y(g^2-1)}=\phi(y):=-\tfrac12\log(1-2y)-y$. For $0\le y<\frac12$, $\phi(y)=\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{(2y)^k}{2k}\le\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{(2y)^k}{4}=\frac{y^2}{1-2y}$; for $y<0$, $\phi(0)=\phi'(0)=0$ and $\phi''(y)=2/(1-2y)^2\le2$, so $\phi(y)\le y^2$. Hence for $0\le\theta<1/(2\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}})$,
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\theta X^TMX}=\sum_j\phi(\theta\lambda_j)\le\frac{\theta^2\sum_j\lambda_j^2}{1-2\theta\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}}=\frac{\theta^2\cdot2\|M\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}{2(1-2\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}\theta)},$$
i.e. $X^TMX$ is sub-gamma on the right tail with variance factor $v=2\|M\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$ and scale $c=2\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ (Section 2.4, as in Example 2.12). The sub-gamma tail bound $\mathbb{P}\{Y>\sqrt{2vx}+cx\}\le e^{-x}$ gives, whenever $\|M\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le a$ and $\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le b$,
$$\mathbb{P}\big\{X^TMX>2a\sqrt x+2bx\big\}\le e^{-x}\qquad(x>0).\tag{1}$$
For $A,B\in\mathcal{T}$, $A-B$ is symmetric with zero diagonal, so (1) applies to $Y_A-Y_B$ where $Y_A=X^TAX$.

*Step 2 (reduction to finite $\mathcal{T}$).* Suppose the corrected statement holds for every finite $\mathcal{T}'$ with $|\mathcal{T}'|\ge2$. The space of symmetric $n\times n$ matrices is finite-dimensional, so $\mathcal{T}$ has a countable dense subset $\mathcal{T}_\infty\ni A_0$, and $A\mapsto X^T(A-A_0)X$ is continuous, so $Z=\sup_{\mathcal{T}_\infty}$. Let $\mathcal{T}_k\uparrow\mathcal{T}_\infty$ be finite with $A_0\in\mathcal{T}_k$, $|\mathcal{T}_k|\ge2$, and $Z_k$ the corresponding suprema, so $Z_k\uparrow Z$. Packing numbers and diameters are monotone under inclusion, hence $E(\mathcal{T}_k)\le E$. Thus $\mathbb{P}\{Z>KuE\}=\lim_k\mathbb{P}\{Z_k>KuE\}\le\limsup_k\mathbb{P}\{Z_k\ge KuE(\mathcal{T}_k)\}\le\kappa e^{-u}$, and $\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge KuE\}\le\mathbb{P}\{Z>Ku'E\}\le\kappa e^{-u'}$ for every $u'\in(1,u)$; letting $u'\uparrow u$ gives the claim. So assume from now on that $\mathcal{T}$ is finite with $|\mathcal{T}|\ge2$; then $\delta_\nu>0$ and $E>0$.

*Step 3 (nets and partitions).* For $m\ge0$ let $\varepsilon_{\nu,m}=\inf\{\varepsilon>0:H_\nu(\varepsilon)\le2^m\}$; this is nonincreasing in $m$, and by monotonicity of $H_\nu$: $H_\nu(t)>2^m$ for $0<t<\varepsilon_{\nu,m}$, while $H_\nu(\eta)\le2^m$ for every $\eta>\varepsilon_{\nu,m}$. Also $\varepsilon_{\nu,m}=0$ as soon as $2^m\ge\log|\mathcal{T}|$; let $m^*$ be the least such $m$. Fix $\gamma>0$ smaller than every nonzero $\nu$-distance between points of $\mathcal{T}$ (both $\nu$), and put $\eta_{\nu,m}=\varepsilon_{\nu,m}+\gamma$. Let $S_{\nu,m}\subset\mathcal{T}$ be a maximal $\eta_{\nu,m}$-separated set: $|S_{\nu,m}|\le N_\nu(\eta_{\nu,m})\le e^{2^m}$, and by maximality every $A\in\mathcal{T}$ is within $\nu$-distance $\eta_{\nu,m}$ of $S_{\nu,m}$. For $m\ge m^*$, $\eta_{\nu,m}=\gamma$ and $S_{\nu,m}=\mathcal{T}$. Let $\pi_{\nu,m}:\mathcal{T}\to S_{\nu,m}$ be a fixed nearest-point map, $\mathcal{B}_m,\mathcal{C}_m$ the partitions of $\mathcal{T}$ into the fibres of $\pi_{\mathrm{op},m}$, $\pi_{\mathrm{HS},m}$, $\mathcal{A}_0=\{\mathcal{T}\}$ and $\mathcal{A}_m=\mathcal{B}_{m-1}\wedge\mathcal{C}_{m-1}$ for $m\ge1$. Then $|\mathcal{A}_m|\le e^{2^{m-1}}e^{2^{m-1}}=e^{2^m}$. Let $\Pi_mA\in\mathcal{T}$ be a distinguished point of the cell of $\mathcal{A}_m$ containing $A$, with $\Pi_0A=A_0$. For $m\ge1$, $A$ and $\Pi_mA$ have the same image under $\pi_{\nu,m-1}$, so $\|\Pi_mA-A\|_\nu\le2\eta_{\nu,m-1}$. With $J=m^*+1$, the cells of $\mathcal{A}_J$ are singletons, so $\Pi_JA=A$ and
$$Y_A-Y_{A_0}=\sum_{m=0}^{J-1}\big(Y_{\Pi_{m+1}A}-Y_{\Pi_mA}\big).$$
Link sizes: $\|\Pi_1A-A_0\|_\nu\le\delta_\nu$; for $m\ge1$, $\|\Pi_{m+1}A-\Pi_mA\|_\nu\le2\eta_{\nu,m}+2\eta_{\nu,m-1}\le4\eta_{\nu,m-1}$. Link counts: at level $0$ the pair $(\Pi_0A,\Pi_1A)$ takes at most $|\mathcal{A}_1|\le e^2$ values; at level $m\ge1$, $(\Pi_mA,\Pi_{m+1}A)$ takes at most $|\mathcal{A}_m||\mathcal{A}_{m+1}|\le e^{3\cdot2^m}$ values.

*Step 4 (union bound).* Fix $u\ge1$. Put $x_0=u+2$ and $x_m=(u+3)2^m$ for $m\ge1$, and thresholds
$$t_0=2\delta_{\mathrm{HS}}\sqrt{x_0}+2\delta_{\mathrm{op}}x_0,\qquad t_m=8\eta_{\mathrm{HS},m-1}\sqrt{x_m}+8\eta_{\mathrm{op},m-1}x_m\ (m\ge1).$$
Let $\Omega_{\mathrm{bad}}$ be the event that some link $Y_{\Pi_{m+1}A}-Y_{\Pi_mA}$ ($A\in\mathcal{T}$, $0\le m<J$) exceeds $t_m$. By (1) and the link counts,
$$\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{\mathrm{bad}})\le e^2e^{-(u+2)}+\sum_{m\ge1}e^{3\cdot2^m}e^{-(u+3)2^m}=e^{-u}+\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-u2^m}\le e^{-u}\Big(1+\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-(2^m-1)}\Big)\le1.42\,e^{-u},$$
using $u(2^m-1)\ge2^m-1$ for $u\ge1$ and $\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-(2^m-1)}=0.41858\ldots$

*Step 5 (the good event).* Outside $\Omega_{\mathrm{bad}}$, $Z\le\sum_{m=0}^{J-1}t_m$. For $u\ge1$: $x_0\le3u$ and $x_m\le4u2^m$, and $\sqrt u\le u$, so $t_0\le u(2\sqrt3\,\delta_{\mathrm{HS}}+6\delta_{\mathrm{op}})$ and, for $m\ge1$, with $k=m-1$,
$$t_m\le8\eta_{\mathrm{HS},k}\cdot2\sqrt u\,2^{(k+1)/2}+8\eta_{\mathrm{op},k}\cdot4u\,2^{k+1}\le u\big(16\sqrt2\,\eta_{\mathrm{HS},k}2^{k/2}+64\,\eta_{\mathrm{op},k}2^k\big).$$
Hence, outside $\Omega_{\mathrm{bad}}$, $Z\le uB_\gamma$, where
$$B_\gamma=2\sqrt3\,\delta_{\mathrm{HS}}+6\delta_{\mathrm{op}}+\sum_{k=0}^{m^*}\Big(16\sqrt2\,(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HS},k}+\gamma)2^{k/2}+64(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{op},k}+\gamma)2^k\Big).$$
So $\mathbb{P}\{Z>uB_\gamma\}\le1.42e^{-u}$ for every admissible $\gamma$. As $\gamma\downarrow0$, $B_\gamma\downarrow B_0$ (the same expression with $\gamma=0$; $m^*$ does not depend on $\gamma$), and $\{Z>uB_0\}=\bigcup_\gamma\{Z>uB_\gamma\}$ is an increasing union, so $\mathbb{P}\{Z>uB_0\}\le1.42e^{-u}$ for all $u\ge1$.

*Step 6 (comparison with the integrals).* Diameters: two points at distance $\delta_\nu$ are $t$-separated for $t<\delta_\nu$, so $H_\nu(t)\ge\log2$ there. Hence $I_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{HS}}}{2}\sqrt{2\log2}$ and $I_{\mathrm{op}}\ge\delta_{\mathrm{op}}\log2$, i.e.
$$\delta_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\sqrt{2/\log2}\;I_{\mathrm{HS}}\le1.699\,I_{\mathrm{HS}},\qquad\delta_{\mathrm{op}}\le I_{\mathrm{op}}/\log2\le1.443\,I_{\mathrm{op}}.$$
Sums: write $\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon_{\nu,k}$, which vanishes for $k\ge m^*$. Since $H_\nu(t)>2^j$ on $(0,\varepsilon_j)$, $\int_{\varepsilon_{j+1}}^{\varepsilon_j}\sqrt{H_\nu}\ge(\varepsilon_j-\varepsilon_{j+1})2^{j/2}$ and $\int_{\varepsilon_{j+1}}^{\varepsilon_j}H_\nu\ge(\varepsilon_j-\varepsilon_{j+1})2^j$. Also $\varepsilon_0\le\delta_\nu$ (as $H_\nu(t)=0$ for $t\ge\delta_\nu$), and since $H_\nu$ is nonincreasing, $\int_0^{\delta_\nu}f(H_\nu)\le2\int_0^{\delta_\nu/2}f(H_\nu)$ for $f$ nondecreasing. Summation by parts ($\varepsilon_k=\sum_{j\ge k}(\varepsilon_j-\varepsilon_{j+1})$) gives
$$\sum_{k\ge0}\varepsilon_k2^{k/2}=\sum_{j\ge0}(\varepsilon_j-\varepsilon_{j+1})\sum_{k\le j}2^{k/2}\le\frac{1}{1-2^{-1/2}}\sum_j(\varepsilon_j-\varepsilon_{j+1})2^{j/2}\le\frac{1}{1-2^{-1/2}}\int_0^{\delta_{\mathrm{HS}}}\sqrt{H_{\mathrm{HS}}}\le\frac{\sqrt2}{1-2^{-1/2}}I_{\mathrm{HS}}\le4.829\,I_{\mathrm{HS}}$$
for $\nu=\mathrm{HS}$, and for $\nu=\mathrm{op}$, using $\sum_{k\le j}2^k\le2^{j+1}$,
$$\sum_{k\ge0}\varepsilon_k2^k\le2\int_0^{\delta_{\mathrm{op}}}H_{\mathrm{op}}\le4\int_0^{\delta_{\mathrm{op}}/2}H_{\mathrm{op}}=2I_{\mathrm{op}}.$$
Therefore
$$B_0\le\big(2\sqrt3\cdot1.699+16\sqrt2\cdot4.829\big)I_{\mathrm{HS}}+\big(6\cdot1.443+64\cdot2\big)I_{\mathrm{op}}\le115.2\,I_{\mathrm{HS}}+136.7\,I_{\mathrm{op}}\le137\,E.$$

*Conclusion.* For $u>1$ and any $u'\in(1,u)$, by Steps 5–6, $\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge137uE\}\le\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge uB_0\}\le\mathbb{P}\{Z>u'B_0\}\le1.42e^{-u'}$ (using $B_0>0$); letting $u'\uparrow u$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge137\,uE\}\le1.42\,e^{-u}\qquad(u>1),$$
which is the corrected statement with $K=137$, $\kappa=1.42$. By the erratum, some factor $K\ge1.31$ is unavoidable. ∎
