**Central idea.** Identify $\mathrm{tr}(x)$ with a set $S\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$; the 1-inclusion graph is the subgraph of the cube induced by $S$. Every bound comes from splitting $S$ along one coordinate: $S_0,S_1\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n-1}$ (the two fibres), the cross edges are in bijection with $S_0\cap S_1$. For (a) this gives a recursion closed by an elementary calculus inequality. For (b) the overlap $S_0\cap S_1$ has VC-dimension at most $V-1$, which gives the Haussler–Littlestone–Warmuth bound $|E|\le V(|S|-1)$, i.e. $\mathrm{dens}<V$. **The printed claim $\mathrm{dens}\le V/2$ is false** (thresholds give a path with density $n/(n+1)$, VC-dimension $1$); the correct bound $V$ is asymptotically tight but never attained. **The printed claim that $Z$ is self-bounding is also false**: the leave-one-out density can exceed $Z$, and a single change of one sample point can lower $\mathrm{dens}$ by $1.5>1$, which no self-bounding function allows. What is true is that the VC-entropy $\log_2|\mathrm{tr}(x)|$ is self-bounding, and it dominates $2\,\mathrm{dens}$ by (a).

**Errata.**

1. *$\mathrm{dens}\le V/2$ is false.* Let $\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}$, $\mathcal{C}=\{(-\infty,a]:a\in\mathbb{R}\}$ (thresholds; VC-dimension $1$: one point is shattered, and for $y<z$ no set contains $z$ but not $y$). For $x_1<\dots<x_n$ the trace consists of the $n+1$ prefixes $\{1,\dots,k\}$, $0\le k\le n$, and consecutive prefixes differ in one coordinate, so the 1-inclusion graph is a path: $\mathrm{dens}=n/(n+1)>1/2$ for $n\ge2$ (e.g. $2/3$, $3/4$, $10/11$ for $n=2,3,10$; checked by enumeration). Similarly $\mathcal{C}=\{\emptyset\}\cup\{\{y\}:y\in\mathcal{X}\}$ (VC-dimension $1$) gives a star with density $n/(n+1)$. The correct bound is $\mathrm{dens}\le V$ (proved below, in the sharper form $\mathrm{dens}\le V(1-1/|\mathrm{tr}(x)|)$). The value $V/2$ is the density of the full $V$-cube, which is not extremal.

2. *$Z$ is not self-bounding.* Recall (Section 3.3) that $f:\mathcal{X}^n\to[0,\infty)$ is self-bounding if there are $f_i:\mathcal{X}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}$ with $0\le f(x)-f_i(x^{(i)})\le1$ and $\sum_i(f(x)-f_i(x^{(i)}))\le f(x)$ for all $x$.
   - *The natural leave-one-out choice fails.* Take $f_i=\mathrm{dens}$ on the sample with $x_i$ deleted. Let $\mathcal{X}=\{1,2,3\}$, $x=(1,2,3)$, and let $\mathcal{C}$ consist of the five sets with indicator patterns $S=\{000,001,011,110,111\}$. Its edges are $000\!-\!001$, $001\!-\!011$, $011\!-\!111$, $110\!-\!111$, so $Z=4/5=0.8$. Deleting coordinate $1$ or $2$ leaves the full square $\{00,01,10,11\}$ (density $1$), deleting coordinate $3$ leaves the path $\{00,01,11\}$ (density $2/3$). So $Z_i=(1,1,0.667)$ and $Z-Z_1=-0.2<0$, violating $f-f_i\ge0$.
   - *No choice of $f_i$ works.* If $f$ is self-bounding and $x,x'$ differ only in coordinate $i$, then $x^{(i)}=x'^{(i)}$ and $f(x)-f(x')=(f(x)-f_i(x^{(i)}))-(f(x')-f_i(x'^{(i)}))\le1-0=1$. Let $\mathcal{X}=\{1,2,a,b\}$ and $\mathcal{C}=\{B\cup T\cup P(B):B\subseteq\{1,2\},\,T\subseteq\{a\}\}$ with $P(B)=\{b\}$ if $|B|$ is odd and $P(B)=\emptyset$ otherwise. On $x=(1,2,a)$ the trace is all of $\{0,1\}^3$: $\mathrm{dens}=12/8=1.5$. On $x'=(1,2,b)$ the trace is $\{000,011,101,110\}$ (third bit = parity of the first two), which has no edges: $\mathrm{dens}=0$. So $f(x)-f(x')=1.5>1$, and $\mathrm{dens}$ is not self-bounding for any choice of the $f_i$. If $X_1,X_2,X_3$ are i.i.d. uniform on $\mathcal{X}$, both samples have positive probability, so the failure cannot be avoided by ignoring null sets. (All numbers above were checked by direct enumeration.)

**Solution.** Throughout, $S\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ is nonempty, $E(S)$ is the set of edges of the subgraph of the cube induced by $S$ (pairs of elements of $S$ at Hamming distance $1$), and $\mathrm{dens}=|E(S)|/|S|$. For $S=\mathrm{tr}(x)$ this is $\mathrm{dens}(x)$ (we assume $\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset$ so that $\mathrm{tr}(x)\ne\emptyset$). For $n\ge1$ put
$$S_b=\{y\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}:(y,b)\in S\}\ (b=0,1),\qquad U=S_0\cup S_1,\qquad C=S_0\cap S_1 .$$
An edge of $S$ either changes the last coordinate — it is then $\{(y,0),(y,1)\}$ with $y\in C$ — or it joins $(y,b),(y',b)$ with $\{y,y'\}\in E(S_b)$. Hence
$$|E(S)|=|E(S_0)|+|E(S_1)|+|C|,\qquad |S|=|S_0|+|S_1|=|U|+|C|.\tag{1}$$

**(a) $\mathrm{dens}(x)\le\frac12\log_2|\mathrm{tr}(x)|$.** We prove $|E(S)|\le\frac12|S|\log_2|S|$ for every $S\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ (with $0\log0=0$) by induction on $n$. For $n=0$, $|S|\le1$ and $|E(S)|=0$. Let $n\ge1$, $m_b=|S_b|$, $m=m_0+m_1$. If $m_0=0$ or $m_1=0$, $|E(S)|=|E(S_{1-b})|$ and the claim is the induction hypothesis. Otherwise, by symmetry assume $1\le m_0\le m_1$; then $|C|\le m_0$ and by (1) and induction
$$|E(S)|\le\tfrac{m_0}{2}\log_2m_0+\tfrac{m_1}{2}\log_2m_1+m_0 .$$
With $r=m_1/m_0\ge1$,
$$\tfrac m2\log_2m-\tfrac{m_0}{2}\log_2m_0-\tfrac{m_1}{2}\log_2m_1=\tfrac{m_0}{2}\log_2(1+r)+\tfrac{m_1}{2}\log_2(1+1/r)=\tfrac{m_0}{2}f(r),$$
where $f(r)=\log_2(1+r)+r\log_2(1+1/r)$. Now $f(1)=2$ and
$$f'(r)=\frac{1}{(1+r)\ln2}+\log_2(1+1/r)-\frac{1}{(1+r)\ln2}=\log_2(1+1/r)>0,$$
so $f(r)\ge2$ for $r\ge1$, i.e. $\frac{m_0}{2}f(r)\ge m_0$. Hence $|E(S)|\le\frac m2\log_2m$. Applied to $S=\mathrm{tr}(x)$ this is $\mathrm{dens}(x)\le\frac12\log_2|\mathrm{tr}(x)|$.

**(b) VC classes: $\mathrm{dens}(x)\le V\big(1-1/|\mathrm{tr}(x)|\big)<V$.** Say $S$ *shatters* $Y\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ if $\{(y_i)_{i\in Y}:y\in S\}=\{0,1\}^Y$, and let $\mathrm{vc}(S)$ be the largest size of a shattered set.

*Trace.* If $\mathcal{C}$ has VC-dimension $V$, then $\mathrm{vc}(\mathrm{tr}(x))\le V$: if $\mathrm{tr}(x)$ shatters $Y$, the points $x_i$, $i\in Y$, are distinct (if $x_i=x_j$ with $i\neq j$ in $Y$, no pattern has $i$ in and $j$ out), and $\{x_i:i\in Y\}$ is shattered by $\mathcal{C}$, so $|Y|\le V$.

*Claim.* For every nonempty $S\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ with $\mathrm{vc}(S)\le V$, $|E(S)|\le V(|S|-1)$.

*Proof by induction on $n$, for all $V\ge0$ simultaneously.* If $V=0$, $|S|=1$ (two distinct patterns differ in some coordinate $i$, and then $\{i\}$ is shattered), so $|E(S)|=0$. If $n=0$, $|S|=1$ and $|E(S)|=0$. Let $n\ge1$, $V\ge1$. Three facts:

- $|E(S_0)|+|E(S_1)|\le|E(U)|+|E(C)|$. Indeed, for an edge $e$ of the $(n-1)$-cube let $a=\mathbf 1[e\subseteq S_0]$, $b=\mathbf 1[e\subseteq S_1]$; then $\mathbf 1[e\subseteq U]\ge\max(a,b)$ and $\mathbf 1[e\subseteq C]=ab=\min(a,b)$, so $a+b\le\mathbf 1[e\subseteq U]+\mathbf 1[e\subseteq C]$. Sum over $e$.
- $\mathrm{vc}(U)\le V$: a set $Y\subseteq\{1,\dots,n-1\}$ shattered by $U$ is shattered by $S$.
- $\mathrm{vc}(C)\le V-1$ when $C\ne\emptyset$: if $C$ shatters $Y$, then for every pattern on $Y$ there is $y\in C$ realizing it, and $(y,0),(y,1)\in S$, so $S$ shatters $Y\cup\{n\}$; thus $|Y|+1\le V$.

$U$ is nonempty. If $C=\emptyset$, then by (1) and the first fact $|E(S)|\le|E(U)|\le V(|U|-1)=V(|S|-1)$. If $C\neq\emptyset$, the induction hypothesis (dimension $n-1$; parameters $V$ and $V-1\ge0$) gives
$$|E(S)|\le|E(U)|+|E(C)|+|C|\le V(|U|-1)+(V-1)(|C|-1)+|C|=V|S|-2V+1\le V(|S|-1),$$
using $|S|=|U|+|C|$ and $V\ge1$. $\square$

Applying the claim to $S=\mathrm{tr}(x)$: $\mathrm{dens}(x)\le V(1-1/|\mathrm{tr}(x)|)<V$. In particular $\mathrm{dens}(x)\le V$ (Haussler, Littlestone and Warmuth, 1994). The weaker bound $|E(S)|\le V|S|$ can also be obtained by down-shifting: shifts do not decrease the edge count or increase $\mathrm{vc}$, and for a down-closed $S$, $|E(S)|=\sum_{y\in S}|y|$ with all weights $|y|\le V$.

**Is the bound tight?** The printed bound $V/2$ is not even valid (Erratum 1). For the correct bound:

- *$V$ is asymptotically tight.* Let $\mathcal{X}$ be infinite and $\mathcal{C}=\{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:|A|\le V\}$, which has VC-dimension $V$ (every $V$-set is shattered; a $(V+1)$-set is not, since it is not itself traced). On $n\ge V$ distinct points the trace is $\{J:|J|\le V\}$; every $J$ with $|J|=k\ge1$ has its $k$ down-neighbours in the trace, so counting edges by their upper endpoint,
$$\mathrm{dens}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^Vk\binom nk}{\sum_{k=0}^V\binom nk}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}V,$$
because $\binom n{k}/\binom nV\to0$ for $k<V$. (Numerically: $0.9995,\ 1.9990,\ 2.9985,\ 4.9975$ for $V=1,2,3,5$ at $n=2000$; for $V=1$ this is the star, $n/(n+1)$.) So no bound $cV$ with $c<1$ holds, and $\sup_x\mathrm{dens}(x)=V$ for this class.
- *$V$ is never attained*: by the claim, $\mathrm{dens}\le V-V/|\mathrm{tr}(x)|<V$ for every finite sample. For $V=1$ the claim says the 1-inclusion graph is a forest (a cycle on $k$ vertices of $S$ would give a subset with $\mathrm{vc}\le1$ and at least $k>k-1$ induced edges), and the path/star examples show $1-1/|\mathrm{tr}(x)|$ is attained.
- *The full $V$-cube* ($\mathcal{C}=2^{\{1,\dots,V\}}$, $x=(1,\dots,V)$) has density exactly $V/2$; this is the source of the printed value, but it is not the extremal configuration.

(Exhaustive enumeration of all nonempty $S\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ for $n\le4$, plus $15{,}000$ random subsets for $n=5,6$: no violation of $|E(S)|\le\mathrm{vc}(S)(|S|-1)$ or of (a).)

**(c) Self-bounding.** As shown in Erratum 2, $Z=\mathrm{dens}(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ is in general *not* self-bounding: a self-bounding function changes by at most $1$ when one coordinate changes, while $\mathrm{dens}$ can drop from $1.5$ to $0$. What does hold is the following, which controls $Z$ from above.

*The VC-entropy $h(x)=\log_2|\mathrm{tr}(x)|$ is self-bounding*, with $h_i(x^{(i)})=\log_2|\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})|$, where $\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})$ is the trace on the sample with $x_i$ deleted, i.e. the projection of $\mathrm{tr}(x)$ deleting coordinate $i$.

- $0\le h-h_i\le1$: the projection $\mathrm{tr}(x)\to\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})$ is onto and each fibre has one or two elements, so $|\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})|\le|\mathrm{tr}(x)|\le2|\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})|$.
- $\sum_i(h-h_i)\le h$: let $Y=(Y_1,\dots,Y_n)$ be uniform on $\mathrm{tr}(x)$, so its Shannon entropy (base $2$) is $H(Y)=h(x)$, and $Y^{(i)}$ takes values in $\mathrm{tr}(x^{(i)})$, so $H(Y^{(i)})\le h_i$. Han's inequality (Theorem 4.1), $\sum_{i=1}^n\big(H(Y)-H(Y^{(i)})\big)\le H(Y)$, gives $\sum_i(h-h_i)\le\sum_i(H(Y)-H(Y^{(i)}))\le h$.

So $h(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ is a self-bounding random variable, and by (a) $Z\le h/2$ pointwise. Consequently the upper-tail inequalities for self-bounding functions apply to $h$ and transfer to $Z$ through $\{Z\ge t\}\subseteq\{h\ge2t\}$; for a VC class additionally $Z<V$ by (b). Nothing in this solution establishes a lower-tail or variance bound for $Z$ itself. ∎
