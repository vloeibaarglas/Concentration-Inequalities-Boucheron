**Central idea.** Turn $L_2(Q)$-packing of $\mathcal{F}$ into $L_1$-packing of the subgraphs under one explicit probability measure on $\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}$, then apply Haussler's bound (Lemma 13.6) to the subgraph class, which is a VC class. The measure is $R(dx,dt)=\frac{F(x)\mathbb 1\{|t|\le F(x)\}}{2\|F\|_Q^2}\,Q(dx)\,dt$. It turns $R(C_f\triangle C_{f'})$ into $Q[F|f-f'|]/(2\|F\|_Q^2)$, and the envelope inequality $|f-f'|^2\le2F|f-f'|$ then gives $\|f-f'\|_Q^2\le4\|F\|_Q^2\,R(C_f\triangle C_{f'})$. So $L_2(Q)$-separation $\delta\|F\|_Q$ forces $L_2(R)$-separation $\delta/2$ of the subgraph indicators. Haussler's bound at scale $\delta/2$ gives $e(V+1)(2e/\delta)^{2V}\le e(V+1)(16e)^{V+1}\delta^{-2V}$.

**Solution.** *Conventions and remarks on the statement.* As in Section 13.3, $N(\varepsilon,\mathcal{F},Q)$ is the packing number: the largest $N$ for which there are $f_1,\dots,f_N\in\mathcal{F}$ with $\|f_i-f_j\|_Q>\varepsilon$ for all $i\ne j$. The members of $\mathcal{F}$ and $F$ are measurable, which makes every subgraph $C_f=\{(x,t):t<f(x)\}$ a measurable subset of $\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}$. The exercise has three printing slips. "Theorem 13.6" is the book's Lemma 13.6 (Haussler's VC bound for universal entropy). The "$\wedge$" in the hint should be "$\vee$", because the set in the hint is $C_f\triangle C_{f'}$. And $\kappa$ is a universal constant: we show that $\kappa=e$ works for $0<\delta\le2$. For $\delta>2$, $N=1$ trivially, since $\|f-f'\|_Q\le2\|F\|_Q$. (With $V\ge1$ the printed right side drops below $1$ once $\delta$ is very large, for example $\delta>101.5$ when $V=1$ and $\kappa=e$. So the bound only makes sense for bounded $\delta$, which is where it matters.)

If $\|F\|_Q=0$, then every $f\in\mathcal{F}$ vanishes $Q$-a.e., all $L_2(Q)$ distances are $0$, and $N=1$. If $\|F\|_Q=\infty$, then no pair is $\infty$-separated and again $N=1$. Both cases satisfy the bound, so assume $0<\|F\|_Q<\infty$ and $0<\delta\le2$.

*Step 1: an auxiliary probability measure.* On $\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}$ define
$$R(dx,dt)=\frac{F(x)\,\mathbb 1\{|t|\le F(x)\}}{2\|F\|_Q^2}\,Q(dx)\,dt .$$
By Fubini its total mass is $\int F(x)\cdot2F(x)\,Q(dx)/(2\|F\|_Q^2)=1$, so $R$ is a probability measure. Take $f,f'\in\mathcal{F}$. The $x$-section of $C_f\triangle C_{f'}$ is the interval between $f(x)$ and $f'(x)$ (closed at the lower end, open at the upper end). It has Lebesgue length $|f(x)-f'(x)|$ and lies in $[-F(x),F(x)]$ because $|f|,|f'|\le F$. On that interval the density of $R$ in $t$ is the constant $F(x)/(2\|F\|_Q^2)$. Fubini therefore gives
$$R(C_f\triangle C_{f'})=\frac{Q\big[F\,|f-f'|\big]}{2\|F\|_Q^2}.$$
This is the hint's layer-cake identity, weighted by $F$.

*Step 2: comparing $L_2(Q)$ with $L_1(R)$.* Since $|f-f'|\le2F$ pointwise,
$$\|f-f'\|_Q^2=Q|f-f'|^2\le2\,Q\big[F|f-f'|\big]=4\|F\|_Q^2\,R(C_f\triangle C_{f'}).$$
So $\|f-f'\|_Q>\delta\|F\|_Q$ implies $R(C_f\triangle C_{f'})>\delta^2/4=(\delta/2)^2$. Now let $f_1,\dots,f_N$ be $\delta\|F\|_Q$-separated in $L_2(Q)$. Their subgraphs $C_i=C_{f_i}$ satisfy $R(C_i\triangle C_j)>(\delta/2)^2$ for $i\ne j$, and in particular they are distinct.

*Step 3: passing to a finitely supported measure.* Lemma 13.6 bounds $H(\cdot,\mathcal{A})=\sup_{Q'}\log N(\cdot,\mathcal{A},Q')$, where the supremum runs over probability measures concentrated on finite sets, and $R$ is not one of these. Let $Y_1,Y_2,\dots$ be i.i.d. with law $R$, and let $R_m=\frac1m\sum_{k\le m}\delta_{Y_k}$. By the strong law of large numbers, $R_m(C_i\triangle C_j)\to R(C_i\triangle C_j)$ almost surely for each of the finitely many pairs $i<j$. So for some realization and some $m$, $R_m(C_i\triangle C_j)>(\delta/2)^2$ holds for all $i\ne j$ at once. Here $R_m$ is concentrated on a finite subset of $\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}$. Write $\mathcal{S}=\{C_f:f\in\mathcal{F}\}$. Then
$$N\le N(\delta/2,\mathcal{S},R_m)\le\exp H(\delta/2,\mathcal{S}).$$

*Step 4: Haussler's bound.* By hypothesis $\mathcal{S}$ is a VC class of subsets of $\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}$ with VC dimension $V$. Lemma 13.6 at scale $\delta/2\in(0,1]$ gives
$$H(\delta/2,\mathcal{S})\le2V\log\frac{2e}{\delta}+\log\big(e(V+1)\big).$$
This holds for every finite family of $\delta\|F\|_Q$-separated functions, so
$$N(\delta\|F\|_Q,\mathcal{F},Q)\le e(V+1)\Big(\frac{2e}{\delta}\Big)^{2V}=e(V+1)\,(4e^2)^V\,\delta^{-2V}\le e(V+1)(16e)^{V+1}\Big(\frac1\delta\Big)^{2V},$$
using $4e^2\approx29.56\le16e\approx43.49$ and $16e\ge1$. This is the claim with $\kappa=e$, uniformly in $Q$. ∎
