**Central idea.** Symmetrize, then chain conditionally on the sample (Dudley, Corollary 13.2), measuring distances in $L_2(P_n)$. The chain runs from the empty set, so the bound is $\mathbb{E}Z^\pm\le C\,\mathbb{E}F(\delta_n)$, where $F(r)=\int_0^r\sqrt{\psi}$ and $\delta_n^2=\sup_sP_n(A_s)$. The map $t\mapsto F(\sqrt t)$ is concave, so Jensen moves the expectation inside, and $\mathbb{E}\delta_n^2\le\sigma^2+\mathbb{E}Z^+/\sqrt n$. Karamata's theorem gives $F(\rho)\asymp\rho\sqrt{\psi(\rho)}$. Concavity of $F$ gives $F(r)\le(r/\sigma)F(\sigma)$ for $r\ge\sigma$. The fixed-point inequality that results has solution $\mathbb{E}Z^\pm\lesssim\sigma\sqrt{\psi(\sigma/2)}+\psi(\sigma/2)/\sqrt n$. **The claim as printed is false:** a bound uniform in $n$ is impossible. It holds under a condition $n\sigma^2\ge\psi(\sigma/2)$, the analogue of the proviso in the book's Lemma 13.5 and Theorem 13.7. The hint's first display is also false as printed: $\sqrt{\sigma^2+\mathbb{E}Z^+/\sqrt n}$ belongs in the upper limit of the integral, not in front of it.

**Solution.**

*Conventions.* As in Section 13.3, $N(\delta,\mathcal{T},Q)$ is the largest number of indices that are pairwise $\delta$-separated in $d_Q(s,t)=Q(A_s\triangle A_t)^{1/2}$. The universal entropy $H(\delta,\mathcal{T})$ is the supremum of $\log N(\delta,\mathcal{T},Q)$ over probability measures $Q$ concentrated on finite sets, such as $P_n=\frac1n\sum_i\delta_{X_i}$. Since $\psi$ is nonincreasing, $\psi(1/x)$ is nondecreasing in $x$, so its index satisfies $\alpha\in[0,2)$. Write $Z^\pm=Z^\pm_{\mathcal T}$ and $F(r)=\int_0^r\sqrt{\psi(u)}\,du$ for $r\in[0,1]$.

**Erratum 1: the bound cannot be uniform in $n$.** Let $P$ be uniform on $[0,1]$, and let $\mathcal{T}$ index the intervals $[a,a+\sigma^2)$ with $a\in\mathbb{Q}$. This class is countable, $P(A_s)\le\sigma^2$, and it is contained in the class of intervals, which has VC dimension at most $2$. The right side of Lemma 13.6 increases in $V$ for $\delta\le e$, so the lemma gives $H(\delta,\mathcal{T})\le\psi(\delta):=4\log(e/\delta)+\log(3e)$. This $\psi$ is nonincreasing, $\psi\ge\psi(1)\approx6.10\ge1$, and $\psi(1/x)=4\log(ex)+\log(3e)$ is regularly varying of index $0<2$. Every sample has some rational $a\in(X_1-\sigma^2,X_1]$, and for it $\sum_i\mathbb 1\{X_i\in[a,a+\sigma^2)\}\ge1$ while $nP(A_s)\le n\sigma^2$. Hence
$$Z^+\ge\frac{1-n\sigma^2}{\sqrt n}\quad\text{always}.$$
Take $n=1$. Then $\mathbb{E}Z^+\ge1-\sigma^2$, whereas $\sigma\sqrt{\psi(\sigma/2)}$ equals $0.0522$ at $\sigma=10^{-2}$, $6.76\times10^{-4}$ at $\sigma=10^{-4}$, and tends to $0$ as $\sigma\to0$. So no constant $K$ works. (Here $n\sigma^2\ge\psi(\sigma/2)$ fails, which is consistent with the corrected statement below.)

**Erratum 2: the hint's first display is false as printed.** Take a single set $A$ with $P(A)=\sigma^2$, and take $\psi\equiv1$. Then $H\equiv0\le\psi$, and $\psi(1/x)$ is regularly varying of index $0$. The display reads $\mathbb{E}Z^+\le K\sqrt{\sigma^2+\mathbb{E}Z^+/\sqrt n}\cdot\sigma/2$, where $\mathbb{E}Z^+=\mathbb{E}(B-n\sigma^2)_+/\sqrt n$ and $B\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,\sigma^2)$. For $n=10^6$ (exact binomial computation):
- at $\sigma=0.1$, $\mathbb{E}Z^+=0.0397$ while the right side is $0.00501\,K$, a ratio of about $7.9$ when $K=1$;
- at $\sigma=0.03$, the values are $0.0120$ against $0.000453\,K$;
- at $\sigma=0.01$, they are $0.00399$ against $0.000051\,K$.

By the CLT (with uniform integrability) the ratio tends to $2\sqrt{1-\sigma^2}/(\sqrt{2\pi}K\sigma)$ as $n\to\infty$, and this is unbounded as $\sigma\to0$. So the display fails for every fixed $K$. The correct form, proved in Step 1, is $\mathbb{E}Z^\pm\le C\,F\big(\sqrt{\sigma^2+\mathbb{E}Z^+/\sqrt n}\big)$. In addition, the hint's "$\lim_{\sigma\to0}\int_0^{\sigma/2}\sqrt\psi=\dots$" should be a limit of the ratio of the two sides (Step 2).

**Corrected statement.** Assume in addition $\psi\ge1$ on $(0,1]$. Then there is $K$ depending only on $\psi$ such that for all $n\ge1$ and $\sigma\in(0,1)$,
$$\max(\mathbb{E}Z^-,\mathbb{E}Z^+)\le K\Big(\sigma\sqrt{\psi(\sigma/2)}+\frac{\psi(\sigma/2)}{\sqrt n}\Big),$$
and therefore
$$\max(\mathbb{E}Z^-,\mathbb{E}Z^+)\le2K\sigma\sqrt{\psi(\sigma/2)}\qquad\text{whenever }n\sigma^2\ge\psi(\sigma/2).$$
*On the assumption $\psi\ge1$.* Replacing $\psi$ by $\psi_1=\psi\vee1$ keeps every hypothesis. It is still nonincreasing and still dominates $H$. If $\psi(1/x)\to\infty$, then $\psi_1(1/x)=\psi(1/x)$ for large $x$. Otherwise $\psi(1/x)$ increases to a finite limit $L>0$, so $\alpha=0$ and $\psi_1(1/x)\to L\vee1$, which also has index $0$. If moreover $\psi(1/2)>0$, then $\psi_1(\sigma/2)\le\max(1,1/\psi(1/2))\,\psi(\sigma/2)$ for $\sigma<1$, so the bound transfers to $\psi$ with a larger $K$. When $\mathcal{T}$ contains two distinct sets, $\psi(\sigma/2)\ge H(\sigma/2,\mathcal{T})\ge\log2$, because the two sets are at $d_{\delta_x}$-distance $1$ for a point $x$ of their symmetric difference. Some positivity is genuinely needed. Take a single set with $P(A)=0.64$, $\psi(\delta)=(\log\frac{1}{4\delta})_+$, $\sigma=0.8$ and $n=1$. Then $\psi(\sigma/2)=0$, yet $\mathbb{E}Z^+=0.64\cdot0.36=0.2304>0$.

*Proof of the corrected statement.* Assume $\psi\ge1$.

*Step 0: finiteness of $F$ and the Karamata estimate.* Substituting $u=1/x$ gives $F(\rho)=\int_{1/\rho}^\infty g(x)\,dx$ with $g(x)=\sqrt{\psi(1/x)}\,x^{-2}$. For $t>0$,
$$\frac{g(tx)}{g(x)}=\sqrt{\frac{\psi(1/(tx))}{\psi(1/x)}}\;t^{-2}\to t^{\alpha/2-2}\quad(x\to\infty),$$
so $g$ is regularly varying of index $\beta=\alpha/2-2<-1$. It is also locally bounded on $[1,\infty)$, because $\psi(1/x)$ is monotone and finite there. Karamata's theorem (Bingham–Goldie–Teugels, Prop. 1.5.10) says that $\int_y^\infty g$ converges and $\int_y^\infty g\sim y\,g(y)/(-\beta-1)$ as $y\to\infty$. With $y=1/\rho$ this gives $F(1)<\infty$ and
$$\lim_{\rho\to0^+}\frac{F(\rho)}{\rho\sqrt{\psi(\rho)}}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha/2},$$
which is the hint's second display, read as a ratio. So $F(\rho)\le K_1\rho\sqrt{\psi(\rho)}$ on $(0,\rho_0]$ for some $\rho_0>0$. On $[\rho_0,1]$ we have $F(\rho)\le F(1)$ and $\rho\sqrt{\psi(\rho)}\ge\rho_0$. Hence, enlarging $K_1$ (and taking $K_1\ge1$),
$$F(\rho)\le K_1\,\rho\sqrt{\psi(\rho)}\qquad(0<\rho\le1).\tag{0}$$
Since $F'=\sqrt\psi$ is nonincreasing, $F$ is concave with $F(0)=0$. So $F(\rho)/\rho$ is nonincreasing, and $F$ is nondecreasing.

*Step 1: symmetrization and conditional chaining.* First, one-sided symmetrization. Let $(X_i')$ be an independent copy of the sample and $(\varepsilon_i)$ independent Rademacher signs. For bounded $g_s$ indexed by the countable set $\mathcal T$, Jensen's inequality (conditionally on $X$) gives $\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_i(g_s(X_i)-Pg_s)\le\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_i(g_s(X_i)-g_s(X_i'))$. The summands are symmetric, so the right side equals $\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_i\varepsilon_i(g_s(X_i)-g_s(X_i'))$. Splitting the supremum and using that $-\varepsilon$ has the law of $\varepsilon$, this is at most $2\,\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_i\varepsilon_ig_s(X_i)$. (This is the last inequality of Lemma 11.4, used in the same form in the proof of Lemma 13.5.) Applied to $g_s=\pm\mathbb 1_{A_s}$, and again using that $-\varepsilon$ has the law of $\varepsilon$,
$$\mathbb{E}Z^\pm\le2\,\mathbb{E}\sup_s Y_s,\qquad Y_s=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_i\varepsilon_i\mathbb 1_{A_s}(X_i).$$
Next, chaining given $X$. Adjoin an index $s_0$ with $A_{s_0}=\emptyset$, so that $Y_{s_0}=0$. For any indices $s,t$, Hoeffding's lemma gives $\log\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon e^{\lambda(Y_s-Y_t)}\le\lambda^2d_n(s,t)^2/2$ with $d_n=d_{P_n}$. Also $d_n(s,s_0)=P_n(A_s)^{1/2}\le\delta_n$, where $\delta_n^2:=\sup_sP_n(A_s)\le1$. For a finite $T'\subset\mathcal T$, the packing numbers of $T'\cup\{s_0\}$ in $d_n$ are at most $N(u,\mathcal T,P_n)+1\le e^{\psi(u)}+1\le e^{2\psi(u)}$ (since $\psi\ge1$). Corollary 13.2 therefore gives
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\max_{s\in T'}Y_s\le\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\max_{s\in T'\cup\{s_0\}}(Y_s-Y_{s_0})\le12\int_0^{\delta_n/2}\sqrt{2\psi(u)}\,du\le12\sqrt2\,F(\delta_n).$$
Letting $T'\uparrow\mathcal T$ (monotone convergence; $|Y_s|\le\sqrt n$) yields $\mathbb{E}Z^\pm\le24\sqrt2\,\mathbb{E}F(\delta_n)$.

Then Jensen. The function $G(t)=F(\sqrt t)=\int_0^t\frac{\sqrt{\psi(\sqrt v)}}{2\sqrt v}\,dv$ is the integral of a nonincreasing function, so it is concave on $[0,1]$. Hence $\mathbb{E}F(\delta_n)=\mathbb{E}G(\delta_n^2)\le F(\rho)$ with $\rho^2:=\mathbb{E}\delta_n^2\le1$. Since $P(A_s)\le\sigma^2$, we have $P_n(A_s)\le\sigma^2+n^{-1/2}\cdot n^{-1/2}\sum_i(\mathbb 1_{A_s}(X_i)-P(A_s))$, so
$$\rho^2\le r^2:=\sigma^2+\frac{\mathbb{E}Z^+}{\sqrt n}.$$
Let $\bar r=\max(\sigma,\rho)\in[\sigma,1]$. By monotonicity of $F$ and of $F(\rho)/\rho$, followed by (0) and the monotonicity of $\psi$,
$$\mathbb{E}Z^\pm\le24\sqrt2\,F(\bar r)\le24\sqrt2\,\frac{\bar r}{\sigma}F(\sigma)\le A\,r,\qquad A:=24\sqrt2\,K_1\sqrt{\psi(\sigma/2)}.\tag{1}$$
(This is the corrected first display of the hint, $\mathbb{E}Z^\pm\le24\sqrt2\,F(r)$, combined with $F(r)\le(r/\sigma)F(\sigma)$.)

*Step 2: the fixed point.* Let $x=\mathbb{E}Z^+$, which is finite because $Z^+\le\sqrt n$. Since $r\le\sigma+(x/\sqrt n)^{1/2}$, (1) gives $y^2\le by+c$ with $y=\sqrt x$, $b=An^{-1/4}$ and $c=A\sigma$. Hence $y\le\frac{b+\sqrt{b^2+4c}}{2}$ and
$$x\le\frac{b^2+(b^2+4c)}{2}=2A\sigma+\frac{A^2}{\sqrt n}.$$
Next, $\mathbb{E}Z^-\le A\sigma+An^{-1/4}\sqrt x$. Here $\sqrt x\le\sqrt{2A\sigma}+An^{-1/4}$, and $An^{-1/4}\sqrt{2A\sigma}=\sqrt{2\cdot A\sigma\cdot A^2/\sqrt n}\le A\sigma+A^2/\sqrt n$. So
$$\max(\mathbb{E}Z^+,\mathbb{E}Z^-)\le2A\sigma+\frac{2A^2}{\sqrt n}=48\sqrt2K_1\,\sigma\sqrt{\psi(\sigma/2)}+2304K_1^2\,\frac{\psi(\sigma/2)}{\sqrt n}.$$
This is the general bound with $K=2304K_1^2$. If $n\sigma^2\ge\psi(\sigma/2)$, then $\psi(\sigma/2)/\sqrt n=\sqrt{\psi(\sigma/2)}\cdot\sqrt{\psi(\sigma/2)/n}\le\sigma\sqrt{\psi(\sigma/2)}$, and the bound becomes $\max(\mathbb{E}Z^-,\mathbb{E}Z^+)\le2K\sigma\sqrt{\psi(\sigma/2)}$. For VC classes, $\psi(\delta)=2V\log(e/\delta)+\log(e(V+1))$ by Lemma 13.6, which recovers the form of Theorem 13.7. ∎
