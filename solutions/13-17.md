**Central idea.** Symmetrize and chain conditionally on the sample, adjoining the zero function. The Dudley integral is then the perspective $\Phi(a,c)=aH(c/a)$ of a concave entropy integral $H$, evaluated at $a=\|g\|_{L_2(P_n)}$ and $c=\delta_n=\sup_s\|f_s\|_{L_2(P_n)}$. $\Phi(\sqrt s,\sqrt t)$ is jointly concave and monotone, so Jensen's inequality replaces both random quantities by $\|g\|_{L_2(P)}$ and $\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\delta_n^2}$ at no cost. Contraction bounds $\mathbb{E}\delta_n^2$ by $\sigma^2+O(b(\sigma+\mathbb{E}Z+\mathbb{E}Z')/\sqrt n)$. Karamata's theorem gives $H(y)\lesssim y\sqrt{\psi(\kappa/y)}$. Solving the resulting quadratic fixed-point inequality yields $\sigma\sqrt{\psi}+b\psi/\sqrt n$. As printed, the exercise has a garbled entropy hypothesis and a proviso without $n$; both are corrected below.

**Solution.**

**Erratum (statement as printed).**

*(i) The entropy hypothesis is garbled.* As printed, $\psi$ is nonincreasing, so $\psi(\kappa\|g\|_{L_2(Q)}/\delta)\le\psi(1)$ whenever $\delta\le\kappa\|g\|_{L_2(Q)}$. The hypothesis would then force $\log N(\delta,\mathcal T,L_2(Q))\le\psi(1)$ for all small $\delta$, i.e. every class would be finite in each $L_2(Q)$. The regular-variation assumption on $\psi(1/x)$ would only constrain $\psi$ near $0$, where it plays no role. VC classes, with $\log N\asymp V\log(1/\delta)$, would be excluded, although the exercise is meant to generalize Theorem 13.7. The consistent reading is the one of Exercise 13.15 with the envelope scaling: **$\psi:(0,\infty)\to[1,\infty)$ is nondecreasing and regularly varying at $\infty$ with index $\alpha<2$.** Equivalently, $u\mapsto\psi(1/u)$ is nonincreasing and satisfies the hypothesis of Exercise 13.15. With $\psi\ge1$, this is a harmless normalization (replace $\psi$ by $\psi\vee1$). The entropy hypothesis is then $\log N(\delta,\mathcal T,L_2(Q))\le\psi(\kappa\|g\|_{L_2(Q)}/\delta)$, as printed.

*(ii) The proviso must involve $n$.* Even under the corrected reading, "$\sigma\ge\sqrt{\psi(\kappa\|g\|_{L_2(P)}/\sigma)}$" does not suffice; compare Theorem 13.7, whose proviso $\sigma\ge24\sqrt{V\log(4e^2/\sigma)/(5n)}$ contains $n$. Counterexample: take $P$ uniform on $[0,1]$, $b\ge1$, $p\in(0,1)$, and the countable class $f_a=b\,\mathbb 1_{[a,a+p]}$, $a\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1-p]$, with envelope $g\equiv b$.

- *Entropy.* For any probability $Q$ and $\eta\in(0,1]$, let $\nu(B)=Q(B)+Q(B+p)$, a measure of total mass $2$. Choose cut points $0=a_0<a_1<\dots<a_m=1-p$ greedily, with $a_{j+1}=\min\{1-p,\sup\{t:\nu((a_j,t))\le\eta\}\}$. Every open cell then has $\nu((a_j,a_{j+1}))\le\eta$, and every cell but the last has $\nu((a_j,a_{j+1}])\ge\eta$, so $m\le2/\eta+1$. If $a<a'$ lie in the same open cell, then $[a,a+p]\,\triangle\,[a',a'+p]\subseteq[a,a')\cup(a+p,a'+p]$, which has $Q$-mass at most $\nu((a_j,a_{j+1}))\le\eta$. One rational center per open cell plus the cut points therefore gives $N(\sqrt\eta,\{\mathbb 1_{[a,a+p]}\},L_2(Q))\le2m+1\le4/\eta+3$. Since $\|b\mathbb 1_I-b\mathbb 1_J\|_{L_2(Q)}=b\sqrt{Q(I\triangle J)}$ and $\|g\|_{L_2(Q)}=b$, we get $N(\delta)\le4b^2/\delta^2+3\le7b^2/\delta^2$ for $\delta\le b$, and $N(\delta)=1$ for $\delta\ge b$. So the hypothesis holds with $\kappa=1$ and $\psi(x)=2+2\log(1+x)$, since $\log7<2$. This $\psi$ is nondecreasing, $\ge1$, and regularly varying with index $0$.
- *Choice of parameters.* Let $\sigma=b^{1/10}$ and $p=\sigma^2/b^2=b^{-9/5}$, so that $Pf_a^2=b^2p=\sigma^2$. The printed proviso reads $b^{1/5}\ge2+2\log(1+b^{9/10})$. It holds for all $b\ge4.31\times10^{7}$ (checked numerically up to $b=10^{20}$; for larger $b$ it follows because $b^{1/5}$ grows faster than $\log b$).
- *Failure of the bound.* Almost surely, some $a\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1-p]$ has $X_1\in[a,a+p]$. Hence $Z\ge\big(b-nbp\big)/\sqrt n=b(1-np)/\sqrt n$ a.s. The claimed bound is $\kappa'\sigma\sqrt{\psi(b/\sigma)}=\kappa' b^{1/10}\sqrt{2+2\log(1+b^{9/10})}$. For fixed $n$, the ratio of the two tends to $\infty$ as $b\to\infty$. For example, with $n=1$ and $b=10^{12}$: $\mathbb{E}Z\ge10^{12}(1-10^{-21.6})$, while $\sigma\sqrt{\psi(b/\sigma)}\approx114.0$, a ratio of about $8.8\times10^{9}$. So no constant $\kappa'$ works. (The corrected proviso below fails here, as it must.)

**Corrected statement.** Let $\psi$ be as in (i), and suppose $\log N(\delta,\mathcal T,L_2(Q))\le\psi(\kappa\|g\|_{L_2(Q)}/\delta)$ for every probability $Q$ with $\|g\|_{L_2(Q)}>0$ and every $\delta>0$. Let $Z=\sup_s n^{-1/2}\sum_i(f_s(X_i)-Pf_s)$ and $Z'=\sup_s n^{-1/2}\sum_i(Pf_s-f_s(X_i))$, as in Theorem 13.7. Write $G=\|g\|_{L_2(P)}$ and $L=\sqrt{\psi(\kappa G/\sigma)}$. There are constants $\kappa',\kappa''$ depending only on $\psi$ such that
$$\max(\mathbb{E}Z,\mathbb{E}Z')\le\kappa''\Big(\sigma L+\frac{bL^2}{\sqrt n}\Big)\quad\text{always, and}\quad\max(\mathbb{E}Z,\mathbb{E}Z')\le\kappa'\sigma\sqrt{\psi\Big(\frac{\kappa\|g\|_{L_2(P)}}{\sigma}\Big)}\quad\text{if}\quad\sqrt n\,\sigma\ge b\sqrt{\psi\Big(\frac{\kappa\|g\|_{L_2(P)}}{\sigma}\Big)}.$$
With $b=1$ and $g\equiv1$, this is the form of Exercises 13.15–13.16: the bound $\sigma\sqrt\psi+\psi/\sqrt n$, and the condition $n\sigma^2\ge\psi$.

**Proof.**

*Step 0: reductions and two constants.* By pointwise separability, there is a countable $\mathcal T_0\subseteq\mathcal T$ such that every $f_s$ is a pointwise limit of $f_{s_k}$ with $s_k\in\mathcal T_0$. Since $|f_s|\le b$, dominated convergence gives $Pf_{s_k}\to Pf_s$. So $Z$, $Z'$ and $\delta_n$ (below) are unchanged when $\mathcal T$ is replaced by $\mathcal T_0$; assume $\mathcal T$ countable. If $\sigma=0$ or $G=0$, then $f_s(X_i)=0$ a.s. for all $s,i$, and everything vanishes. So assume $\sigma>0$ and $G>0$. Note $0\le\mathbb{E}Z,\mathbb{E}Z'\le2b\sqrt n<\infty$.

Let $F(z)=\int_0^z\sqrt{2\psi(1/v)}\,dv=\int_{1/z}^\infty\ell(x)\,dx$, where $\ell(x)=\sqrt{2\psi(x)}\,x^{-2}$. The function $\ell$ is regularly varying at $\infty$ with index $\alpha/2-2<-1$. By Karamata's theorem (Bingham–Goldie–Teugels, Prop. 1.5.10), $F(z)<\infty$ and $F(z)\sim z\sqrt{2\psi(1/z)}/(1-\alpha/2)$ as $z\to0^+$. We claim that
$$K:=\sup_{z>0}\frac{F(z)}{z\sqrt{\psi(1/z)}}<\infty .$$
Choose $z_0\in(0,1]$ such that the ratio is at most $2\sqrt2/(1-\alpha/2)$ on $(0,z_0]$. On $[z_0,1]$, the ratio is at most $F(1)/z_0$, because $\psi\ge1$. For $z\ge1$, $F(z)\le F(1)+z\sqrt{2\psi(1)}$ and $z\sqrt{\psi(1/z)}\ge z\ge1$, so the ratio is at most $F(1)+\sqrt{2\psi(1)}$. We also need
$$c_\psi:=\sup_{x>0}\psi(2x)/\psi(x)<\infty .$$
Since $\psi(2x)/\psi(x)\to2^\alpha$, the ratio is at most $2^\alpha+1$ for $x\ge x_0$. For $x<x_0$, it is at most $\psi(2x_0)$, because $\psi\ge1$ and $\psi$ is nondecreasing. Both $K$ and $c_\psi$ depend only on $\psi$.

*Step 1: symmetrization.* Let $X'$ be an independent copy of the sample, and let $\varepsilon_i$ be independent Rademacher variables independent of everything else. By Jensen's inequality with respect to $X'$, and since swapping $X_i\leftrightarrow X_i'$ preserves the joint law,
$$\mathbb{E}Z\le\mathbb{E}\sup_s\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_i\big(f_s(X_i)-f_s(X_i')\big)=\mathbb{E}\sup_s\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_i\varepsilon_i\big(f_s(X_i)-f_s(X_i')\big)\le2R,$$
where $R=\mathbb{E}\sup_s n^{-1/2}\sum_i\varepsilon_if_s(X_i)$. By the symmetry of $\varepsilon$, also $\mathbb{E}Z'\le2R$.

*Step 2: chaining given the sample.* Fix $X$. Let $\|g\|_n=\|g\|_{L_2(P_n)}$ and $\delta_n=\sup_s\|f_s\|_{L_2(P_n)}\le\|g\|_n$. If $\|g\|_n=0$, the conditional Rademacher average is $0$; assume $\|g\|_n>0$. Let $\mathcal T_m$ be the first $m$ indices, and adjoin an index $0$ with $f_0\equiv0$. The Rademacher process $s\mapsto n^{-1/2}\sum_i\varepsilon_if_s(X_i)$ satisfies the condition of Corollary 13.2 with $d(s,t)=\|f_s-f_t\|_{L_2(P_n)}$, and $\sup_{t}d(t,0)\le\delta_n$. A $u/2$-cover of $\mathcal T$ yields a $u$-cover of $\mathcal T_m$ by points of $\mathcal T_m$. Adding $0$ and using $e^{\psi}+1\le e^{2\psi}$ for $\psi\ge1$,
$$\log N(u,\mathcal T_m\cup\{0\})\le\log\big(N(u/2,\mathcal T,L_2(P_n))+1\big)\le2\psi(2\kappa\|g\|_n/u).$$
Corollary 13.2 with $t_0=0$, followed by monotone convergence as $m\to\infty$, gives
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\sup_s\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_i\varepsilon_if_s(X_i)\le12\int_0^{\delta_n}\sqrt{2\psi\Big(\frac{2\kappa\|g\|_n}{u}\Big)}\,du=12\,\|g\|_n\,H\Big(\frac{\delta_n}{\|g\|_n}\Big)=:12\,\Phi(\|g\|_n,\delta_n),$$
where $H(y)=\int_0^y\sqrt{2\psi(2\kappa/w)}\,dw=2\kappa F\big(y/(2\kappa)\big)$, after substituting $u=\|g\|_nw$ and then $w=2\kappa v$. The integrand of $H$ is nonincreasing, because $\psi$ is nondecreasing. So $H$ is finite, nondecreasing and concave with $H(0)=0$, and $H(y)/y$ is nonincreasing. By the definition of $K$, with $z=y/(2\kappa)$,
$$\frac{H(y)}{y}=\frac{F(z)}{z}\le K\sqrt{\psi(2\kappa/y)}\qquad(y>0).\tag{1}$$

*Step 3: Jensen with a random envelope norm.* Put $\Phi(a,c)=aH(c/a)$ for $a>0$, $c\ge0$, and $\Phi(0,0)=0$, on the convex set $D=\{a>0,c\ge0\}\cup\{(0,0)\}$.

- *$\Phi$ is concave on $D$.* For $a=\lambda a_1+(1-\lambda)a_2>0$, concavity of $H$ gives
$$\Phi\big(a,\lambda c_1+(1-\lambda)c_2\big)=aH\Big(\tfrac{\lambda a_1}{a}\tfrac{c_1}{a_1}+\tfrac{(1-\lambda)a_2}{a}\tfrac{c_2}{a_2}\Big)\ge\lambda\Phi(a_1,c_1)+(1-\lambda)\Phi(a_2,c_2).$$
Mixtures with the origin are covered by $\Phi(\lambda a,\lambda c)=\lambda\Phi(a,c)$.
- *$\Phi$ is nondecreasing in each coordinate.* It is nondecreasing in $c$ because $H$ is. It is nondecreasing in $a$ because $\Phi=c\cdot H(y)/y$ with $y=c/a$ decreasing in $a$.
- *Hence $\Psi(s,t)=\Phi(\sqrt s,\sqrt t)$ is concave* on the convex set $\{s>0,t\ge0\}\cup\{(0,0)\}$, since $\sqrt\cdot$ is concave and $\Phi$ is monotone and concave.

The random point $W=(\|g\|_n^2,\delta_n^2)$ lies in this set, and $\mathbb{E}W=(G^2,\mathbb{E}\delta_n^2)$. If $\mathbb{E}\delta_n^2=0$, then $\delta_n=0$ a.s. and $\Phi(\|g\|_n,\delta_n)=0$. Otherwise $\mathbb{E}W$ is an interior point, where $\Psi$ has a supergradient, so Jensen's inequality applies:
$$\mathbb{E}\,\Phi(\|g\|_n,\delta_n)\le\Phi\Big(G,\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\delta_n^2}\Big).$$

*Step 4: bounding $\mathbb{E}\delta_n^2$.* Throughout Steps 4–6, adjoin the index $0$ with $f_0\equiv0$ to $\mathcal T$. This changes neither $\sigma$ nor $\delta_n$. It makes every supremum below nonnegative, as Theorem 11.6 requires (its convex function is defined on $[0,\infty)$; we use it with the identity). It replaces $Z,Z'$ by $Z_+=\max(Z,0)$ and $Z'_+=\max(Z',0)$. Steps 1–3 hold verbatim for the enlarged class, since Step 2 already chains over $\mathcal T_m\cup\{0\}$; hence $\max(\mathbb{E}Z_+,\mathbb{E}Z'_+)\le24\,\mathbb{E}\Phi(\|g\|_n,\delta_n)$. We have $\delta_n^2\le\sup_sPf_s^2+\sup_s(P_n-P)f_s^2\le\sigma^2+\sup_s(P_n-P)f_s^2$. The argument of Step 1, applied to $f_s^2$, gives $\mathbb{E}\sup_s(P_n-P)f_s^2\le\frac2n\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_i\varepsilon_if_s(X_i)^2$. The function $\varphi(x)=\min(|x|,b)^2/(2b)$ is $1$-Lipschitz with $\varphi(0)=0$ and equals $x^2/(2b)$ on $[-b,b]$. Theorem 11.6, applied conditionally on $X$ to finite subclasses and then in the limit, gives
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_i\varepsilon_if_s(X_i)^2\le2b\,\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_i\varepsilon_if_s(X_i).$$
Next, $\sum_i\varepsilon_if_s(X_i)=\sum_i\varepsilon_i(f_s(X_i)-Pf_s)+Pf_s\sum_i\varepsilon_i$, and $|Pf_s|\le\sigma$. Also $\mathbb{E}|\sum_i\varepsilon_i|\le\sqrt n$, and, as in Step 1 (Jensen in $X'$, then removing $\varepsilon$ by the swap),
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_i\varepsilon_i(f_s(X_i)-Pf_s)\le\mathbb{E}\sup_s\sum_i(f_s(X_i)-f_s(X_i'))\le\sqrt n\,(\mathbb{E}Z_++\mathbb{E}Z'_+).$$
With $S=\mathbb{E}Z_++\mathbb{E}Z'_+$, this gives
$$\mathbb{E}\delta_n^2\le r^2:=\sigma^2+\frac{4b\sigma}{\sqrt n}+\frac{4bS}{\sqrt n}.$$

*Step 5: regular variation.* Since $r\ge\sigma$, $H(y)/y$ is nonincreasing, and by (1),
$$\Phi(G,r)=r\,\frac{H(r/G)}{r/G}\le r\,\frac{H(\sigma/G)}{\sigma/G}\le rK\sqrt{\psi(2\kappa G/\sigma)}\le rK\sqrt{c_\psi}\,L .$$
Combining Steps 1–3 with the monotonicity of $\Phi$ in $c$, and taking expectations over $X$ in Step 2,
$$\max(\mathbb{E}Z,\mathbb{E}Z')\le\max(\mathbb{E}Z_+,\mathbb{E}Z'_+)\le24\,\mathbb{E}\Phi(\|g\|_n,\delta_n)\le24\,\Phi(G,r)\le Ar,\qquad A:=c_0L,\quad c_0:=24K\sqrt{c_\psi}.$$

*Step 6: fixed point.* Let $\beta=b/\sqrt n$. Then $r\le\sigma+2\sqrt{\beta\sigma}+2\sqrt{\beta S}\le2\sigma+\beta+2\sqrt{\beta S}$. From $S\le2Ar$,
$$S\le2A(2\sigma+\beta)+4A\sqrt\beta\,\sqrt S .$$
Since $x^2\le p+qx$ implies $x^2\le2p+q^2$, and $L\ge1$,
$$S\le8A\sigma+4A\beta+16A^2\beta\le8c_0\,\sigma L+(4c_0+16c_0^2)\frac{bL^2}{\sqrt n}.$$
This is the general bound, with $\kappa''=4c_0+16c_0^2$. Under the corrected proviso, $bL/\sqrt n\le\sigma$, so $bL^2/\sqrt n\le\sigma L$, and
$$\max(\mathbb{E}Z,\mathbb{E}Z')\le S\le(12c_0+16c_0^2)\,\sigma\sqrt{\psi\Big(\frac{\kappa\|g\|_{L_2(P)}}{\sigma}\Big)} .$$
So $\kappa'=12c_0+16c_0^2$, which depends only on $\psi$ through $K$ and $c_\psi$, and not on $\sigma$, $P$, $\mathcal T$, $n$, $b$ or $\kappa$.

**Remark.** With $g\equiv b$, this recovers Theorem 13.7 for VC classes, up to constants. When $\|g\|_{L_2(P)}\ll b$, the logarithmic factor improves to $\psi(\kappa\|g\|_{L_2(P)}/\sigma)$ (Giné–Koltchinskii 2006, Theorem 3.1). $\blacksquare$
