**Central idea.** Symmetrize to a Rademacher process, then condition on the $U_i$'s: only the $N\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,\sigma^2)$ points falling in $[0,\sigma^2]$ matter, and the supremum becomes $\max_{k\le N}S_k$ for a simple random walk. Lévy's maximal inequality (Lemma 11.12) plus Hoeffding bounds the tail of this maximum by $2e^{-t^2/(2N)}$ with no union bound over the $n$ order statistics — that is where the $\sqrt{\log n}$ factor is avoided — and integrating the tail gives $2\sqrt N$, whence Jensen over $N$ yields $4\sigma$.

**Solution.** The class $\{A_s=[0,s]:s\in[0,\sigma^2]\}$ has VC-dimension $1$: for $x_1<x_2$ the traces are $\emptyset$, $\{x_1\}$, $\{x_1,x_2\}$ (the set $\{x_2\}$ never occurs), so no two-point set is shattered, while one point is. Put $f_s(u)=\mathbb{1}_{\{u\le s\}}$ so that $X_{i,s}=f_s(U_i)-\mathbb{E}f_s(U_i)$ with the $U_i$ i.i.d. uniform on $[0,1]$; the $f_s$ are centered in the sense of Theorem 11.4. By the symmetrization inequality (Theorem 11.4), with i.i.d. Rademacher $\varepsilon_i$ independent of the $U_i$,
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in[0,\sigma^2]\cap\mathbb{Q}}\sum_{i=1}^n X_{i,s}\le 2\,\mathbb{E}_{U,\varepsilon}\sup_{s\in[0,\sigma^2]\cap\mathbb{Q}}\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i f_s(U_i),$$
the restriction to rational $s$ being immaterial by right-continuity of $s\mapsto\sum_i\varepsilon_i f_s(U_i)$.

Condition on $U_1,\dots,U_n$ and let $N=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{1}_{\{U_i\le\sigma^2\}}$, so $N\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,\sigma^2)$. Order the $U_i\le\sigma^2$ as $V_1\le\cdots\le V_N$ with paired Rademachers $\eta_1,\dots,\eta_N$ (still i.i.d. Rademacher given $U$). For $s\in[0,\sigma^2]$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i f_s(U_i)=\sum_{j:V_j\le s}\eta_j,$$
a step function whose supremum over $s$ equals $\max_{0\le k\le N}S_k$ with $S_k=\sum_{j=1}^k\eta_j$, $S_0=0$.

Fix $U$ (hence $N$). Lévy's maximal inequality (Lemma 11.12) applied to the symmetric variables $\eta_j$ gives, for $t\ge0$,
$$\mathbb{P}_\varepsilon\Big\{\max_{k\le N}S_k\ge t\Big\}\le 2\,\mathbb{P}_\varepsilon\{S_N\ge t\}\le 2e^{-t^2/(2N)},$$
the last step by Hoeffding's inequality (for $N=0$ both sides are $0$). Hence, with $M_N=\max_{0\le k\le N}S_k\ge0$,
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon[M_N^2]=\int_0^\infty 2t\,\mathbb{P}_\varepsilon\{M_N\ge t\}\,dt\le\int_0^\infty 4t e^{-t^2/(2N)}\,dt=4N,$$
so $\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon M_N\le\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon M_N^2}\le 2\sqrt N$. Taking expectation over $U$ and using Jensen,
$$\mathbb{E}_U[2\sqrt N]\le 2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}N}=2\sqrt{n\sigma^2}=2\sigma\sqrt n.$$
Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}\left[\frac1{\sqrt n}\sup_{s\in[0,\sigma^2]}\sum_{i=1}^n X_{i,s}\right]\le\frac{2}{\sqrt n}\cdot 2\sigma\sqrt n=4\sigma,$$
as claimed. The logarithmic factor of Theorem 13.7 never appears because Lévy's inequality controls the maximum of the partial sums directly, without a union bound over the $n$ jump points.
