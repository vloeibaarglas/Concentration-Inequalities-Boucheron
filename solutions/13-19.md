**Central idea.** Symmetrize, then fix the sample and expand in an $L_2(P)$-orthonormal basis of $\mathcal{T}$: the supremum over the $L_2$-ball of radius $R$ becomes $R$ times the Euclidean norm of the $d$-vector $(\sum_i\varepsilon_i e_j(X_i))_j$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. Jensen (twice) and orthonormality then give $R\sqrt{nd}$.

**Solution.** Since $\mathcal{T}$ consists of centered functions, $\mathbb{E}f(X_i)=0$ for $f\in\mathcal{T}$, and the symmetrization inequality (Theorem 11.4) gives
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{\substack{f\in\mathcal{T}\\\mathbb{E}f(X)^2\le R^2}}\sum_{i=1}^n f(X_i)
=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\substack{f\in\mathcal{T}\\\|f\|_{L_2(P)}\le R}}\sum_{i=1}^n\big(f(X_i)-\mathbb{E}f\big)
\le 2\,\mathbb{E}_{X,\varepsilon}\sup_{\substack{f\in\mathcal{T}\\\|f\|_{L_2(P)}\le R}}\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i f(X_i),$$
with i.i.d. Rademacher $\varepsilon_i$ independent of the $X_i$.

Fix $X_1,\dots,X_n$ and let $e_1,\dots,e_d$ be an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal{T}$ in $L_2(P)$. Every $f\in\mathcal{T}$ with $\|f\|_{L_2(P)}\le R$ writes $f=\sum_{j=1}^d\theta_j e_j$ with $\|\theta\|_2\le R$, and
$$\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i f(X_i)=\sum_{j=1}^d\theta_j\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i e_j(X_i)=\langle\theta,v\rangle,\qquad v_j:=\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i e_j(X_i).$$
Maximizing over $\|\theta\|_2\le R$ (Cauchy–Schwarz) yields
$$\sup_{\substack{f\in\mathcal{T}\\\|f\|_{L_2(P)}\le R}}\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i f(X_i)=R\|v\|_2=R\left(\sum_{j=1}^d\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i e_j(X_i)\Big)^2\right)^{1/2}.$$
Taking $\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon$ and applying Jensen,
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\sup_f\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i f(X_i)\le R\left(\sum_{j=1}^d\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i e_j(X_i)\Big)^2\right)^{1/2}=R\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^d e_j(X_i)^2\right)^{1/2},$$
since $\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon(\sum_i\varepsilon_i a_i)^2=\sum_i a_i^2$. Finally, Jensen over $X$ and orthonormality $\mathbb{E}e_j(X)^2=1$ give
$$\mathbb{E}_X\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^d e_j(X_i)^2\right)^{1/2}\le\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^d\mathbb{E}e_j(X_i)^2\right)^{1/2}=\sqrt{nd}.$$
Combining the steps,
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{\substack{f\in\mathcal{T}\\\mathbb{E}[f(X)^2]\le R^2}}\sum_{i=1}^n f(X_i)\le 2R\sqrt{nd},$$
as required. The same symmetrization-plus-basis argument is the content of Exercise 13.5.
