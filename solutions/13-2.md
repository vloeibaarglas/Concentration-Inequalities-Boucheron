**Central idea.** Condition on the $X_{i,s}$: the $2|\mathcal{T}|$ variables $\pm\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}$ are sub-Gaussian, so Theorem 2.5 bounds the Rademacher supremum by $\sqrt{2\max_s\sum_iX_{i,s}^2\cdot\log(2|\mathcal{T}|)}$. The random variance proxy $W=\max_s\sum_iX_{i,s}^2$ is controlled via symmetrization and the absolute-value form of the contraction principle (Theorem 11.6 with the $1$-Lipschitz map $\varphi(x)=x^2/2$ on $[-1,1]$), which feeds $W$ back into the original quantity and yields a quadratic inequality for $R=\mathbb{E}\sup_s|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}|$ solving to the claimed bound with $\kappa=34$. (See Koltchinskii, 2008.)

**Remark (repair).** An earlier version bounded $\mathbb{E}\max_s|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}^2|$ by $2R$ by applying the one-sided contraction principle on $\mathcal{T}\times\{-1,1\}$ to the vectors $\tau\varphi(X_{i,s})$. That step is unjustified: $\tau\varphi(X_{i,s})$ is not $\varphi$ applied to the coordinates $\tau X_{i,s}$ (here $\varphi$ is even), so Theorem 11.6 does not apply in that form. The proof below uses the absolute-value statement of Theorem 11.6 instead; this costs a factor $2$ and changes only the value of $\kappa$.

**Solution.** Assume $|\mathcal{T}|\geq2$: for $|\mathcal{T}|=1$ the right-hand side is $0$, so the statement implicitly requires $|\mathcal{T}|\ge2$. We take $\mathcal{T}$ finite, as $\log|\mathcal{T}|$ in the statement presupposes. Let
$$R=\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}\Big|,\qquad W=\max_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^nX_{i,s}^2,\qquad L=\log(2|\mathcal{T}|),$$
expectations being over both $\varepsilon$ and $X$ (which are independent).

*Step 1 (conditional sub-Gaussian bound).* Fix the $X_{i,s}$. For $\tau\in\{-1,1\}$, $Z_{s,\tau}=\tau\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon e^{\lambda Z_{s,\tau}}=\prod_i\cosh(\lambda X_{i,s})\leq\exp(\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\sum_iX_{i,s}^2)\le\exp(\frac{\lambda^2}{2}W)$, using $\cosh y\le e^{y^2/2}$. So the $2|\mathcal{T}|$ variables $Z_{s,\tau}$ are sub-Gaussian with variance factor $W$, and Theorem 2.5 gives
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\sup_{s}\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}\Big|=\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\max_{s,\tau}Z_{s,\tau}\leq\sqrt{2WL}.$$
Taking $\mathbb{E}_X$ and using Jensen's inequality ($\sqrt{\cdot}$ is concave), $R\leq\sqrt{2L\,\mathbb{E}W}$.

*Step 2 (controlling $\mathbb{E}W$).* Since $\sup_s\sum_i\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}^2\leq n\sigma^2$,
$$\mathbb{E}W\leq n\sigma^2+\mathbb{E}\max_{s}\sum_i\big(X_{i,s}^2-\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}^2\big).$$
Symmetrize as in the proof of Lemma 11.4: let $X'$ be an independent copy of $X$ and $\mathbb{E}'$ the expectation in $X'$. By Jensen's inequality (a maximum of linear functions is convex),
$$\mathbb{E}\max_{s}\sum_i\big(X_{i,s}^2-\mathbb{E}'X_{i,s}'^2\big)\le\mathbb{E}\max_{s}\sum_i\big(X_{i,s}^2-X_{i,s}'^2\big)=\mathbb{E}\max_{s}\sum_i\varepsilon_i\big(X_{i,s}^2-X_{i,s}'^2\big),$$
the equality because the vectors $(X_{i,s}^2-X_{i,s}'^2)_{s}$, $i\le n$, are independent and symmetric. Splitting the maximum and using that $-\varepsilon$ has the law of $\varepsilon$,
$$\mathbb{E}\max_{s}\sum_i\big(X_{i,s}^2-\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}^2\big)\le2\,\mathbb{E}\max_{s}\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}^2\le2\,\mathbb{E}\max_{s}\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}^2\Big|.$$
Let $\varphi(y)=y^2/2$ for $|y|\le1$ and $\varphi(y)=|y|-\tfrac12$ for $|y|>1$. Then $\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is $1$-Lipschitz ($|\varphi'|\le1$ everywhere and $\varphi$ is $C^1$), $\varphi(0)=0$, and $\varphi(X_{i,s})=X_{i,s}^2/2$ because $|X_{i,s}|\le1$. Condition on $X$ and apply the second (absolute-value) inequality of Theorem 11.6 to the vectors $x_i=(X_{i,s})_{s\in\mathcal{T}}$ with $\varphi_i=\varphi$ and $\Psi(t)=t$ (nondecreasing and convex on $[0,\infty)$):
$$\frac12\,\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\max_{s}\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_i\frac{X_{i,s}^2}{2}\Big|\le\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\max_s\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}\Big|.$$
Multiplying by $4$ and taking $\mathbb{E}_X$ gives $\mathbb{E}\max_s|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}^2|\le4R$. Hence
$$\mathbb{E}W\leq n\sigma^2+8R.$$

*Step 3 (quadratic inequality).* Combining Steps 1 and 2, $R^2\le2L(n\sigma^2+8R)$, i.e. $R^2-16LR-2Ln\sigma^2\le0$, so
$$R\leq8L+\sqrt{64L^2+2Ln\sigma^2}\leq16L+\sigma\sqrt{2nL},$$
using $\sqrt{a+b}\le\sqrt a+\sqrt b$. For $|\mathcal{T}|\geq2$ we have $\log2\le\log|\mathcal{T}|$, so $L=\log 2+\log|\mathcal{T}|\leq2\log|\mathcal{T}|$. Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_iX_{i,s}\Big|\le32\log|\mathcal{T}|+2\sigma\sqrt{n\log|\mathcal{T}|}\le34\max\Big(\sigma\sqrt{n\log|\mathcal{T}|},\ \log|\mathcal{T}|\Big),$$
which is the claim with the universal constant $\kappa=34$. (The argument never uses $\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}=0$: the symmetrization in Step 2 is applied to the uncentered vectors $(X_{i,s}^2)_s$ via an independent copy.) ∎
