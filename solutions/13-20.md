**Central idea.** Since the $g_j$ are centered, symmetrization (Lemma 11.4) reduces the problem to a Rademacher process $\sup_f \sum_i \varepsilon_i f(X_i)$; writing $f = \sum_j \alpha_j g_j$ turns this into a linear program over the coefficient set $A = \{\sum_j \alpha_j^2/\lambda_j \le 1,\ \sum_j \alpha_j^2 \le R^2\}$. The key observation is that the intersection of the two ellipsoids sits inside a single ellipsoid with effective axes $\mu_j = \lambda_j \wedge R^2$ (up to a factor $2$), since $\alpha_j^2/\mu_j \le \alpha_j^2/\lambda_j + \alpha_j^2/R^2$. Cauchy–Schwarz then bounds the supremum by $\sqrt{2\sum_j \mu_j S_j^2}$, and Jensen in $\varepsilon$ and in $X$ (using orthonormality $\mathbb{E}g_j^2 = 1$) closes the estimate — exactly the mechanism of Exercise 13.5.

**Solution.** Let $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{E}_1(1) \cap \mathcal{E}_2(R)$. Each $f \in \mathcal{F}$ is a linear combination of the centered functions $g_j$, hence centered: $\mathbb{E}f(X) = 0$. The class $\mathcal{F}$ is symmetric ($f \in \mathcal{F}$ implies $-f \in \mathcal{F}$), so $\sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \sum_i f(X_i) = \sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} |\sum_i f(X_i)|$. By the symmetrization lemma (Lemma 11.4), with i.i.d. Rademachers $\varepsilon_1, \ldots, \varepsilon_n$ independent of the $X_i$,
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \sum_{i=1}^n f(X_i) \le 2\,\mathbb{E}_{X,\varepsilon}\sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i f(X_i).$$
Write $f = \sum_{j=1}^d \alpha_j g_j$ and set $S_j = \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i g_j(X_i)$. Then
$$\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i f(X_i) = \sum_{j=1}^d \alpha_j S_j,$$
and we must bound $\sup_{\alpha \in A} \sum_j \alpha_j S_j$ where
$$A = \left\{\alpha : \sum_{j=1}^d \frac{\alpha_j^2}{\lambda_j} \le 1,\ \sum_{j=1}^d \alpha_j^2 \le R^2\right\}.$$
Put $\mu_j = \lambda_j \wedge R^2$. Since $\frac{1}{\mu_j} = \max\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_j}, \frac{1}{R^2}\right) \le \frac{1}{\lambda_j} + \frac{1}{R^2}$, for $\alpha \in A$,
$$\sum_{j=1}^d \frac{\alpha_j^2}{\mu_j} \le \sum_{j=1}^d \frac{\alpha_j^2}{\lambda_j} + \frac{1}{R^2}\sum_{j=1}^d \alpha_j^2 \le 1 + 1 = 2.$$
Hence $A \subseteq \{\alpha : \sum_j \alpha_j^2/\mu_j \le 2\}$, and by Cauchy–Schwarz, with $\beta_j = \alpha_j/\sqrt{\mu_j}$,
$$\sup_{\alpha \in A} \sum_{j=1}^d \alpha_j S_j \le \sup_{\|\beta\|_2 \le \sqrt{2}} \sum_{j=1}^d \beta_j \sqrt{\mu_j}\, S_j \le \sqrt{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^d \mu_j S_j^2\right)^{1/2}.$$
Fix the $X_i$ and take expectation over $\varepsilon$. By Jensen's inequality,
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon \sup_{\alpha \in A} \sum_j \alpha_j S_j \le \sqrt{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^d \mu_j\, \mathbb{E}_\varepsilon S_j^2\right)^{1/2} = \sqrt{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^d \mu_j \sum_{i=1}^n g_j(X_i)^2\right)^{1/2},$$
using $\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon S_j^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n g_j(X_i)^2$. Taking expectation over $X$ and applying Jensen again,
$$\mathbb{E}_X\left(\sum_{j=1}^d \mu_j \sum_{i=1}^n g_j(X_i)^2\right)^{1/2} \le \left(\sum_{j=1}^d \mu_j \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}g_j(X_i)^2\right)^{1/2} = \sqrt{n\sum_{j=1}^d \mu_j},$$
since $\mathbb{E}g_j(X)^2 = 1$ by orthonormality. Combining with the symmetrization factor $2$,
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{f \in \mathcal{E}_1(1) \cap \mathcal{E}_2(R)} \sum_{i=1}^n f(X_i) \le 2\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{n\sum_{j=1}^d (\lambda_j \wedge R^2)} \le 4\sqrt{n\sum_{j=1}^d \lambda_j \wedge R^2},$$
as claimed.
