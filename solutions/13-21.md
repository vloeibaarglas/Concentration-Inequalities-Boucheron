**Central idea.** The domination is pointwise, not probabilistic: for every realization of the $X_i$ and $\varepsilon_i$, the triangle inequality gives $|\sum_i \varepsilon_i g(X_i)| \le \sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} |\sum_i \varepsilon_i f(X_i)|$ for each $g \in \overline{\mathrm{sconv}}(\mathcal{F})$, because the weights satisfy $\sum_j |\lambda_j| \le 1$. Hence the inequality holds with $\kappa = 1$ (no chaining or VC theory needed); we assume the suprema are measurable, e.g. $\mathcal{F}$ countable, or interpret $\mathbb{E}$ as outer expectation.

**Solution.** Fix a realization of $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ and $\varepsilon_1, \ldots, \varepsilon_n$. Let $g \in \overline{\mathrm{sconv}}(\mathcal{F})$, so $g = \sum_{j=1}^k \lambda_j f_j$ for some $k \in \mathbb{N}$, $f_j \in \mathcal{F}$, and scalars with $\sum_{j=1}^k |\lambda_j| \le 1$. Then
\begin{align*}
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i g(X_i)\right|
&= \left|\sum_{j=1}^k \lambda_j \sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i f_j(X_i)\right| \\
&\le \sum_{j=1}^k |\lambda_j| \left|\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i f_j(X_i)\right| \\
&\le \left(\sum_{j=1}^k |\lambda_j|\right) \sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \left|\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i f(X_i)\right| \\
&\le \sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \left|\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i f(X_i)\right|.
\end{align*}
Since $g \in \overline{\mathrm{sconv}}(\mathcal{F})$ was arbitrary, taking the supremum over $g$ on the left yields the pointwise inequality
$$\sup_{g \in \overline{\mathrm{sconv}}(\mathcal{F})} \left|\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i g(X_i)\right| \le \sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \left|\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i f(X_i)\right|,$$
valid for every realization. Taking expectations (assuming the suprema are measurable, or using outer expectations, under which the pointwise domination is preserved) gives
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{g \in \overline{\mathrm{sconv}}(\mathcal{F})} \left|\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i g(X_i)\right| \le \mathbb{E}\sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \left|\sum_{i=1}^n \varepsilon_i f(X_i)\right|,$$
i.e. the claimed bound with the universal constant $\kappa = 1$. ∎
