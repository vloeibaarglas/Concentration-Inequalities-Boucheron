**Central idea.** The Banach–Mazur distance measures how far $\mathbb{B}$ is from a Hilbert space $\mathbb{H}$; pushing the sum through a near-optimal isomorphism $T:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{H}$ reduces the problem to the Hilbert case, where independence and centering give the exact identity $\mathbb{E}\|TS_n\|_{\mathbb{H}}^2=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\|TX_i\|_{\mathbb{H}}^2$ (all cross terms vanish), and the operator norms $\|T\|$, $\|T^{-1}\|$ convert back to $\mathbb{B}$ at the cost of $(\|T\|\|T^{-1}\|)^2$; optimizing over $T$ yields $d_{\mathrm{bm}}(\mathbb{B},\mathbb{H})^2$.

**Solution.** Let $X_1,\dots,X_n$ be independent with $\mathbb{E}X_i=0$ and put $V=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\|X_i\|_{\mathbb{B}}^2<\infty$ (the "strong variance", as in Exercise 13.23; centering is needed for the conclusion). Let $T:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{H}$ be a linear isomorphism. Then
$$\|S_n\|_{\mathbb{B}}=\|T^{-1}(TS_n)\|_{\mathbb{B}}\le\|T^{-1}\|\cdot\|TS_n\|_{\mathbb{H}},$$
so $\mathbb{E}\|S_n\|_{\mathbb{B}}^2\le\|T^{-1}\|^2\,\mathbb{E}\|TS_n\|_{\mathbb{H}}^2$. Now $TS_n=\sum_{i=1}^nTX_i$ with the $TX_i$ independent and $\mathbb{E}[TX_i]=T(\mathbb{E}X_i)=0$. In a Hilbert space,
$$\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\Big\|_{\mathbb{H}}^2=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\|Y_i\|_{\mathbb{H}}^2+2\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{E}\langle Y_i,Y_j\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\|Y_i\|_{\mathbb{H}}^2$$
for independent mean-zero $Y_i$, since $\mathbb{E}\langle Y_i,Y_j\rangle=\langle\mathbb{E}Y_i,\mathbb{E}Y_j\rangle=0$. Hence
$$\mathbb{E}\|TS_n\|_{\mathbb{H}}^2=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\|TX_i\|_{\mathbb{H}}^2\le\|T\|^2\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\|X_i\|_{\mathbb{B}}^2=\|T\|^2V,$$
using $\|Tx\|_{\mathbb{H}}\le\|T\|\,\|x\|_{\mathbb{B}}$. Combining,
$$\mathbb{E}\|S_n\|_{\mathbb{B}}^2\le\|T\|^2\|T^{-1}\|^2V.$$
Taking the infimum over all isomorphisms $T:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{H}$ gives $\mathbb{E}\|S_n\|_{\mathbb{B}}^2\le d_{\mathrm{bm}}(\mathbb{B},\mathbb{H})^2V$.
