**Central idea.** For unit vectors the sum and difference $W_i(\alpha)\pm W_i(\alpha')$ are independent centered Gaussians (the cross-covariance $\|\alpha\|^2-\|\alpha'\|^2$ vanishes), so $W_i(\alpha)^2-W_i(\alpha')^2$ factors as a product of two independent Gaussians with variances $2(1\mp\cos\theta)$; the polarization identity $Z_1Z_2=\tfrac12(Y_1-Y_2)$ for the rotated independent $\chi^2_1$ variables $Y_1,Y_2$ turns this product into $\sin\theta\,(Y_1-Y_2)$, whose mgf is explicit; the Bernstein-type bound then follows from $-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x)$ and $\sin^2\theta\le\|\alpha-\alpha'\|^2$.

**Solution.** In Section 13.6, $T$ consists of unit vectors, so let $\|\alpha\|=\|\alpha'\|=1$ and let $\theta$ be the angle between them, i.e. $\langle\alpha,\alpha'\rangle=\cos\theta$. Write $W_i(\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^D\alpha_jX_{i,j}$ with $X_{i,j}$ iid $N(0,1)$.

Fix $i$. Then $(W_i(\alpha),W_i(\alpha'))$ is bivariate Gaussian with $\mathrm{Var}(W_i(\alpha))=\mathrm{Var}(W_i(\alpha'))=1$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(W_i(\alpha),W_i(\alpha'))=\cos\theta$. Put $A_i=W_i(\alpha)-W_i(\alpha')$ and $B_i=W_i(\alpha)+W_i(\alpha')$. Then $A_i\sim N(0,2-2\cos\theta)$, $B_i\sim N(0,2+2\cos\theta)$, and $\mathrm{Cov}(A_i,B_i)=\|\alpha\|^2-\|\alpha'\|^2=0$, so $A_i$ and $B_i$ are independent. Since $W_i(\alpha)^2-W_i(\alpha')^2=A_iB_i$ and $2\mp2\cos\theta=4\sin^2(\theta/2)$, $4\cos^2(\theta/2)$ respectively, writing $A_i=2\sin(\theta/2)Z_1$, $B_i=2\cos(\theta/2)Z_2$ with $Z_1,Z_2$ iid $N(0,1)$ gives
$$W_i(\alpha)^2-W_i(\alpha')^2=2\sin\theta\cdot Z_1Z_2.$$
With $U=(Z_1+Z_2)/\sqrt2$, $V=(Z_1-Z_2)/\sqrt2$ iid $N(0,1)$, we have $U^2-V^2=2Z_1Z_2$; setting $Y_1=U^2$, $Y_2=V^2$, which are independent $\chi^2_1$, yields $Z_1Z_2=(Y_1-Y_2)/2$. Hence
$$W_i(\alpha)^2-W_i(\alpha')^2=\sin\theta\,Y_1-\sin\theta\,Y_2,$$
as claimed.

Now $\|W(\alpha)\|^2-\|W(\alpha')\|^2=\frac1d\sum_{i=1}^d\sin\theta\,(Y_{1,i}-Y_{2,i})$, a sum of $d$ iid copies. For $|t|<1/2$ the $\chi^2_1$ mgf is $(1-2t)^{-1/2}$, so for $2|\lambda|\sin\theta/d<1$,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda\sin\theta(Y_1-Y_2)/d}=(1-2\lambda\sin\theta/d)^{-1/2}(1+2\lambda\sin\theta/d)^{-1/2}=(1-4\lambda^2\sin^2\theta/d^2)^{-1/2}.$$
Thus, with $\sin^2\theta=1-\langle\alpha,\alpha'\rangle^2$,
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(\|W(\alpha)\|^2-\|W(\alpha')\|^2)}=-\frac d2\log\!\left(1-\frac{4\lambda^2(1-\langle\alpha,\alpha'\rangle^2)}{d^2}\right).$$
For the bound, put $x=4\lambda^2\sin^2\theta/d^2$ and use $-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x)$:
$$-\frac d2\log(1-x)\le\frac d2\cdot\frac{x}{1-x}=\frac{2\lambda^2\sin^2\theta/d}{1-x}.$$
Since $\sin^2\theta=(1-\cos\theta)(1+\cos\theta)=\|\alpha-\alpha'\|^2\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}\le\|\alpha-\alpha'\|^2$, the numerator is at most $2\lambda^2\|\alpha-\alpha'\|^2/d$. Moreover $1-x\ge1-8\lambda\|\alpha-\alpha'\|/d$: indeed $x\le4\lambda^2\|\alpha-\alpha'\|^2/d^2$, so it suffices that $\lambda\|\alpha-\alpha'\|/d\le2$, which follows from $\lambda<d/(8\|\alpha-\alpha'\|)$ (this also gives $x<1/16$, so the mgf is valid). Hence
$$-\frac d2\log(1-x)\le\frac{2\lambda^2\|\alpha-\alpha'\|^2/d}{1-8\lambda\|\alpha-\alpha'\|/d}\le\frac{\lambda^28\|\alpha-\alpha'\|^2/d}{2(1-8\lambda\|\alpha-\alpha'\|/d)},$$
the last step using $2\le4$. This is the claimed inequality (in fact with a slightly sharper constant).
