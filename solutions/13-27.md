**Central idea.** The constraint set $\mathcal F_r=\{(s,\alpha):\alpha\in[0,1],\ \alpha L(s)\le r\}$ is star-shaped in $\alpha$. Shrinking $\alpha$ by $c=r/r'$ maps $\mathcal F_{r'}$ into $\mathcal F_r$, so $\psi^*(r)/r$ is nonincreasing. Splitting $\alpha$ proportionally gives subadditivity. Monotonicity is immediate. These facts force continuity on $(0,\infty)$. But the book's definition of sub-linear (p. 388) also asks for continuity on $[0,\infty)$ and $\psi(1)\ge1$, and both can fail for $\psi^*$. So the first claim holds only after a harmless modification. The weighted-supremum bound holds exactly as printed: $\alpha(s)=r/\max(r,L(s))$ is a feasible choice.

**Solution.**

**Erratum (statement as printed).** The book (p. 388; Exercise 13.41) calls $\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ *sub-linear* if it is nondecreasing, continuous, $\psi(x)/x$ is nonincreasing, and $\psi(1)\ge1$. In this sense, $\psi^*$ need **not** be sub-linear:

- *Normalization fails.* If $X_{i,s}\equiv0$ (with any admissible $L$), then $\psi^*\equiv0$, so $\psi^*(1)=0<1$. A non-degenerate example: $|\mathcal T|=1$, $n=1$, $X_{1,s}=\pm\varepsilon$ with probability $1/2$ each, $L(s)=\varepsilon<1$. Then $\psi^*(1)=\mathbb{E}(X_{1,s})_+=\varepsilon/2<1$.
- *Continuity at $0$ fails.* Take $n\ge1$ and $\mathcal T=\{1,2,\dots\}$. Let all $X_{i,s}$ be independent (over $i$ and $s$), with $X_{i,s}=+1$ and $X_{i,s}=-1$ each with probability $p_s/2$, and $X_{i,s}=0$ otherwise, where $p_s=1/(s+1)$. These are centered and $|X_{i,s}|\le1$. Put $L(s)=\sqrt{p_s}=(\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}^2)^{1/2}$. Since every $L(s)>0$, $r=0$ forces $\alpha=0$, so $\psi^*(0)=0$. Now fix $r>0$ and let $S_r=\{s:p_s\le\min(r^2,1/2)\}$, an infinite tail of $\mathcal T$. For $s\in S_r$, $\alpha=1$ is feasible, so $Y_r\ge\sup_{s\in S_r}(\sum_iX_{i,s})_+$. The events $E_s=\{\sum_iX_{i,s}\ge1\}$ are independent across $s$, and
$$\mathbb{P}(E_s)\ge\mathbb{P}\{X_{1,s}=1,\ X_{i,s}=0\ (i\ge2)\}=\tfrac{p_s}{2}(1-p_s)^{n-1}\ge2^{-n}p_s,$$
so $\sum_{s\in S_r}\mathbb{P}(E_s)=\infty$. By the second Borel–Cantelli lemma, some $E_s$ with $s\in S_r$ occurs almost surely. The sums are integers, so $Y_r\ge1$ a.s. Hence $\psi^*(r)\ge1$ for every $r>0$, while $\psi^*(0)=0$: $\psi^*$ jumps at $0$. (Numerically, for $n=1$, $r=0.1$, the probability bound $\prod_{s}(1-\mathbb{P}(E_s))$ that no $E_s$ occurs with $99\le s\le S$ decreases toward $0$ as $S$ grows. It equals $\exp(\sum\log(1-p_s/2))$, which behaves like $(100/S)^{1/2}$: about $0.0996$ at $S=10^4$, $0.00996$ at $S=10^6$ and $0.000996$ at $S=10^8$.)

**Corrected statement.** Assume, as in Lemma 13.18 and Theorem 13.19, that $\mathcal T$ is countable, so that all suprema below are measurable. Then:

(a) $0\le\psi^*\le n$, $\psi^*(0)=0$, and $\psi^*$ is nondecreasing and subadditive on $[0,\infty)$. On $(0,\infty)$, $\psi^*(r)/r$ is nonincreasing and $\psi^*$ is continuous.

(b) Let $\tilde\psi(0)=\psi^*(0+)=\lim_{r\downarrow0}\psi^*(r)$ and $\tilde\psi(r)=\psi^*(r)$ for $r>0$. Then $\bar\psi(r)=\tilde\psi(r)+r$ is sub-linear in the book's sense and $\bar\psi\ge\psi^*$. (The property actually used in slicing arguments, $\psi(2^kr)\le2^k\psi(r)$ for $r>0$ as in the proof of Lemma 13.18, already holds for $\psi^*$ itself by (a).)

(c) For every $r>0$, $\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in\mathcal T}\frac{r}{\max(r,L(s))}\sum_{i=1}^nX_{i,s}\le\psi^*(r)$, as printed.

The counterexamples above show that (a)–(b) cannot be upgraded to "$\psi^*$ is sub-linear".

**Proof.** Note that $L(s)\ge(\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}^2)^{1/2}\ge0$. Let
$$Y_r=\sup_{(s,\alpha)\in\mathcal F_r}\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha X_{i,s},\qquad\psi^*(r)=\mathbb{E}Y_r .$$
For fixed $s$, the feasible $\alpha$ form an interval $[0,a_r(s)]$ with $a_r(s)=\min(1,r/L(s))$ (and $a_r(s)=1$ if $L(s)=0$). Hence $Y_r=\sup_s a_r(s)\big(\sum_iX_{i,s}\big)_+$, a countable supremum, so $Y_r$ is measurable.

*Bounds and $\psi^*(0)=0$.* Since $\alpha=0$ is always feasible and $|X_{i,s}|\le1$, we have $0\le Y_r\le n$. At $r=0$: if $L(s)>0$, then $a_0(s)=0$; if $L(s)=0$, then $\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}^2=0$, so $X_{i,s}=0$ a.s. As $\mathcal T$ is countable, $Y_0=0$ a.s., and $\psi^*(0)=0$.

*Monotonicity.* For $r\le r'$, $\mathcal F_r\subseteq\mathcal F_{r'}$, so $Y_r\le Y_{r'}$ and $\psi^*(r)\le\psi^*(r')$.

*$\psi^*(r)/r$ nonincreasing on $(0,\infty)$.* Let $0<r<r'$ and $c=r/r'\in(0,1)$. If $(s,\alpha)\in\mathcal F_{r'}$, then $(s,c\alpha)\in\mathcal F_r$, because $c\alpha\in[0,1]$ and $c\alpha L(s)\le cr'=r$. Hence $Y_r\ge cY_{r'}$ pointwise. Taking expectations, $\psi^*(r)/r\ge\psi^*(r')/r'$.

*Continuity on $(0,\infty)$.* For $0<r<r'$, the last two properties give $\psi^*(r)\le\psi^*(r')\le\frac{r'}{r}\psi^*(r)$. Letting $r'\downarrow r$ or $r\uparrow r'$, and using that $\psi^*$ is finite, gives continuity at every point of $(0,\infty)$.

*Subadditivity.* Let $r_1,r_2\ge0$ with $r_1+r_2>0$. For $(s,\alpha)\in\mathcal F_{r_1+r_2}$, put $\alpha_j=\alpha r_j/(r_1+r_2)$. Then $\alpha_j\in[0,1]$, $\alpha_jL(s)\le r_j$, and $\alpha=\alpha_1+\alpha_2$. So $\sum_i\alpha X_{i,s}=\sum_i\alpha_1X_{i,s}+\sum_i\alpha_2X_{i,s}\le Y_{r_1}+Y_{r_2}$. Taking the supremum over $\mathcal F_{r_1+r_2}$ and then expectations, $\psi^*(r_1+r_2)\le\psi^*(r_1)+\psi^*(r_2)$. The case $r_1=r_2=0$ is trivial. This proves (a).

*(b).* $\tilde\psi(0)=\inf_{r>0}\psi^*(r)$ exists by monotonicity, and $0\le\tilde\psi(0)\le\tilde\psi(r)$. So $\tilde\psi$ is nondecreasing, nonnegative, and continuous on $[0,\infty)$: at $0$ by construction, elsewhere by (a). Also $\tilde\psi\ge\psi^*$, since they agree on $(0,\infty)$ and $\tilde\psi(0)\ge0=\psi^*(0)$. Therefore $\bar\psi=\tilde\psi+r$ is nondecreasing and continuous. On $(0,\infty)$, $\bar\psi(x)/x=\psi^*(x)/x+1$ is nonincreasing, and $\bar\psi(1)=\psi^*(1)+1\ge1$. So $\bar\psi$ is sub-linear and $\bar\psi\ge\psi^*$.

*(c) The star-shaping inequality.* Fix $r>0$ and, for each $s$, put $\alpha(s)=r/\max(r,L(s))$. This is well defined, and $\alpha(s)\in(0,1]$ because $r\le\max(r,L(s))$. Also
$$\alpha(s)L(s)=\frac{rL(s)}{\max(r,L(s))}\le r,$$
so $(s,\alpha(s))\in\mathcal F_r$ for every $s$. Hence, pointwise,
$$\sup_{s\in\mathcal T}\frac{r}{\max(r,L(s))}\sum_{i=1}^nX_{i,s}=\sup_{s\in\mathcal T}\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha(s)X_{i,s}\le Y_r .$$
Taking expectations,
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in\mathcal T}\frac{r}{\max(r,L(s))}\sum_{i=1}^nX_{i,s}\le\psi^*(r)\ \big(\le\bar\psi(r)\big). \qquad\blacksquare$$
