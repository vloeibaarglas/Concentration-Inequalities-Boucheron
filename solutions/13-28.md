**Central idea.** The $m$-th power map is locally Lipschitz with constant $m\max(\|X\|,\|Y\|)^{m-1}$ (telescoping sum), while $\exp(A/m)\exp(B/m)$ and $\exp((A+B)/m)$ agree to first order in $1/m$, differing only by $O(1/m^2)$. The $O(1/m^2)$ discrepancy, amplified by the Lipschitz constant $m$, vanishes as $m\to\infty$.

**Solution.** Let $\|\cdot\|$ be a submultiplicative matrix norm. For any $n\times n$ matrices $X,Y$,
$$X^m-Y^m=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}X^{m-1-j}(X-Y)Y^j,$$
whence, with $M=\max(\|X\|,\|Y\|)$,
$$\|X^m-Y^m\|\le\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\|X\|^{m-1-j}\|X-Y\|\|Y\|^j\le mM^{m-1}\|X-Y\|.\tag{*}$$
Also $\|\exp(Z)\|\le e^{\|Z\|}$ for any $Z$.

Set $X_m=\exp((A+B)/m)$ and $Y_m=\exp(A/m)\exp(B/m)$. Then $\|X_m\|\le e^{\|A+B\|/m}$ and $\|Y_m\|\le e^{(\|A\|+\|B\|)/m}$, so with $M_m=\max(\|X_m\|,\|Y_m\|)$,
$$M_m^{\,m-1}\le e^{(\|A\|+\|B\|)(m-1)/m}\le e^{\|A\|+\|B\|}.$$

For any $Z$, writing $\exp(Z)=I+Z+R(Z)$,
$$\|R(Z)\|\le\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\|Z\|^k}{k!}=\|Z\|^2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|Z\|^k}{(k+2)!}\le\|Z\|^2e^{\|Z\|}.$$
Hence, for $m\ge 1$,
$$\Bigl\|\exp\frac{A}{m}-\Bigl(I+\frac{A}{m}\Bigr)\Bigr\|\le\frac{\|A\|^2e^{\|A\|}}{m^2},$$
and similarly for $B$ and $A+B$. Expanding the product,
$$Y_m-\Bigl(I+\frac{A+B}{m}\Bigr)=R_A+R_B+R_AR_B+\frac{A}{m}R_B+R_A\frac{B}{m}+\frac{AB}{m^2},$$
each term of which is $O(1/m^2)$ with constant depending only on $A,B$. Since $X_m-(I+(A+B)/m)=R_{A+B}=O(1/m^2)$ as well,
$$\|X_m-Y_m\|=O(1/m^2).$$
Applying $(*)$ to $X_m,Y_m$ gives
$$\|X_m^m-Y_m^m\|\le m\,e^{\|A\|+\|B\|}\cdot O(1/m^2)=O(1/m)\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{}0.$$
Finally $X_m^m=(\exp((A+B)/m))^m=\exp(A+B)$, because $(A+B)/m$ commutes with itself. Therefore
$$\Bigl(\exp\frac{A}{m}\exp\frac{B}{m}\Bigr)^m=Y_m^m\longrightarrow \exp(A+B)\quad\text{as }m\to\infty,$$
which is the Lie product formula. $\blacksquare$
