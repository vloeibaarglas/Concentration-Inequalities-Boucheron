**Central idea.** Golden–Thompson follows from the Lie product formula once we can "unsplit" $\mathrm{trace}((XY)^{2^{k+1}})$ into $\mathrm{trace}(X^{2^{k+1}}Y^{2^{k+1}})$ by iterating the hint. The hint itself is Weyl's eigenvalue–singular-value majorization for $XY$ combined with convexity of $t\mapsto t^{2m}$: $\sum\lambda_i^{2m}\le\sum\sigma_i^{2m}=\mathrm{trace}((X^2Y^2)^m)$.

**Solution.** We first prove the hint. Let $X,Y$ be Hermitian positive semi-definite and $m\ge 1$ an integer.

*Lemma.* $\mathrm{trace}((XY)^{2m})\le\mathrm{trace}((X^2Y^2)^m)$.

*Proof of Lemma.* The eigenvalues $\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n\ge 0$ of $XY$ are real and nonnegative (since $XY$ has the same nonzero eigenvalues as $X^{1/2}YX^{1/2}\ge 0$), so $\mathrm{trace}((XY)^{2m})=\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i^{2m}$. Let $\sigma_1\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_n$ be the singular values of $XY$. By Weyl's majorization inequalities, $\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i\le\sum_{i=1}^k\sigma_i$ for all $k$. Since $\phi(t)=t^{2m}$ is convex, increasing on $[0,\infty)$ with $\phi(0)=\phi'(0)=0$, we have $\phi(t)=\int_0^\infty(t-s)_+\phi''(s)\,ds$, and for $c\ge 0$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^k(\lambda_i-c)_+=\max_{0\le j\le k}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^j\lambda_i-jc\Bigr)\le\max_{0\le j\le k}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^j\sigma_i-jc\Bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^k(\sigma_i-c)_+,$$
whence integrating against $\phi''(s)\,ds\ge 0$ gives $\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i^{2m}\le\sum_{i=1}^k\sigma_i^{2m}$ for all $k$. For $k=n$,
$$\mathrm{trace}((XY)^{2m})=\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i^{2m}\le\sum_{i=1}^n\sigma_i^{2m}=\mathrm{trace}\bigl(((XY)^*(XY))^m\bigr)=\mathrm{trace}\bigl((YX^2Y)^m\bigr).$$
Cyclically permuting the leading $Y$ to the end gives $\mathrm{trace}((YX^2Y)^m)=\mathrm{trace}((X^2Y^2)^m)$. ∎

Now let $A,B$ be Hermitian and put $m=2^{k+1}$, $X=\exp(A/m)$, $Y=\exp(B/m)$, which are Hermitian positive definite. For $j=0,\dots,k$, apply the Lemma to the positive semi-definite pair $(X^{2^j},Y^{2^j})$ with exponent $2^{k-j}$:
$$\mathrm{trace}\bigl((X^{2^j}Y^{2^j})^{2\cdot 2^{k-j}}\bigr)\le\mathrm{trace}\bigl(((X^{2^j})^2(Y^{2^j})^2)^{2^{k-j}}\bigr).$$
With $S_j=\mathrm{trace}((X^{2^j}Y^{2^j})^{2^{k+1-j}})$ this reads $S_j\le S_{j+1}$, so telescoping,
$$\mathrm{trace}\bigl((XY)^{2^{k+1}}\bigr)=S_0\le S_{k+1}=\mathrm{trace}\bigl(X^{2^{k+1}}Y^{2^{k+1}}\bigr).$$
Since $X^m=\exp(A)$ and $Y^m=\exp(B)$ (as $A/m$ commutes with itself),
$$\mathrm{trace}\Bigl(\bigl(\exp(A/m)\exp(B/m)\bigr)^m\Bigr)\le\mathrm{trace}\bigl(\exp(A)\exp(B)\bigr)\qquad(m=2^{k+1}).$$
By the Lie product formula (Exercise 13.28), $(\exp(A/m)\exp(B/m))^m\to\exp(A+B)$ as $m\to\infty$, hence along $m=2^{k+1}$ as $k\to\infty$. The trace is continuous, so passing to the limit yields
$$\mathrm{trace}(\exp(A+B))\le\mathrm{trace}(\exp(A)\exp(B)),$$
the Golden–Thompson inequality. $\blacksquare$
