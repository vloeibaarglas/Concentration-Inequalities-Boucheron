**Central idea.** In one dimension, $\phi'(x)=-x\phi(x)$ turns $\int xF(x)\phi(x)dx$ into a boundary term (killed by the growth hypothesis) plus $\int F'(x)\phi(x)dx$ via integration by parts. For a general centered Gaussian vector write $X=AZ$ with $Z$ standard Gaussian in $\mathbb{R}^r$ ($r=\mathrm{rank}\,\Sigma$); conditioning on $Z_{-k}$ reduces $\mathbb{E}[Z_kF(AZ)]$ to the one-dimensional identity, and $\partial_k[F(A\,\cdot\,)]=\sum_jA_{jk}(\partial_jF)(A\,\cdot\,)$ produces the covariance weights $\Sigma_{ij}=\sum_kA_{ik}A_{jk}$.

**Solution.** (We write $d$ for the dimension; the "$\mathbb{R}^n$" in the statement is a typo.)

*Dimension one.* Let $Z\sim N(0,1)$ with density $\phi$. The growth hypothesis gives $\mathbb{E}|ZF(Z)|<\infty$: indeed $|F(x)|\leq e^{x^2/8}$ for large $|x|$ (take $a=1/8$), so $|xF(x)|\phi(x)\leq|x|e^{-3x^2/8}/\sqrt{2\pi}$, integrable. For $M>0$,
$$\int_{-M}^{M}xF(x)\phi(x)\,dx=\big[-F(x)\phi(x)\big]_{-M}^{M}+\int_{-M}^{M}F'(x)\phi(x)\,dx,$$
since $(F\phi)'=F'\phi-xF\phi$. The boundary term vanishes as $M\to\infty$: $|F(\pm M)|\phi(M)\leq e^{-3M^2/8}/\sqrt{2\pi}\to0$. Hence, with the symmetric improper integral,
$$\mathbb{E}[ZF(Z)]=\lim_{M\to\infty}\int_{-M}^{M}F'(x)\phi(x)\,dx,$$
and if $\mathbb{E}|F'(Z)|<\infty$ this equals $\mathbb{E}[F'(Z)]$. For $X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $\sigma>0$, write $X=\mu+\sigma Z$ and apply this to $\tilde{F}(z)=F(\mu+\sigma z)$ (the growth hypothesis transfers: $|\tilde{F}(z)|e^{-az^2}\leq e^{2a'\mu^2}|F(\mu+\sigma z)|e^{-a'|\mu+\sigma z|^2}\to0$ with $a'=a/(2\sigma^2)$); if $\sigma=0$ both sides below are $0$. This gives $\mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)F(X)]=\sigma^2\mathbb{E}[F'(X)]$.

*General $d$.* Let $\Sigma=\mathbb{E}[XX^T]$. If $\Sigma=0$ both sides are $0$. Otherwise write $X=AZ$ with $Z\sim N(0,I_r)$, $r=\mathrm{rank}(\Sigma)\geq1$, $A$ $d\times r$ of full column rank, $\Sigma=AA^T$. Then
$$\mathbb{E}[X_iF(X)]=\sum_{k=1}^{r}A_{ik}\,\mathbb{E}[Z_kF(AZ)].$$
Fix $k$ and put $G(z)=F(Az)$. Conditioning on $Z_{-k}$: given $Z_{-k}$, $Z_k\sim N(0,1)$ is independent of $Z_{-k}$, and $t\mapsto G(Z_{-k},t)$ is $C^1$ with $|G(z)|e^{-a\|z\|^2}\to0$ (since $\|Az\|\geq\sigma_{\min}(A)\|z\|\to\infty$ and $|G(z)|e^{-a\|z\|^2}\leq|F(Az)|e^{-a'\|Az\|^2}$ with $a'=a/\|A\|_{op}^2$). The one-dimensional identity gives $\mathbb{E}[Z_kG(Z)\mid Z_{-k}]=\mathbb{E}[\partial_kG(Z)\mid Z_{-k}]$ a.s. By the chain rule $\partial_kG(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{jk}(\partial_jF)(Az)$, so
$$\mathbb{E}[Z_kF(AZ)]=\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{jk}\,\mathbb{E}[(\partial_jF)(X)].$$
Therefore $\mathbb{E}[X_iF(X)]=\sum_{k,j}A_{ik}A_{jk}\mathbb{E}[(\partial_jF)(X)]=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Sigma_{ij}\,\mathbb{E}[(\partial_jF)(X)]$, as claimed. ∎

*Remark (integrability subtlety).* The growth hypothesis implies $\mathbb{E}|X_iF(X)|<\infty$ and kills the boundary terms, but it does not imply $\mathbb{E}|\partial_jF(X)|<\infty$, so the right-hand side need not exist as a Lebesgue integral. E.g., in one dimension $F(x)=\sin(e^{x^2})$ satisfies the growth hypothesis ($|F|\leq1$) and $\mathbb{E}[ZF(Z)]=0$ by symmetry, yet $\mathbb{E}|F'(Z)|=\infty$ since $|F'(x)|\approx2xe^{x^2}$ and $e^{x^2}\phi(x)=e^{x^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$ is not integrable at infinity. The identity holds as stated whenever $\mathbb{E}|\nabla F(X)|<\infty$ (true in all applications, e.g. Slepian's lemma, Theorem 13.3, where $F$ has bounded derivatives); otherwise it holds with $\mathbb{E}[\partial_jF(X)]$ read as the corresponding improper integral.
