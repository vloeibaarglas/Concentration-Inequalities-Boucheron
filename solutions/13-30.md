**Central idea.** For the variance, use the Efron–Stein inequality (Theorem 3.1) with the $i$-th sign resampled, as the hint suggests (Example 3.6 replacing the Gaussian Poincaré inequality). Resampling a Rademacher variable either does nothing or flips it. A flip can lower the norm by at most $2|\langle u,A_iu\rangle|$, where $u$ is the top eigenvector of the *unflipped* sum, and $\sum_i\langle u,A_iu\rangle^2\le\sigma^2$. Only the positive part of $Z-Z_i'$ enters, so this one-sided control is enough. The result is $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le2\sigma^2$; this method does not reach the printed $\sigma^2$ (which we neither prove nor refute). For the expectation, compare the Rademacher sum with a Gaussian sum (Exercise 11.14, constant $\sqrt{\pi/2}$) and apply the Gaussian bound $\sqrt{2\log(2d)}\,\sigma$ of Theorem 13.8.

**Solution.**

**Status: incomplete** — the printed variance bound $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sigma^2$ is **not proved** here; only $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le2\sigma^2$ is. The expectation bound $\mathbb{E}Z\le\sqrt{\pi\log(2d)}\,\sigma$ is proved in full.

**Erratum (to an earlier version of this solution).** An earlier version claimed $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sigma^2$ through the pointwise bound $\sum_i(Z-Z_i)^2\le\sum_i\langle u,A_iu\rangle^2\le\sigma^2$, with $Z_i=\|\sum_{j\ne i}X_jA_j\|$. That step is invalid. The argument only controls $Z-Z_i$ from above, by $sX_i\langle u,A_iu\rangle$. But $Z_i$ can exceed $Z$, and the size of $Z_i-Z$ is governed by the top eigenvector of $\sum_{j\ne i}X_jA_j$, not by $u$. Counterexample to the pointwise bound: $d=2$, $n=3$, with
$$A_1=\begin{pmatrix}-0.9&-0.2\\-0.2&0.4\end{pmatrix},\quad A_2=\begin{pmatrix}0.4&-0.1\\-0.1&0.4\end{pmatrix},\quad A_3=\begin{pmatrix}-0.5&-0.3\\-0.3&0.7\end{pmatrix},\quad x=(-1,-1,1).$$
Here $Z=0.1$, $\sum_i\langle u,A_iu\rangle^2=0.81$, and $\sigma^2\approx1.3639$, but $\sum_i(Z-Z_i)^2\approx1.4105>\sigma^2$. These values were checked numerically.

**Notation.** Write $\|\cdot\|$ for the operator norm. For symmetric $M$, $\|M\|=\max_{\|v\|=1}|\langle v,Mv\rangle|$, so $\|M\|\ge \pm\langle v,Mv\rangle$ for every unit vector $v$. For $x\in\{-1,1\}^n$, put $M(x)=\sum_ix_iA_i$ and $Z=\|M(X)\|$. Let $\bar x^{(i)}$ denote $x$ with its $i$-th coordinate replaced by $-x_i$.

*Key estimate.* For each $x$, choose a unit vector $u=u(x)$ and a sign $s=s(x)\in\{-1,1\}$ with $\|M(x)\|=s\langle u,M(x)u\rangle$: take an eigenvector for an eigenvalue of largest absolute value. Then
$$\sum_i\langle u,A_iu\rangle^2\le\sum_i\|A_iu\|^2=\Big\langle u,\sum_iA_i^2\,u\Big\rangle\le\sigma^2,\tag{1}$$
by Cauchy–Schwarz ($\langle u,A_iu\rangle^2\le\|u\|^2\|A_iu\|^2$) and the symmetry of $A_i$. Moreover, for each $i$,
$$\|M(\bar x^{(i)})\|\ge s\langle u,M(\bar x^{(i)})u\rangle=s\langle u,M(x)u\rangle-2sx_i\langle u,A_iu\rangle=\|M(x)\|-2sx_i\langle u,A_iu\rangle .$$
Hence
$$\big(\|M(x)\|-\|M(\bar x^{(i)})\|\big)_+\le2|\langle u(x),A_iu(x)\rangle|.\tag{2}$$

*Variance: $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le2\sigma^2$.* Let $X_i'$ be an independent copy of $X_i$, and let $Z_i'$ be $Z$ computed with $X_i'$ in place of $X_i$. The Efron–Stein inequality (Theorem 3.1) gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sum_i\mathbb{E}(Z-E^{(i)}Z)^2=\frac12\sum_i\mathbb{E}(Z-Z_i')^2$. Since $(Z,Z_i')$ is exchangeable, $\mathbb{E}(Z-Z_i')^2=2\,\mathbb{E}(Z-Z_i')_+^2$, so
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}(Z-Z_i')_+^2 .$$
With probability $1/2$, independently of $X$, $X_i'=X_i$ and $Z_i'=Z$; otherwise $Z_i'=\|M(\bar X^{(i)})\|$. Therefore, by (2) and then (1),
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big(\|M(X)\|-\|M(\bar X^{(i)})\|\big)_+^2\le\frac12\,\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^n4\langle u(X),A_iu(X)\rangle^2\le2\sigma^2 .$$

*Why this stops at $2\sigma^2$.* In the Gaussian case (Theorem 13.8), the Poincaré inequality uses $\|\nabla Z\|^2=\sum_i\langle u,A_iu\rangle^2\le\sigma^2$ with constant $1$. With signs, a resampled coordinate moves by $2$, not infinitesimally. Efron–Stein then costs exactly the factor $\frac12\cdot2^2=2$. We could not find a valid argument giving constant $1$.

*Numerical evidence on the constant $1$ (not a proof).* We computed $\mathrm{Var}(Z)/\sigma^2$ exactly by enumerating all $2^n$ sign patterns. This covered 4,000 random instances with $n\le6$, $d\le3$ and random symmetric Gaussian matrices with random scales, plus 60 hill-climbing searches that maximized the ratio. The largest ratio found was $0.4996$. The value $1/2$ is attained at $d=1$, $n=2$, $A_1=A_2=1$: there $Z\in\{0,2\}$ with equal probability, so $\mathrm{Var}(Z)=1$, while $\sigma^2=2$. So the evidence suggests $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sigma^2/2$, which would imply the printed bound. Whether $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sigma^2$ holds in general remains **open** here.

*Expectation: $\mathbb{E}Z\le\sqrt{\pi\log(2d)}\,\sigma$.* Let $Y_1,\dots,Y_n$ be independent standard Gaussians. Then $(\mathrm{sign}\,Y_i)_i$ are independent Rademacher variables, independent of $(|Y_i|)_i$, and $\mathbb{E}|Y_i|=\sqrt{2/\pi}$. Symmetric $d\times d$ matrices with the operator norm form a separable Banach space. Exercise 11.14 with $p=1$, $v_i=A_i$ and $C=\sqrt{\pi/2}$ gives
$$\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^nX_iA_i\Big\|\le\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\;\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^nY_iA_i\Big\|.$$
Theorem 13.8 gives $\mathbb{E}\|\sum_iY_iA_i\|\le\sqrt{2\log(2d)}\,\sigma$. Combining,
$$\mathbb{E}Z\le\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\cdot\sqrt{2\log(2d)}\,\sigma=\sqrt{\pi\log(2d)}\,\sigma .$$

*Summary.* Proved: $\mathbb{E}Z\le\sqrt{\pi\log(2d)}\,\sigma$ and $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le2\sigma^2$. Not proved: the printed $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sigma^2$. Exact enumeration on small cases never exceeded $\sigma^2/2$.
