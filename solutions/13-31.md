**Central idea.** Symmetrization (Lemma 11.4) reduces the expectation to the Rademacher sum $\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_i$. Two facts give the matrix mgf bound $\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\mathrm{tr}\exp(\theta\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_i)\le\mathrm{tr}\exp(\frac{\theta^2}{2}\sum_iX_i^2)$: the scalar bound $\cosh t\le e^{t^2/2}$, applied through $\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon_i}e^{\theta\varepsilon_iX_i}=\cosh(\theta X_i)$, and the Golden–Thompson telescoping argument of Section 13.4 (proof of Theorem 13.8). Combine this with $e^{\theta\|Y\|}\le\mathrm{tr}\,e^{\theta Y}+\mathrm{tr}\,e^{-\theta Y}$ and with $X_i^2\preceq A_i^2$, which gives $\|\sum_iX_i^2\|\le\sigma^2$. Chernoff optimization then yields $\mathbb{E}Z\le2\sqrt{2\log(2d)}\,\sigma$. For the variance and the tail, write $Z=|u^T(\sum_jX_j)u|$ for an $X$-measurable unit vector $u$. Replacing one $X_i$ by an independent copy changes $Z$ by at most $|u^T(X_i-X_i')u|$ from above. Since $X_i'$ is centered, this gives $V_+\le2\sigma^2$ almost surely. Efron–Stein then gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le2\sigma^2$. The exponential Efron–Stein inequality (Theorem 6.16) gives $\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\le e^{-t^2/(8\sigma^2)}$, with no dimensional prefactor.

**Solution.** **Corrections to the statement.**
- $\log d$ must be $\log(2d)$: the printed expectation bound is false for $d=1$.
- The tail must read $e^{-t^2/(8\sigma^2)}$. The printed $e^{-t^2/8}$ is the case $\sigma=1$, and a $\sigma^2$ is forced by scaling.

A pre-repair version of this solution also claimed that the tail needs a $2d$ prefactor. That claim is false and is withdrawn: the bound below has none.

**Status: incomplete** (variance constant only). We prove $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le2\sigma^2$, not the printed $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sigma^2$. The independent audit also proves only $2\sigma^2$.
- In the special case $X_i=\varepsilon_iA_i$ with Rademacher $\varepsilon_i$, the book asserts after Theorem 13.8 that Rudelson's bound $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sigma^2$ remains valid. It leaves the details to the reader, and we do not prove it here.

The expectation and tail bounds below are complete.

*Preliminaries.* "Symmetric" means self-adjoint, $X_i^T=X_i$. The $X_i$ are centered but need not be symmetrically distributed, hence the need for symmetrization. $A_i^2-X_i^2\succeq0$ a.s. means $X_i^2\preceq A_i^2$ in the PSD order. Summing, $\sum_iX_i^2\preceq\sum_iA_i^2$ a.s. Both sides are PSD, so $\|\sum_iX_i^2\|\le\|\sum_iA_i^2\|=\sigma^2$ a.s. Also $\mathbb{E}[X_i^2]\preceq A_i^2$. For a unit vector $u$ and symmetric $B$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $(u^TBu)^2\le\|Bu\|^2=u^TB^2u$.

*Matrix mgf lemma.* Let $X_1,\dots,X_n$ be fixed symmetric $d\times d$ matrices and $\varepsilon_i$ i.i.d. Rademacher variables. For all $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$,
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\mathrm{tr}\exp\Big(\theta\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_iX_i\Big)\le\mathrm{tr}\exp\Big(\frac{\theta^2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^nX_i^2\Big).$$
To see this, follow the proof of Theorem 13.8 in Section 13.4, with Rademacher signs in place of Gaussians. Put $D_0=\frac{\theta^2}{2}\sum_iX_i^2$ and $D_j=D_{j-1}+Y_j$ with $Y_j=\theta\varepsilon_jX_j-\frac{\theta^2}{2}X_j^2$, so $D_n=\theta\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_i$. The two terms of $Y_j$ commute, so $e^{Y_j}=e^{\theta\varepsilon_jX_j}e^{-\theta^2X_j^2/2}$ and
$$C_j:=\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon_j}e^{Y_j}=\cosh(\theta X_j)\,e^{-\theta^2X_j^2/2}.$$
By the scalar inequality $\cosh t\le e^{t^2/2}$ and the spectral mapping theorem, $0\preceq C_j\preceq I$. By the Golden–Thompson inequality (Exercise 13.29), $\mathrm{tr}\,e^{D_{j-1}+Y_j}\le\mathrm{tr}(e^{D_{j-1}}e^{Y_j})$. Since $\varepsilon_j$ is independent of $D_{j-1}$, linearity of the trace gives
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\mathrm{tr}\,e^{D_j}\le\mathrm{tr}\big(\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon[e^{D_{j-1}}]\,C_j\big)\le\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\mathrm{tr}\,e^{D_{j-1}}.$$
The last step uses $\mathrm{tr}(PC)=\mathrm{tr}(P^{1/2}CP^{1/2})\le\mathrm{tr}P$ for PSD $P$ and $C\preceq I$. Iterating from $j=n$ down to $j=1$ gives $\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\mathrm{tr}\,e^{D_n}\le\mathrm{tr}\,e^{D_0}$, which is the lemma. Moreover, $\mathrm{tr}\exp(\frac{\theta^2}{2}\sum_iX_i^2)\le d\exp(\frac{\theta^2}{2}\|\sum_iX_i^2\|)$.

*Expectation.* By symmetrization (Lemma 11.4), $\mathbb{E}Z\le2\,\mathbb{E}_{X,\varepsilon}\|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_i\|$. Fix $X$ and put $Y=\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_i$. Since
$$e^{\theta\|Y\|}=\max(e^{\theta\lambda_{\max}(Y)},e^{\theta\lambda_{\max}(-Y)})\le\mathrm{tr}\,e^{\theta Y}+\mathrm{tr}\,e^{-\theta Y},$$
the lemma gives, for $\theta>0$,
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon e^{\theta\|Y\|}\le2d\exp\Big(\frac{\theta^2\sigma^2}{2}\Big)\quad\text{a.s.}$$
By Jensen, $\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\|Y\|\le\frac{1}{\theta}\log\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon e^{\theta\|Y\|}\le\frac{\log(2d)}{\theta}+\frac{\theta\sigma^2}{2}$. Take $\mathbb{E}_X$ and choose $\theta=\sqrt{2\log(2d)}/\sigma$. This gives $\mathbb{E}_{X,\varepsilon}\|\sum_i\varepsilon_iX_i\|\le\sqrt{2\log(2d)}\,\sigma$, and hence
$$\mathbb{E}Z\le2\sqrt{2\log(2d)}\,\sigma.$$
*Remark.* The printed $2\sqrt{2\log d}\,\sigma$ is false for $d=1$. Take $d=1$, $n=1$, $X_1=\pm1$ with probability $1/2$ each, and $A_1=1$. Then $\mathbb{E}Z=1$ and $\sigma=1$, but the printed right-hand side is $0$.

*A one-coordinate perturbation bound.* Let $S=\sum_jX_j$. Choose an $X$-measurable unit vector $u$ and sign $\eta\in\{\pm1\}$ with $Z=\|S\|=\eta\,u^TSu$. For instance, take a unit eigenvector for the eigenvalue of largest modulus, chosen by a fixed measurable rule. Let $X_i'$ be an independent copy of $X_i$, independent of $X$. Let $Z_i'=\|S-X_i+X_i'\|$. Then
$$Z_i'\ge\eta\,u^T(S-X_i+X_i')u=Z-\eta\,u^T(X_i-X_i')u,$$
so $(Z-Z_i')_+\le|u^TX_iu-u^TX_i'u|$. Let $\mathbb{E}'$ denote expectation over $X'$ only. Since $u$ is $X$-measurable and $\mathbb{E}X_i'=0$, the cross term vanishes:
$$\mathbb{E}'\big[(u^TX_iu-u^TX_i'u)^2\big]=(u^TX_iu)^2+\mathbb{E}'(u^TX_i'u)^2\le u^TX_i^2u+u^T\mathbb{E}[X_i^2]u\le2\,u^TA_i^2u.$$
Summing over $i$,
$$V_+:=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}'\big[(Z-Z_i')_+^2\big]\le2\,u^T\Big(\sum_iA_i^2\Big)u\le2\sigma^2\quad\text{a.s.}$$

*Variance.* The Efron–Stein inequality in the form $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\mathbb{E}V_+$ (Theorem 3.1, in its form with independent copies $X_i'$) gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le2\sigma^2.$$
(See the status note above for the printed $\sigma^2$.)

*Tail.* Theorem 6.16 states: for $\theta,\lambda>0$ with $\theta\lambda<1$ and $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda V_+/\theta}<\infty$,
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}\le\frac{\lambda\theta}{1-\lambda\theta}\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda V_+/\theta}.$$
Since $V_+\le2\sigma^2$ a.s., the right-hand side is at most $\frac{\lambda\theta}{1-\lambda\theta}\cdot\frac{2\lambda\sigma^2}{\theta}=\frac{2\lambda^2\sigma^2}{1-\lambda\theta}$. Letting $\theta\downarrow0$ gives $\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}\le2\lambda^2\sigma^2$ for all $\lambda>0$. By the Chernoff bound with $\lambda=t/(4\sigma^2)$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\le\exp\Big(-\lambda t+2\lambda^2\sigma^2\Big)=\exp\Big(-\frac{t^2}{8\sigma^2}\Big),\qquad t>0.$$

The same bound also follows from Theorem 6.7, the bounded-differences-type inequality of the hint. Let $Z_i=\inf_{x_i'}f(X_1,\dots,x_i',\dots,X_n)$, the infimum taken over values $x_i'$ with $x_i'^2\preceq A_i^2$. The argument above gives $Z-Z_i\le\sup_{x_i'}|u^TX_iu-u^Tx_i'u|\le2\sqrt{u^TA_i^2u}$. Hence $\sum_i(Z-Z_i)^2\le4\sigma^2$, and Theorem 6.7 gives $e^{-t^2/(2\cdot4\sigma^2)}$.

*Remark.* The printed $e^{-t^2/8}$ cannot hold as written. Scaling $(X_i,A_i,t)\mapsto(cX_i,cA_i,ct)$ leaves the left-hand side unchanged but sends $e^{-t^2/8}$ to $e^{-c^2t^2/8}$. So $\sigma^2$ must appear in the exponent. No dimensional prefactor is needed. Plain bounded differences would give $\sum_i\|A_i\|^2$ in place of $\sigma^2=\|\sum_iA_i^2\|$, which can be much larger. ∎
