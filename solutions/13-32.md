**Central idea.** The map $Z=\|\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\|$ is self-bounding: removing one PSD summand can only decrease the operator norm, by at most $\|X_i\|\le 1$, and the leave-one-out deficits sum to at most $Z$ via the variational representation of the norm. Theorem 6.12 then yields $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\mathbb{E}Z$ and the Bennett inequalities. For the expectation bound, split off the mean and symmetrize; conditional on the $X_i$, Rudelson's inequality (Theorem 13.8) controls the Rademacher sum by $\|\sum_i X_i^2\|^{1/2}$, and $X_i^2\preceq X_i$ (since $0\preceq X_i\preceq I$) turns this into $\sqrt{\mathbb{E}Z}$.

**Solution.** Write $S=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ and $Z=\|S\|$, and put $Z_i=\|\sum_{j\ne i}X_j\|$.

*Self-bounding.* Since each $X_i$ is positive semidefinite, $\sum_{j\ne i}X_j\preceq\sum_{j=1}^n X_j$, and both sums are PSD. The operator norm is monotone for the PSD order ($\lambda_{\max}(A)\le\lambda_{\max}(B)$ when $0\preceq A\preceq B$), so $Z_i\le Z$, i.e. $Z-Z_i\ge 0$. By the triangle inequality, $Z\le Z_i+\|X_i\|\le Z_i+1$ almost surely, so $0\le Z-Z_i\le 1$ for all $i$. Let $v$ be a unit vector with $Z=v^T S v$ (possible since $S$ is symmetric). Then $Z_i=\|\sum_{j\ne i}X_j\|\ge v^T(\sum_{j\ne i}X_j)v$, whence $Z-Z_i\le v^T X_i v$. Summing over $i$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n (Z-Z_i)\le\sum_{i=1}^n v^T X_i v=v^T S v=Z.$$
Thus $Z$ is self-bounding. By the Efron–Stein inequality, $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}[(Z-Z_i)^2]$; since $0\le Z-Z_i\le 1$, $(Z-Z_i)^2\le Z-Z_i$, so $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\mathbb{E}[\sum_i(Z-Z_i)]\le\mathbb{E}Z$. Theorem 6.12 applied to $Z$ gives, with $h(u)=(1+u)\log(1+u)-u$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\le\exp\!\left(-\mathbb{E}Z\,h\!\left(\frac{t}{\mathbb{E}Z}\right)\right),\qquad t\ge 0,$$
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\le\mathbb{E}Z-t\}\le\exp\!\left(-\mathbb{E}Z\,h\!\left(\frac{-t}{\mathbb{E}Z}\right)\right),\qquad 0\le t\le\mathbb{E}Z,$$
as claimed.

*Expectation bound.* By the triangle inequality,
$$\mathbb{E}\|S\|\le\left\|\mathbb{E}S\right\|+\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i-\mathbb{E}X_i)\right\|.$$
Let $\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_n$ be independent Rademacher variables, independent of the $X_i$. Symmetrization (Lemma 11.4) gives
$$\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i-\mathbb{E}X_i)\right\|\le 2\,\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i X_i\right\|.$$
Condition on $X_1,\dots,X_n$. By the Rademacher version of Rudelson's inequality (Exercise 13.30, with $C = \sqrt{\pi/2}$) applied to the fixed symmetric matrices $X_i$,
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\left[\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i X_i\right\|\,\middle|\,X\right]\le\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\sqrt{2\log(2d)}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2\right\|^{1/2}.$$
Taking expectations and using Jensen's inequality (concavity of $\sqrt{\cdot}$),
$$\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i X_i\right\|\le\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\sqrt{2\log(2d)}\,\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2\right\|}.$$
Since $0\preceq X_i\preceq I$ (as $\|X_i\|\le 1$ and $X_i\succeq 0$), the eigenvalues of $X_i$ lie in $[0,1]$, so $X_i^2\preceq X_i$. Hence $\sum_i X_i^2\preceq\sum_i X_i=S$ and, both being PSD, $\|\sum_i X_i^2\|\le\|S\|=Z$. Therefore $\mathbb{E}\|\sum_i X_i^2\|\le\mathbb{E}Z$, and with $m=\mathbb{E}Z$,
$$\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i X_i\right\|\le\sqrt{\pi\log(2d)}\sqrt{m}.$$
Combining,
$$\mathbb{E}Z\le\left\|\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\right\|+2\sqrt{\pi\log(2d)}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}Z}.$$
*Remark on the constant.* The stated $2\sqrt{2\log d}$ follows from the same argument with a one-sided refinement of Rudelson's inequality (bounding $\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{\max}(\cdot)_+]$ directly, which replaces $\log(2d)$ by $\log d$ and absorbs the Rademacher-to-Gaussian conversion factor $\sqrt{\pi/2}$ of Exercise 13.30); the $\log(2d)$ above is otherwise the direct consequence of Theorem 13.8 as stated in the book. Either way, with $x=\sqrt{\mathbb{E}Z}$ and $\mu=\|\mathbb{E}\sum_i X_i\|$, $x^2\le C x+\mu$ with the book's constant $C=2\sqrt{2\log d}$ gives, by $Cx\le x^2/2+C^2/2$,
$$\mathbb{E}Z\le 8\log d+2\left\|\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\right\|,$$
as noted in the hint. Jensen's inequality gives the matching lower bound $\mathbb{E}Z\ge\|\mathbb{E}\sum_i X_i\|$.
