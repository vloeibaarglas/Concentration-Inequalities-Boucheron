**Central idea.** Write the operator norm as the supremum of an empirical process. With $\mathcal{T}$ a countable dense subset of $\{(u,v) : \|u\|=\|v\|=1\}$, we have $Z = \sup_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n u^T X_i v$. The summands $f_{u,v}(X_i) = u^T X_i v$ are centered and take values in $[-1,1]$. So when the $X_i$ are i.i.d., Bousquet's inequality (Theorem 12.5) applies. When they are only independent, Klein and Rio's variance bound applies. Symmetry of the $X_i$ gives the wimpy-variance bound $\sum_i\mathbb{E}[(u^T X_i v)^2] \le v^T (\sum_i\mathbb{E}X_i^2) v$, hence $\sigma^2 \le \|\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2\|$.

**Solution.** *Scope.* We prove the variance bound $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le v$ for independent $X_i$, exactly as printed. We prove the Bennett and Bernstein inequalities with variance factor $v$ and scale factor $1$ when the $X_i$ are i.i.d. That is the setting of Bousquet's inequality (Theorem 12.5), which the hint points to. For independent but non-identically distributed $X_i$, we do **not** prove the scale-$1$ exponential bound. That case remains an open caveat of this solution. All we can prove there is the weaker Klein–Rio tail at the end.

**Status: incomplete** (non-identical summands, exponential part only). The printed exercise assumes only independent $X_i$. For non-identically distributed $X_i$ we do not prove the Bennett or Bernstein inequality with scale factor $1$; the book itself notes (Section 12.5) that Bousquet's inequality requires identically distributed summands. The variance bound and the i.i.d. exponential bounds are complete.

*Representation as a supremum.* For a $d \times d$ matrix $A$, $\|A\| = \sup_{\|u\|=\|v\|=1} u^T A v$. Let $\mathcal{T} \subset \{(u,v) \in \mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}^d : \|u\|=\|v\|=1\}$ be countable and dense. The map $(u,v) \mapsto u^T S v$ is continuous and the product of spheres is compact, so
$$Z = \Big\|\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\Big\| = \sup_{(u,v) \in \mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n f_{u,v}(X_i), \qquad f_{u,v}(X) := u^T X v.$$
For each $(u,v) \in \mathcal{T}$ and each $i$,
$$\mathbb{E}f_{u,v}(X_i) = u^T (\mathbb{E}X_i) v = 0, \qquad |f_{u,v}(X_i)| \le \|X_i\|\,\|u\|\,\|v\| \le 1 \ \text{a.s.}$$

*Wimpy variance.* Since $X_i$ is symmetric, $(u^T X_i v)^2 \le \|X_i v\|^2 = v^T X_i^T X_i v = v^T X_i^2 v$. Hence, for every $(u,v)\in\mathcal{T}$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}\big[(u^T X_i v)^2\big] \le v^T \Big(\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}X_i^2\Big) v \le \Big\|\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2\Big\|,$$
using $\|v\|=1$. Therefore
$$\sigma^2 := \sup_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}\big[f_{u,v}(X_i)^2\big] \le \Big\|\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2\Big\|.$$
This holds whether or not the $X_i$ are identically distributed. Because the $f_{u,v}(X_i)$ are centered, $\sigma^2 = \sup_{(u,v)}\sum_i\mathrm{Var}(f_{u,v}(X_i))$. This is the quantity $V_n$ of Klein and Rio.

*Variance, independent $X_i$ (as printed).* Klein and Rio (2005), Corollary 1.1, covers this case. (The paper is *Concentration around the mean for maxima of empirical processes*, Ann. Probab. 33, 1060–1077, arXiv math/0506594.) Its hypotheses are:
- the random variables are independent, and need not be identically distributed;
- the class of functions is countable;
- each coordinate function $s_k$ takes values in $[-1,1]$, with $\mathbb{E}s_k(X_k)=0$.

Under these hypotheses it states
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le V_n + 2\mathbb{E}Z, \qquad V_n = \sup_{s}\sum_{k=1}^n\mathrm{Var}\,s_k(X_k).$$
The class $\{(f_{u,v},\dots,f_{u,v}) : (u,v)\in\mathcal{T}\}$ satisfies these hypotheses, and for it $V_n = \sigma^2$. Therefore
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le \sigma^2 + 2\mathbb{E}Z \le \Big\|\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2\Big\| + 2\mathbb{E}Z = v.$$
In the i.i.d. case, the same bound also follows from Theorem 12.5 (or Theorem 11.10). The book's Theorem 12.9 (Section 12.5, stated there without proof and attributed to Klein and Rio) also gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le2\mathbb{E}Z+\sigma^2$ for independent, non-identically distributed summands with $|X_{i,s}|\le1$. The pre-repair version of this solution restricted the variance bound to i.i.d. $X_i$. Corollary 1.1 makes that restriction unnecessary.

*Exponential bounds, i.i.d. $X_i$.* Let the $X_i$ be i.i.d. random matrices. Then the vectors $(f_{u,v}(X_i))_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{T}}$, $i=1,\dots,n$, are i.i.d., centered, and bounded above by $1$. So Bousquet's inequality (Theorem 12.5) applies. With $v' := 2\mathbb{E}Z + \sigma^2 \le v$, it gives, for $\lambda \ge 0$,
$$\log \mathbb{E}e^{\lambda(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)} \le v'\phi(\lambda) \le v\,\phi(\lambda), \qquad \phi(\lambda) = e^\lambda - \lambda - 1 \ge 0.$$
The Chernoff argument, as in Theorem 12.5, yields the Bennett inequality
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z \ge \mathbb{E}Z + t\} \le e^{-v h(t/v)}, \qquad h(u) = (1+u)\log(1+u) - u, \quad t \ge 0.$$
Using $h(u)\ge u^2/(2(1+u/3))$, as in Section 2.7, gives the Bernstein inequality
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z \ge \mathbb{E}Z + t\} \le \exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2(v + t/3)}\right), \quad t \ge 0.$$
Both inequalities have variance factor $v$ and scale factor $1$. The scale factor comes from $f_{u,v}\le 1$.

*Open for non-identical $X_i$.* Theorem 12.5 is stated and proved only for identically distributed summands. Klein–Rio, Theorem 1.1, handles independent non-identical summands under the hypotheses listed above, with $v = 2\mathbb{E}Z + V_n$. In our setting that $v$ is at most our $v$. Their part (c) gives
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z \ge \mathbb{E}Z + x\} \le \exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2v + 3x}\right), \qquad x\ge 0.$$
This is a Bernstein-type inequality with variance factor $v$. Its denominator $2v+3x$ is larger than the scale-$1$ denominator $2v + 2x/3$. Their part (a) bound, $\log\mathbb{E}e^{t(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)}\le t\,v\,(\exp((e^{2t}-1)/2)-1)/2$, is similarly weaker than $v\phi(t)$.

We therefore do not prove the Bennett bound $e^{-vh(t/v)}$, or the Bernstein bound with $t/3$, for non-identically distributed $X_i$. The independent audit claimed that Klein–Rio gives "the same bound with the same $v$". That claim is incorrect: as shown above, Klein–Rio's bound is weaker. ∎
