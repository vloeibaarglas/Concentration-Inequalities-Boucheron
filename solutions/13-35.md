**Central idea.** Condition on $N_n$ and split the variance: $\mathrm{Var}(Z) = \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Var}(Z \mid N_n)] + \mathrm{Var}(\mathbb{E}[Z \mid N_n])$. The first term is handled by Theorem 11.10 applied to $Z_k$ (giving $k\sigma^2 + 2\mathbb{E}Z_k$); the second is $\mathrm{Var}(h(N_n))$ for the deterministic $h(k) = \mathbb{E}Z_k$, bounded by the Poisson Poincaré inequality (Exercise 3.21). The increments $h(k+1)-h(k)$ are controlled by the leave-one-out symmetrization $(k+1)h(k) \ge k\,h(k+1)$.

**Solution.** Write $h(k) = \mathbb{E}[Z_k] = \mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}} \sum_{i=1}^k X_{i,s}$ (deterministic; $h(0)=0$). By independence of $N_n$ and the $X_i$, $\mathbb{E}[Z \mid N_n] = h(N_n)$, and the variance decomposition gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z) = \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Var}(Z \mid N_n)] + \mathrm{Var}(h(N_n)).$$
Given $N_n = k$, $Z = Z_k$. Theorem 11.10 applies to $Z_k$ (the $X_i$ are i.i.d., centered, $|X_{i,s}| \le 1$): with the problem's $\sigma^2 = \sup_s \mathbb{E}X_{1,s}^2$,
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z_k) \le 2\mathbb{E}Z_k + k\sigma^2 = 2h(k) + k\sigma^2.$$
Hence $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Var}(Z \mid N_n)] \le 2\mathbb{E}Z + \mathbb{E}N_n\,\sigma^2$.

For the second term, the Poisson Poincaré inequality (Exercise 3.21) with $Df(k) = f(k+1)-f(k)$ gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(h(N_n)) \le \mathbb{E}N_n \cdot \mathbb{E}\big[(h(N_n+1) - h(N_n))^2\big].$$
Given $N_n = k$, $\mathbb{E}[Z_{N_n+1} - Z_{N_n} \mid N_n = k] = \mathbb{E}[Z_{k+1} - Z_k] = h(k+1)-h(k)$ (here $N_{n+1}$ denotes $N_n+1$), so
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z) \le \mathbb{E}N_n\,\sigma^2 + 2\mathbb{E}Z + \mathbb{E}N_n\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(\mathbb{E}[Z_{N_n+1} - Z_{N_n} \mid N_n]\right)^2\right],$$
the first claimed inequality.

It remains to bound the increments. Fix $k \ge 1$ and, for $j = 1,\ldots,k+1$, let $Z_{k+1}^{(j)} = \max_{s\in\mathcal{T}} \sum_{i \ne j} X_{i,s}$ be the leave-one-out supremum; $\mathbb{E}Z_{k+1}^{(j)} = h(k)$ by identical distribution. Let $s^*$ achieve $Z_{k+1} = \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} X_{i,s^*}$ ($\mathcal{T}$ finite). Then $Z_{k+1}^{(j)} \ge \sum_{i \ne j} X_{i,s^*} = Z_{k+1} - X_{j,s^*}$, and summing over $j$,
$$\sum_{j=1}^{k+1} Z_{k+1}^{(j)} \ge (k+1)Z_{k+1} - \sum_{j=1}^{k+1} X_{j,s^*} = kZ_{k+1}.$$
Taking expectations, $(k+1)h(k) \ge k\,h(k+1)$, i.e.
$$h(k+1) - h(k) \le \frac{h(k)}{k}, \qquad k \ge 1,$$
and for $k=0$, $h(1)-h(0) = h(1) = h(\max(0,1))/\max(0,1)$. Thus, with $\mathbb{E}[Z_{\max(N_n,1)} \mid N_n] = h(\max(N_n,1))$,
$$\left(\mathbb{E}[Z_{N_n+1} - Z_{N_n} \mid N_n]\right)^2 = \big(h(N_n+1)-h(N_n)\big)^2 \le \left(\frac{\mathbb{E}[Z_{\max(N_n,1)} \mid N_n]}{\max(N_n,1)}\right)^2,$$
and taking expectations and multiplying by $\mathbb{E}N_n$ yields the second inequality. ∎
