**Central idea.** Binomial approximation of the Poisson law: for $M>n$, replace $N_n$ by $N^{(M)}\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(M,n/M)$ and $Z$ by $Z^{(M)}=\sup_s\sum_{k=1}^MB_kX_{k,s}$ with $B_k\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(n/M)$ independent. For fixed $M$ the pairs $(B_k,X_k)$ are independent across $k$, so Efron–Stein applies directly: the resampling cost splits into $B_kX_{k,\hat s}^2$ (telescoping to the weak variance) plus $(n/M)\sigma^2$ per coordinate. Letting $M\to\infty$ (Skorokhod coupling + uniform integrability) recovers the Poissonized bound. This avoids Poisson thinning of a single draw $Y_iX_{i,s}$, which does not have the claimed law.

**Remark (erratum fixed).** The identity $(\sum_{i=1}^mY_iX_{i,s})_s\stackrel{d}{=}(\sum_{k=1}^{N_n}X_{k,s})_s$ with $Y_i\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(n/m)$ is false: e.g.\ with Rademacher $V_i$, $n=6$, $m=4$, $\mathbb{E}[(\sum_iY_iV_i)^2]=n(1+n/m)=15\ne6=\mathbb{E}[(\sum_{k\le N_n}V_k)^2]$. The correct thinning is $\sum_{k\le N_n}X_{k,s}\stackrel{d}{=}\sum_i\sum_{j\le Y_i}X_{i,j,s}$ (independent copies per group). The characteristic-function computation in the earlier version also dropped the rate $n/m$ and moved an expectation inside an exponential. The proof below establishes the claimed bound without any thinning identity.

**Solution.** Use the notation of Exercise 13-35: $\mathcal{T}$ is finite, the $X_i=(X_{i,s})_{s\in\mathcal{T}}$ are i.i.d.\ centered with $|X_{i,s}|\le1$, $N_n\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(n)$ is independent of the $X_i$, $Z=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_n}X_{i,s}$, and $\sigma^2=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\mathbb{E}X_{1,s}^2$.

Fix an integer $M>n$. Let $B_1,\dots,B_M$ be i.i.d.\ $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(n/M)$, independent of the $X_i$, and put
$$N^{(M)}=\sum_{k=1}^MB_k\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(M,n/M),\qquad Z^{(M)}=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{k=1}^MB_kX_{k,s}.$$
The pairs $(B_k,X_k)$ are independent across $k$. By the Efron–Stein inequality,
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z^{(M)})\le\sum_{k=1}^M\mathbb{E}\big[(Z^{(M)}-Z^{(M)}_k)_+^2\big],$$
where $Z^{(M)}_k$ replaces $(B_k,X_k)$ by an independent copy $(B_k',X_k')$. Let $\hat s\in\mathcal{T}$ attain the supremum in $Z^{(M)}$ ($\mathcal{T}$ finite). Then
$$(Z^{(M)}-Z^{(M)}_k)_+\le\big(B_kX_{k,\hat s}-B_k'X_{k,\hat s}'\big)_+,$$
and, with $\mathbb{E}'$ over the primed variables ($\hat s$ fixed),
$$\mathbb{E}'\big[(B_kX_{k,\hat s}-B_k'X_{k,\hat s}')_+^2\big]\le\mathbb{E}'\big[(B_kX_{k,\hat s}-B_k'X_{k,\hat s}')^2\big]=B_kX_{k,\hat s}^2+\tfrac{n}{M}\mathbb{E}[X_{1,\hat s}^2],$$
since $\mathbb{E}'[B_k'X_{k,\hat s}']=\mathbb{E}[B_k']\mathbb{E}[X_{1,\hat s}]=0$ (centered) and $\mathbb{E}'[(B_k'X_{k,\hat s}')^2]=\mathbb{E}[(B_k')^2]\mathbb{E}[X_{1,\hat s}^2]=\frac{n}{M}\mathbb{E}[X_{1,\hat s}^2]$. With $\mathbb{E}[X_{1,\hat s}^2]\le\sigma^2$ and taking $\mathbb{E}$ over the unprimed variables,
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z^{(M)})\le\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{k=1}^MB_kX_{k,\hat s}^2\Big]+n\sigma^2\le\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{k=1}^MB_kX_{k,s}^2+n\sigma^2,\tag{*}$$
using $\sum_kB_kX_{k,\hat s}^2\le\sup_s\sum_kB_kX_{k,s}^2$ pointwise.

Given $N^{(M)}=m$, the set $\{k:B_k=1\}$ is a uniform $m$-subset independent of the $X_k$, so $(\sum_kB_kX_{k,s})_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\stackrel{d}{=}(\sum_{j=1}^{N^{(M)}}X_{j,s})_{s\in\mathcal{T}}$ with $N^{(M)}$ independent of the $X_j$ on the right. Hence, $\mathcal{T}$ being finite,
$$Z^{(M)}\stackrel{d}{=}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{N^{(M)}}X_{j,s},\qquad\mathbb{E}\sup_{s}\sum_{k}B_kX_{k,s}^2=\mathbb{E}\sup_{s}\sum_{j=1}^{N^{(M)}}X_{j,s}^2.$$

Let $M\to\infty$. By Skorokhod's representation, couple $\tilde N^{(M)}\stackrel{d}{=}N^{(M)}$ and $\tilde N\stackrel{d}{=}N_n$ on one space with $\tilde N^{(M)}\to\tilde N$ a.s.; fix i.i.d.\ $(X_j)$ independent of these. Then $\tilde Z^{(M)}:=\sup_s\sum_{j\le\tilde N^{(M)}}X_{j,s}\stackrel{d}{=}Z^{(M)}$ converges a.s.\ to $\tilde Z:=\sup_s\sum_{j\le\tilde N}X_{j,s}\stackrel{d}{=}Z$ (finite $\mathcal{T}$; the integer upper limits are eventually equal). Since $|\tilde Z^{(M)}|\le\tilde N^{(M)}$, $|X_{j,s}|\le1$, and $\mathbb{E}[(\tilde N^{(M)})^2]=n^2+n(1-n/M)\to n^2+n=\mathbb{E}[\tilde N^2]$, uniform integrability gives $\mathrm{Var}(\tilde Z^{(M)})\to\mathrm{Var}(\tilde Z)$. Likewise $\tilde W^{(M)}:=\sup_s\sum_{j\le\tilde N^{(M)}}X_{j,s}^2\to\sup_s\sum_{j\le\tilde N}X_{j,s}^2$ a.s.\ with $0\le\tilde W^{(M)}\le\tilde N^{(M)}$, so its expectations converge. Passing to the limit in $(*)$,
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_n}X_{j,s}^2+n\sigma^2=\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_n}X_{i,s}^2+\mathbb{E}N_n\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\mathbb{E}X_{1,s}^2,$$
as claimed. ∎
