**Central idea.** Apply Exercise 13.35 to $X_{i,t}=\mathbb{1}_{\{U_i\in A_t\}}-P(A_t)$, where the $U_i\sim P$ are i.i.d. This gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le n\sigma^2+2\mathbb{E}Z+n\,\mathbb{E}[(g(N')/N')^2]$, with $g(k)=\mathbb{E}Z_k$ and $N'=\max(N_n,1)$. The chaining bound behind Theorem 13.7 holds at every sample size $k$ in the form $g(k)\le\kappa_0(\sigma\sqrt{kV\ell}+V\ell)$, $\ell=\log(e/\sigma)$; this is the quadratic inequality of Lemma 13.5 before its proviso is used. Feeding it through $\mathbb{E}\sqrt{N_n}\le\sqrt n$, $\mathbb{E}[1/N']\le 2/n$ and $\mathbb{E}[1/N'^2]\le6/n^2$ gives the stated bound plus $\kappa(V\ell+V^2\ell^2/n)$. These extra terms are absorbed exactly when $n\sigma^2\ge V\ell$, which is the regime of Theorem 13.7. Without that regime the printed bound is false. A class of $1/\sigma^2$ disjoint sets with $n=1$ shows the ratio of the variance to the bound is unbounded.

**Solution.** **Erratum (statement needs Theorem 13.7's proviso).** Theorem 13.7 is proved only under the proviso $\sigma\ge24\sqrt{V\log(4e^2/\sigma)/(5n)}$, that is, $n\gtrsim V\log(e/\sigma)/\sigma^2$. If "the notation of Theorem 13.7" is read as importing that proviso, the printed statement is correct and is proved below; the weaker condition $n\sigma^2\ge V\log(e/\sigma)$ suffices. Read literally, for all $n$, the statement is false. (The pre-repair version of this solution said that outside the regime "the trivial bound is absorbed into the universal constant". That is wrong, as the following counterexample shows.)

*Counterexample.* Fix $\sigma\in(0,1)$ and let $m=\lfloor1/\sigma^2\rfloor$. Let $\mathcal{A}=\{A_1,\dots,A_m\}$ be disjoint sets with $P(A_t)=\sigma^2$. The class has VC dimension $V=1$: any point of $A_1$ is shattered, since $A_1$ contains it and $A_2$ does not ($m\ge2$), but no two points are, since no set contains both. By Poisson thinning, the counts $N_t=\#\{i\le N_n:U_i\in A_t\}$ are i.i.d. Poisson$(n\sigma^2)$. The Kac supremum is
$$Z=\max_t N_t-\sigma^2N_n.$$
Take $n=1$. Then $\max_tN_t$ is the maximum of $m\approx\sigma^{-2}$ i.i.d. Poisson$(\sigma^2)$ variables. It is nonzero with probability $\approx1-e^{-1}$, so $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\approx(1-e^{-1})e^{-1}\approx0.2325$ for all small $\sigma$. With $\ell=\log(e/\sigma)$ and $V=1$, the bound without $\kappa$ is
$$B=n\sigma^2+\sigma\sqrt{Vn\ell}+V\sigma^2\ell=\sigma^2+\sigma\sqrt{\ell}+\sigma^2\ell\to0.$$
We computed $\mathrm{Var}(Z)$ exactly, with Poisson sums at 40-digit precision, keeping the $-\sigma^2N_n$ term. The results:

| $\sigma$ | $0.1$ | $0.03$ | $0.01$ | $0.003$ | $0.001$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathrm{Var}(Z)$ | $0.2337$ | $0.2327$ | $0.2326$ | $0.2325$ | $0.2325$ |
| $\mathrm{Var}(Z)/B$ | $1.04$ | $3.39$ | $9.56$ | $29.4$ | $82.4$ |

A Monte Carlo check with $4\times10^5$ replications agrees: $0.2336$ at $\sigma=0.1$ and $0.2328$ at $\sigma=0.03$.

The ratio grows like $1/(\sigma\sqrt\ell)$, so no universal $\kappa$ makes the bound hold. The term $n\sigma^2$ cannot help here: it equals $\sigma^2$. The same failure occurs when the Poisson mean is not an integer. With mean $n=\sigma$, the ratio is $0.92, 1.89, 3.36, 6.09, 10.3$ at the same five values of $\sigma$ (exact; growth $\asymp\sigma^{-1/2}\ell^{-1/2}$). Here $n\sigma^2=\sigma^3\ll V\ell$, far outside the regime.

**Corrected statement.** Let $\sigma\in(0,1]$ and $\ell=\log(e/\sigma)\ge1$. There is a universal constant $\kappa$ such that, for every $n\ge1$,
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le n\sigma^2+\kappa\sigma\sqrt{Vn\ell}+\kappa V\sigma^2\ell+\kappa V\ell+\kappa\frac{V^2\ell^2}{n}.$$
In particular, if $n\sigma^2\ge V\ell$, then
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le n\sigma^2+\kappa\sigma\sqrt{Vn\log\frac e\sigma}+\kappa V\sigma^2\log\frac e\sigma.$$
The condition $n\sigma^2\ge V\ell$ is implied by Theorem 13.7's proviso, since that proviso gives $n\sigma^2\ge\frac{576}{5}V\log(4e^2/\sigma)\ge V\ell$.

*Proof.* **Setting.** Let $U_1,U_2,\dots$ be i.i.d. with law $P$, and let $N_n\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(n)$ be independent of them. Let $\mathcal{A}=\{A_t:t\in\mathcal{T}\}$ be a VC class of dimension $V$ with $P(A_t)\le\sigma^2$. Exercise 13.35 is stated for finite $\mathcal{T}$. We prove the bound for every finite subclass, whose VC dimension is at most $V$. It then passes to countable $\mathcal{T}$ by monotone convergence of the suprema along an increasing sequence of finite subclasses, with $\mathbb{E}Z^2<\infty$ since $|Z|\le N_n+n$.

If $V=0$, no point is shattered. Then all $A_t$ have the same trace on every point, so the class is a single set $A$. In that case $Z=\sum_{i\le N_n}(\mathbb{1}_A(U_i)-P(A))$ is compound Poisson with variance $n\,\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{1}_A-P(A))^2=nP(A)(1-P(A))\le n\sigma^2$. So assume $V\ge1$.

Put $X_{i,t}=\mathbb{1}_{\{U_i\in A_t\}}-P(A_t)$. The vectors $X_i$ are i.i.d., centered, and satisfy $|X_{i,t}|\le1$. Moreover $\sup_t\mathbb{E}X_{1,t}^2=\sup_tP(A_t)(1-P(A_t))\le\sigma^2$. Let $Z_k=\sup_t\sum_{i\le k}X_{i,t}$, $g(k)=\mathbb{E}Z_k$ for $k\ge1$, $g(0)=0$, and $N'=\max(N_n,1)$. Exercise 13.35 gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le n\sigma^2+2\mathbb{E}Z+n\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\frac{g(N')}{N'}\Big)^2\Big].\tag{1}$$

**Step 1: $g(k)\le\kappa_0(\sigma\sqrt{kV\ell}+V\ell)$ for every $k\ge1$.** Let $\mathcal{A}'=\mathcal{A}\cup\{\emptyset\}$. Then $P(\emptyset)=0\le\sigma^2$, and $\sup_{\mathcal{A}'}\sum_{i\le k}X_{i,t}=(Z_k)_+\ge Z_k$, so $g(k)\le\mathbb{E}(Z_k)_+$.

Adding $\emptyset$ raises the VC dimension by at most one. Suppose $\mathcal{A}'$ shatters a set $S$ with $|S|\ge1$, and fix $x\in S$. Put $S'=S\setminus\{x\}$. For any $T\subseteq S'$, some $B\in\mathcal{A}'$ has $B\cap S=T\cup\{x\}$. Then $B\ne\emptyset$, so $B\in\mathcal{A}$ and $B\cap S'=T$. Thus $\mathcal{A}$ shatters $S'$. Hence $V'\le V+1\le2V$.

Write $Y_k=k^{-1/2}(Z_k)_+$ for the normalized supremum over $\mathcal{A}'$, and $y=\mathbb{E}Y_k\ge0$. The proof of Lemma 13.5 shows, without using its proviso, that
$$y\le D\sqrt{\sigma^2+y/\sqrt k}.$$
The ingredients are symmetrization, Lemma 13.1 applied conditionally, $\delta_k^2\le\sigma^2+Y_k/\sqrt k$, and Jensen. Here $D=D_\sigma$ is the entropy integral of Lemma 13.5 for $\mathcal{A}'$. As in the proof of Theorem 13.7, Haussler's bound (Lemma 13.6) gives $D\le24\sqrt{V'\log(4e^2/\sigma)}$. Since $\log(4e^2/\sigma)=\log4+1+\ell\le(2+\log4)\ell$ for $\ell\ge1$,
$$D^2\le576\cdot2(2+\log4)\,V\ell=:c_0V\ell.$$
Squaring, $y^2\le D^2\sigma^2+D^2y/\sqrt k$. Hence
$$y\le\frac{D^2}{2\sqrt k}+\sqrt{\frac{D^4}{4k}+D^2\sigma^2}\le\frac{D^2}{\sqrt k}+D\sigma.$$
Therefore $g(k)\le\sqrt k\,y\le D^2+D\sigma\sqrt k\le\kappa_0(V\ell+\sigma\sqrt{kV\ell})$ with $\kappa_0=c_0$, using $c_0\ge\sqrt{c_0}$. The bound also holds trivially for $k=0$.

**Step 2: the mean.** By Step 1 and Jensen ($\mathbb{E}\sqrt{N_n}\le\sqrt n$),
$$\mathbb{E}Z=\mathbb{E}\,g(N_n)\le\kappa_0\big(\sigma\sqrt{nV\ell}+V\ell\big).$$

**Step 3: the Poisson term.** By Step 1 and $(a+b)^2\le2a^2+2b^2$,
$$\Big(\frac{g(N')}{N'}\Big)^2\le2\kappa_0^2\Big(\frac{\sigma^2V\ell}{N'}+\frac{V^2\ell^2}{N'^2}\Big).$$
Write $N=N_n$. Two elementary inequalities hold for all integers $N\ge0$:
- $1/N'\le2/(N+1)$, since $1\le2$ for $N=0$ and $N+1\le2N$ for $N\ge1$;
- $1/N'^2\le6/((N+1)(N+2))$, since $1\le3$ for $N=0$, and $(N+1)(N+2)\le6N^2$ for $N\ge1$, with equality at $N=1$.

For $N\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(n)$,
$$\mathbb{E}\frac1{N+1}=\frac{1-e^{-n}}n\le\frac1n,\qquad\mathbb{E}\frac1{(N+1)(N+2)}=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{j\ge2}e^{-n}\frac{n^j}{j!}\le\frac1{n^2}.$$
Hence $\mathbb{E}[1/N']\le2/n$, $\mathbb{E}[1/N'^2]\le6/n^2$, and
$$n\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\frac{g(N')}{N'}\Big)^2\Big]\le4\kappa_0^2\sigma^2V\ell+12\kappa_0^2\frac{V^2\ell^2}n.$$

**Step 4: conclusion.** Insert Steps 2 and 3 into (1):
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le n\sigma^2+2\kappa_0\sigma\sqrt{nV\ell}+2\kappa_0V\ell+4\kappa_0^2\sigma^2V\ell+12\kappa_0^2\frac{V^2\ell^2}n.$$
This is the corrected general bound with $\kappa=12\kappa_0^2$.

If $n\sigma^2\ge V\ell$, then $V\ell=\sqrt{V\ell}\sqrt{V\ell}\le\sqrt{V\ell}\,\sigma\sqrt n$ and $V^2\ell^2/n\le\sigma^2V\ell$. The last bound then becomes
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le n\sigma^2+\kappa\,\sigma\sqrt{Vn\log\frac e\sigma}+\kappa\,V\sigma^2\log\frac e\sigma,$$
with $\kappa$ a universal constant. ∎

*Remark.* The leading term $n\sigma^2$ is $\mathbb{E}N_n\cdot\sigma^2$, the Poisson-count variance of a single set of mass $\sigma^2$. In the counterexample above, the variance is instead driven by the event that some set is hit at all, which has probability of order $1-e^{-nm\sigma^2}$. This is exactly the small-$n$ effect that the terms $V\ell+V^2\ell^2/n$ track.
