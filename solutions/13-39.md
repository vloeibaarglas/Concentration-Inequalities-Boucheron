**Central idea.** Write $Z=\sqrt n\,\|g\|_2$ with $g(x)=(F_n(x)-x)/\sqrt{x(1-x)}\in L^2[0,1]$ (the empirical measure needs its $1/n$ normalization, as in Exercise 13-38); by Riesz–Fischer $\|g\|_2$ is the supremum of $\langle h,g\rangle$ over a countable dense subset of the $L^2$ unit ball, so $Z$ is the supremum of the centered empirical process $\sum_iY_{i,h}$. For the variance, Efron–Stein with $|Z-Z^{(i)}|\le\sqrt n\,\|g-g^{(i)}\|_2$ reduces to $\frac1{n^2}\int_{X_i\wedge X_i'}^{X_i\vee X_i'}\frac{dx}{x(1-x)}=\frac1{n^2}|\mathrm{logit}(X_i')-\mathrm{logit}(X_i)|$; since $\mathrm{logit}(X)$ is standard logistic and $\mathbb{E}|L-L'|=2$ for iid logistics, Efron–Stein gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le1=\mathbb{E}Z^2$ — no better than the trivial bound, the heavy-tailed logit influence being the price of the $1/(x(1-x))$ weight.

**Solution.** As in Exercise 13-38, $P_n([0,x])$ must be read as the empirical distribution function $F_n(x)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{1}_{\{X_i\le x\}}$ with $X_1,\dots,X_n\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]$; without the $1/n$ the stated $\mathbb{E}Z^2=1$ fails. Put
$$Z^2=n\int_0^1\frac{(F_n(x)-x)^2}{x(1-x)}dx=n\|g\|_2^2,\qquad g(x)=\frac{F_n(x)-x}{\sqrt{x(1-x)}}.$$
$Z<\infty$ a.s.: on $[0,X_{(1)})$ we have $F_n=0$ and the integrand is $x/(1-x)$; on $[X_{(1)},\tfrac12]$ it is at most $1/(x(1-x))\le2/x$, integrable since $X_{(1)}>0$ a.s.; the right half is symmetric, using $X_{(n)}<1$ a.s. Hence $g\in L^2[0,1]$ a.s.

*Supremum representation.* By Riesz–Fischer, $\|g\|_2=\sup_{\|h\|_2\le1}\langle h,g\rangle$. Fix a countable dense $\mathcal{D}$ in the $L^2$ unit ball ($L^2[0,1]$ is separable); by continuity of $h\mapsto\langle h,g\rangle$,
$$Z=\sqrt n\sup_{h\in\mathcal{D}}\int_0^1h(x)g(x)dx=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{i=1}^nY_{i,h},$$
where $Y_{i,h}=\frac1{\sqrt n}\int_0^1h(x)\,\frac{\mathbb{1}_{\{X_i\le x\}}-x}{\sqrt{x(1-x)}}dx$. For fixed $h$ the $Y_{i,h}$ are independent across $i$ (each depends only on $X_i$), integrable by Cauchy–Schwarz since both factors are in $L^2$, and centered because $\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{1}_{\{X_i\le x\}}]=x$. Thus $Z$ is the supremum of an empirical process.

*Mean.* By Tonelli, $\mathbb{E}Z^2=n\int_0^1\frac{\mathbb{E}[(F_n(x)-x)^2]}{x(1-x)}dx$. Since $\mathbb{E}[(F_n(x)-x)^2]=\mathrm{Var}(F_n(x))=x(1-x)/n$,
$$\mathbb{E}Z^2=n\int_0^1\frac{x(1-x)/n}{x(1-x)}dx=1,$$
the usual Anderson–Darling normalization.

*Variance (Efron–Stein).* Let $X_i'$ be an independent copy of $X_i$ and $Z^{(i)}$ the statistic with $X_i$ replaced by $X_i'$. By the reverse triangle inequality,
$$|Z-Z^{(i)}|=\sqrt n\,\big|\|g\|_2-\|g^{(i)}\|_2\big|\le\sqrt n\,\|g-g^{(i)}\|_2.$$
Since $F_n-F_n^{(i)}=\frac1n(\mathbb{1}_{\{X_i\le\cdot\}}-\mathbb{1}_{\{X_i'\le\cdot\}})$ takes values in $\{-\frac1n,0,\frac1n\}$,
$$\|g-g^{(i)}\|_2^2=\frac1{n^2}\int_0^1\frac{|\mathbb{1}_{\{X_i\le x\}}-\mathbb{1}_{\{X_i'\le x\}}|}{x(1-x)}dx=\frac1{n^2}\int_{X_i\wedge X_i'}^{X_i\vee X_i'}\frac{dx}{x(1-x)},$$
the integrand being $\mathbb{1}_{[X_i\wedge X_i',\,X_i\vee X_i')}(x)$. With $\frac1{x(1-x)}=\frac1x+\frac1{1-x}$,
$$\int\frac{dx}{x(1-x)}=\log\frac{x}{1-x}=:\mathrm{logit}(x),$$
so $(Z-Z^{(i)})^2\le\frac1n\,|\mathrm{logit}(X_i')-\mathrm{logit}(X_i)|$. Efron–Stein (Theorem 3.1) gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big[(Z-Z^{(i)})^2\big]\le\frac1{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big|\mathrm{logit}(X_i')-\mathrm{logit}(X_i)\big|.$$

*The logit computation.* For $X\sim\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]$, $L:=\mathrm{logit}(X)$ satisfies $\mathbb{P}\{L\le l\}=\mathbb{P}\{X\le e^l/(1+e^l)\}=1/(1+e^{-l})$: $L$ is standard logistic. For iid $L,L'$ with cdf $F$,
$$|l-l'|=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big|\mathbb{1}_{\{l>t\}}-\mathbb{1}_{\{l'>t\}}\big|dt,$$
and $\mathbb{E}|\mathbb{1}_{\{L>t\}}-\mathbb{1}_{\{L'>t\}}|=2F(t)(1-F(t))$ (difference of iid Bernoullis), so by Tonelli $\mathbb{E}|L-L'|=2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(t)(1-F(t))dt$. For the standard logistic, $F(t)(1-F(t))=e^t/(1+e^t)^2$, and $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^t}{(1+e^t)^2}dt=1$ (substitute $u=e^t$). Hence
$$\mathbb{E}\big|\mathrm{logit}(X_i')-\mathrm{logit}(X_i)\big|=2.$$
Therefore
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\frac12\cdot2=1.$$

*Comparison.* $\mathrm{Var}(Z)=\mathbb{E}Z^2-(\mathbb{E}Z)^2\le\mathbb{E}Z^2=1$, so the Efron–Stein bound $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le1$ coincides with the trivial bound and yields no improvement — the $1/(x(1-x))$ weight makes the single-observation influence $|\mathrm{logit}(X_i')-\mathrm{logit}(X_i)|$ heavy-tailed (its mean is $2$, versus $\mathbb{E}|X_i-X_i'|=1/3$ for Cramér–von Mises), which is exactly what the Anderson–Darling weight pays for its $\mathbb{E}Z^2=1$ normalization. ∎
