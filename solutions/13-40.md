**Central idea.** On $[0,1)$, the process $M(s)=(F_n(s)-s)/(1-s)$ is a martingale in $s$. Its variance at time $t$ is $v_t/n$ with $v_t=t/(1-t)$. On $[0,1/2]$ its summands are bounded by $1$. Doob's inequality, applied to $e^{\lambda\sqrt nM}$, turns Bernstein's bound at a fixed time into a sub-Gaussian bound for the running supremum.

Peel $[n^{-1/2},1/2]$ into geometric shells $[t_{k+1},t_k]$ with $t_k=\rho^{-k}/2$. On each shell, $|V(s)|$ is at most $\sqrt{\rho/(1-t_k)}$ times a normalized running supremum. There are $K\approx\log n/(2\log\rho)$ shells. A maximal inequality over $2K$ nearly-Gaussian variables then gives $\mathbb{E}Z\le\sqrt{2\log\log n}\,(1+o(1))$.

The exact inequality $\mathbb{E}Z\le\sqrt{2\log\log n}$ is false. An exact finite computation refutes it at $n=16$, and the Gumbel limit says it fails for all large $n$. The Efron–Stein inequality gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le3+o(1)$. The $1/\log\log n$ variance rate is not proved here.

**Solution.** Notation:
- $F_n(s)=P_n([0,s])$;
- $V(s)=\sqrt n\,(F_n(s)-s)/\sqrt{s(1-s)}$;
- $J=[n^{-1/2},1-n^{-1/2}]$, so that $Z=\sup_{s\in J}|V(s)|$;
- $\ell_n=\log\log n$.

All processes here are right-continuous with left limits, so the suprema are measurable.

**Erratum (the exact inequality is false).** As printed, $\mathbb{E}Z\le\sqrt{2\log\log n}$ fails. Here are three pieces of evidence.

*Exact finite computation at $n=16$.* For any points $s_1<\dots<s_k$ in $J$, we have $Z\ge\max_j|V(s_j)|$. The vector $(nF_n(s_j))_j$ is a function of multinomial cell counts, so $\mathbb{E}\max_j|V(s_j)|$ is a finite sum. For $n=16$ and the $7$ equally spaced points $s_j=\frac14+\frac{j}{12}$, $j=0,\dots,6$, which fill $J=[\frac14,\frac34]$, enumerating all cell-count vectors gives
$$\mathbb{E}Z\ge\mathbb{E}\max_j|V(s_j)|=1.44118>1.42813=\sqrt{2\log\log16}.$$
The probabilities sum to $1$ to twelve digits.

*Simulation.* We computed the supremum exactly from the order statistics and checked both one-sided values at every jump inside $J$ and at both endpoints:

| $n$ | $16$ | $10^2$ | $10^3$ | $10^4$ | $10^5$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| replications | $2\times10^5$ | $10^5$ | $2\times10^4$ | $4000$ | $1500$ |
| $\mathbb{E}Z$ (s.e.) | $1.672\ (.001)$ | $1.980\ (.002)$ | $2.177\ (.004)$ | $2.320\ (.009)$ | $2.408\ (.014)$ |
| $\sqrt{2\log\log n}$ | $1.428$ | $1.748$ | $1.966$ | $2.107$ | $2.211$ |

*Asymptotically.* Assume uniform integrability, which we do not prove. The Gumbel limit in the hint then gives
$$\mathbb{E}Z-\sqrt{2\ell_n}\sim\frac{\log\sqrt{\ell_n/(2\pi)}+\gamma}{\sqrt{2\ell_n}}.$$
This is positive once $\ell_n>2\pi e^{-2\gamma}\approx1.98$. So the inequality is not merely off at small $n$; under that assumption it fails for every large $n$.

**Corrected statement.** We prove
$$\mathbb{E}Z\le\sqrt{2\log\log n}\,(1+o(1)),\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}Z}{\sqrt{2\log\log n}}\le1.$$
We also prove $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le3+n^{-1/4}\mathbb{E}Z=3+o(1)$.

**Step 1: a martingale and its maximal inequality.** For a single uniform $X$ and $s\in[0,1)$, put $m(s)=(\mathbb 1\{X\le s\}-s)/(1-s)$. Let $\mathcal G_s=\sigma(\mathbb 1\{X\le r\}:r\le s)$ and take $s<s'<1$.
- On $\{X\le s\}$: $m(s')=1=m(s)$.
- On $\{X>s\}$: $\mathbb{P}(X\le s'\mid X>s)=(s'-s)/(1-s)$, so
$$\mathbb{E}[m(s')\mid\mathcal G_s]=\frac{\frac{s'-s}{1-s}-s'}{1-s'}=\frac{-s(1-s')}{(1-s)(1-s')}=-\frac{s}{1-s}=m(s).$$

So $m$ is a martingale, with $\mathbb{E}m(t)=0$ and $\mathrm{Var}\,m(t)=t(1-t)/(1-t)^2=v_t$. For $t\le1/2$, $m(t)\in[-t/(1-t),1]\subset[-1,1]$.

Summing over the sample, $\sqrt nM(t)=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^nm_i(t)$ is a martingale in $t$ for the joint filtration. At each $t\le\frac12$ it is a sum of independent centered terms, each bounded by $n^{-1/2}$ in absolute value, with total variance $v_t$. Bernstein's moment-generating-function bound (Section 2.8) gives, for $0\le\lambda<3\sqrt n$,
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\pm\lambda\sqrt nM(t)}\le\frac{\lambda^2v_t}{2(1-\lambda/(3\sqrt n))}.$$
Since $e^{\pm\lambda\sqrt nM(\cdot)}$ is a nonnegative right-continuous submartingale, Doob's maximal inequality gives
$$\mathbb{P}\Big\{\sup_{s\le t}\pm\sqrt nM(s)\ge x\Big\}\le e^{-\lambda x}\mathbb{E}e^{\pm\lambda\sqrt nM(t)}.$$
Optimizing over $\lambda$ as in the proof of Bernstein's inequality and adding the two signs:
$$\mathbb{P}\Big\{\sup_{s\le t}\sqrt n|M(s)|\ge x\Big\}\le2\exp\Big(-\frac{x^2}{2(v_t+x/(3\sqrt n))}\Big),\qquad t\le\tfrac12.$$
Define $Y(t)=\sup_{s\le t}\sqrt n|M(s)|/\sqrt{v_t}$. If $t\ge n^{-1/2}$, then $nv_t\ge nt\ge\sqrt n$. Putting $x=y\sqrt{v_t}$ gives, for all $y\ge0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Y(t)\ge y\}\le2\exp\Big(-\frac{y^2}{2(1+y\delta)}\Big),\qquad\delta:=\frac{n^{-1/4}}3.\tag{1}$$

**Step 2: peeling the left half.** Fix $\eta\in(0,\frac12)$ and put $\rho=1+\eta$, $t_k=\rho^{-k}/2$. Let $K$ be the least integer with $t_K\le n^{-1/2}$; then $K\le1+\log(\sqrt n/2)/\log\rho$. The shells $[t_{k+1},t_k]$, $0\le k<K$, cover $[n^{-1/2},\frac12]$, and every $t_k$ with $k<K$ satisfies $t_k>n^{-1/2}$. So (1) applies to each $Y_k:=Y(t_k)$.

For $s\in[t_{k+1},t_k]$,
$$|V(s)|=\sqrt n|M(s)|\sqrt{\frac{1-s}{s}}\le\sqrt n|M(s)|\,\frac1{\sqrt{t_{k+1}}}\le Y_k\sqrt{\frac{\rho\,v_{t_k}}{t_k}}=c_kY_k,\qquad c_k=\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{1-t_k}}.$$
There are two kinds of shells:
- *Small shells*, $t_k\le\eta$: here $c_k\le c_\eta:=\sqrt{(1+\eta)/(1-\eta)}$.
- *Large shells*, $t_k>\eta$: here $c_k\le\sqrt{2\rho}$, since $t_k\le\frac12$. Since $t_k>\eta$ forces $k<\log(1/(2\eta))/\log\rho$, there are at most $L_0:=1+\log(1/(2\eta))/\log\rho$ large shells, a number independent of $n$.

**Step 3: the right half.** Use the reflection $X_i\mapsto1-X_i$. Then $nF_n(s)=n-\#\{i:1-X_i<1-s\}$. The weight $\sqrt{s(1-s)}$ is symmetric. Almost surely no $X_i$ equals $n^{-1/2}$, $\frac12$, or $1-n^{-1/2}$. Hence, almost surely, $\sup_{s\in[1/2,\,1-n^{-1/2}]}|V(s)|$ equals the left-half supremum computed from the reflected sample. That sample has the same law.

So $Z\le\max(\max_kc_kY_k,\ \max_kc_k\tilde Y_k)$, where the $\tilde Y_k$ are the reflected copies and also satisfy (1). Since the maximum of nonnegative quantities is at most their sum,
$$Z\le c_\eta\max_{\text{small }k,\ Y\text{ or }\tilde Y}Y+\sqrt{2\rho}\max_{\text{large }k,\ Y\text{ or }\tilde Y}Y.\tag{2}$$
The first maximum is over at most $2K$ variables, the second over at most $2L_0$.

**Step 4: a maximal inequality.** Let $Y_1,\dots,Y_m\ge0$ each satisfy (1), and put $Y^*=\delta^{-1/2}$ and $a=1+\delta^{1/2}$.
- For $y\le Y^*$, the exponent in (1) is at least $y^2/(2a)$.
- For $y>Y^*$, we have $1+y\delta\le y(\delta^{1/2}+\delta)\le2y\delta^{1/2}$, using $\delta\le1$. So the exponent is at least $y/(4\delta^{1/2})$.

Hence, for any $y_0>0$,
$$\mathbb{E}\max_jY_j\le y_0+\sum_{j=1}^m\int_{y_0}^\infty\mathbb{P}\{Y_j\ge y\}\,dy\le y_0+2m\int_{y_0}^\infty e^{-y^2/(2a)}dy+2m\int_{Y^*}^\infty e^{-y/(4\sqrt\delta)}dy.$$
Use $\int_{y_0}^\infty e^{-y^2/(2a)}dy\le\frac a{y_0}e^{-y_0^2/(2a)}$ and choose $y_0=\sqrt{2a\log(2m)}$. This gives
$$\mathbb{E}\max_{j\le m}Y_j\le\sqrt{2a\log(2m)}+\sqrt{\frac a{2\log(2m)}}+8m\sqrt\delta\,e^{-1/(4\delta)}.\tag{3}$$

**Step 5: conclusion for $\mathbb{E}Z$.** Apply (3) to both maxima in (2), with $m=2K$ and $m=2L_0$, and take expectations:
$$\mathbb{E}Z\le c_\eta\Big(\sqrt{2a\log(4K)}+1+16K\sqrt\delta e^{-1/(4\delta)}\Big)+\sqrt{2\rho}\Big(\sqrt{2a\log(4L_0)}+1+16L_0\sqrt\delta e^{-1/(4\delta)}\Big).$$
Here we used $\sqrt{a/(2\log 2m)}\le1$, since $a\le2$ and $m\ge2$.

Now fix $\eta$ and let $n\to\infty$:
- $a=1+\delta^{1/2}\to1$;
- $\log(4K)\le\log\log n+\log(2/\log\rho)+o(1)$, so $\sqrt{2a\log(4K)}/\sqrt{2\ell_n}\to1$;
- $K\sqrt\delta e^{-1/(4\delta)}=O(\log n)\,n^{-1/8}e^{-3n^{1/4}/4}\to0$;
- the second bracket is $O(1)$, because $L_0$ does not depend on $n$.

Therefore $\limsup_n\mathbb{E}Z/\sqrt{2\ell_n}\le c_\eta=\sqrt{(1+\eta)/(1-\eta)}$. Since $\eta\in(0,\frac12)$ is arbitrary,
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}Z}{\sqrt{2\log\log n}}\le1,\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad\mathbb{E}Z\le\sqrt{2\log\log n}\,(1+o(1)).$$
The same display with $\eta$ fixed, for example $\eta=\frac14$, gives an explicit non-asymptotic bound $\mathbb{E}Z\le C_1\sqrt{\log\log n}+C_2$ for $n\ge16$.

**Step 6: an $O(1)$ variance bound.** Write $f_s(x)=(\mathbb 1\{x\le s\}-s)/\sqrt{s(1-s)}$. Then $\mathbb{E}f_s(X)=0$, $\mathbb{E}f_s(X)^2=1$, and $V(s)=n^{-1/2}\sum_if_s(X_i)$. Note that $|V(s)|\le\sqrt{2n}\,n^{1/4}$ on $J$, so $Z$ is bounded.

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and choose $s^*\in J$ and a sign $\theta\in\{\pm1\}$, depending on $X$, with $\theta V(s^*)\ge Z-\varepsilon$. Replace $X_i$ by an independent copy $X_i'$ and call the resulting statistic $Z_i'$. Then
$$Z_i'\ge\theta V(s^*)+\theta\,\frac{f_{s^*}(X_i')-f_{s^*}(X_i)}{\sqrt n},$$
so $(Z-Z_i')_+\le\varepsilon+D_i/\sqrt n$ with $D_i=|f_{s^*}(X_i)-f_{s^*}(X_i')|$. Since $s^*$ depends only on $X$,
$$\mathbb{E}'D_i^2=f_{s^*}(X_i)^2+1.$$
For any $\varepsilon'>0$, $(\varepsilon+D_i/\sqrt n)^2\le(1+\varepsilon')D_i^2/n+(1+1/\varepsilon')\varepsilon^2$. Hence
$$V_+:=\sum_i\mathbb{E}'(Z-Z_i')_+^2\le(1+\varepsilon')\Big(1+\frac1n\sum_if_{s^*}(X_i)^2\Big)+(1+1/\varepsilon')n\varepsilon^2.$$
Next,
$$\frac1n\sum_if_s(X_i)^2=\frac{F_n(s)(1-s)}{s}+\frac{(1-F_n(s))s}{1-s}.$$
- If $s\le\frac12$: the second term is at most $1$. For the first,
$$\frac{F_n(s)}s=1+\frac{F_n(s)-s}{s}\le1+\frac{|V(s)|}{\sqrt{ns}}\le1+Zn^{-1/4},$$
because $s\ge n^{-1/2}$.
- If $s>\frac12$: symmetrically, the first term is at most $1$ and the second is at most $1+Zn^{-1/4}$.

Thus $\frac1n\sum_if_{s^*}(X_i)^2\le2+Zn^{-1/4}$. Let $\varepsilon\to0$ and then $\varepsilon'\to0$; the left side $V_+$ does not depend on them. This gives $V_+\le3+Zn^{-1/4}$ a.s. The Efron–Stein inequality (Theorem 3.1) then gives
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\mathbb{E}V_+\le3+n^{-1/4}\,\mathbb{E}Z=3+o(1).$$

**Variance rate.**

**Status: incomplete.** We do not prove that $\mathrm{Var}(Z)$ is bounded by a function of $1/(2\log\log n)$ that tends to $0$. The Gumbel limit suggests $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\approx\frac{\pi^2}{6}\cdot\frac1{2\log\log n}$.

*The gap.* Steps 1–5 control $\mathbb{E}Z$ only up to a factor $1+o(1)$, and the Efron–Stein bound of Step 6 is $O(1)$. Getting the rate requires a superconcentration argument, which we do not have. Possible routes are a Talagrand $L^1$–$L^2$ (hypercontractive) bound exploiting the small influence of each shell, or a quantitative extreme-value argument. The Gumbel convergence in law alone does not give convergence of variances: uniform integrability of $\sqrt{2\ell_n}(Z-b_n)$ would have to be proved.

The pre-repair version of this solution derived the rate from a peeled tail bound with constant $c_3\le1/27$. That derivation needed $c_3\ge\frac12$ to absorb the $\log n$ shell count, and it assumed this uniform integrability, so it is withdrawn.

For orientation only, the simulation above gives $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\approx0.32,\,0.32,\,0.30,\,0.30,\,0.29$ at $n=16,\,10^2,\,10^3,\,10^4,\,10^5$; an independent rerun gave $0.326,\,0.323,\,0.306,\,0.276$ at $n=16,\dots,10^4$, so the second digit is noisy for $n\ge10^4$. This decreases slowly. It is below the heuristic $\frac{\pi^2}{6}/(2\log\log n)$, which takes the values $0.81,\,0.54,\,0.43,\,0.37,\,0.34$ at the same $n$. ∎
