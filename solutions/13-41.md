**Central idea.** The engine behind all four parts is the single hypothesis that $\psi(x)/x$ is nonincreasing: it yields subadditivity via the weighted-average trick $\psi(u+v)=(u+v)\cdot\frac{\psi(u+v)}{u+v}$, the bound $\mathbb{E}\psi(X)\le 2\psi(\mathbb{E}X)$ by splitting at $\mathbb{E}X$ and dominating $\psi(X)/X$ by $\psi(\mathbb{E}X)/\mathbb{E}X$ on $\{X>\mathbb{E}X\}$, strict decrease of $\psi(r)/r^2$ for the fixed-point uniqueness, and closure under composition and sums via products and sums of nonincreasing ratios (using that a sub-linear function is strictly positive on $(0,\infty)$).

**Solution.** Let $\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ be non-decreasing, continuous, with $\psi(x)/x$ nonincreasing on $(0,\infty)$ and $\psi(1)\ge 1$. First, $\psi(x)>0$ for every $x>0$: if $\psi(x_0)=0$ for some $x_0>0$, then monotonicity gives $\psi\equiv 0$ on $[0,x_0]$, and $\psi(x)/x\le \psi(x_0)/x_0=0$ gives $\psi\equiv 0$ on $[x_0,\infty)$; either way $\psi(1)=0<1$, a contradiction. Also, for $0<x\le 1$, $\psi(x)/x\ge \psi(1)\ge 1$, i.e. $\psi(x)\ge x$.

(a) $\psi+\rho$ is non-decreasing and continuous, $(\psi+\rho)(x)/x=\psi(x)/x+\rho(x)/x$ is a sum of nonincreasing functions hence nonincreasing, and $(\psi+\rho)(1)=\psi(1)+\rho(1)\ge 2\ge 1$. For $\psi\circ\rho$: it is non-decreasing and continuous as a composition of such. For $x>0$, $\rho(x)>0$ by the remark above, so
$$\frac{(\psi\circ\rho)(x)}{x}=\frac{\psi(\rho(x))}{\rho(x)}\cdot\frac{\rho(x)}{x},$$
a product of two nonnegative nonincreasing functions of $x$ (the first because $x\mapsto\rho(x)$ is non-decreasing and $y\mapsto\psi(y)/y$ is nonincreasing; the second by hypothesis), hence nonincreasing. Finally $(\psi\circ\rho)(1)=\psi(\rho(1))\ge\psi(1)\ge 1$ since $\rho(1)\ge 1$ and $\psi$ is non-decreasing.

(b) For $r>0$, $\alpha r^2=\psi(r)$ is equivalent to $\alpha=h(r)$ where $h(r)=\psi(r)/r^2$. Write $h(r)=\frac{\psi(r)}{r}\cdot\frac1r$: for $0<r_1<r_2\le 1$, $\frac{\psi(r_1)}{r_1}\ge\frac{\psi(r_2)}{r_2}>0$ and $\frac1{r_1}>\frac1{r_2}>0$, so $h(r_1)>h(r_2)$; i.e. $h$ is continuous and strictly decreasing on $(0,1]$. Moreover $h(r)\ge \psi(1)/r\to\infty$ as $r\to 0^+$, while $h(1)=\psi(1)\le\alpha$. If $\alpha=\psi(1)$ then $r=1$ is the (unique) solution; otherwise $h(1)<\alpha<h(\delta)$ for small $\delta>0$, and the intermediate value theorem on $[\delta,1]$ gives a root in $(\delta,1)\subset(0,1]$. Strict monotonicity gives uniqueness.

(c) Let $u,v\ge 0$; if either is $0$ the claim is trivial since $\psi\ge 0$. For $u,v>0$, since $u\le u+v$ and $\psi(x)/x$ is nonincreasing,
$$\frac{\psi(u+v)}{u+v}\le\frac{\psi(u)}{u},\qquad \frac{\psi(u+v)}{u+v}\le\frac{\psi(v)}{v}.$$
Hence $\psi(u+v)=(u+v)\frac{\psi(u+v)}{u+v}=u\frac{\psi(u+v)}{u+v}+v\frac{\psi(u+v)}{u+v}\le u\frac{\psi(u)}{u}+v\frac{\psi(v)}{v}=\psi(u)+\psi(v)$.

(d) Let $X\ge 0$ with $\mathbb{E}X<\infty$. If $\mathbb{E}X=0$ then $X=0$ a.s. and $\mathbb{E}\psi(X)=\psi(0)\le 2\psi(0)=2\psi(\mathbb{E}X)$. Assume $\mathbb{E}X>0$ and split
$$\mathbb{E}\psi(X)=\mathbb{E}[\psi(X)\mathbf 1_{\{X\le\mathbb{E}X\}}]+\mathbb{E}[\psi(X)\mathbf 1_{\{X>\mathbb{E}X\}}].$$
On $\{X\le\mathbb{E}X\}$, $\psi(X)\le\psi(\mathbb{E}X)$ by monotonicity, so the first term is at most $\psi(\mathbb{E}X)$. On $\{X>\mathbb{E}X\}$, write $\psi(X)=X\cdot\frac{\psi(X)}{X}\le X\cdot\frac{\psi(\mathbb{E}X)}{\mathbb{E}X}$ since $\psi(x)/x$ is nonincreasing; thus the second term is at most $\frac{\psi(\mathbb{E}X)}{\mathbb{E}X}\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf 1_{\{X>\mathbb{E}X\}}]\le\psi(\mathbb{E}X)$. Adding gives $\mathbb{E}\psi(X)\le 2\psi(\mathbb{E}X)$.
