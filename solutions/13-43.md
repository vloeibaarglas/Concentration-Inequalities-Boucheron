**Central idea.** The geometric engine is the inclusion $A\subseteq 2\,\mathrm{conv}(A_\varepsilon)$, proved by duality: for every unit vector $w$, some net point $p$ satisfies $\langle w,p\rangle\ge 1-\varepsilon^2/2$, so by a separation argument $\mathrm{conv}(A_\varepsilon)$ contains the ball of radius $1-\varepsilon^2/2$. Writing $\alpha\in A$ as twice a convex combination of net points, linearity of $W$ and the triangle inequality dominate $\|W(\alpha)\|$ by twice the maximum of $\|W\|$ over the net — the deterministic core of the contraction principle. For Lemma 13.13, expanding the centered quantity $\|W(\alpha)\|^2-1=\langle\alpha,(W^*W-I)\alpha\rangle$ bilinearly in the net coefficients preserves centering and reduces the supremum over $T$ to a finite maximum of averages of i.i.d. centered sub-exponential variables, controlled by Bernstein's inequality and a union bound. No chaining is needed.

**Solution.** Let $E=\mathrm{span}\{e_1,\dots,e_k\}\subset\mathbb{R}^D$, so that $A=\{x\in E:\|x\|_2=1\}$ is the unit sphere of $E$. Let $A_\varepsilon$ be a finite $\varepsilon$-net of $A$ with $0<\varepsilon<1$ (possible since $A$ is compact).

*The convex-hull inclusion.* Let $w\in E$ with $\|w\|_2=1$ and choose $p\in A_\varepsilon$ with $\|w-p\|_2\le\varepsilon$. Then
$$\langle w,p\rangle=\tfrac12\big(\|w\|_2^2+\|p\|_2^2-\|w-p\|_2^2\big)=1-\tfrac12\|w-p\|_2^2\ge 1-\varepsilon^2/2.$$
For $q=\sum_i\mu_i p_i\in\mathrm{conv}(A_\varepsilon)$ (finite convex combination), $\langle w,q\rangle=\sum_i\mu_i\langle w,p_i\rangle\le\max_{p\in A_\varepsilon}\langle w,p\rangle$, so
$$\max_{q\in\mathrm{conv}(A_\varepsilon)}\langle w,q\rangle=\max_{p\in A_\varepsilon}\langle w,p\rangle\ge 1-\varepsilon^2/2\qquad\text{for every unit }w\in E.$$
We claim $(1-\varepsilon^2/2)B_E\subseteq\mathrm{conv}(A_\varepsilon)$, where $B_E$ is the unit ball of $E$. Otherwise, since $\mathrm{conv}(A_\varepsilon)$ is a compact convex polytope, strict separation in $E$ yields a unit vector $w\in E$ and $z\notin\mathrm{conv}(A_\varepsilon)$ with $\|z\|_2\le 1-\varepsilon^2/2$ such that $\langle w,z\rangle>\max_{q\in\mathrm{conv}(A_\varepsilon)}\langle w,q\rangle\ge 1-\varepsilon^2/2$, contradicting $\langle w,z\rangle\le\|z\|_2$. Since $A\subset B_E$,
$$A\subseteq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon^2/2}\,\mathrm{conv}(A_\varepsilon)=\frac{2}{2-\varepsilon^2}\,\mathrm{conv}(A_\varepsilon)\subseteq 2\,\mathrm{conv}(A_\varepsilon),$$
as $\varepsilon^2<1$. The same argument applies in any $k$-dimensional coordinate subspace.

*Domination by the net.* Put $\mathcal{A}=A$ and $\mathcal{A}_\varepsilon=A_\varepsilon$. Let $\alpha\in\mathcal{A}$. By the inclusion, $\alpha=2\sum_{r=1}^m\lambda_r\alpha_r$ for some $m<\infty$, $\alpha_r\in\mathcal{A}_\varepsilon$, $\lambda_r\ge0$, $\sum_r\lambda_r=1$. By linearity of $W$,
$$W(\alpha)=2\sum_{r=1}^m\lambda_rW(\alpha_r),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\|W(\alpha)\|\le 2\sum_{r=1}^m\lambda_r\|W(\alpha_r)\|\le 2\max_{1\le r\le m}\|W(\alpha_r)\|\le 2\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal{A}_\varepsilon}\|W(\beta)\|.$$
The right-hand side does not depend on $\alpha$; taking $\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}$ and then expectations,
$$\mathbb{E}\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\|W(\alpha)\|\le 2\,\mathbb{E}\sup_{\beta\in\mathcal{A}_\varepsilon}\|W(\beta)\|.$$
This domination of a convex combination by the maximum over its extreme points is the deterministic mechanism behind the contraction principle (Theorem 11.5, cited as Lemma 11.5 in the exercise; the Gaussian form is Exercise 11.11).

*Another proof of Lemma 13.13.* Let $T$ be the set of $2k$-sparse unit vectors of $\mathbb{R}^D$, $V=\sup_{\alpha\in T}(\|W(\alpha)\|^2-1)$ and $V'=\sup_{\alpha\in T}(1-\|W(\alpha)\|^2)$. Partition $T=\bigcup_{|S|=2k}A^{(S)}$, where $A^{(S)}$ is the unit sphere of the coordinate subspace indexed by $S\subset[D]$. Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and let $A^{(S)}_\varepsilon$ be an $\varepsilon$-net of $A^{(S)}$ with $|A^{(S)}_\varepsilon|\le(1+2/\varepsilon)^{2k}$ (Lemma 13.11); by the inclusion proved above, $A^{(S)}\subseteq 2\,\mathrm{conv}(A^{(S)}_\varepsilon)$.

For $\alpha\in A^{(S)}$, write $\alpha=2\sum_r\lambda_r\alpha^{(r)}$ as above and put $M=W^*W-I$. Since $\|\alpha\|_2=1$,
$$\|W(\alpha)\|^2-1=\langle\alpha,M\alpha\rangle=4\sum_{r,s}\lambda_r\lambda_s\langle\alpha^{(r)},M\alpha^{(s)}\rangle\le 4\max_{r,s}\langle\alpha^{(r)},M\alpha^{(s)}\rangle,$$
because $\sum_{r,s}\lambda_r\lambda_s=1$ with $\lambda_r\lambda_s\ge0$. Moreover,
$$\langle\alpha^{(r)},M\alpha^{(s)}\rangle=\frac1d\sum_{i=1}^d W_i(\alpha^{(r)})W_i(\alpha^{(s)})-\langle\alpha^{(r)},\alpha^{(s)}\rangle=:\frac1d\sum_{i=1}^d\zeta_i^{(rs)},$$
where, the $X_{ij}$ being independent across $i$ with $\mathbb{E}X_{ij}=0$ and $\mathbb{E}X_{ij}^2=1$, the variables $\zeta_i^{(rs)}$ are i.i.d. across $i$ and centered, since $\mathbb{E}[W_i(\alpha^{(r)})W_i(\alpha^{(s)})]=\langle\alpha^{(r)},\alpha^{(s)}\rangle$. Each $W_i(\alpha^{(r)})$ is sub-gaussian with $O(1)$ $\psi_2$-norm (for $X$ standard Gaussian or Rademacher), so $\|\zeta_i^{(rs)}\|_{\psi_1}\le K_0$ for an absolute constant $K_0$; i.e., the $\zeta_i^{(rs)}$ are sub-exponential. Hence, with $Z_{rs}=\frac1d\sum_i\zeta_i^{(rs)}$,
$$V\le 4\max_{(r,s)}Z_{rs},\qquad V'\le 4\max_{(r,s)}(-Z_{rs}),$$
the maxima over the at most $M:=\binom{D}{2k}(1+2/\varepsilon)^{4k}$ pairs $(r,s)$ from $\bigcup_SA^{(S)}_\varepsilon$. Each $Z_{rs}$ is an average of $d$ i.i.d. centered sub-exponential variables, hence sub-gamma with variance factor $O(1/d)$ and scale $O(1/d)$; by Bernstein's inequality (Theorem 2.10) and the maximal inequality for sub-gamma variables (cf. Corollary 2.6),
$$\mathbb{E}\max_{(r,s)}Z_{rs}\le C_1\sqrt{\frac{\log M}{d}}+C_2\frac{\log M}{d},$$
and the same bound holds for $-Z_{rs}$. Take $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_*$ (admissible since $\varepsilon_*<1/2$). Then
$$\log M\le 2k\log\frac{eD}{2k}+4k\log\!\left(1+\frac{2}{\varepsilon_*}\right)\le 2k\log\frac{2eD}{k\varepsilon_*}+4k\log\frac{3}{\varepsilon_*}\le 3d\varepsilon_*^2,$$
using $d\varepsilon_*^2=2k\log\frac{2eD}{k\varepsilon_*}$ together with $\varepsilon_*\le4$ and $3\le 2eD/k$ (as $D\ge2k$). Therefore
$$\max(\mathbb{E}V,\mathbb{E}V')\le 4C_1\sqrt3\,\varepsilon_*+12C_2\varepsilon_*^2\le C\varepsilon_*$$
for an absolute constant $C$, using $\varepsilon_*\le1/2$. This is Lemma 13.13 up to the value of the absolute constant, proved without chaining and without Lemma 13.12.
