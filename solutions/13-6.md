**Central idea.** The stated constant $1/\sqrt2$ is false (see the remark below), but the $\sqrt{\log N}$ rate is correct. For $N=2$ the maximum has the exact value $1/\sqrt\pi$; for large $N$, $t_N=\bar\Phi^{-1}(1/N)$ satisfies $t_N\ge\sqrt{\log N}$ (via a lower Mill's-ratio bound), and $\mathbb{E}M\ge t_N(1-e^{-1})-\mathbb{E}[M_-]$ with $\mathbb{E}[M_-]\le2^{-(N+1)/2}$ negligible. Interpolating gives $\mathbb{E}\max\ge\frac14\sqrt{\log N}$ for all $N\ge2$.

**Remark (erratum).** As stated the inequality is false: for $N=2$, $\mathbb{E}\max(X_1,X_2)=1/\sqrt\pi\approx0.564<\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\sqrt{\log2}\approx0.589$. The attempt in the problem's hint has two gaps: (i) $\mathbb{E}M\ge t\,\mathbb{P}\{M\ge t\}$ is not valid for the (possibly negative) maximum $M$ — one must pass through $\mathbb{E}[M_+]$ and control $\mathbb{E}[M_-]$; (ii) Mill's ratio $\bar\Phi(t)\le\phi(t)/t$ is an upper bound on the tail, hence gives an upper bound on the quantile $t_N$, not the lower bound $t_N\ge\sqrt{2\log N}$ that the hint claims. We prove the corrected bound $\mathbb{E}\max_{s\in\mathcal{T}}X_s\ge\frac14\sqrt{\log|\mathcal{T}|}$.

**Solution.** Let $N=|\mathcal{T}|\ge2$, $M_N=\max_{1\le i\le N}X_i$ with $X_i$ i.i.d.\ $N(0,1)$.

For $N=2$, $\max(X_1,X_2)=\frac12(X_1+X_2+|X_1-X_2|)$, and $X_1-X_2\sim N(0,2)$, so
$$\mathbb{E}M_2=\tfrac12\mathbb{E}|X_1-X_2|=\tfrac12\cdot\sqrt2\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt\pi}\approx0.5642.$$
Since $M_N\ge\max(X_1,X_2)$ pointwise, $\mathbb{E}M_N\ge1/\sqrt\pi$ for all $N\ge2$. As $\frac14\sqrt{\log100}\approx0.5365<1/\sqrt\pi$, this gives
$$\mathbb{E}M_N\ge\frac14\sqrt{\log N}\qquad\text{for }2\le N\le100.\tag{1}$$

Let now $N\ge101$ and put $t_N=\bar\Phi^{-1}(1/N)$, so $\bar\Phi(t_N)=1/N$. Write $M=M_N$. Since $\mathbb{E}M=\mathbb{E}[M_+]-\mathbb{E}[M_-]$ and, by Cauchy–Schwarz, $\mathbb{E}[M_-]=\mathbb{E}[(-M)_+]\le(\mathbb{E}[(-M)_+^2])^{1/2}\mathbb{P}\{M<0\}^{1/2}\le(1/2)^{1/2}(2^{-N})^{1/2}=2^{-(N+1)/2}$ (using $(-M)_+\le(-X_1)_+$ and $\mathbb{E}[(-X_1)_+^2]=1/2$),
$$\mathbb{E}M\ge\mathbb{E}[M_+]-2^{-(N+1)/2}.$$
Moreover, with $(1-x)^N\le e^{-Nx}$,
$$\mathbb{E}[M_+]=\int_0^\infty\mathbb{P}\{M>t\}\,dt\ge\int_0^{t_N}\!\big(1-e^{-N\bar\Phi(t)}\big)dt\ge t_N(1-e^{-1}),$$
since $\bar\Phi(t)\ge\bar\Phi(t_N)=1/N$ for $t\le t_N$. Hence
$$\mathbb{E}M_N\ge(1-e^{-1})t_N-2^{-(N+1)/2}.\tag{2}$$

We show $t_N\ge\sqrt{\log N}$ for $N\ge101$. Since $\bar\Phi$ is decreasing, it suffices that $\bar\Phi(\sqrt{\log N})\ge1/N$. By the lower Mill's-ratio bound $\bar\Phi(t)\ge\phi(t)\,\frac{t}{t^2+1}$ (Birnbaum),
$$\bar\Phi(\sqrt{\log N})\ge\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\frac{\sqrt{\log N}}{\log N+1},$$
so it suffices that $\frac{\sqrt N}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\ge\frac{\log N+1}{\sqrt{\log N}}=\sqrt{\log N}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log N}}$. Since $\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log N}}\le1$ for $N\ge3$, it suffices that $\sqrt N\ge\sqrt{2\pi}\,(\sqrt{\log N}+1)$, i.e.\ $p(N):=\sqrt N-\sqrt{2\pi\log N}\ge\sqrt{2\pi}$. Now $p'(N)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt N}\big(1-\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{N\log N}}\big)\ge0$ for $N\ge101$ (as $\sqrt{N\log N}\ge\sqrt{101\log101}>21>\sqrt{2\pi}$), and $p(101)=\sqrt{101}-\sqrt{2\pi\log101}\approx4.66\ge\sqrt{2\pi}\approx2.51$. Thus $p(N)\ge\sqrt{2\pi}$ for all $N\ge101$, and $t_N\ge\sqrt{\log N}$.

From (2), for $N\ge101$,
$$\mathbb{E}M_N\ge(1-e^{-1})\sqrt{\log N}-2^{-51}\ge0.63\sqrt{\log N}\ge\frac14\sqrt{\log N},$$
since $1-e^{-1}\approx0.6321$ and $2^{-51}<10^{-15}$. Together with (1) this proves
$$\mathbb{E}\max_{s\in\mathcal{T}}X_s\ge\frac14\sqrt{\log|\mathcal{T}|}\qquad\text{for }|\mathcal{T}|\ge2,$$
the $\sqrt{\log N}$ rate with a valid constant. ∎
