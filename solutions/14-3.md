**Central idea.** Differentiate $\theta$: with $m(r)=\|f\|_r^2=(\mathbb E[f^r])^{2/r}$, one gets $\theta'(r)\ge0\iff \mathbb E[f^2]-m(r)\ge\frac{2-r}{r}m(r)H_r$ where $H_r=D(\mu_r\|\mathbb P)\ge0$ is the KL divergence for $d\mu_r=(f^r/\mathbb E[f^r])d\mathbb P$ (this is exactly $m'(r)=m(r)\cdot2H_r/r^2$). The stronger exponential form follows from $1+x\le e^x$ and Jensen, collapsing to $\mathbb E[f^2]\ge\mathbb E[f^r]\exp((2-r)\mathbb E_{\mu_r}\log f)$.

**Solution.** Assume $0<\mathbb E[f^2]<\infty$ (else $\theta\equiv\infty$); then $\mathbb E[f^r]<\infty$ for $r<2$. Put $A=\mathbb E[f^2]$, $m(r)=(\mathbb E[f^r])^{2/r}$. Then
$$\theta'(r)=\frac{2\big[\,2(A-m(r))-r(2-r)m'(r)\,\big]}{(2-r)^2},$$
so $\theta'(r)\ge0\iff 2(A-m(r))\ge r(2-r)m'(r)$.

Let $d\mu_r=(f^r/\mathbb E[f^r])\,d\mathbb P$ (if $\mathbb E[f^r]=0$ there is nothing to prove) and $H_r=\mathbb E_{\mu_r}[\log(f^r/\mathbb E[f^r])]\ge0$, the KL divergence. Since $\log m(r)=\frac2r\log\mathbb E[f^r]$,
$$\frac{d}{dr}\log m(r)=\frac2r\left(\frac{\mathbb E[f^r\log f]}{\mathbb E[f^r]}-\frac1r\log\mathbb E[f^r]\right)=\frac{2H_r}{r^2},$$
i.e. $m'(r)=m(r)\cdot2H_r/r^2$. Thus we need $A-m(r)\ge\frac{2-r}{r}m(r)H_r$.

Since $1+x\le e^x$, it suffices that $A\ge m(r)\exp(\frac{2-r}{r}H_r)$. Now
$$\log\!\left(m(r)e^{\frac{2-r}{r}H_r}\right)=\frac2r\log\mathbb E[f^r]+\frac{2-r}{r}H_r=\log\mathbb E[f^r]+(2-r)\mathbb E_{\mu_r}[\log f],$$
using $H_r=r\,\mathbb E_{\mu_r}[\log f]-\log\mathbb E[f^r]$. Hence $m(r)\exp(\frac{2-r}{r}H_r)=\mathbb E[f^r]\exp((2-r)\mathbb E_{\mu_r}[\log f])$, and by Jensen,
$$\exp\big((2-r)\mathbb E_{\mu_r}[\log f]\big)=\exp\big(\mathbb E_{\mu_r}[\log f^{\,2-r}]\big)\le\mathbb E_{\mu_r}[f^{2-r}]=\frac{\mathbb E[f^2]}{\mathbb E[f^r]},$$
so $m(r)\exp(\frac{2-r}{r}H_r)\le A$, as required. Thus $\theta'(r)\ge0$ on $[1,2)$. ∎
