**Central idea.** Mimic the proof of the Gaussian log-Sobolev inequality (Theorem 5.4): approximate the standard Gaussian vector by normalized Rademacher sums, apply the Bernoulli $\Phi$-Sobolev inequality (Theorem 14.8) to the approximating functions on the hypercube, and pass to the limit via the CLT. The Dirichlet form $\mathcal{E}$ converges to $\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f\|^2$ while the $\Phi$-entropy converges to $H_{\Phi}(f^r)$.

**Solution.** Let $r\in[1,2)$ and $\Phi(z)=z^{2/r}$. For $r=1$, $H_{\Phi}(f)=\mathrm{Var}(f)$ and the claim is the Gaussian Poincaré inequality, so assume $1<r<2$. By a standard density argument it suffices to prove the inequality for $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to[0,\infty)$ twice continuously differentiable with bounded support (hence all derivatives bounded).

Let $(\varepsilon_{ij})_{i\le n,\,j\ge1}$ be independent Rademacher variables and put, for $m\ge1$,
$$S^{(m)}_i=\frac1{\sqrt m}\sum_{j=1}^m\varepsilon_{ij},\qquad S^{(m)}=(S^{(m)}_1,\dots,S^{(m)}_n)\in\mathbb{R}^n.$$
Define $g_m:\{-1,1\}^{nm}\to[0,\infty)$ by $g_m(\varepsilon)=f(S^{(m)})$. By the multivariate CLT, $S^{(m)}\Rightarrow X$ where $X$ is a standard Gaussian vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$, and since $f$ is bounded with bounded support,
$$\mathbb{E}[g_m(\varepsilon)^2]\to\mathbb{E}[f(X)^2],\qquad \mathbb{E}[g_m(\varepsilon)^r]\to\mathbb{E}[f(X)^r],$$
hence with $H_{\Phi}(h^r)=\mathbb{E}[h^2]-(\mathbb{E}[h^r])^{2/r}$,
$$H_{\Phi}(g_m^r)\longrightarrow H_{\Phi}(f^r).\tag{1}$$

Apply Theorem 14.8 to $g_m$ on $\{-1,1\}^{nm}$:
$$H_{\Phi}(g_m^r)\le(2-r)\,\mathcal{E}(g_m),\qquad \mathcal{E}(g_m)=\frac14\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb{E}\big[(g_m(\varepsilon)-g_m(\varepsilon^{(ij)}))^2\big],$$
where $\varepsilon^{(ij)}$ flips $\varepsilon_{ij}$. Flipping $\varepsilon_{ij}$ replaces $S^{(m)}_i$ by $S^{(m)}_i-2\varepsilon_{ij}/\sqrt m$. By Taylor's formula with integral remainder, for $f\in C^2_b$,
$$f(S^{(m)})-f\!\left(S^{(m)}-\tfrac{2\varepsilon_{ij}}{\sqrt m}e_i\right)=\tfrac{2\varepsilon_{ij}}{\sqrt m}\,\partial_i f(S^{(m)})+R_{ij},\qquad |R_{ij}|\le \tfrac{C}{m}$$
with $C$ depending only on $\|f''\|_\infty$. Hence
$$(g_m(\varepsilon)-g_m(\varepsilon^{(ij)}))^2=\tfrac{4}{m}(\partial_i f(S^{(m)}))^2+O(m^{-3/2}),$$
and therefore
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{E}(g_m)&=\frac14\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^m\left[\tfrac{4}{m}\mathbb{E}\big[(\partial_i f(S^{(m)}))^2\big]+O(m^{-3/2})\right]\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big[(\partial_i f(S^{(m)}))^2\big]+O(m^{-1/2}).
\end{align*}
Since $\partial_i f$ is bounded with bounded support, the CLT gives $\mathbb{E}[(\partial_i f(S^{(m)}))^2]\to\mathbb{E}[(\partial_i f(X))^2]$, whence
$$\mathcal{E}(g_m)\longrightarrow \sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\big[(\partial_i f(X))^2\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f(X)\|^2\big].\tag{2}$$

Combining Theorem 14.8 with (1)–(2) and letting $m\to\infty$ yields
$$H_{\Phi}(f^r)\le(2-r)\,\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f(X)\|^2\big],$$
as claimed. ∎
