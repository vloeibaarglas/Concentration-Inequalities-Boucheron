**Central idea.** The integrand on the right is the Bregman divergence $A^\Phi(u,v)=\Phi(u+v)-\Phi(u)-\Phi'(u)v$ evaluated at $u=f(x)$, $v=Df(x)$. For a single Bernoulli variable, convexity of $\Phi$ gives the exact two-point bound $H_\Phi(y_B)\le p\,A^\Phi(y_0,y_1-y_0)$. Since $\Phi(x)=x^{2/r}$ belongs to the class $\mathcal{C}$, $H_\Phi$ tensorizes (Theorem 14.1); applying the two-point bound coordinatewise to $\mathrm{Bin}(m,\mu/m)$ *before* letting $m\to\infty$ keeps the sharp Bregman form, and the Poisson limit follows for bounded $f$. Unbounded $f$ is handled by truncation $f\wedge M$, using that the Bregman integrand can only decrease under truncation.

**Solution.** Let $\mu=\mathbb{E}X$. If $\mu=0$ then $X\equiv0$ and both sides vanish, so assume $\mu>0$. Throughout, $\Phi(x)=x^{2/r}$ on $[0,\infty)$ with $2/r\in(1,2)$, so $\Phi$ is $C^1$ on $[0,\infty)$ with $\Phi'(x)=\frac2rx^{2/r-1}$ (and $\Phi'(0)=0$), convex, and $C^2$ on $(0,\infty)$. We read $H_\Phi(f(X))=\mathbb{E}\Phi(f(X))-\Phi(\mathbb{E}f(X))$ for $\mathbb{E}f(X)<\infty$ (with value $+\infty$ if $\mathbb{E}\Phi(f(X))=\infty$); this is the setting in which the left side is defined.

*Rewriting the right-hand side.* With $A^\Phi(u,v)=\Phi(u+v)-\Phi(u)-\Phi'(u)v$,
$$D(\Phi\circ f)(x)-\tfrac2rf(x)^{2/r-1}Df(x)=A^\Phi\big(f(x),Df(x)\big),$$
so the claim is $H_\Phi(f(X))\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}\big[A^\Phi(f(X),Df(X))\big]$. By convexity $A^\Phi\ge0$, so the right side is a well-defined element of $[0,\infty]$.

*$\Phi\in\mathcal{C}$.* $\Phi$ is convex, and $1/\Phi''(x)=c\,x^{2-2/r}$ with $c>0$ and $2-2/r\in(0,1)$ is concave on $(0,\infty)$, so $-1/\Phi''$ is convex. Hence the tensorization inequality of Theorem 14.1 applies to $H_\Phi$.

*Step 1 (two-point bound).* Let $B\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$, $y_0,y_1\ge0$, $\Delta=y_1-y_0$, and $y_B$ the variable equal to $y_1$ if $B=1$ and $y_0$ if $B=0$. Then $\mathbb{E}y_B=y_0+p\Delta$, and convexity gives $\Phi(y_0+p\Delta)\ge\Phi(y_0)+p\Phi'(y_0)\Delta$. Therefore
$$H_\Phi(y_B)=p\Phi(y_1)+(1-p)\Phi(y_0)-\Phi(y_0+p\Delta)\le p\big[\Phi(y_1)-\Phi(y_0)-\Phi'(y_0)\Delta\big]=p\,A^\Phi(y_0,\Delta).$$

*Step 2 (binomial approximation, bounded $f$).* Assume $0\le f\le K$. For $m>\mu$ let $B_1,\dots,B_m$ be i.i.d. $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\mu/m)$, $X_m=\sum_{j\le m}B_j$ and $S_{-j}=X_m-B_j\sim\mathrm{Bin}(m-1,\mu/m)$. Conditionally on $(B_k)_{k\ne j}$, $f(X_m)=f(S_{-j}+B_j)$ is a two-point variable with $y_0=f(S_{-j})$, $y_1=f(S_{-j}+1)$, so by Step 1 the conditional $\Phi$-entropy in coordinate $j$ is at most $\frac\mu m A^\Phi(f(S_{-j}),Df(S_{-j}))$. Theorem 14.1 then gives
$$H_\Phi(f(X_m))\le\sum_{j=1}^m\frac\mu m\,\mathbb{E}\big[A^\Phi(f(S_{-j}),Df(S_{-j}))\big]=\mu\,\mathbb{E}\big[A^\Phi(f(X'_{m-1}),Df(X'_{m-1}))\big],\qquad X'_{m-1}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(m-1,\mu/m).$$

*Step 3 (the limit, bounded $f$).* For each fixed $k$, $\mathbb{P}\{X_m=k\}\to e^{-\mu}\mu^k/k!$ and $\mathbb{P}\{X'_{m-1}=k\}\to e^{-\mu}\mu^k/k!$; since all are probability mass functions on $\mathbb{N}$, Scheffé's lemma gives convergence of both laws to $\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)$ in total variation. The functions $f$, $\Phi\circ f$ and $x\mapsto A^\Phi(f(x),Df(x))$ are bounded (by $K$, $\Phi(K)$ and $2\Phi(K)+\Phi'(K)K$), so their expectations converge; $\Phi$ is continuous, so $\Phi(\mathbb{E}f(X_m))\to\Phi(\mathbb{E}f(X))$. Letting $m\to\infty$ in Step 2,
$$H_\Phi(f(X))\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}\big[A^\Phi(f(X),Df(X))\big]\qquad(f\text{ bounded}).$$

*Step 4 (unbounded $f$).* Let $f_M=f\wedge M$. We claim that pointwise, for $u,w\ge0$,
$$A^\Phi(u\wedge M,\;w\wedge M-u\wedge M)\le A^\Phi(u,\;w-u).\tag{$*$}$$
Write $D_\Phi(w,u)=A^\Phi(u,w-u)=\Phi(w)-\Phi(u)-\Phi'(u)(w-u)$. For fixed $u$, $\partial_wD_\Phi(w,u)=\Phi'(w)-\Phi'(u)\ge0$ for $w\ge u$, so $D_\Phi(\cdot,u)$ is nondecreasing on $[u,\infty)$. For fixed $w$ and $u>0$, $\partial_uD_\Phi(w,u)=\Phi''(u)(u-w)\ge0$ for $u\ge w$, so $D_\Phi(w,\cdot)$ is nondecreasing on $[w,\infty)$ (continuity at $u=0$ covers $w=0$). Now: if $u,w\le M$ the two sides of $(*)$ are equal; if $u\le M<w$, the left side is $D_\Phi(M,u)\le D_\Phi(w,u)$ by the first monotonicity; if $w\le M<u$, it is $D_\Phi(w,M)\le D_\Phi(w,u)$ by the second; if $u,w>M$, it is $0\le D_\Phi(w,u)$. This proves $(*)$.

Since $Df_M(x)=f_M(x+1)-f_M(x)$, Step 3 applied to the bounded $f_M$ and $(*)$ give
$$H_\Phi(f_M(X))\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}\big[A^\Phi(f_M(X),Df_M(X))\big]\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}\big[A^\Phi(f(X),Df(X))\big].$$
As $M\uparrow\infty$, monotone convergence gives $\mathbb{E}f_M(X)\uparrow\mathbb{E}f(X)<\infty$ and $\mathbb{E}\Phi(f_M(X))\uparrow\mathbb{E}\Phi(f(X))\in[0,\infty]$; by continuity of $\Phi$, $\Phi(\mathbb{E}f_M(X))\to\Phi(\mathbb{E}f(X))$. Hence $H_\Phi(f_M(X))\to H_\Phi(f(X))$ (including the case $H_\Phi(f(X))=\infty$, in which the right side is then infinite as well), and
$$H_\Phi(f(X))\le(\mathbb{E}X)\,\mathbb{E}\Big[D(\Phi\circ f)(X)-\tfrac2rf(X)^{2/r-1}Df(X)\Big].$$
∎

*Remarks.* (i) The argument uses only that $\Phi\in\mathcal{C}$ is $C^1$ and convex; with $\Phi(x)=x^2$ it gives the Poisson Poincaré inequality ($A^{x^2}(u,v)=v^2$, Exercise 3.21), and with $\Phi(x)=x\log x$ the Poisson modified log-Sobolev inequality (Exercise 6.12).
(ii) The bound is at least as sharp as $H_\Phi(f(X))\le\mu\,\mathbb{E}[(f(X+1)^{1/r}-f(X)^{1/r})^2]$, which is what the binomial limit of Theorem 14.9 applied to $f^{1/r}$ gives: with $a=u^{1/r}$, $b=w^{1/r}$, the inequality $A^\Phi(u,w-u)\le(b-a)^2$ is equivalent (for $a>0$, $\tau=b/a$) to $\tau-1\le(\tau^r-1)/r$, i.e. convexity of $\tau^r$; equality holds when $u=0$ or $u=w$, so the comparison is "$\le$", not strict in general.
(iii) Numerical check (Python): the inequality holds in all $45$ cases $r\in\{1.2,1.5,1.8\}$, $\mu\in\{0.5,2,5\}$, $f\in\{x+1,(x+1)^2,\mathbb 1_{\{x\ge1\}},\sqrt{x+1},e^{x/3}\}$ (largest ratio LHS/RHS $\approx0.998$), and $(*)$ and Remark (ii) had no violations in $2\cdot10^5$ random draws.

Applying the exact two-point bound per coordinate before the limit, as above, closes the gap.
