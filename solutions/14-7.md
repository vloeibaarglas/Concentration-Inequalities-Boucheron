**Central idea.** Prove a hypercontractive estimate for the biased cube: the noise operator $T_\sigma$, which multiplies every centered coordinate function by $\sigma$, maps $L^2$ contractively into $L^r$ for $\sigma=1/(10C_{r,p})$ when $r\ge\log(1/p)$. Since $T_\sigma Z=\sigma Z$, this is exactly $\|Z\|_r\le10C_{r,p}\|Z\|_2$. By Minkowski's inequality the contraction tensorizes, so it reduces to a two-point inequality $\mathbb{E}|1+tX|^r\le(1+Dpq\,t^2)^{r/2}$, proved by a second-order Taylor bound and a two-case analysis. The range $2<r\le\log(1/p)$ follows by Hölder interpolation between $L^2$ and $L^{\log(1/p)}$ at the cost of a factor $e$. (This hypercontractivity plays the role of the $\Phi$-Sobolev input of Theorem 14.9; Theorem 14.9 itself is not invoked.)

**Solution.** The coefficients are $\alpha_i\in\mathbb{R}$; the "$\mathbb{R}^n$" in the statement is a typo. Write $q=1-p\in(1/2,1)$, $L=\log(1/p)>\log2$, and let $X$ denote a generic $X_i$. Then $\mathbb{E}X=0$, $\mathbb{E}X^2=pq$, $|X|\le1$, and $\mathbb{E}Z^2=pq\sum_i\alpha_i^2$. We prove
- $\|Z\|_r\le10\,C_{r,p}\|Z\|_2$ for $r>2$, $r\ge L$ (Steps 1–2);
- $\|Z\|_r\le10e\,C_{r,p}\|Z\|_2$ for $2<r\le L$ (Step 3).

So $\kappa=10e$ works. (At $r=L$ the two formulas for $C_{r,p}$ differ by the factor $e$, namely $(1/p)^{1/2-1/L}=e^{-1}p^{-1/2}$ versus $p^{-1/2}$; Step 2 gives $10p^{-1/2}$, which equals $10e\cdot e^{-1}p^{-1/2}$ and is at most $10e\,p^{-1/2}$, so it is within $\kappa C_{L,p}$ under either formula.)

*Step 1 (reduction to a two-point inequality).* All functions below are functions of $X=(X_1,\dots,X_n)$ on a finite product space. For $\sigma\in(0,1]$ and a coordinate $i$, let $T^{(i)}_\sigma g=\mathbb{E}_ig+\sigma(g-\mathbb{E}_ig)$, where $\mathbb{E}_i$ integrates out $X_i$, and let $T_\sigma=T^{(1)}_\sigma\cdots T^{(n)}_\sigma$ (the factors act on different coordinates and commute). For $j\ne i$, $\mathbb{E}_i(\alpha_jX_j)=\alpha_jX_j$, so $T^{(i)}_\sigma(\alpha_jX_j)=\alpha_jX_j$; and $\mathbb{E}_i(\alpha_iX_i)=0$, so $T^{(i)}_\sigma(\alpha_iX_i)=\sigma\alpha_iX_i$. By linearity, $T_\sigma Z=\sigma Z$.

*Tensorization.* Fix $r\ge2$ and suppose that, on one coordinate, $\|T^{(1)}_\sigma g\|_r\le\|g\|_2$ for every $g=g(X_1)$. We show $\|T_\sigma f\|_r\le\|f\|_2$ for every $f$, by induction on $n$. Write $x=(x_1,x')$ and $T_\sigma=T^{(1)}_\sigma T'$ with $T'$ acting on $x'$, and put $h=T'f$, so $h(x_1,\cdot)=T'[f(x_1,\cdot)]$. Applying the one-coordinate bound for each fixed $x'$, then Minkowski's inequality in $L^{r/2}(x')$ (valid as $r/2\ge1$), then the induction hypothesis:
$$\|T_\sigma f\|_r^r=\mathbb{E}_{x'}\mathbb{E}_{x_1}|T^{(1)}_\sigma h|^r\le\mathbb{E}_{x'}\big(\mathbb{E}_{x_1}h^2\big)^{r/2}=\big\|\mathbb{E}_{x_1}h^2\big\|_{L^{r/2}(x')}^{r/2}\le\Big(\mathbb{E}_{x_1}\|h(x_1,\cdot)\|_{L^r(x')}^2\Big)^{r/2}\le\Big(\mathbb{E}_{x_1}\|f(x_1,\cdot)\|_{L^2(x')}^2\Big)^{r/2}=\|f\|_2^r.$$
Applied to $f=Z$: $\sigma\|Z\|_r=\|T_\sigma Z\|_r\le\|Z\|_2$.

*The one-coordinate statement.* Every $g=g(X_1)$ is of the form $a+bX_1$ (two points), and $T^{(1)}_\sigma g=a+\sigma bX_1$. The required bound is $\mathbb{E}|a+\sigma bX|^r\le(a^2+b^2pq)^{r/2}$. For $a\ne0$ divide by $|a|^r$ and set $t=\sigma b/a$; it becomes
$$F(t):=\mathbb{E}|1+tX|^r\le\big(1+Dpq\,t^2\big)^{r/2}\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}),\qquad D=\sigma^{-2}.\tag{$\star$}$$
For $a=0$, divide ($\star$) by $|t|^r$ and let $|t|\to\infty$: $\mathbb{E}|X|^r\le(Dpq)^{r/2}$, which is the required bound $\sigma^r\mathbb{E}|bX|^r\le(b^2pq)^{r/2}$. So ($\star$) for all $t$ implies the one-coordinate contraction.

*Step 2 (proof of ($\star$) for $r>2$, $r\ge L$, with $D=100r/(pL)$).* This choice gives $\sigma=\sqrt{pL/(100r)}=1/(10C_{r,p})$, with $C_{r,p}=\sqrt{r/(pL)}$ the second formula. Put $c:=Dpq=100qr/L\ge50$ (as $q\ge1/2$, $r\ge L$).

*A bound on $F$.* The map $x\mapsto|x|^r$ is $C^2$ with second derivative $r(r-1)|x|^{r-2}$. Taylor's formula with Lagrange remainder, and $|\xi|\le\max(1,|1+u|)$ for $\xi$ between $1$ and $1+u$, give
$$|1+u|^r\le1+ru+\tfrac{r(r-1)}2u^2\max(1,|1+u|)^{r-2}.$$
Take $u=tX$. Then $\mathbb{E}[ru]=0$, $\mathbb{E}X^2=pq$, and $\max(1,|1+tX|)\le1+|t|$ since $|X|\le1$. With $r(r-1)\le r^2$,
$$F(t)\le1+\tfrac{r^2}2pq\,t^2(1+|t|)^{r-2},$$
so it suffices to show
$$\tfrac{r^2}2pq\,t^2(1+|t|)^{r-2}\le(1+ct^2)^{r/2}-1.\tag{1}$$

*Case $ct^2\le1$.* Since $\log(1+y)\ge y-y^2/2\ge y/2$ on $[0,1]$, we have $(1+ct^2)^{r/2}\ge e^{rct^2/4}$. Since $e^x-1=2e^{x/2}\sinh(x/2)\ge xe^{x/2}$ for $x\ge0$, the right side of (1) is at least $\frac{rct^2}4e^{rct^2/8}=\frac{25qr^2t^2}{L}e^{rct^2/8}$. On the left, $(1+|t|)^{r-2}\le e^{|t|(r-2)}\le e^s$ with $s=r|t|$. Dividing by $qr^2t^2$ (the case $t=0$ is trivial), (1) follows from $\frac p2e^s\le\frac{25}Le^{rct^2/8}$. Since $rct^2/8=\frac{100q\,s^2}{8L}\ge\frac{100s^2}{16L}$ and $p=e^{-L}$, it suffices that
$$s-L-\frac{100s^2}{16L}\le\log\frac{50}{L}.$$
The maximum over $s$ of $s-\frac{100s^2}{16L}$ is $\frac{16L}{400}=0.04L$, so the left side is at most $-0.96L$. Finally $0.96L+\log(50/L)=\log50+(0.96L-\log L)\ge\log50+1+\log0.96>0$ for every $L>0$, since $0.96L-\log L$ is minimized at $L=1/0.96$.

*Case $ct^2>1$.* Then $(1+ct^2)^{r/2}\ge2^{r/2}\ge2$, so the right side of (1) is at least $\frac12(1+ct^2)^{r/2}$. Write $|t|=a w$ with $a=c^{-1/2}\le1/\sqrt{50}$ and $w=\sqrt{ct^2}>1$. Then $g(w)=(1+aw)^2/(1+w^2)$ has $g'(w)=2(1+aw)(a-w)/(1+w^2)^2<0$ for $w>a$, so on $w\ge1$ it is decreasing, and
$$\frac{(1+|t|)^2}{1+ct^2}\le g(1)=\frac{(1+a)^2}{2}\le\frac{1+2/\sqrt{50}+1/50}2=0.6514\ldots<0.66.$$
Hence $(1+|t|)^{r-2}\le0.66^{(r-2)/2}(1+ct^2)^{(r-2)/2}$. Then (1) follows from $r^2pq\,t^2\,0.66^{(r-2)/2}\le1+ct^2$, which is implied by $r^2pq\,0.66^{(r-2)/2}\le c=100qr/L$, i.e.
$$r\,pL\,0.66^{(r-2)/2}\le100.$$
This holds because $pL=p\log(1/p)\le1/e$ and $\max_{r\ge2}r\,0.66^{(r-2)/2}=2.683\ldots$ (attained at $r=2/\log(1/0.66)\approx4.81$), so the left side is below $1$.

Thus ($\star$) holds, and Step 1 gives $\|Z\|_r\le\sigma^{-1}\|Z\|_2=10\,C_{r,p}\|Z\|_2$ for $r>2$, $r\ge L$.

*Step 3 (the range $2<r\le L$; nonempty only if $L>2$).* Define $\theta\in(0,1]$ by $\frac1r=\frac{1-\theta}2+\frac\theta L$, i.e. $\theta=\frac{1/2-1/r}{1/2-1/L}$. Hölder's (log-convexity) inequality and Step 2 at $r=L>2$, where $C_{L,p}=\sqrt{L/(pL)}=p^{-1/2}$, give
$$\|Z\|_r\le\|Z\|_2^{1-\theta}\|Z\|_L^{\theta}\le10^\theta p^{-\theta/2}\|Z\|_2\le10\,p^{-\theta/2}\|Z\|_2.$$
Now, with $C_{r,p}=(1/p)^{1/2-1/r}=e^{L(1/2-1/r)}$,
$$p^{-\theta/2}=\exp\Big(L\big(\tfrac12-\tfrac1r\big)\tfrac{L}{L-2}\Big)=C_{r,p}\exp\Big(L\big(\tfrac12-\tfrac1r\big)\tfrac{2}{L-2}\Big)=C_{r,p}\exp\Big(\frac{L(r-2)}{r(L-2)}\Big)\le e\,C_{r,p},$$
because $\frac{r-2}{r}\le\frac{L-2}{L}$ for $r\le L$. Hence $\|Z\|_r\le10e\,C_{r,p}\|Z\|_2$.

*Conclusion.* For all $r>2$ and all real $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$,
$$\mathbb{E}\big[|Z|^r\big]^{1/r}\le\kappa\,C_{r,p}\,\mathbb{E}[Z^2]^{1/2},\qquad\kappa=10e.$$
∎

*Remarks.* (i) For $r\le L$ the order of $C_{r,p}$ is attained by a single summand: $\|X\|_r^r=qp^r+pq^r\ge p\,2^{-r}$ and $\|X\|_2\le\sqrt p$, so $\|X\|_r/\|X\|_2\ge\frac12p^{1/r-1/2}=\frac12C_{r,p}$.
(ii) Numerical checks (Python): ($\star$) with $D=100r/(pL)$ has no violation on a grid of $192{,}000$ points ($40$ values $p\in[10^{-12},0.49]$, $40$ values $r\in[\max(2,L),50\max(2,L)]$, $|t|\in[10^{-6},10^6]$ of both signs); the constants $0.6514$, $2.683$ and $\min_L(0.96L+\log(50/L))=4.87>0$ were recomputed; exact enumeration for $n\le10$, $p\in\{0.3,0.1,10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-8}\}$, $r\in[2.5,100]$ and random $\alpha$ gives $\max\|Z\|_r/(C_{r,p}\|Z\|_2)\approx1.00$, well below $10e$.

**Erratum (previous version of this file).** The earlier version applied Theorem 14.9 to $|Z|^{r/2}$ and claimed the Dirichlet-form bound $\mathcal{E}(|Z|^{r/2})\le r^2B^2A_r^{r-2}$ ($B=\|Z\|_2$, $A_r=\|Z\|_r$), justified "by symmetry and Jensen", and from it $A_r\lesssim rB$ uniformly in $p$. Both are false for biased bits: the flip of a rare coordinate is weighted by its probability, so symmetry does not hold. Counterexample: $n=1$, $\alpha_1=1$, $r=3$, $p=10^{-6}$. Then $\mathcal{E}(|X|^{3/2})=pq\,(q^{3/2}-p^{3/2})^2=1.0\cdot10^{-6}$, whereas $r^2B^2A_3=9pq\,(qp^3+pq^3)^{1/3}=9.0\cdot10^{-8}$. Moreover $A_3/B=3.16,\ 10.0,\ 100.0$ at $p=10^{-3},10^{-6},10^{-12}$ (it grows like $p^{-1/6}$), so no bound $A_r\lesssim rB$ uniform in $p$ exists. The earlier proof of the regime $r\le\log(1/p)$ rested on this, and the regime $r\ge\log(1/p)$ was left unproved. The proof above replaces both.
