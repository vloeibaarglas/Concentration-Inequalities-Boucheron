**Central idea.** Truncate each summand at level $\rho=8b\log n$. The truncated, recentered process has summands bounded by $2\rho$, so Bousquet's inequality in the Bernstein form of Exercise 12.15 applies to it; this produces the Gaussian term $\exp(-t^2/(2(1+\delta)\sigma^2))$ and an exponential term at scale $\rho\asymp b\log n$. The remainder has an exponential moment at scale $2b$, which costs only $O(b)$ in the mean and a tail $2e^{-x/(2b)}$. Adding the pieces proves Adamczak's inequality in its original form, with "$+$" between the two terms. The printed "$\vee$" form follows from this only up to a factor $2$ on the right-hand side; it is not proved here.

**Solution.** **Status: incomplete**. What is proved below is the "$+$" form (Adamczak 2008), for $n\ge2$: for all $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and $\delta>0$ there is $\kappa=\kappa(\varepsilon,\delta)$ such that for all $t>0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\le\exp\Big(-\frac{t^2}{2(1+\delta)\sigma^2}\Big)+3\exp\Big(-\frac{t}{\kappa\,b\log n}\Big).\tag{A}$$
Since $x+y\le2(x\vee y)$, (A) gives the printed inequality with its right-hand side multiplied by $2$. The printed "$\vee$" form itself, with the factor $1$ and a $\kappa$ depending only on $(\varepsilon,\delta)$, is **not** proved; see the Gap section at the end. ($n\ge2$ is needed for $\log n>0$; for $n=1$ the printed right-hand side reads $\exp(-t^2/(2(1+\delta)\sigma^2))\vee0$ and the claim is not meaningful in general.)

*Reduction.* Index over $\mathcal{T}'=\mathcal{T}\times\{\pm1\}$ with $X_{i,(s,\epsilon)}=\epsilon X_{i,s}$. Then $\mathcal{T}'$ is countable, the $X_{i,(s,\epsilon)}$ are centered, $Y_i$ and $\sigma^2$ are unchanged, and $Z=\sup_{\mathcal{T}'}\sum_iX_{i,(s,\epsilon)}$. Also $Z\le\sum_iY_i$ and $\mathbb{E}Y_i\le b\log\mathbb{E}e^{Y_i/b}\le b\log2$ by Jensen, so $\mathbb{E}Z<\infty$.

*Step 1 (truncation).* Let $\rho=8b\log n$ and decompose $X_{i,s}=U_{i,s}+R_{i,s}$ with
$$U_{i,s}=X_{i,s}\mathbb 1\{Y_i\le\rho\}-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{i,s}\mathbb 1\{Y_i\le\rho\}\big],\qquad R_{i,s}=X_{i,s}\mathbb 1\{Y_i>\rho\}-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{i,s}\mathbb 1\{Y_i>\rho\}\big].$$
The identity holds because the two subtracted means add up to $\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}=0$. The vectors $U_i=(U_{i,s})_s$ are i.i.d. in $i$ (each is the same fixed function of $X_i$), centered, satisfy $|U_{i,s}|\le\rho+\rho=2\rho$, and $\mathbb{E}U_{i,s}^2=\mathrm{Var}(X_{i,s}\mathbb 1\{Y_i\le\rho\})\le\mathbb{E}X_{i,s}^2$. Put
$$Z_1=\sup_s\Big|\sum_iU_{i,s}\Big|,\qquad Z_2=\sup_s\Big|\sum_iR_{i,s}\Big|,\qquad R=\sum_iY_i\mathbb 1\{Y_i>\rho\}.$$
Then $|R_{i,s}|\le Y_i\mathbb 1\{Y_i>\rho\}+\mathbb{E}[Y_i\mathbb 1\{Y_i>\rho\}]$, so $Z_2\le R+\mathbb{E}R$. Moreover $Z\le Z_1+Z_2$ and $Z_1\le Z+Z_2$, so $\mathbb{E}Z_1\le\mathbb{E}Z+\mathbb{E}Z_2$.

*Step 2 (the remainder).* By Markov, $\mathbb{P}\{Y_1>\rho\}\le e^{-\rho/b}\mathbb{E}e^{Y_1/b}\le2n^{-8}$. By independence and Cauchy–Schwarz,
$$\mathbb{E}e^{R/(2b)}=\Big(\mathbb{P}\{Y_1\le\rho\}+\mathbb{E}\big[e^{Y_1/(2b)}\mathbb 1\{Y_1>\rho\}\big]\Big)^n\le\Big(1+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}e^{Y_1/b}}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}\{Y_1>\rho\}}\Big)^n\le(1+2n^{-4})^n\le e^{2n^{-3}}\le e^{1/4}$$
for $n\ge2$. Consequences:
- By Jensen, $\mathbb{E}R\le2b\log\mathbb{E}e^{R/(2b)}\le b/2$, hence $\mathbb{E}Z_2\le2\mathbb{E}R\le b$.
- For $x\ge b/2$: $\mathbb{P}\{Z_2\ge x\}\le\mathbb{P}\{R\ge x-b/2\}\le e^{-(x-b/2)/(2b)}\mathbb{E}e^{R/(2b)}\le e^{1/2}e^{-x/(2b)}\le2e^{-x/(2b)}$. For $x<b/2$ the bound $2e^{-x/(2b)}>1$ holds trivially.

*Step 3 (splitting the event).* Fix $\gamma\in(0,1/2)$. Since $\varepsilon<1$ and $\mathbb{E}Z_2\le b$, we have $(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}Z\ge(1+\varepsilon)(\mathbb{E}Z_1-b)\ge(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}Z_1-2b$. Using $Z\le Z_1+Z_2$,
$$\{Z\ge(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\subseteq\{Z_1\ge(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}Z_1+(1-\gamma)t-2b\}\cup\{Z_2\ge\gamma t\}.$$
*Small $t$.* If $t\le2b/\gamma$, then $3\exp(-t/(\kappa b\log n))\ge3\exp(-2/(\gamma\kappa\log2))\ge1$ as soon as $\kappa\ge2/(\gamma\log2\log3)$. In that case (A) is trivial.
*Large $t$.* If $t>2b/\gamma$, then $(1-\gamma)t-2b\ge(1-2\gamma)t$, and
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\le\mathbb{P}\{Z_1\ge(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}Z_1+(1-2\gamma)t\}+2e^{-\gamma t/(2b)}.$$

*Step 4 (the truncated process).* Let $W=Z_1/(2\rho)=\sup_{(s,\epsilon)\in\mathcal{T}'}\sum_i\epsilon U_{i,s}/(2\rho)$. Its summands are i.i.d. in $i$ and centered, and they are $\le1$, so Bousquet's inequality (Theorem 12.5) and hence Exercise 12.15 apply. The wimpy variance of $W$ is at most $\sigma^2/(4\rho^2)$, and the bound of Exercise 12.15 is increasing in the wimpy variance. Take $\eta=\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, any $\delta'>0$, and $u=(1-2\gamma)t/(2\rho)$:
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z_1\ge(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}Z_1+(1-2\gamma)t\}\le\exp\Big(-\frac{(1-2\gamma)^2t^2}{2(1+\delta')\sigma^2}\Big)+\exp\Big(-\frac{\delta'(1-2\gamma)t}{32(1+\delta')(2/\varepsilon+1/3)\,b\log n}\Big),$$
since $2(1+\delta')(2/\varepsilon+1/3)\cdot2\rho=32(1+\delta')(2/\varepsilon+1/3)b\log n$.

*Conclusion.* Choose $\gamma=\min\big(\tfrac18,\tfrac12(1-(1+\delta)^{-1/4})\big)$ and $\delta'=(1+\delta)(1-2\gamma)^2-1$. Then $1-2\gamma\ge(1+\delta)^{-1/4}$ gives $\delta'\ge(1+\delta)^{1/2}-1>0$, and $(1+\delta')/(1-2\gamma)^2=1+\delta$, so the first term equals $\exp(-t^2/(2(1+\delta)\sigma^2))$. Since $n\ge2$, $e^{-\gamma t/(2b)}\le e^{-\gamma\log2\,t/(2b\log n)}$. With
$$\kappa=\max\Big(\frac{32(1+\delta')(2/\varepsilon+1/3)}{\delta'(1-2\gamma)},\ \frac{2}{\gamma\log2}\Big)$$
(note $2/(\gamma\log2)\ge2/(\gamma\log2\log3)$, so the small-$t$ condition holds as well), Steps 3–4 give, for all $t>0$,
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z\ge(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\le\exp\Big(-\frac{t^2}{2(1+\delta)\sigma^2}\Big)+3\exp\Big(-\frac{t}{\kappa\,b\log n}\Big).$$
Here $\kappa$ depends only on $(\varepsilon,\delta)$. This proves (A), and hence the printed inequality with its right-hand side doubled. ∎ (for (A))

*Numerical checks (Python).* $\max_{n\ge2}n\log(1+2n^{-4})=0.236\le1/4$ and $e^{1/2}=1.649\le2$.

**Gap: the "$\vee$" form.** Write $G_\delta=\exp(-t^2/(2(1+\delta)\sigma^2))$ and $E_\kappa=3\exp(-t/(\kappa b\log n))$. The printed claim is $\mathbb{P}\{\cdot\}\le G_\delta\vee E_\kappa$. Proving (A) with any constants $(\delta',\kappa')$, $\delta'\le\delta$, does not yield it by comparing right-hand sides, for any $\kappa$:
- If $\delta'=\delta$, then $G_\delta+E_{\kappa'}>G_\delta\vee E_\kappa$ whenever $G_\delta\ge E_\kappa$.
- If $\delta'<\delta$, fix $t>\kappa\log3\,b\log n$, so that $E_\kappa<1$, and let $\sigma\to\infty$. Then $G_{\delta'}+E_{\kappa'}\to1+E_{\kappa'}>1$, while $G_\delta\vee E_\kappa\le1$. Numerically, with $\delta=1$, $\delta'=0.5$, $\kappa'=1$, $\kappa=10$, $b\log n=1$, $\sigma=10^3$ and $t=11.10$, the "$+$" side is $1.0000045$ and the "$\vee$" side is $0.99997$.

So the "$\vee$" form would need $\kappa$ to depend on $\sigma/(b\log n)$, or a genuinely different argument; neither is given here. This does not show that the "$\vee$" form is false. Adamczak's theorem, which the problem cites, is the "$+$" form (A). The earlier version of this file wrongly called the printed "$\vee$" statement "a true theorem (Adamczak 2008)".

*Why the earlier moment-method route was abandoned.* The earlier version tried Theorem 15.14, $\|(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_+\|_q\lesssim\sqrt q\,\sigma+q\|\max_iY_i\|_q$, with $\|\max_iY_i\|_q\le9b(q+\log n)$. That lemma is correct and follows from $\mathbb{P}\{\max_iY_i>u\}\le\min(1,2ne^{-u/b})$. But it gives a $bq^2$ term in the $q$-th moment bound. With Markov's inequality this yields at best $\exp(-O(\sqrt{t/b}))$ tails, never $\exp(-t/(\kappa b\log n))$. The truncation argument above avoids this, because it applies Bousquet's inequality to a bounded process.
