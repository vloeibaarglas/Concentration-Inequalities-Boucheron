**Central idea.** Under $0\le Z-Z_i\le M$, the $V$-version of Lemma 15.3 applies, giving the recurrence $m_q^q\le m_{q-1}^q+\frac{q}{2}\mathbb{E}[V((Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_-+M)^{q-2}]$ with coefficient $q/2$ instead of the $q$ in the $V^+$-version used for Theorem 15.7. The base case $q\in[2,3)$ follows by subadditivity as in Theorem 15.7. For $q\ge3$, the $\theta$-splitting of $((Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_-+M)^{q-2}$ does NOT close the induction: its last-term factor $(1+1/\sqrt{C_2q})^{\,q-3}$ is unbounded in $q$ (it grows like $e^{\sqrt{q/C_2}}$), so no finite $C_2$ works that way. Instead, split on the size of $m_q$ itself: if $m_q\le\sqrt{C_2}\,q\|M\|_q$ then $x_q\le1$ directly; otherwise $(1+\|M\|_q/m_q)^{q-2}\le e^{1/\sqrt{C_2}}$, and the concavity argument closes with $C_2$ solving $C_2=e^{1/\sqrt{C_2}}/(2(1-e^{-1/2}))<2.42$.

**Solution.** Write $m_q=\|(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_-\|_q$ and $c_q=\|V\|_{1\vee q/2}\vee q\|M\|_q^2$. Since $0\le Z-Z_i\le M$, Lemma 15.3 ($V$-version) with $\alpha=q-1$ gives, for $q\ge 2$,
$$m_q^q\le m_{q-1}^q+\frac{q}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[V\big((Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_-+M\big)^{q-2}\big].\tag{*}$$
For $q\in[1,2]$, H\"older and Efron--Stein ($\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\mathbb{E}V$) give $m_q\le\sqrt{\|V\|_1}$; hence $m_{q-1}\le\sqrt{c_{q-1}}\le\sqrt{c_q}$ for $q\in[2,3)$. If $c_q=0$ then $(*)$ and the base case give $m_q=0$; assume $c_q>0$ and put $x_q=(m_q/\sqrt{C_2qc_q})^q$. Note $c_q$ is nondecreasing in $q$ (Lyapunov's inequality for $\|V\|_{q/2}$, and $q\|M\|_q^2$ is a product of nondecreasing factors).

*Base case $q\in[2,3)$.* By subadditivity of $x\mapsto x^{q-2}$ and H\"older applied to $(*)$,
$$m_q^q\le m_{q-1}^q+\frac{q}{2}\|V\|_{q/2}\|M\|_q^{\,q-2}+\frac{q}{2}\|V\|_{q/2}m_q^{\,q-2}.$$
Since $\|V\|_{q/2}\le c_q$ and $\|M\|_q^{\,q-2}\le(c_q/q)^{(q-2)/2}$, the middle term is at most $\frac12q^{(4-q)/2}c_q^{q/2}\le\frac32c_q^{q/2}$ for $q\in[2,3)$, because $\frac{4-q}{2}\ln q<\ln3$ there, so $q^{(4-q)/2}<3$. Dividing by $(C_2qc_q)^{q/2}$ and using $(C_2q)^{-q/2}\le1/(2C_2)$ for $q\ge2$,
$$x_q\le\frac{1}{2C_2}+\frac{3}{4C_2}+\frac{1}{2C_2}x_q^{1-2/q}.$$
The function $g_q(x)=\frac{5}{4C_2}+\frac1{2C_2}x^{1-2/q}-x$ is strictly concave on $[0,\infty)$, $g_q(0)>0$, and $g_q(1)=\frac{7}{4C_2}-1<0$ since $C_2>2.4>7/4$; since $g_q(x_q)\ge0$, concavity forces $x_q\le1$, i.e. $m_q\le\sqrt{C_2qc_q}$ for $q\in[2,3)$.

*Induction step $q\ge3$.* By H\"older (exponents $q/2$, $q/(q-2)$) and Minkowski applied to $(*)$,
$$m_q^q\le m_{q-1}^q+\frac{q}{2}\|V\|_{q/2}\big\|(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_-+M\big\|_q^{\,q-2}\le m_{q-1}^q+\frac{q}{2}c_q\big(m_q+\|M\|_q\big)^{q-2}.\tag{**}$$
We argue by induction on $k\ge2$ for $q\in[k,k+1)$, assuming $m_{q-1}\le\sqrt{C_2(q-1)c_{q-1}}$. Then
$$\frac{m_{q-1}^q}{(C_2qc_q)^{q/2}}\le\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)^{q/2}\le e^{-1/2}.\tag{***}$$
If $m_q\le\sqrt{C_2}\,q\|M\|_q$, then since $\|M\|_q\le\sqrt{c_q/q}$,
$$m_q\le\sqrt{C_2}\,q\sqrt{c_q/q}=\sqrt{C_2qc_q},\qquad\text{i.e. }x_q\le1,$$
directly. Otherwise $\|M\|_q/m_q<1/(\sqrt{C_2}q)$, and
$$\big(m_q+\|M\|_q\big)^{q-2}=m_q^{q-2}\left(1+\frac{\|M\|_q}{m_q}\right)^{q-2}\le m_q^{q-2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_2}q}\right)^{q-2}\le e^{1/\sqrt{C_2}}m_q^{q-2},$$
using $1+x\le e^x$. Inserting into $(**)$ and dividing by $(C_2qc_q)^{q/2}$, with $(***)$,
$$x_q\le\left(1-\frac1q\right)^{q/2}+\frac{e^{1/\sqrt{C_2}}}{2C_2}\,x_q^{1-2/q}=:f_q(x_q),$$
since $\frac{q}{2}c_qe^{1/\sqrt{C_2}}m_q^{q-2}/(C_2qc_q)^{q/2}=\frac{e^{1/\sqrt{C_2}}}{2C_2}x_q^{(q-2)/q}$.
The function $f_q$ is strictly concave on $[0,\infty)$ (as $0<(q-2)/q<1$) with $f_q(0)=(1-1/q)^{q/2}>0$. Defining $C_2$ by $e^{-1/2}+e^{1/\sqrt{C_2}}/(2C_2)=1$, i.e.
$$C_2=\frac{e^{1/\sqrt{C_2}}}{2(1-e^{-1/2})}\approx2.4176<2.42,$$
gives $f_q(1)\le e^{-1/2}+e^{1/\sqrt{C_2}}/(2C_2)-1=0$. Since $g_q:=f_q-\mathrm{id}$ is strictly concave with $g_q(0)>0\ge g_q(1)$ and $g_q(x_q)\ge0$, strict concavity forces $x_q\le1$ (past the unique zero of $g_q$ in $(0,1]$, $g_q$ is strictly negative).

Thus $m_q\le\sqrt{C_2qc_q}$ for all $q\ge2$, i.e.
$$\|(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_-\|_q\le\sqrt{C_2q\big(\|V\|_{q/2}\vee q\|M\|_q^2\big)},\qquad q\ge2,$$
with $C_2<2.42$. ∎

*Remark (audit note).* An earlier draft bounded the $q\ge3$ step by the $\theta$-split $\big((Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_-+M\big)^{q-2}\le\theta^{-q+3}M^{q-2}+(1-\theta)^{-q+3}(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_-^{\,q-2}$ with $\theta=1/(1+\sqrt{C_2q})$, claiming $(1+1/\sqrt{C_2q})^{q-2}\le e^{1/\sqrt{C_2}}$ and dropping the unbounded last-term factor $(1-\theta)^{-q+3}=(1+1/\sqrt{C_2q})^{q-3}$ from the fixed-point equation. Both claims are false: $(1+1/\sqrt{C_2q})^{q-3}\sim e^{\sqrt{q/C_2}}\to\infty$, so no finite $C_2$ closes the induction that way. The case split above repairs this. (The threshold $\sqrt{C_2}$ is optimal: with threshold $\beta q\|M\|_q$ the fixed point is $C=e^{1/\beta}/(2(1-e^{-1/2}))$ subject to $\beta\le\sqrt C$, minimized at $\beta=\sqrt C$.)
