**Central idea.** Symmetrization replaces the centered sum $\sum_i X_i$ by the Rademacher sum $\sum_i \varepsilon_i X_i$ at the cost of a factor $2$; conditioning on the $X_i$, Theorem 15.9 (Khinchine's inequality) bounds the $q$-th moment of the Rademacher sum by $(2^{1/q}\sqrt{2Kq})^q(\sum_i X_i^2)^{q/2}$, and taking expectations over $X$ turns $(\sum_i X_i^2)^{q/2}$ into $\|\sum_i X_i^2\|_{q/2}^{q/2}$. The constant $2^{1+1/q}$ is exactly the symmetrization factor $2$ times Khinchine's $2^{1/q}$.

**Solution.** Let $X_1',\ldots,X_n'$ be independent copies of $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ and let $\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n$ be i.i.d. Rademacher variables independent of all the $X_i, X_i'$. If $\|\sum_i X_i^2\|_{q/2} = \infty$ there is nothing to prove, so assume it is finite; then $\mathbb{E}|X_i|^q \le \mathbb{E}[(\sum_j X_j^2)^{q/2}] < \infty$ for each $i$, so all moments below exist. Theorem 15.9 is stated for integer $q \ge 2$, but its proof (via Theorem 15.4 and Lemma 15.1, the latter stated for real $q > 2$) works verbatim for every real $q \ge 2$, which we use.

Since $\mathbb{E}X_i = 0$, writing $\mathbb{E}_{X'}$ for expectation over the copies,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n X_i = \mathbb{E}_{X'}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - X_i')\Big].$$
By Jensen's inequality for conditional expectations ($|\cdot|^q$ is convex for $q \ge 1$),
$$\Big|\sum_i X_i\Big|^q \le \mathbb{E}_{X'}\Big|\sum_i (X_i - X_i')\Big|^q,$$
and taking $\mathbb{E}_X$ gives $\|\sum_i X_i\|_q \le \|\sum_i (X_i - X_i')\|_q$. Each $X_i - X_i'$ is symmetric, so $\sum_i (X_i - X_i') \stackrel{d}{=} \sum_i \varepsilon_i (X_i - X_i')$, and by Minkowski's inequality,
$$\Big\|\sum_i X_i\Big\|_q \le \Big\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i (X_i - X_i')\Big\|_q \le \Big\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i X_i\Big\|_q + \Big\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i X_i'\Big\|_q = 2\Big\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i X_i\Big\|_q. \tag{1}$$

Condition on $X_1,\ldots,X_n$. Theorem 15.9 applied to the Rademacher sum $\sum_i \varepsilon_i a_i$ with the (nonrandom, given the conditioning) coefficients $a_i = X_i$ gives, for $q \ge 2$,
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\Big|\sum_i \varepsilon_i X_i\Big|^q \le \Big(2^{1/q}\sqrt{2Kq}\Big)^q \Big(\sum_i X_i^2\Big)^{q/2} = 2(2Kq)^{q/2}\Big(\sum_i X_i^2\Big)^{q/2},$$
where $K = 1/(e-\sqrt{e}) < 0.935$ is the constant from Theorems 15.4 and 15.9. (The proof of Theorem 15.9 uses only $a_i^2$, via $V^+ \le 2\sum_i a_i^2$, so it applies to arbitrary real $a_i$, not just $a_i > 0$.) Taking expectations over $X$,
$$\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_i \varepsilon_i X_i\Big|^q \le 2(2Kq)^{q/2}\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_i X_i^2\Big)^{q/2}\Big] = 2(2Kq)^{q/2}\Big\|\sum_i X_i^2\Big\|_{q/2}^{q/2},$$
and taking $q$-th roots,
$$\Big\|\sum_i \varepsilon_i X_i\Big\|_q \le 2^{1/q}\sqrt{2Kq}\,\sqrt{\Big\|\sum_i X_i^2\Big\|_{q/2}}. \tag{2}$$
Combining (1) and (2) yields, for every $q \ge 2$,
$$\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\right\|_q \leq 2^{1+1/q}\sqrt{2Kq}\sqrt{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2\right\|_{q/2}},$$
which is Marcinkiewicz' inequality. ∎
