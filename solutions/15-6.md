**Central idea.** Write $Z=\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i$ with $Y_i=a_i(X_i^4-3)$ independent and centered: the variance is then exact by independence, and for $\|Z_+\|_q$ the right tool is the Rosenthal-type inequality of Theorem 15.11, which bounds $\|Z_+\|_q$ by the variance term $\sigma\sqrt{q}$ plus a maximum term $q\|Y\|_q$ with $Y=\max_i|Y_i|$; the only remaining work is the Gaussian moment $\|X^4-3\|_q$, controlled via $(4q-1)!!\le(4q)^{2q}$.

**Solution.** Put $Y_i=a_i(X_i^4-3)$, so the $Y_i$ are independent, $\mathbb{E}Y_i=0$, and $Z=\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i$ with $\mathbb{E}Z=0$. For a standard Gaussian, $\mathbb{E}X^{2k}=(2k-1)!!$, hence $\mathbb{E}X^4=3!!=3=\mu$ and $\mathbb{E}X^8=7!!=105$. Therefore
$$\mathrm{Var}(Y_i)=a_i^2\big(\mathbb{E}X_i^8-(\mathbb{E}X_i^4)^2\big)=96a_i^2,$$
and by independence
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathrm{Var}(Y_i)=96\sum_{i=1}^n a_i^2.$$

For the $q$-th moment of the positive part, apply Theorem 15.11 to the centered independent variables $Y_1,\dots,Y_n$. With $\sigma^2=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}Y_i^2=96\sum_{i=1}^n a_i^2$ and $Y=\max_{1\le i\le n}|Y_i|$, for every integer $q\ge 2$,
$$\|Z_+\|_q \le \sigma\sqrt{6\kappa q}+q\kappa\sqrt{2}\,\|Y\|_q,$$
where $\kappa$ is the universal constant from Theorem 15.5. Now
$$\|Y\|_q^q=\mathbb{E}\max_{1\le i\le n}|a_i|^q|X_i^4-3|^q\le\sum_{i=1}^n|a_i|^q\,\mathbb{E}|X^4-3|^q,$$
so $\|Y\|_q\le\big(\sum_{i=1}^n|a_i|^q\big)^{1/q}\|X^4-3\|_q$. By Minkowski's inequality,
$$\|X^4-3\|_q\le\|X^4\|_q+3=\big((4q-1)!!\big)^{1/q}+3.$$
Since $(2m-1)!!=\prod_{j=1}^m(2j-1)\le\prod_{j=1}^m 2j=2^m m!\le(2m)^m$, with $2m=4q$,
$$\big((4q-1)!!\big)^{1/q}\le\big((4q)^{2q}\big)^{1/q}=16q^2,$$
hence $\|X^4-3\|_q\le 16q^2+3\le 17q^2$ for $q\ge 2$. Consequently, for integer $q\ge 2$,
$$\|Z_+\|_q \le 24\sqrt{\kappa q\sum_{i=1}^n a_i^2}+17\sqrt{2}\,\kappa q^3\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n|a_i|^q\Big)^{1/q}.$$
For real $q\ge 2$, monotonicity of $L_q$ norms gives $\|Z_+\|_q\le\|Z_+\|_{\lceil q\rceil}$, and the same bound holds with $q$ replaced by $\lceil q\rceil\le q+1$.
