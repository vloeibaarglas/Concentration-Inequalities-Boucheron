**Central idea.** This is the Gluskin–Kwapień splitting: a Bernstein-type tail for sums of symmetric exponentials gives the crude moment bound $\|Z\|_p \lesssim \sqrt p\,\sigma + pM$; splitting the sum at the $p$-th largest weight, the first block is controlled by the exponential term ($p s_1 \le p\sum_{i\le p}s_i$) and the remaining block of small weights by the Gaussian term, with monotonicity of $s$ absorbing the leftover $p s_{p+1}$ into $\sum_{i\le p}s_i$.

**Solution.** The $X_i$ are Laplace$(1)$: density $\tfrac12e^{-|x|}$, $\mathbb{E}X_i=0$, $\mathbb{E}X_i^2=2$, and $\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda X_i}=(1-\lambda^2)^{-1}$ for $|\lambda|<1$. For real $p\ge2$ interpret $\sum_{i=1}^{p}s_i=\sum_{1\le i\le p}s_i$ and $\sum_{i=p+1}^{n}s_i^2=\sum_{p<i\le n}s_i^2$; put $m=\lfloor p\rfloor\wedge n\ge1$.

*Bernstein bound.* Let $Z=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_iX_i$, $\sigma^2=2\sum a_i^2=\mathrm{Var}(Z)$, $M=\max_i|a_i|$ (if $M=0$ there is nothing to prove). For $0\le\lambda\le1/(2M)$ we have $\lambda^2a_i^2\le1/4$, and $-\log(1-x)\le2x$ on $[0,1/4]$ (since $x\mapsto\log(1-x)+2x$ has derivative $(1-2x)/(1-x)\ge0$ there), so
$$\log\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda Z}=\sum_i-\log(1-\lambda^2a_i^2)\le2\lambda^2\sum_ia_i^2=\lambda^2\sigma^2.$$
Chernoff's bound $\mathbb{P}\{Z>t\}\le e^{-\lambda t+\lambda^2\sigma^2}$ with $\lambda=t/(2\sigma^2)$ for $t\le\sigma^2/M$ and $\lambda=1/(2M)$ for $t>\sigma^2/M$ gives, using symmetry for the lower tail,
$$\mathbb{P}\{|Z|>t\}\le2\exp\Big(-\tfrac14\min\big(t^2/\sigma^2,\;t/M\big)\Big),\qquad t\ge0.\tag{1}$$

*Tail to moment.* For $q\ge1$, $\|Z\|_q^q=\int_0^\infty qt^{q-1}\mathbb{P}\{|Z|>t\}\,dt$. With $t_0=\sigma^2/M$ and (1),
\begin{align*}
\|Z\|_q^q &\le 2q\!\int_0^{t_0}\!t^{q-1}e^{-t^2/(4\sigma^2)}dt+2q\!\int_{t_0}^{\infty}\!t^{q-1}e^{-t/(4M)}dt\\
&\le 2q\!\int_0^{\infty}\!t^{q-1}e^{-t^2/(4\sigma^2)}dt+2q\!\int_0^{\infty}\!t^{q-1}e^{-t/(4M)}dt.
\end{align*}
With $u=t^2/(4\sigma^2)$ the first integral is $q2^q\sigma^q\Gamma(q/2)$; with $u=t/(4M)$ the second is $2q(4M)^q\Gamma(q)$ (the prefactor is $2q$, not $q$: the integrand carries the full $2q$ from the tail bound). Using $\Gamma(x)\le x^x$ for $x\ge1$ (standard, from log-convexity of $\Gamma$ and the integer case),
$$q2^q\sigma^q\Gamma(q/2)\le q(\sigma\sqrt{2q})^q,\qquad 2q(4M)^q\Gamma(q)\le2q(4qM)^q.$$
Since $q^{1/q}\le2$ for $q\ge1$, taking $q$-th roots and using $(a^q+b^q)^{1/q}\le a+b$: the first term contributes at most $q^{1/q}2\sigma\sqrt{q/2}\le2\sqrt2\sqrt q\,\sigma$, the second at most $(2q)^{1/q}4Mq\le8qM$, so
$$\|Z\|_q\le C_0(\sqrt q\,\sigma+qM)\tag{2}$$
for an absolute constant $C_0$ (e.g. $C_0=16$), valid for every real $q\ge1$.

*Splitting.* Write $Z=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_iX_i=Z_1+Z_2$ with $Z_1=\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_iX_i$, $Z_2=\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}s_iX_i$ (empty sum $=0$). Apply (2) with $q=p$ to $Z_1$: $\sigma_1^2=2\sum_{i\le m}s_i^2\le2ms_1^2$ and $M_1=s_1$, so with $m\le p$,
$$\|Z_1\|_p\le C_0\big(\sqrt p\sqrt{2m}\,s_1+ps_1\big)\le C_0(\sqrt2+1)\,p\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_i,$$
using $s_1\le\sum_{i\le m}s_i$ (as $s_i\ge0$, $m\ge1$). Apply (2) to $Z_2$ (if $m<n$; otherwise $Z_2=0$): $\sigma_2^2=2\sum_{i>m}s_i^2$, $M_2=s_{m+1}$, so
$$\|Z_2\|_p\le C_0\sqrt{2p\Big(\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}s_i^2\Big)}+C_0ps_{m+1}.$$
Since $s$ is nonincreasing, $s_{m+1}\le s_m\le\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_i$, whence $ps_{m+1}\le\frac pm\sum_{i\le m}s_i\le2\sum_{i\le m}s_i$, because $m=\lfloor p\rfloor\ge p-1\ge p/2$ for $p\ge2$ (when $\lfloor p\rfloor\le n$). Thus
$$\|Z_2\|_p\le C_0\sqrt{2p\Big(\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}s_i^2\Big)}+2C_0\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_i.$$

Finally, by the triangle inequality and $p\ge1$,
\begin{align*}
\|Z\|_p&\le\|Z_1\|_p+\|Z_2\|_p\\
&\le\kappa\left(p\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_i+\sqrt{p\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}s_i^2}\right)
\end{align*}
with $\kappa=C_0(\sqrt2+3)+C_0\sqrt2$ absolute, i.e.
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_iX_i\Big|^p\Big]^{1/p}\le\kappa\left(p\sum_{i=1}^{p}s_i+\sqrt{p\sum_{i=p+1}^{n}s_i^2}\right),$$
with the stated convention for non-integer $p$. ∎
