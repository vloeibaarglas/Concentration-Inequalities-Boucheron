**Central idea.** Apply the Efron–Stein inequality and the generalized Efron–Stein moment inequality (Theorem 15.5) to the supremum $Z$. The key step is controlling the Efron–Stein proxy $V^+$: resampling $X_i$ can raise the supremum by at most $A_i|X_i^4-(X_i')^4|$ with $A_i=\sup_s|a_{i,s}|$. Gaussian moments ($\mathbb{E}X^8=105$, $\|X\|_p\le\sqrt p$) then give explicit constants for $\mathrm{Var}(Z)$ and for the centered moments $\|(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_+\|_q$. The uncentered moments follow from $Z_+\le(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_++\mathbb{E}Z$, and the mean $\mathbb{E}Z$ must be kept as a separate term (it is bounded by $\mathbb{E}|X^4-3|\sum_iA_i$, and it cannot be absorbed into the centered moments).

**Solution.** Put $\mu=\mathbb{E}X_i^4=3$ and $Y_s=\sum_{i=1}^n a_{i,s}(X_i^4-3)$, so $Z=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}Y_s$ with $\mathcal{T}$ countable. Let $A_i:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}|a_{i,s}|$ and $S=(\sum_iA_i^2)^{1/2}$; we assume all $A_i<\infty$ (otherwise the bounds below are trivial). Then $Y_{s_0}\le Z\le\sum_iA_i|X_i^4-3|$ for any fixed $s_0$, so $Z$ is integrable, and $\mathbb{E}Z\ge\mathbb{E}Y_{s_0}=0$.

For each $i$, let $X_i'$ be an independent copy of $X_i$, $X^{(i)}$ the vector $X$ with $X_i$ replaced by $X_i'$, and $Z^{(i)}=\sup_s Y_s^{(i)}$ defined analogously. Since $\sup_s Y_s\le\sup_s(Y_s-Y_s^{(i)})+\sup_s Y_s^{(i)}$,
$$(Z-Z^{(i)})_+\le\Bigl(\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}(Y_s-Y_s^{(i)})\Bigr)_+=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}(Y_s-Y_s^{(i)})_+,$$
and $Y_s-Y_s^{(i)}=a_{i,s}(X_i^4-(X_i')^4)$. Therefore
$$(Z-Z^{(i)})_+\le A_i\,|X_i^4-(X_i')^4|,\qquad (Z-Z^{(i)})_+^2\le A_i^2\,(X_i^4-(X_i')^4)^2.$$
Hence, with $V^+=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}[(Z-Z^{(i)})_+^2\mid X]$ as in Theorem 15.5,
$$V^+\le\sum_{i=1}^n A_i^2\,\mathbb{E}[(X_i^4-(X_i')^4)^2\mid X_i]=\sum_{i=1}^n A_i^2\,(X_i^8-6X_i^4+105),$$
since $\mathbb{E}[(X_i')^4]=3$ and $\mathbb{E}[(X_i')^8]=105$.

*Variance.* By the Efron–Stein inequality (Theorem 3.1), $\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le\mathbb{E}V^+$, and $\mathbb{E}[X_i^8-6X_i^4+105]=105-18+105=192$, so
$$\mathrm{Var}(Z)\le 192\sum_{i=1}^n\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}a_{i,s}^2.$$

*Centered moments.* For $q\ge2$, Theorem 15.5 gives $\|(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_+\|_q\le\sqrt{2\kappa_q q}\,\|\sqrt{V^+}\|_q$ with $\kappa_q=\frac12(1-(1-1/q)^{q/2})^{-1}\uparrow\kappa=\frac{\sqrt e}{2(\sqrt e-1)}<1.271$ (the $\sqrt q$ is essential: e.g. for a Rademacher sum, $\|Z_+\|_q\asymp\sqrt{qn}$, so no $q$-independent multiple of $\|\sqrt{V^+}\|_q$ can work). By Minkowski in $L^{q/2}$ and the triangle inequality,
$$\|V^+\|_{q/2}\le\sum_{i=1}^n A_i^2\,\|X_i^8-6X_i^4+105\|_{q/2}\le\sum_{i=1}^n A_i^2\bigl(\|X_i^8\|_{q/2}+6\|X_i^4\|_{q/2}+105\bigr).$$
For $X\sim N(0,1)$, $\|X\|_p\le\sqrt p$ ($p\ge1$), so $\|X_i^8\|_{q/2}=\|X_i\|_{4q}^8\le(4q)^4=256q^4$ and $\|X_i^4\|_{q/2}=\|X_i\|_{2q}^4\le(2q)^2=4q^2$. For $q\ge2$, $256q^4+24q^2+105\le269q^4$, whence $\|\sqrt{V^+}\|_q=\|V^+\|_{q/2}^{1/2}\le\sqrt{269}\,q^2S$ and
$$\|(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_+\|_q\le\sqrt{538\kappa_q q}\;q^2\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^n\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}a_{i,s}^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le\sqrt{538\kappa}\;q^{5/2}S,\qquad q\ge2.$$

*Uncentered moments.* Since $\mathbb{E}Z\ge0$, $Z_+\le(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_++\mathbb{E}Z$ pointwise (trivial if $Z\le0$; otherwise $Z=(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)+\mathbb{E}Z$). By Minkowski,
$$\|Z_+\|_q\le\mathbb{E}Z+\sqrt{538\kappa_q q}\;q^2S,\qquad q\ge2.$$
Moreover $\mathbb{E}Z\le\mathbb{E}\sum_iA_i|X_i^4-3|=c_4\sum_{i=1}^nA_i$ with $c_4:=\mathbb{E}|X^4-3|=4.1801\ldots$, so explicitly
$$\|Z_+\|_q\le4.181\sum_{i=1}^n\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}|a_{i,s}|+\sqrt{538\kappa_q q}\;q^2\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^n\sup_{s\in\mathcal{T}}a_{i,s}^2\Bigr)^{1/2},\qquad q\ge2.$$
∎

*Remark (the mean term is necessary).* No bound of the form $\|Z_+\|_q\le C(q)\,S$ holds with $C(q)$ independent of $n$. Take $\mathcal{T}=\{\pm1\}^n$ and $a_{i,s}=s_i/\sqrt n$. Then $S=1$ and $Z=n^{-1/2}\sum_i|X_i^4-3|$, so $\mathbb{E}Z=c_4\sqrt n$ is unbounded in $n$. In this example the $\mathbb{E}Z$ bound $c_4\sum_iA_i=c_4\sqrt n$ is attained with equality.

**Erratum.** A previous version of this file concluded
$$\|Z_+\|_q\le2\sqrt{538\kappa_qq}\,q^2S\le Cq^{5/2}S,\qquad C=2\sqrt{538\kappa}<53,$$
via the step $(\mathbb{E}Z)_+\le\mathbb{E}[(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_+]$. That step is false: the right side depends only on fluctuations, the left side on the location. Counterexample (the example of the Remark, $n=10^4$, $q=2$): $c_4=4.180129$ and $\mathbb{E}(X^4-3)^2=96$ (numerical integration), so $\mathbb{E}Z=418.013$, $\mathrm{Var}(Z)=96-c_4^2=78.53$, and $\|Z_+\|_2=\sqrt{(\mathbb{E}Z)^2+\mathrm{Var}(Z)}=418.107$ (Monte Carlo with $2000$ draws: $418.10$). The old bound gives $2\sqrt{538\cdot\kappa_2\cdot2}\cdot2^2=262.42$ (with $\kappa_2=1$), and $53\cdot2^{5/2}=299.8$; both are below $418.1$. The false step itself fails by a factor of about $47$: $(\mathbb{E}Z)_+=418.0$, whereas $\mathbb{E}[(Z-\mathbb{E}Z)_+]\le\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(Z)}=8.86$. The corrected bound above gives $418.01+\sqrt{538\cdot2}\cdot4=549.2\ge418.1$. The variance bound and the centered bound of the earlier version are unaffected and are retained above.
