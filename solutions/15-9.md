**Central idea.** Apply Bernstein's inequality at the inflated deviation $s=\theta\mathbb{E}Z+t$; the $\mathbb{E}Z$-dependent part of the variance factor is absorbed into an effective scale $A=1/3+2/\theta$ via $2\mathbb{E}Z\le 2s/\theta$, and the resulting exponent $s^{2}/(2(\sigma^{2}+As))=1/(u+w)$ is split into a Gaussian term and an exponential term by Adamczak's elementary estimate $\exp(-1/(u+w))\le\exp(-\lambda/u)\vee\exp(-(1-\lambda)/w)$. One caveat: as stated the exercise omits the hypothesis $\mathbb{E}Z\ge0$, without which it is false — see the Solution.

**Solution.** The claimed inequality is false without the additional hypothesis $\mathbb{E}Z\ge0$ (which holds in the intended application, e.g. Adamczak (2008), where $Z\ge0$ a.s.). Counterexample: let $Z\equiv-1$, so $\mathbb{E}Z=-1$, and take $\sigma^{2}=2$, so the variance factor is $\sigma^{2}+2\mathbb{E}Z=0$. Then $Z$ satisfies Bernstein's inequality with variance factor $0$ and scale factor $1/3$: for $u>0$, $\mathbb{P}\{Z-\mathbb{E}Z\ge u\}=\mathbb{P}\{0\ge u\}=0\le e^{-3u/2}$, and both sides equal $1$ at $u=0$. But with $\theta=1$, $t=1$, $\lambda=1/2$, the deviation is $\theta\mathbb{E}Z+t=0$, so the left-hand side equals $\mathbb{P}\{0\ge0\}=1$, while the right-hand side is $e^{-1/8}\vee e^{-3/28}\approx0.898<1$. We prove the corrected statement under $\mathbb{E}Z\ge0$.

*Hint.* Let $u,v>0$ and $0\le\lambda\le1$. If $\exp(-1/(u+v))>\exp(-\lambda/u)$ and $\exp(-1/(u+v))>\exp(-(1-\lambda)/v)$, then $1/(u+v)<\lambda/u$ and $1/(u+v)<(1-\lambda)/v$, i.e. $\lambda(u+v)>u$ and $(1-\lambda)(u+v)>v$; adding gives the contradiction $u+v>u+v$. Hence $\exp(-1/(u+v))\le\exp(-\lambda/u)\vee\exp(-(1-\lambda)/v)$. The same conclusion holds for $u=0$ (with $\exp(-\lambda/0):=0$ for $\lambda>0$ and $:=1$ for $\lambda=0$): if $\lambda>0$ the right side is $\exp(-(1-\lambda)/v)\ge\exp(-1/v)$, and if $\lambda=0$ it equals $1$.

Now fix $\theta>0$ and $\lambda\in[0,1]$. If $t\le0$, then $-(1-\lambda)t\ge0$, so $\exp(-(1-\lambda)t/(2(1/3+2/\theta)))\ge1$ and the claimed bound is trivial. Assume $t>0$ and put $s=\theta\mathbb{E}Z+t$. Since $\mathbb{E}Z\ge0$ and $\theta>0$, $s\ge t>0$. Bernstein's inequality with variance factor $v:=\sigma^{2}+2\mathbb{E}Z$ and scale factor $c:=1/3$ gives
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z-\mathbb{E}Z\ge s\}\le\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{2(v+cs)}\right).$$
Since $2s/\theta-2\mathbb{E}Z=2t/\theta\ge0$,
$$v+cs=\sigma^{2}+2\mathbb{E}Z+cs\le\sigma^{2}+\frac{2s}{\theta}+cs=\sigma^{2}+As,\qquad A:=\frac13+\frac{2}{\theta},$$
so that
$$\mathbb{P}\{Z-\mathbb{E}Z\ge\theta\mathbb{E}Z+t\}\le\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+As)}\right).$$
Writing $\frac{s^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+As)}=\frac{1}{u+w}$ with $u:=\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{s^{2}}\ge0$ and $w:=\frac{2A}{s}>0$, the hint yields
$$\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+As)}\right)\le\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\vee\exp\left(-\frac{(1-\lambda)s}{2A}\right).$$
Finally $s\ge t$ gives $\exp(-\lambda s^{2}/(2\sigma^{2}))\le\exp(-\lambda t^{2}/(2\sigma^{2}))$ and $\exp(-(1-\lambda)s/(2A))\le\exp(-(1-\lambda)t/(2A))$, which is the claimed bound. ∎
